## 问题

近年美国公司在政治领域越来越积极：反对某些法案、推动各种社会议题，常常是应股东或员工团体的要求。这些目标也许值得称赞，但有可能疏远大量客户和员工。CEO 该怎么决定在哪些议题上表态，或者公司到底该不该介入政治？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特说这是他认真想过很多次的问题：他当 CEO 时说过「我不会把公民身份存进盲目信托」，但结论改了。
2. 一条经验：就任何议题发声，「你能让很多人长期恼火，却只能让少数人短暂高兴」。
3. 代价由别人承担：客户会针对公司，员工可能因此被裁，股东受损——「凭什么让他们付这个价？」
4. 所以他「明确退后」：不愿让「伯克希尔的巴菲特」这个标签替他讲的话买单；「如果我认为发声这么重要，我就该辞掉这份我最爱的工作」。
5. 两人性格不同：芒格说自己比巴菲特更得小心说话，巴菲特则「忍不住多说两句」。
6. 根源是媒体归因：标题写「巴菲特在买某某」，实际可能是另外两个人的决定；最简单的解法就是闭嘴，不要制造别人没问的问题。

## 关键引述

> **“I don't put my citizenship in a blind trust when I take the job as CEO of Berkshire.”**
>
> 「我出任伯克希尔 CEO 时，并没有把自己的公民身份放进一个盲目信托。」

> **“you can make a whole lot more people sustainably mad than you can make temporarily happy by speaking out on any subject”**
>
> 「在任何议题上发声，你能让更多人长期恼火，而只能让更少人短暂高兴。」

> **“I'm not going to go around and take positions”**
>
> 「我不打算四处去站队表态。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:00:48）：第四提问点。

**Jeff Vuvoloy**（00:00:52）：沃伦、查理，你们好。我是 Jeff Vuvoloy，旧金山的股东。近几年，美国公司在政治领域扮演了更积极的角色：或者公开反对某些法案，或者推动各种社会议题，往往是应股东或员工团体的要求。这些运动的目标也许值得称赞，但它们可能疏远相当一部分客户和员工。CEO 该怎么决定在哪些议题上表态，或者公司到底该不该介入政治？谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（00:01:58）：这是个很好的问题，显然也是我必须反复想的问题。我曾经说过：「我出任伯克希尔 CEO 时，并没有把自己的公民身份放进一个盲目信托。」但我也学到：在任何议题上发声，你能让更多人长期恼火，而只能让更少人短暂高兴。在某些议题上，他们会把气撒在我们公司身上，那意味着我们的员工，其中有些人最后会被我们裁掉；意味着股东受损。我真的认为我对每一个可能让人极度不满的议题都必须说话吗？他们该被要求付这个代价吗？我得出结论：不该。我为什么要伤害那边房间里为伯克希尔做各种事的人？我为什么要因为说了一句全国 20% 的人立刻反对的话，就伤害你们？有时他们会恼火到试图报复，而他们没法冲我喊，就可能会针对我们的公司发起运动。

所以，就我而言，我不会四处去站队表态，因为那样说出来的不会是「巴菲特说」，而会变成「伯克希尔·哈撒韦」，或者「伯克希尔·哈撒韦的巴菲特」，我被绑在一起。我不愿意让你们的处境因此变糟，我已经决定了，我不会那么做。如果我真想那么做，我就该辞职。如果我认为作为公民发声那么重要，我就放弃我最爱的东西，也就是这份工作。（笑）我不想那样。所以我已经明确退后了：我不想说任何会被归到伯克希尔头上的话，让别人替我讲的话承担后果。这就是我的立场。我还要说，在大多数公司，或者很多公司，这样说不太公平，但在非常多的公司里，CEO 得考虑董事会怎么说，而他们特意选了一些社会接受的人进董事会，代表不同群体，有时立场非常强。

如果他们要顾及这个群体、那个群体、再一个群体的利益相关方，董事会就会压他们表态。这是一块我们不打算踏进去的地。查理，你怎么看？

**Charlie Munger**（00:05:13）：这个嘛，我比你更得小心自己说的话。（笑声）那么——（笑声）

**Male Voice**（00:05:51）：是啊。这——（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:05:55）：我们两个的区别是，我忍不住要多说两句。（笑声）我一次又一次在报纸上看到标题写「巴菲特在买某某」。可我没有在买某某，是伯克希尔·哈撒韦在买，而且可能是伯克希尔另外两个人的手笔。写文章的人根本不知道那是我授意的，还是我压根没听说过。但标题写「巴菲特买入」比写「伯克希尔·哈撒韦买入，我们不知道是他手下还是他本人」更能吸引人。标题就是为了把人引进故事里。这种混淆很糟糕。而最简单的办法就是闭嘴，不要给别人一堆他们本来没问的、让人着迷的问题。不过我很高兴你问了这个问题，这是个好问题。我大概想这个问题的时间，比想这只股票那只股票便宜不便宜还多。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:00:48）：Station four.

**Jeff Vuvoloy**（00:00:52）：Hi Warren and Charlie. I'm Jeff Vuvoloy (PH), shareholder from San Francisco. In recent years, American companies have taken on a more active role in the political realm. Whether it is speaking out against specific bills, or promoting various social causes, often at the behest of shareholder or employee groups. While the goals of these movements can be laudable, they risk alienating significant portions of customer and employee bases. How should CEOs decide which issues to take a stand on, or whether their companies should engage in the political realm at all? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（00:01:58）：That's a terrific question, and that is one obviously I've had to think about plenty. And at one point I said, "I don't put my citizenship in a blind trust when I take the job as CEO of Berkshire." But I've also learned that — you can make a whole lot more people sustainably mad than you can make temporarily happy by speaking out on any subject. And on certain subjects, they will take it out on our companies, and that means that the people that are employed by us, some of them we would end up letting go. It means that the shareholders get hurt. And do I really think that it's so important that I talk on every possible subject that people can get very upset about? Whether they should be asked to pay that price? And I've come to the conclusion, the answer is no. Why in the world do I want to hurt the people in that other room that do all kinds of things for Berkshire. Why do I want to hurt you because I say something that 20% of the country is going to instantly disagree with, and sometimes they will be so upset about it that they will try and take it out. And since they can't scream at me, they may have campaigns against our companies or anything else. So I think, as it applies to me, I'm not going to go around and take positions where instead of saying, "Warren Buffett says," it will say, you know, "Berkshire Hathaway," or "Warren Buffett of Berkshire Hathaway." I get identified. And I do not want to make the lives of you — and I've just decided I'm not going to be doing that. And if I want to do that, I should quit my job. If I think my citizenship, speaking out is that important, I'll give up what I love the most, which is having this job. (Laughs) I don't want to do that. So, I've decidedly backed off, I don't want to say anything that'll get attributed, basically to Berkshire, and have somebody else bear the consequences of what I talk about. So that's where I stand. And I can tell you that at most companies, or many — that isn't fair. But in the great many companies, you know, CEOs, they have to think about what their board says to them, and they've made a point of electing people to their boards, because it's socially acceptable, who represent different constituencies, sometimes very strongly. And if they think their stakeholders, for this group and that group and that group, they'll get pressured by their boards to take positions. And it's just a territory that we're not going to get into. Charlie, how do you feel about that?

**Charlie Munger**（00:05:13）：Well, even more than you, I have to be very careful about what I say. (Laughter) Now — (Laughter)

**Male Voice**（00:05:51）：Yeah. It's — (Applause)

**Warren Buffett**（00:05:55）：And the difference between the two of us is I can't resist saying a little more. (Laughter) I see headlines in papers, just time after time after time, that say, "Buffett's buying such and such." Well, I'm not buying such and such. Berkshire Hathaway is buying it, and it may be the work of two other people that work at Berkshire." And people who write the articles don't have the faintest idea whether it was at my instigation or whether I'd even heard of it. But the headline will attract more people if it says, "Buffett buying this," than if it says, "Berkshire Hathaway, and we don't know whether it's the people that work for him or him." The headline is designed to bring people into the story. So — The confusion is terrible. And the easiest thing to do is to basically shut up and not have a bunch of people fascinating questions that they didn't ask for in the first place. But I'm glad you asked that question. That is a good question. And I probably thought more about that question than I think about whether this stock or that stock is cheap.
