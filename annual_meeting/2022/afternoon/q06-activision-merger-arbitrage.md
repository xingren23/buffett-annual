## 问题

（本片段没有现场提问。巴菲特主动「发布一条消息」：伯克希尔持有 Activision 约 9.5%，但这笔买入与几个月前某位经理人的买入是两回事，需要说清楚。）

## 回答要点

1. 两类买家要分清：几个月前一位经理人买了约 1500 万股；1 月 17、18 日微软宣布以每股 95 美元收购之后，这只证券的性质就变了。
2. 「workout／套利」的定义：价值不再取决于市场报价，而取决于「已宣布的公司事件是否发生」——这是他和芒格 50 年前常做的活。
3. 赔率结构：上限是 95 美元（不会给 96），下限可能是 40 美元；唯一确定的是微软有钱，所以「融资风险」这一项被划掉。
4. 现在持有约 9.5%，超过 10% 就要向 SEC 申报；他提前把事实讲清，是因为过去有报道把「谁在买」搞错。
5. 更正的传播学：错误报道道了歉，但转载的媒体不会转载更正，几百万人被误导。
6. 芒格对这类操作的态度一向是「沃伦干吗要碰这些」，巴菲特自嘲是「听到铃响就冲出去的老消防马」。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（00:31:27）：好。哦，我应该在这儿发布一点消息。你们都来了，可能看到也可能没看到：我们买的、我买的其中一样东西，是几个月前由另一位经理人为了另一个目的买入的。他们买了大约 1500 万股 Activision。我知道这家公司，我只是在月度报表上看到它。但后来在 1 月 17、18 日左右，微软宣布要以每股 95 美元收购 Activision。他们一宣布，Activision 就变成了另一种证券。它变成了查理和我以前所说的——其实 50 年前大家都这么说——我们叫它「workout」之类，后来被称为套利。其实它并不是真正的套利，而是这样一种证券（这里是一只普通股）：它的价值不取决于市场报价怎么走，而取决于某个已宣布的公司事件是否发生。

微软想买 Activision，他们说每股 95 美元，而且他们有钱。显然，并购、大型并购、科技公司、各种因素，在舆论上都有各种麻烦。所以你不知道司法部会怎么做，也不知道欧盟会怎么做。但在那一刻，它变成了另一种证券。查理和我 50 年前做过很多这类事，高盛的 Gus Levy 也做。我们甚至为不列颠哥伦比亚电力公司跑过一趟，是吧，查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:33:59）：对，我们确实做过。

**Warren Buffett**（00:34:01）：对。（笑）那边有位叫 Bennett 的人，我们当时想弄清楚电力业务的某种收购。我们花了很多时间分析已宣布交易通过的概率，我们叫它 workout，后来这个词变成了 arb。而近几年，整体上并不太好。偶尔我会在这个领域看到想做的东西，但很少，因为规模必须大。利润是有上限的：他们说给你 95 美元，你不会拿到 96；而如果交易崩了，你手上的股票可能是 40 美元。五六年前拜耳收购孟山都时我们做过，运气很好，那笔收购对拜耳来说是糟糕的交易，但它确实完成了，因为拜耳有钱、也确实做完了，尽管孟山都带来的问题没人真正知道有多大。IBM 收购红帽时我们也做过。总之，在 1 月 17、18、19 日某天，微软宣布了。

那只股票原来在 60 多美元，我这儿可能有张幻灯片，我找找。总之，股价从 60 多涨到 81、82。有几天我觉得这个价差不够大。后来它稍微回落了一点。所以现在我们持有 Activision 大约 9.5%。超过 10% 我们就要申报。所以为了让新闻界不至于觉得这儿没新闻，我可以告诉你们：截至昨天，我们持有约 9.5%；一旦超过 10%，会向 SEC 提交表格等等。但这是我的买入，不是几个月前那位经理人的买入。（打响指）如果交易完成，我们赚一点；如果交易不成，谁知道会怎样。我只是想确保，如果我们真的提交那份报告，人们能非常清楚地理解，因为过去在这件事上有过非常混乱的报道。我们要非常明确：这个具体案例是沃伦·巴菲特的决定，而他不知道司法部会怎么做（笑），他不知道欧盟会怎么做，他从没跟微软任何人谈过。

我想他只是读了一份文件，自己做了评估。而且这评估会变。有一次我们甚至卖了一点，当时我觉得价格比该有的高一些，事后看卖得不算差。所以我就给你们造一点新闻。同时我想尽可能阻止那些过去不正确、然后被其他媒体转载、而更正从来没人写的报道，更正都写了，但出自一篇不准确的报道。那家媒体甚至向我道了歉，记者和编辑都给我写了私人道歉信。他们也没想到会出错。可其他刊物转载了那篇报道，却懒得转载更正。几百万人被误导了，很可能，传播开来之后就是这样。这一次我要提前阻止它，现在就（笑）把事实准确地告诉你们。我们会不会超过 10%，走着看。但很可能，如果它涨几美元，这仍然是 95 美元的交易，我们仍然不知道司法部会怎么做、欧盟会怎么做、其他 30 个司法辖区会怎么做。

我们唯一确定的是微软有钱，这就把那一项风险去掉了。总之，查理，你有什么消息要发布吗？（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（00:38:52）：没有。

**Warren Buffett**（00:38:53）：是啊。（笑声）顺便说，这件事我没跟查理商量。他知道我偶尔会看到一个套利交易就去做。50 年前我们一起做，而他大致的感觉是：「沃伦干吗要碰这些东西。」但这就是那匹老消防马，偶尔看起来赔率站在我们这边。但我们完全可能在这家公司上亏钱，而且如果交易崩了，亏的钱可能相当可观。会有很多人希望这笔交易崩掉，而微软不希望它崩，所以我们只能看。

**Warren Buffett**（00:31:27）：OK. Oh, I should make a little news here, so you've all come and you may or may not see this, but it's very possible — one of the things we bought, one of the things I bought, was bought for a different purpose by a different manager months earlier. They bought roughly 15 million shares of Activision. And I knew about the company, I would just see it at the monthly report. But then on January, I don't know, 17th or 18th, something like that, Microsoft announced they were going to buy Activision for $95 a share. Now, when they announced that, at that point Activision becomes a different kind of security. It becomes what Charlie and I used to call — well, and everybody did 50 years ago. We'd call them workouts or something like that. And they become known as arbitrage. Well, they're not really arbitrage, but they're securities that are, in this case a common stock, whose value depends not on what the market price does, but whether a given corporate event occurs. An announced corporate event occurs. Well, Microsoft wants to buy Activision, we'll say — well, they said at $95 a share. And they've got the money, and obviously mergers, and big mergers, tech companies, all kinds of things, have got all kinds of problems with the world generally in terms of opinion. So you don't know what the Justice Department will do or you don't know what the EU will do and all kinds of things. But at that point it becomes a different security. And Charlie and I, 50 years ago, we used to do a lot of that sort of thing. And Gus Levy did it at Goldman Sachs. And we even went back one time I think on British Columbia power, didn't we, Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:33:59）：Yeah. We certainly did.

**Warren Buffett**（00:34:01）：Yeah. (Laughs) A guy named Bennett was up there, and we were trying to figure out some takeover of the power business. I mean we spent a lot of time analyzing the probabilities of announced deals going through, and we called them workouts. Now, the term became arb. And it hasn't worked overall too well in recent years. Now, every now and then I see something that I want do to in that field. But very seldom, because they've got to be big. The profit is limited. You know, if they say you're going to get $95, you're not going to get $96, and if the deal blows up, you may have a stock that's at $40 or something. But we did it with Monsanto five or six years ago when Bayer was buying it, and we got very lucky, because it turned out to be a terrible acquisition for Bayer, but it did go through, because Bayer had the money and they went through with the deal, even though Monsanto came with a problem that nobody really understands the extent of. And we did it with Red Hat when IBM bought it. So in any event, on January whatever it was, 17th, 18th, 19th, Microsoft announces it. And the stock, which had been at $60 — let's see. I may have a slide here, which I'll find. But in any event, the stock, which had been in the 60s, went up to $81 or $82. And that looked like not a big enough spread to me for a few days. And then it settled back a little. So anyway, we now own 9.5%, something like 9.5% of Activision. And if we went over 10% we would file a report. So in order that the news people don't feel that there's no news here, I can tell you that as of yesterday, we own about 9.5%. If we go past 10%, there'll be a form filed with the SEC and so on. But it is my purchases, not the manager who bought it some months ago. (SNAPPING) And if the deal goes through we make some money, and if the deal doesn't go through, who knows what happens. But I just want to be sure that if we do file that report, people understand very clearly, because there's been some very mixed up stories on that in the past. We want to be very clear that it was Warren Buffett's decision in that particular case, and he doesn't know what the Justice Department's going to do. (Laughs) He doesn't know what the EU's going to do. He's never talked to anybody at Microsoft about it. I think he's just read a document. He's made his own assessment. And it can change. And at one time I think we sold a few shares even when I thought it was a little higher than it should be. It turned out those sales were not a bad sale. And so I just want to create a little news for you. And I want to, if possible, head off stories which have been incorrect in the past, and which get then picked up by other media and corrections never get written. That all the corrections were written by one inaccurate story. And they apologized even to me. Both are reporter and the editor sent me a personal note of apology. And they didn't expect to make a mistake. But when the other publications picked up the story, they didn't bother to pick up the correction. And millions of people were misinformed, and probably — literally by the time it gets spread around. And this one I will attempt to head off by telling you exactly what the facts are (Laughs) right now. And we'll see whether we go beyond 10%. But, you know, it could easily be that if it went up a few dollars — it's still a $95 deal. It's still we don't know what the Justice Department will do. We don't know what the EU will do. We don't know what 30 other jurisdictions will do. One thing we do know is Microsoft has the money. So that takes that one risk out of it. So anyway, Charlie, do you have any news to break? (Laughs)

**Charlie Munger**（00:38:52）：No.

**Warren Buffett**（00:38:53）：Yeah. (Laughter) And incidentally, I don't talk this over with Charlie. I mean, you know, he knows that occasionally I'll see an arbitrage deal and do it. And, you know, 50 years ago we were doing it together, and his general feeling is, "Why is Warren fooling around with this kind of stuff, even." But it's the old fire horse that occasionally it looks like the odds are in our favor. But absolutely we can lose money on that company, and, you know, fairly large sums of money, depending on what happened if the deal blows up. And there will be a lot of people that want the deal to blow up. But Microsoft doesn't want it to blow up, so we'll just have to see what happens.
