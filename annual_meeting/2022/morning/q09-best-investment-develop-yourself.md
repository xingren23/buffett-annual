## 问题

通胀已经连续四个月超过 7%，是 1982 年以来第一次。你们 1970 到 1975 年经历过类似局面，账面亏损，却做出了人生中最好的一些投资决定。如果只能选一只股票押注、并且要在通胀里扛得住，你会选哪一只？是什么让它在很可能艰难的市场里表现好？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特的答案不是某只股票，而是「你自己」：成为本地最好的医生、最好的律师，「别人会拿他们生产的小麦、棉花来换你的本事」。
2. 技能不会被通胀拿走，也不用缴税：「你能开发出的最好的东西，就是你自己。」
3. 芒格补了一句反面教材：自己的退休账户，顾问建议全买比特币时——「说不」。
4. 天赋跟学历不完全相关：世界永远需要有人在镜头前把事讲清楚；他拿 Becky Quick、Gladwell 的「一万小时」和自己练拳击当例子。
5. 要找的是交集：你真正喜欢、又擅长、对社会有用的那件事；找到之后，美元贬到一分还是一厘都不重要。
6. 他最后开玩笑：如果她愿意卖掉「未来收入的 10%」，伯克希尔今天就付现买下——「我会在你身上押很多钱」。

## 关键引述

> **“the best thing you can do is to be exceptionally good at something”**
>
> 「你能做的最好的事，是在某件事上做到极其出色。」

> **“the best investment by far is anything that develops yourself”**
>
> 「迄今为止最好的投资，就是任何能开发你自己的东西。」

> **“just say no”**
>
> 「直接说不。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:28）：好。第三提问点，请挑个「祖国、 motherhood 和苹果派」之类的话题来问。（笑声）

**Daphne**（02:10:43）：巴菲特先生、芒格先生，你们好。我叫 Daphne，来自纽约，这是我第五次参加年会。（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:57）：我们很感谢你来，真心的。

**Daphne**（02:11:01）：你们知道，过去连续四个月我们都在经历通胀，通胀率超过 7%，是 1982 年以来第一次。你们两位以前经历过这种情况，1970 到 1975 年，那时候你们的组合账面亏损，却做出了一生中最好的一些投资决定。回想那段，我的问题是：如果只能选一只股票押注——（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:38）：你刚才那一下差点把我们绕进去。（笑声）

**Daphne**（02:11:45）：——而且要在通胀里扛住，你会选哪一只？具体是什么让那只股票在很可能艰难的市场里表现很好？

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:54）：我告诉你一件比那只股票更好的东西。（笑声）也许后面我们会说到某只股票。但你能做的最好的事，是在某件事上做到极其出色。如果你是全城最好的医生、最好的律师、最好的随便什么，那么不管人们付你无数美元还是付你别的什么——他们都会拿自己生产的东西来换你交付的东西。如果你是他们挑中去做某件事的人：唱歌、打棒球、当他们的律师，随便什么，你的本事都拿不走，也没法被通胀掉。别人会把他们种的小麦、棉花给你，来换你手上的技能。所以，迄今为止最好的投资，就是任何能开发你自己的东西。而且再说一次，它不缴税。（掌声）这就是我会做的。

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:18）：我也有个建议给你。（笑声）等你有了自己的退休账户，你那位好心的顾问建议你把所有的钱都买比特币时——（笑声）——直接说不。（笑声）（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:45）：对。没人能拿走你的天赋。事实是，这个世界永远愿意交换。总有人需要做点什么，而有些人没有技能，他们从社会产品里分到的就比有技能的人少。有时候这跟教育有关，但很多时候跟教育一点关系都没有。你只要想清楚你想成为什么，再想清楚怎么去做，而你想成为的，往往就是你会做得好的。这个世界永远需要有人在那块屏幕里告诉我们发生了什么。所以，去研究研究贝基·奎克之类的人（笑），弄清楚她为什么出色，以及你天生带来了什么。我是说，我本来也可以，那个说「你得花一万小时」的人是谁来着？Malcolm Gladwell。他会说，只要在一件事上花一万小时。我可以花一万小时去练成重量级拳手，但（笑）我不觉得一万小时之后感觉会好。

你会撞上自己真正喜欢做、又擅长、对社会有用的那件事。之后美元值多少，购买力是一分、半分还是一厘，都不重要了。如果你是全城最好的医生，他们会给你送鸡，随便什么，但这本事拿不走。我猜，如果你已经来了五次年会，你前面会有很好的前途。这说明，（笑声）这是自我筛选的，如果你想卖掉自己的一部分，我们会买，这是我们能做的最好的投资。我们拿走你未来收入的 10%，现在就付你一笔现金。（笑声）那会是一笔极好的资产。而你保留未来收入的 100%。只要你开发自己的天赋，也许你会成为很棒的舞者，人们愿意花钱看伟大的舞者。Fred Astaire 和他姐姐 Adele 就来自奥马哈，那时候他们家姓 Austerlitz，可他们能跳舞。

Adele 后来跟着他做了那些事，搬去了英国；Fred Astaire 接着做了一大堆别的事。Ginger Rogers 得穿着高跟鞋、把所有动作倒着做一遍，而且因为她是女性，拿得还更少。但你会做得很好的。我会在你身上押很多钱。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:28）：OK. If station 3 will please ask something about motherhood and apple pie, or something like that. (Laughter)

**Daphne**（02:10:43）：Dear Mr. Buffet and Mr. Munger. My name is Daphne, I'm from NYC, and this is my fifth annual shareholders meeting. (Applause)

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:57）：Well, we appreciate you coming. We do, sincerely.

**Daphne**（02:11:01）：As you know, for the past four consecutive months, we've been going through inflation, with an inflation rate north of 7% for the first time since 1982. You both have experienced this before, from 1970 to 1975, at a time where your portfolio took paper losses and yet you made some of the best investment choices of your life. Reflecting on that, my question is, if you had to pick one stock to bet on — (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:38）：You kinda sneaked up on us there for a second. (Laughter)

**Daphne**（02:11:45）：— and be resilient in the inflation, which would you choose? And what specifically enables that stock to do very well in what might very likely be a difficult market?

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:54）：Well, I'll tell you something even better than that one stock. (Laughter) Maybe we'll get to one stock. But the best thing you can do is to be exceptionally good at something. If you're the best doctor in town, if you're the best lawyer in town, if you're the best whatever it may be, no matter whether people are paying you with a zillion dollars or paying with — they're going to give you some of what they produce in exchange for what you deliver. And if you're the one they pick out to do any particular activity, sing, or play baseball, or be their lawyer, whatever it may be, whatever abilities you have can't be taken away from you, they can't actually be inflated away from you. Somebody else will give you some of the wheat they produce, or the cotton, or whatever it may be, and they will trade you for the skill you have. So, the best investment by far is anything that develops yourself. And, again, it's not taxed. (Applause) So that's what I would do.

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:18）：I got some advice for you, too. (Laughter) When you have your own retirement account, and your friendly advisor suggests you put all the money into bitcoin — (laughter) — just say no. (Laughter) (Applause)

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:45）：Yeah. Nobody can take away from you the talent you have. And the truth is that the world will always be willing. They'll need to do something, and some people will not have skills, and they will get less of a the product of the society than somebody who has other skills. And sometimes that has something to do with education, but a good bit of the time it doesn't have anything to do with education. But, just figure out what you'd like to be, and figure out how — and what you'd like to be is what you're going to likely be good at, and, you know — The world will always need somebody on that tube to tell us what's going on. So, you know, study Becky Quick or somebody and (laughs) figure out, you know, what makes her good. And what you sort of naturally bring to the game. I mean, I could have — who's the guy that says you've got to spend 10,000 hours doing this or that, and then? Malcolm Gladwell. Malcolm Gladwell, you know, would say, just spend 10,000 hours on something. Well, I could have spent 10,000 trying to become a heavyweight boxer, but (laughs) I don't think would have felt very good at the end of the 10,000 hours. I mean — And, you know, you stumble into what you really like doing, what you're good at, what is useful to society. And then it doesn't make any difference what the dollar bill, you know, is now worth, in terms of the purchasing power, a cent, or a half-a-cent, or a hundredth of a cent. If you're the best doctor in town, you know — they'll bring you chickens, whatever they may do — but they can't take it away from you. And my guess is that — if you've come to five meetings, you know, you've got a very good future ahead of you. I mean, that shows — (Laughter) It self-selects, I mean — So, if you want to sell a piece of yourself, you know, we'll buy that as the best investment we can make. We'll take 10% of your future earnings, and we'll give you a cash payment now. (Laughter) And, you know, we'll have a terrific asset. And you can have 100% of your future earnings. And if you develop your talent — maybe you'll be a great dancer — people pay money to watch great dancers. We had Fred Astaire and his sister, Adele, that came from Omaha, you know. Their name was Austerlitz then, but they could dance. And Adele did whatever she did with him, moved to England. And Fred Astaire went on to do a whole bunch of other things. And Ginger Rogers had to do it all the same, backwards, in high heels, and she didn't get paid as much because she was a woman. But you're going to do just fine. I'd bet a lot of money on you. (Laughs)
