## 问题

（本片段没有现场提问，是上一段话题的自然延伸：从财富管理业的「收主动的钱、交付指数」谈到人能不能变得更好。）

## 回答要点

1. 财富管理业的问题是「收技能的费、交付偷偷指数化的组合」：没人能承受跟大众和指数差太远，怕丢客户，于是大家做同样的事。
2. 人按自利行事，不值得因此对人类绝望——但要承认「自利」往往并不真的利己。
3. 抽到「卵巢彩票」的人，人生下半场理应比上半场更好；这不该算是对人要求太高。
4. 有些东西只能靠与人打交道学会：两岁孩子脑子里的学习机器再惊人，也换不来三四十年对人性的观察。
5. 巴菲特说他更愿意以自己人生的下半场被评价，芒格说「当然」，并说自己根本不愿回看年轻时的所作所为。
6. 两人都承认这是「从一个很低的基数上」的改善。

## 关键引述

> **“people are charging for skill and delivering closet indexization”**
>
> 「人们收的是主动管理的费用，交付的却是藏在柜子里的指数化。」

> **“you ought to be a better person in the second half of your life than the first half”**
>
> 「你人生下半场应该比上半场做个更好的人。」

> **“I don't even look at what I did when I was young because it would embarrass me.”**
>
> 「我甚至不去看自己年轻时做过什么，因为那会让我难堪。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Charlie Munger**（01:47:52）：我确实觉得有个很有意思的现象：在很多财富管理业务里，人们收的是主动管理的费用，交付的却是藏在柜子里的指数化。换句话说，没人能承受跟大众、跟指数差得太远，他们怕丢掉管理费，于是大家做同样的事。这有点可笑。这个世界有点可笑。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:13）：是啊。但就像查理在电影里指出的——只有到场的人看到了——我们结婚之前，也曾努力让几位年轻女士相信我们比实际上更有魅力。（笑）你不能指望人不按自利行事。当时很关键的一点是，我们在婚前没有把全部缺点都披露出来。所以——

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:03）：沃伦和我正努力变得好一点。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:09）：是的，那是真的——

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:11）：我们可能还会失败一点。我不知道你怎么样，但我比 17 岁时略有进步。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:18）：是啊，好吧，（笑声）这一点很有意思：如果命运已经把所有好东西都撒给了你，你人生下半场就应该比上半场做个更好的人。这真不该算是对人要求太高。如果你中了「卵巢彩票」，生在美国，各种好事都落在你身上，而且你有机会看到自己当初多蠢、做过多少傻事，那为什么不让下半场比上半场更好？我得说，这是从一个很低的基数上说的，按任何智商测试或能力标准，我什么也没学会。但有些东西你只能通过与人打交道才学得到。你不知道，两岁的孩子从世界里吸收知识的学习机器有多惊人，可那跟三四十年真正观察「人这种动物怎么行事」不是一回事，你一直在学这件事。而这就该让你下半场比上半场更好。我还要说：如果你说下半场更好，而且有理由这么说，那就把上半场忘掉。

（笑）享受下半场。查理和我都有这份奢侈：第一，活得够久，能演我们心目中那段有希望、也体面的下半场；第二，我们有足够的悟性弄清楚，嗯，我们弄清楚了自己想要什么才会快乐，也变得更敏感于什么会让别人不快乐。我更愿意以我人生的下半场被评价，查理也是。

**Charlie Munger**（01:51:37）：是啊，当然。

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:38）：好。

**Charlie Munger**（01:51:39）：我甚至不去看自己年轻时做过什么，因为那会让我难堪。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:47）：是啊。好。谁想追问查理具体细节，待会儿可以自己问。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Charlie Munger**（01:47:52）：Well, I do think we have a very interesting phenomenon, in — I would argue that in a lot of the wealth advisory business, people are charging for skill and delivering closet indexization. In other words, nobody can stand being that different from the crowd and results. They're afraid they'll lose their fees. So, everybody does the same thing. It's mildly ridiculous. The world is mildly ridiculous. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:13）：Yeah. But as Charlie pointed out in the movie, which only the people here saw that, that before we were married, you know, we tried to convince a couple of young women that we were really more attractive than we were. I mean — (Laughs) You can't expect people not to behave in their self-interest. And that was very important, that we didn't disclose all of our weaknesses before the marriage. So —

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:03）：Warren and I are trying to be a little better. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:09）：Yeah, that was true--

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:11）：We may fail a little. And I don't know about you, but I've slightly improved since I was 17. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:18）：Yeah, well — (Laughter) That's a really interesting point, because if fortune has just showered you with all kinds of good things you ought to be a better person in the second half of your life than the first half. I mean, that really should not be asking too much of people. If they've won the ovarian lottery, and they're born in the United States, and all kinds of good things have happened to them, and you've had a chance to see how stupid you were and all kinds of things you did, you know, why not have the second half of your life be better than the first half? I would say — working from a very low base — but, I mean, I'm not nearly, you know, by any intelligence test or ability to do any of that, you know, I haven't learned anything. But you do learn certain things only by interacting with people. And you don't know, when you're two years old, no matter how much you're picking up all kinds of knowledge from the world, the learning machine that's going on in a two-year-old's head is just unbelievable. But it's not the same as having 30 or 40 years of experience with actually how the human animal behaves, which is really, you know, you're learning all the time about it. But that should make you a better person in the second half of your life than the first half. And I would say that if you say you're a better person in the second half and have got reason to say it in the first half, you know, forget about the first half. (Laughs) Enjoy the second half. And both Charlie and I have had the luxury of A, living a long time so we get to play, what we would regard as the hopeful and respectable second half. And we have had enough sense to figure out — well, we figured out what makes us happy. And we've gotten somewhat more sensitive to what can make other people unhappy and all that sort of thing. And I'd rather be judged by the second half of my life than the first half, and so would Charlie.

**Charlie Munger**（01:51:37）：Yeah, of course.

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:38）：OK.

**Charlie Munger**（01:51:39）：I'm very — I don't even look at what I did when I was young because it would embarrass me. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:47）：Yeah. OK. Any of you who wish to quiz Charlie on specifics can do so later. (Laughter)
