## 问题

伯克希尔在美国之外整体收购的公司并不多，最早是 Iscar，德国也有 Louis。你们只会对感兴趣的公司回电话，还是会主动去找那些愿意卖的家族企业？

## 回答要点

1. 主动找过：二三十年前专门跑过一圈，想让全世界对伯克希尔产生兴趣，但效果有限——「我们从来没有在美国以外有过那样的经历」。
2. 这一轮 400 亿美元里，大约 50 亿买在欧洲和日本：三家德国公司、增持日本五大商社（按当时口径约 5.85%）。
3. 海外的真正障碍不是意愿，而是「对面十分钟内能不能拍板」：美国成交往往是一封邮件、一次见面、报个价就行，很多国家要复杂得多。
4. 德国那家子公司是理想样本——管理层可靠、热爱生意，「我真希望所有数字后面能加个零」，可惜体量对伯克希尔没有经济影响。
5. 对日本商社的口径：未经对方同意不会实质性改变持仓；「那种公司我们永远不会卖」。
6. 结论是「机会可能在任何地方」（包括以色列的子公司），但伯克希尔更喜欢它自己掉进怀里，而不是逐个去敲门。

## 关键引述

> **“We have so much trouble finding good ideas that we can't afford to ignore any.”**
>
> 「我们找好主意实在太难，所以任何一个都不能放过。」

> **“It just doesn't happen that way.”**
>
> 「事情不是那样发生的。」

> **“we would never, never sell an operation like that, ever.”**
>
> 「那样的生意我们永远不会、永远不会卖掉。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Ola Polem**（01:03:29）：早上好。我叫 Ola Polem，住在德国汉诺威，这是我第一次来奥马哈。我的问题是关于伯克希尔在美国之外整体收购公司。这样的案例不多，Iscar 大概是最早的一个，德国有 Louis。我的问题是：你们只会对有兴趣的公司回电话，还是会主动去接触那些愿意出售的家族企业？

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:17）：这个嘛，我们其实跑过几趟。我记得查理还跟我一起去过其中一次。我们试着让全世界对伯克希尔产生兴趣，大概是 20 年、25 年前的事。那段时间里，就是我刚才给你们看的那一波动作，我们大概花了，至少有 50 亿是在这个方向上：我们买了三家德国公司的证券，在日本买了两家，不对，是买了一家，另外增持了已有的持仓。我们很希望他们来找我们买，但在那边他们不会那么快想到我们。我这儿没有一个跟了 60 年的公司，会有人给我发邮件说「这公司可以卖」，然后我知道能在纽约见到他、能给他报个价，他要是满意就拿去董事会，事情不是那样发生的。在美国以外，我们从来没有过这种经历。你当然可以说，美国有 40 万亿美元的市场，我们应该能在离家近一点的地方找到东西（笑）。

但我们没有偏见。有些公司，只要对面能十分钟内做成生意，我们十分钟就买了。在某些国家买企业比在美国复杂得多，还有各种规定。但显然，很多年前，我们德国那家公司给我们打了电话。现在经营它的两位先生大概就在观众席里，我昨天见过他们。他们非常出色，经营着这门生意，可靠得，之前放的电影里所有人的照片都在上面。我们找好主意实在太难，所以任何一个都不能放过。但它们现在必须有足够体量。说真的，我特别喜欢我们在德国买下的那门生意，跟那里的人共事是件愉快的事，我只希望所有数字后面能再加个零，让它成为一笔大得多的交易。它对伯克希尔不会有经济影响。但他们热爱它、他们在意，你看得见、感觉得到。这就是我们想要的那种生意，我们很高兴它在伯克希尔。

可我们没法一家一家地买 Louis 这样的公司。而那样的生意我们永远不会、永远不会卖掉。我在看你，格雷格。（笑）但如果明天有人打电话来，说在德国、法国、英国、日本，随便哪个国家，有一笔 100 亿、200 亿美元的交易可以做，我们会做。几年前我们买下了日本五家最大商社的股份，后来又稍微增持了一点。但我一开始就告诉他们，未经他们同意，我们不会实质性改变持仓。所以按当时最新的数字，我们把五家都增持到了大约 5.85%，那是好几亿美元、也许一二十亿美元的资金。所以我们会，肯尼迪总统说过，我们会付出任何代价、翻越任何山丘（笑），只要能找到企业。但我们其实更喜欢它们自己掉进怀里，比如收到一封几年没联系的人写来的信，而你知道自己愿意为那门生意付多少钱，也知道如果对方董事会认为价格有吸引力，他们会乐意卖。

他们知道你会出现在交割现场，不会往上压债务、不会乱改东西。他们心里有答案，而你要看他们心里那个问题是不是「什么对 Alleghany 最好」。那一次，一天结束时，或者说晚饭结束时，我们少了 110 亿美元。所以机会可能在任何地方。比如我们在以色列有一家极好的公司，规模也相当可观。我们想再来一家吗？想，我只是不知道另一家在哪里。查理？

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Ola Polem**（01:03:29）：Good morning. My name is Ola Polem (PH). I live in Hanover, Germany. This is my first time in Omaha. My question is on Berkshire buying entire companies outside the U.S. There were a few, Iscar, probably the first one. Louis, in Germany. My question is, would you only answer calls from them if you're interested in? Or would you proactively approach them if they would like to sell their company?

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:17）：I would — we actually made a few trips. I think maybe Charlie went with me on one of them. We tried to stir up interest and all that sort of thing in Berkshire around the world. We probably did that 20 years or 25 years ago. During that period — that I showed you — that burst of action we had, we probably spent — we probably at least 5 billion of that — yeah, maybe pretty — in the area of 5 billion of it — we bought three German securities. We bought two — well, we bought one — in Japan. We rounded up on some of the holdings we already had there. We would love them to buy, but we — they don't think of us as quickly there. I mean, I don't have somebody that's going to send me an email about a company that I've been following for 60 years and I know I can see them in New York and, you know, I can name a number to them, and if he likes it, he can take it to his board and so on. It just doesn't happen that way. We haven't had that experience in — well, anywhere outside the United States. Now, you can say with 40 trillion here, you know, we should be able to find something here (laughs) a little closer to home. But we don't have any bias against doing it. We — There are companies, you know, we'd buy in 10 minutes if we had somebody on the other end that could do business in 10 minutes. It's much more complicated in certain countries than in the United States to purchase businesses, and there are certain rules. But obviously this — you know, we got a call — whenever it was — many years ago, on our company in Germany. And actually, the two fellows that run it are probably here in the audience. I saw them yesterday. And they're marvelous. And they run the business. And, you know — they're as trustworthy as — well, all the pictures were up on the movie we showed before this meeting started here. You know — We have so much trouble finding good ideas that we can't afford to ignore any. But they do have to be sizable now. I mean, there really isn't — there isn't a lot of — I love the operation we bought in Germany, and it's just a pleasure to be associated with the people there. I just wish we could add another zero to all the figures and it was a much larger deal. It's not going to have an economic impact on Berkshire. But they love it. They care. You can see it. You can feel it. And that's the kind of business we'd like to have, and we're very happy we've got it in Berkshire. But we can't do it one (unintelligible) of Louis at a time. And we would never, never sell an operation like that, ever. I'm looking at you, Greg. (Laughs) The — you know — but if — if we get a call tomorrow and we could make a deal that involves 10 or 20 billion dollars that was in Germany or France or Britain or Japan — or name a whole group of countries — we'd do it. We bought the interest in the five leading trading companies in Japan a couple years ago. And I rounded them up a little bit. But I told them originally we weren't going to but a lot of — we weren't going to change our positions materially without their OK. So, we actually, I think, rounded the 5.85% — based on the latest figures we had then — of all five of them. And that was a good many hundreds of millions, or maybe a billion or two, to work. So, we will, you know — President Kennedy said we'll pay any price, climb any hills, you know — (laughs) — whatever it may be — to find businesses. But we actually prefer it when they fall into our lap, like getting a letter from somebody you hadn't heard from for a couple of years, and you know what you'd pay for the business. And you know if the board of directors of that company regards it as attractive, they'll be happy to buy it. And they know you're going to show up at the closing and that you're not going to pile debt on it or change things or anything. They've got an answer, and then you have to see if they've got the question in their mind is what's the best thing for Alleghany Corp? And in that case, we had $11 billion less at the end of the day — or the end of the dinner — than we had at the start of the day. So, opportunity can be any place. And we do have a terrific operation, for example, in Israel, I mean, just terrific. And it's pretty good size. Would we like to have another one like it? Yeah, I just don't know where the other one is. Charlie?
