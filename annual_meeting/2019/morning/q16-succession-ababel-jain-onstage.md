## 问题

请更新接班计划。是否考虑过让格雷格（阿贝尔）、阿吉特（贾因）乃至泰德、托德未来在年会台上就座，接受股东提问？

## 回答要点

1. 「这可能是个好主意」——格雷格和阿吉特当场就在会场，随时可把问题指给他们
2. 巴菲特自嘲：查理和我是怕难看——「那两位比我们强」
3. 泰德与托德基本不回答投资问题：投资决策属于伯克希尔的专有信息，不每日公布买卖
4. 芒格：伯克希尔全世界仅此一家，「你们只能忍受我们」
5. 周六接电话、周日见面即承诺 100 亿美元的信用，是无法复制的企业资产；泰德和托德扩大了可选宇宙

## 关键引述

> **“you're just going to have to endure us.”**
>
> 「你们只能忍受我们。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:33）：安德鲁？

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（01:51:32）：安德鲁·罗斯·索尔金：这个问题来自一位超过 20 年的老股东，他要求匿名，但要我先转达一句：「沃伦、查理，提问之前我想先说，这个问题出于对你们两位的爱，也出于对你们为我们画下的伯克希尔这幅美丽画作的敬意。」

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:47）：但是。（笑声）

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（01:51:51）：索尔金：「请更新一下接班计划。考虑接班问题时，你们是否考虑过让格雷格（阿贝尔）和阿吉特（贾因）在未来年会的台上就座，让我们向他们提问——泰德和托德也一样——以便更好了解他们的想法？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:52:10）：这可能是个好主意，我们讨论过。（掌声）格雷格和阿吉特今天就在现场，任何人想向他们提问，把他们挪过来就行。我们考虑过让四个人都上台。这个形式并非不可改变。

说实话，查理和我其实是怕难看——那两位比我们强。（笑）你找不出比格雷格和阿吉特更好的运营经理。他们成就的业绩不可思议，比我们更懂业务，工作也卖力得多。欢迎大家把问题指给他们。这个形式不会永远延续，如果让他们上台更好，我们乐意。

泰德（韦施勒）和托德（康姆斯）基本不会回答投资问题。我们认为投资决策本质上属于专有信息，属于伯克希尔。我们不是投资咨询机构。让他们谈论持有的证券违背伯克希尔的利益，查理和我说这些也一样。我们干得更好，部分正因为不每天公布买卖。苹果有人研发新产品，或者有人研发新药、收购物业，不会每天向全世界播报自己在干什么。我们试图产生的是投资想法，除了法律要求，我们不认为「每天告诉世界我们在做什么」是正确做法。

不过，让格雷格和阿吉特上台确实是个好主意。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:37）：我们回答这类问题吃力，原因之一是伯克希尔太特别了，全世界仅此一家。我们有一套非官僚的决策方式：总部没有几个人，没有没完没了的委员会把坏决定议而不决。我们彻底不同，与众不同也有代价。但我不想变得和别人一样，因为这套东西运转得更好。所以，你们只能忍受我们。（笑声与掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:55:31）：我们确实认为这是巨大的企业资产，只是偶尔才显形，很大程度上取决于周遭世界如何。周六上午接到电话、周日上午见面就能给出 100 亿美元的承诺——全世界几乎只有这一处，而且没有人怀疑承诺会被兑现，不受公司任何反悔的影响，背后只有伯克希尔的一句话。这种资产时不时值很多钱，而且我认为不会有竞争。

泰德和托德是一条额外的管道，事实证明确实如此——有些机会经由他们进来，不然我未必听说，他们扩大了我们的可选宇宙。近几年的市场里这不算重要，但我看得到他们价值巨大的时期。想想温尼伯那位先生提到的债券弱契约问题——谁知道何时、何地、会不会——垃圾债市场可能出现大规模违约。我们经历过几次，赚了不少钱。那样的时期或其他类型的时期来临时，泰德和托德会大大提升我们的作战效能。他们对伯克希尔非常有用。

那个电话恰好是周五打来的——美国银行 CEO 布莱恩·莫伊尼汉，他干得不可思议。下次市场混乱时，我们接到更多电话、并被恰当地筛分的机会，比上一次更大。这很重要。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:58）：我确实认为，如果世界滑向深渊，你们各位所在的正是那家正确的公司。我们现金充足，也知道恐慌中如何举止得体。而如果世界不滑向深渊——现在的情况有那么糟吗？

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:24）：另外我要报告，你们这位副董事长正获得新的社交荣誉。这次聚会期间，我被比特币的朋友们邀请去参加欢乐时光。（笑声）我一直在想比特币的人在欢乐时光上都干什么，终于想明白了：他们庆祝加略人犹大的生平与事迹。（笑声）

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:58）：你的邀请函还有效吗？（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:03）：比特币——说起来，1952 年我蜜月旅行，新娘 19 岁，我 21 岁，在拉斯维加斯停留。我姑姑艾丽斯把车给我，说「玩得开心」，我们一路向西，住进了火烈鸟酒店。我环顾四周，看到一群衣着考究——那个年代的人穿得更讲究——有些专程从几千英里外赶来。喷气式客机还没发明，交通远不如今天。他们来干一件事，而每一个人都知道这在数学上是愚蠢的。我对苏茜说：「我们会赚很多钱。」（笑声）想想看：人们把钱押在带一个零和双零的轮盘号码上，人人知道概率，人人照样下注。我得说，比特币让我重温了那种感觉。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:33）：Andrew?

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（01:51:32）：This question comes from a shareholder of yours for more than 20 years, who asked to remain anonymous, but wanted me to start by saying, "Warren and Charlie, I want to preface this question by saying it comes from a place of love for both of you and the beautiful painting you've drawn for us in the form of Berkshire."

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:47）：But. (Laughter)

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（01:51:51）："Now, please update us on succession planning. And as you think about succession, would you ever consider having Greg (Abel) and Ajit (Jain) join you onstage at future annual meetings and allow us to ask questions of them and Ted and Todd, as well, so we can get a better sense of their thinking?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:52:10）：That's probably a pretty good idea. And we've talked about it. (Applause) We have Greg and Ajit here. And any questions that anybody wants to direct to them, it's very easy to move them over. So, we thought about having four of us up here. And this format is not set in stone at all. Because you — I can tell you that, actually, the truth is, Charlie and I are afraid of looking bad. Those guys are better than we are. (Laughs) You could not have two better operating managers than Greg and Ajit. I mean, they are — it is just fantastic, what they accomplished. They know the businesses better. They work harder, by far. And you are absolutely invited to ask questions to be directed over to them at this meeting. I don't think — Yeah, this format will not be around forever. And if it's better to get them up on the stage, we'll be happy to do it. Ted (Weschler) and Todd (Combs), they're basically not going to answer investment questions. We regard investment decisions as proprietary, basically. They belong to Berkshire. And we are not an investment advisory organization. So, that is counter to the interests of Berkshire for them to be talking about securities they own. It's counter to the interests of Berkshire for Charlie or me to be doing it. We've done better because we don't publish every day what we're buying and selling. I mean, if somebody's working on a new product at Apple, or somebody's working on a new drug or they're assembling property or something of the sort, they do not go out and tell everybody in the world exactly what they're doing every day. And we're trying to generate ideas in investment. And we do not believe in telling the world what we're doing every day, except to the extent that we're legally required. But it's a good idea. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:37）：Well, one of the reasons we have trouble with these questions is because Berkshire is so very peculiar. There's only one thing like it. We have a different kind of unbureaucratic way of making decisions. There aren't any people in headquarters. We don't have endless committees deliberating forever and making bad decisions. We just — we're radically different. And it's awkward being so different. But I don't want to be like everybody else, because this has worked better. So, I think you're just going to have to endure us. (Laughter and applause)

**Warren Buffett**（01:55:31）：We do think that it's a huge corporate asset, which may only surface very occasionally and depending very much on how the world is around us. But to be the one place, I think, in the world, almost, where somebody can call on a Saturday morning and meet on Sunday morning and have a $10 billion commitment. And nobody in the world doubts whether that commitment will be upheld. And it's not subject to any kind of welching on the part of the company that's doing it. It's got nothing involved over than Berkshire's word. And that's an asset that, every now and then, will be worth a lot of money to Berkshire. And I don't really think it will be subject to competition. So — and Ted and Todd, in particular, are an additional pipeline, and have proven to be an additional pipeline, in terms of facilitating the exercise of that ability. I mean they — things come in through them that, for one reason or another, I might not hear about otherwise. So, they have expanded our universe. In the markets we've had in recent years, that hasn't been important. I can see periods where they would be enormously valuable. Just take the question that was raised by the fellow from Winnipeg about weak covenants and bonds. I mean, we could have a situation — who knows when, who knows where, or who knows whether — but we could have a situation where there could be massive defaults in the junk-bond-type market. We've had those a couple times. And we made a fair amount of money off of them. But Ted and Todd would multiply our effectiveness in a big way, if such a period comes along, or some other types of periods come along. They are very, very, very useful to Berkshire. The call happened to come in on Friday from Brian Moynihan, CEO of Bank of America. And he's done an incredible job. But we have a better chance of getting more calls and having them properly filtered and everything — appropriately filtered — the next time conditions get chaotic than we did last time. And that's important. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:58）：Well, I do think it's true that if the world goes to hell in a hand basket, that you people will be in the right company. We've got a lot of cash and we know how to behave well in a panic. And if the world doesn't go to hell, are things so bad now?

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:24）：And I also want to report that your vice chairman is getting new social distinction. I've been invited during this gathering to go to a happy hour put on by the bitcoin people. (Laughter) And I've tried to figure out what the bitcoin people do in their happy hour, and I finally figured it out. They celebrate the life and work of Judas Iscariot. (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:58）：Is your invitation still good? (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:03）：Bitcoin — actually — on my honeymoon in 1952, my bride, 19, and I, 21 — stopped in Las Vegas. We just got in — my aunt Alice gave me the car and said, "Have a good time," and we went west. So, we stopped in the Flamingo, and I looked around, and I saw all of these well-dressed — they dressed better in those days — well-dressed people who had come, in some cases, from thousands of miles away. And this was before jets, so transportation wasn't as good. And they came to do something that every damn one of them knew was mathematically dumb. And I told Susie, I said, "We are going to make a lot of money." (Laughter) I mean, imagine people going to stick money on some roulette number with a zero and a double-zero there and knowing the percent. They all could do it, and they — they just do it. And I have to say, bitcoin has rejuvenated that feeling in me. (Laughter)
