## 问题

北美除 BNSF 外所有大铁路公司都在采用「精准铁路运营」，普遍改善了盈利但客户服务承压。巴菲特与管理层是否认为 BNSF 应跟进，还是同意批评者的看法？

## 回答要点

1. 精准运营由亨特·哈里森开创，先后在 CN、加拿大太平洋、CSX 落地，利润率大幅改善
2. 推行过程中各家客户服务都出了问题，联合太平洋正在试行改良版
3. BNSF 不排斥借鉴任何成功经验，前提是客户满意度不下降、效率有提升
4. 「不必今天或明天就动手」

## 关键引述

> **“we are not above copying anything that is successful.”**
>
> 「我们从不排斥借鉴任何成功的东西。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:22:07）：乔纳森·勃兰特（Ruane, Cunniff & Goldfarb 研究分析师）：沃伦、查理，你们好，一如既往感谢邀请。除了伯灵顿北方（BNSF）之外，北美每一家大铁路公司都多少采用了「精准铁路运营」（precision-scheduled railroading），普遍改善了盈利。有人认为点对点的班表服务和最少的途中编组对资本回报和客户服务都有利；也有人认为精准运营对准点率帮助不大，其僵化还动摇了铁路公司与监管者、客户之间长期形成的默契。你和 BNSF 现任管理层是否认为 BNSF 现在应该采用精准运营的打法？还是你更同意批评者的看法？

**Warren Buffett**（00:22:54）：「精准运营」这个打法，多半是一个叫亨特·哈里森的人发明的。我想他当时在伊利诺伊中央铁路；大概一年前出了一本写他的书，讲的就是他运营铁路的方法，如果你对铁路感兴趣，值得一读。后来他把这套打法带到了加拿大国家铁路（CN）。北美有六大铁路公司，他在 CN 非常成功——比尔·盖茨可能就是 CN 最大的股东，我想这只股票让他赚得不错。

再后来，加拿大太平洋铁路被维权投资者盯上，他们请来了亨特，还带来了副手基思·克里尔，推行了一套大同小异的方案。现在 CSX 也发生了同样的事。这些公司的利润率都大幅改善，但在推行过程中，客户服务多多少少都出了问题。

我们一直在密切关注——联合太平洋正在做一个略微改良的版本。我们从不排斥借鉴任何成功的东西。观察这四家铁路公司让我们学到不少东西。如果我们确信既能服务好客户、又能提高效率，我们就会采用观察到的任何做法。不必今天或明天就动手，但我们必须找到至少不差、最好更好的客户满意度，同时让铁路更有效率。从这四家公司的行动看，越来越多的证据表明我们确实能从中学到东西。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:25:28）：我怀疑没人会对「不精准」的铁路运营有多大兴趣。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:25:42）：乔尼，查理这是回答你了吗？（笑声）

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:25:45）：是的，谢谢。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:22:07）：OK, Jon Brandt. JONATHAN BRANDT (RESEARCH ANALYST, RUANE, CUNNIFF & GOLDFARB): Hi, Warren and Charlie. Thanks for having me, as always. Every major North American railroad other than Burlington Northern has adopted at least some aspects of precision-scheduled railroading, generally to good effect to their bottom line. Some believe that point-to-point schedule service and minimal in-transit switching is good for both returns on capital and customer service. Others believe precision railroading has done little for on-time performance, and its rigidity has jeopardized the compact that railroads have had with both regulators and customers. Do you and current BNSF management believe that it's now a good idea for BNSF to adopt precision railroading playbook? Or do you agree with its critics?

**Warren Buffett**（00:22:54）：Yeah, precision railroading, as it's labeled, was probably invented by a fellow named Hunter Harrison. I think maybe he was at the Illinois Central Railroad at the time; there's a book that came out about Hunter, who died maybe a year ago or thereabouts. And it describes the — his procedure toward railroading. It's an interesting read if you're interested in railroading. And he took that to Canadian National, CN. There are six big railroads in North America, and he took that to CN, and he was very successful. And actually, Bill Gates is probably the largest holder of CN, and I think he's done very well with that stock. And then later, Canadian Pacific was the subject of an activist, and when they — as they proceeded, they got Hunter to join them and brought in an associate, Keith Creel, who — and they instituted a somewhat similar program. Now the same thing has happened at CSX. And all of those companies dramatically improved their profit margins, and they had varying degrees of difficulty with customer service in the implementing of it. But I would say that we watch very carefully — Union Pacific is doing a somewhat modified version. But the — we are not above copying anything that is successful. And I think that there's been a good deal that's been learned by watching these four railroads, and we will — if we think we can serve our customers well and get more efficient in the process, we will adopt whatever we observe. But we don't have to do it today or tomorrow, but we do have to find something that gets at least equal, and hopefully better, customer satisfaction and that makes our railroad more efficient. And there's been growing evidence that — from the actions of these other four railroads — there's been growing evidence that we can learn something from what they do. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:25:28）：Well, I doubt that anybody is very interested in un-precision in railroading. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:25:42）：Well, Jonny, has Charlie answered your question? (Laughter)

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:25:45）：Yes, thank you.
