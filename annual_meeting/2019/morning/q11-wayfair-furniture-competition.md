## 问题

Wayfair 等线上家具商愿意亏损获客，会否冲击内布拉斯加家具城的盈利？「先客户后利润」的模式是否不可持续？家具盈利会不会永久低于过去？

## 回答要点

1. 「还没定论」——规模高增而仍亏损的业务长期表现未知
2. 内布拉斯加家具城线上占比可观（奥马哈店最高），且很多线上订单到店自提
3. 周二单日 920 万—930 万美元有盈利营业额；公司实收资本仅 2500 美元，从未追加
4. 一季度四家家具业务齐疲软，背后是住宅建造远低于复苏应有水平（单户占比 69% 降到 63%）
5. 不想沦为线上展厅，「价格必须到位」

## 关键引述

> **“it's working so far.”**
>
> 「到目前为止运转良好。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:13:00）：乔纳森？

**Jonathan Brandt**（01:12:59）：乔纳森·勃兰特：Wayfair 这类互联网家具零售商似乎愿意承受当前的大额亏损来获客，指望把客户转化为忠实的线上买家。我一直想知道这种颠覆性竞争会怎样冲击内布拉斯加家具城这类家居零售业务的盈利。如果必须转向更多线上模式，我们是否要花更多钱留住客户而销售并不相应增长？家居板块一季度盈利大幅下滑，或许说明竞争加剧的影响正在扩大。你认为 Wayfair「先客户后利润」的模式不可持续吗？还是认为我们的家具盈利将永久低于过去？

**Warren Buffett**（01:13:48）：家具这块——那些规模增长很快但仍在亏损的业务长期会怎样，还没有定论。眼下市场确实如此：部分是因为亚马逊等大获成功，投资者愿意容忍亏损，只要销售在增长，盼着好日子在后头。

我们的家具业务有相当大比例的销售在线上完成——这可能让你意外。奥马哈店的线上占比最高。有意思的是——具体数字我不给，但很大——线上下单的相当一部分人自己到店里提货。他们完全可以不来，但并不介意来店里取。你就是这样了解客户偏好的，就像快餐业发现人们愿意开车穿过取餐、不愿停车进店。客户行为如何展开，我们就如何学习。

本周二，内布拉斯加家具城做了 920 万或 930 万美元有盈利的营业额。这家公司的实收资本是 2500 美元，据我所知此后从未追加过。所以到目前为止运转良好。

有趣的是，一季度我们四家家具业务全都疲软。但经济的其他部分——比如整个住宅建造——远低于 2008—09 年复苏以来的应有水平。单户住宅占比从 69% 出头降到 63%，略回升了一点。按 2008、09 年之前的数字，加上复苏的程度和资金的便宜程度，人们建房搬家的速度本该更快。这对我们的家具店有影响。

但我认为我们的家具生意非常好——不只是内布拉斯加家具城，其他几家也是。这些模式长期是否成立，走着瞧。我觉得它们有合理的机会。人们正在学会：有些东西原来要去商场买，现在愿意网上买；有些则不然。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:17:49）：我认为我们会做得比大多数家具零售商好。

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:58）：总体上这是确定的。我们有一些好业务。但我们不想沦为线上业务的展厅——人们来店里逛一圈，转身去别处下单。所以价格必须到位。家具城在这方面很在行。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:13:00）：Jonathan?

**Jonathan Brandt**（01:12:59）：Internet-based furniture retailers, like Wayfair, appear willing to stomach large current losses acquiring customers in the hope of converting them to loyal online shoppers. I've been wondering what this disruptive competition might do to our earnings from home-furnishing retail operations like Nebraska Furniture Mart. If we have to transition to more of an online model, might we have to spend more heavily to keep shoppers without a corresponding increase in sales? The sharp decline in first-quarter earnings from home furnishings suggest, perhaps, some widening impact from intensifying competition. Do you believe Wayfair's customers first, profits later model is unsustainable? Or do you think our furniture earnings will likely be permanently lower than they were in the past?

**Warren Buffett**（01:13:48）：I think furnishings — the jury's still out on that, whether the operations which have grown very rapidly in size but still are incurring losses, how they will do over time. It is true that in the present market, partly because of some successes, like, most dramatically, Amazon, in the past, that investors are willing to look at losses as long as sales are increasing, and hope that there will be better days ahead. We do a quite significant percentage of our sales online in the furniture operation. That might surprise you. We do the highest percentage in Omaha. And what's interesting is that we — I won't give you the exact numbers, but it's large — we do a significant dollar volume, but a very significant portion of that volume, people come to the store to pick up, so that they will order something from us online, but they don't seem to mind at all — and they don't have to do it — but they get a pick up at the store. So, you know, you learn what customers like, just like people learned in fast food, you know, that people would buy a lot of food by going through a drive-in, that they don't want to stop and go into the place. We learn about customer behavior as it unfolds. But we did do, now — on Tuesday, we did 9.2 million of — or 9.3 million of profitable volume at the Nebraska Furniture Mart. And I think that company had paid-in capital of $2,500. And I don't think anything's been added since. So, it's working so far. The first quarter — It's interesting — the first quarter was weak at all four of our furniture operations. But there are certain other parts of the economy — well, just home building, generally — it's considerably below what you would've expected, considering the recovery we have had from the 2008-9 period. I mean, if you look at single-family home construction, the model has shifted more to people living in apartment rentals. I think it's gone from 69-and-a-fraction percent. It got down to 63 percent. It's bounced up a little bit. But people are just not building — or moving to houses as rapidly as I would have guessed they would have, based on figures prior to 2008 and '09, and considering the recovery we've had, and considering the fact that money is so cheap. And that has some effect on our furniture stores. But I think we've got a very good furniture operation, not only with the Nebraska Furniture Mart, but at other furniture operations. And we will see whether the models work over the long run. But I think, you know, they have a reasonable chance. Some things people — we're learning that people will buy some things that they've always gone to the mall or to a retail outlet to buy, that they will do it online. And others don't work so well. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:17:49）：I think that we'll do better than most furniture retailers.

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:58）：I think that's a certainty overall, overall. But we've got some good operations there. But we don't want to become a showroom for the online operations and have people come and look around the place and then order someplace else. So, we have to have the right prices. And we're good at that at the Furniture Mart.
