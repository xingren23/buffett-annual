## 问题

消费者口味在变，Velveeta、Jell-O 已少有人问津，3G 砍研发似乎压制了新品开发。护城河眼看干涸，为什么继续持有？还是认为它正在重新蓄水？

## 回答要点

1. 芒格：问题不在砍研发，而在「最后一笔收购买贵了一点」
2. 有些品牌销量年降 2%—3%，另一些年增 1%—2%，没有剧烈变化
3. 卡夫亨氏现在的盈利比六七年前卡夫与亨氏加起来还多
4. 对食品公司而言，触达最终消费者的通道比十年前更难走
5. 查理 1940 年、巴菲特 1941 年就在巴菲特祖父的杂货店见过这些商品，如今照样在卖
6. 「成长型品牌也可能买贵」；一部分品牌强、一部分略降，十年前后皆如此

## 关键引述

> **“You can pay too much for a growing brand.”**
>
> 「成长型品牌也可能买贵。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:35:24）：好，8 号站。

**Audience Member**（02:35:34）：现场股东（关于消费品牌）：你们好，沃伦和查理。消费者口味在变。如果统计今天在场的人里过去一年吃过 Velveeta 芝士的有几个，大概只有一小撮；Jell-O 果冻也许多一点。3G 砍研发的打法似乎压制了新品开发。我的问题是：护城河眼看干涸，为什么继续持有？还是你认为它正在重新蓄水？

**Charlie Munger**（02:35:56）：我不认为问题出在砍研发上。问题在于他们最后一笔收购买贵了一点。

**Warren Buffett**（02:36:08）：Jell-O——我给不了确切数字。有些品牌销量每年下滑 2% 或 3%，另一些每年增长 1% 或 2%，并没有剧烈变化。卡夫亨氏现在赚的钱比六七年前卡夫和亨氏加起来还多，产品仍被大量使用。趋势当然一直存在，但远没有崩塌，利润率还有所拓宽。不过对食品公司来说，把货推到最终消费者面前的通道，比十年前更难走了。

它仍然是一门极好的生意。你提到 Jell-O、Velveeta——查理 1940 年在我祖父的杂货店打工，我 1941 年去的时候，店里就进这些货，现在人们照样买。利润率依然很好，投入资本回报率极佳。但我们为卡夫付贵了。成长型品牌也可能买贵——被吸进去甚至更容易。

所以我基本不担心品牌：一部分很强，一部分略有下滑，十年前如此，十年后也如此，没有戏剧性的事情发生。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:35:24）：OK. Station 8.

**Audience Member**（02:35:34）：Hello Warren and Charlie. Consumer tastes are changing. I think if we asked how many people here in the arena have eaten Velveeta cheese in the last year or so, there'd be only a small handful, maybe more for Jell-O. 3G's playbook of cutting R&D looks to have stifled new product development amidst changing preferences. So, here's my question. Why continue to hold when the moat appears to be dry? Or do you think it is filling back up?

**Charlie Munger**（02:35:56）：Well, I don't think the problem was that they cut research or something. I think the problem was, they paid a little too much for the last acquisition.

**Warren Buffett**（02:36:08）：That Jell-O — I can't give you the exact figures. There are certain brands that may be declining 2 percent a year or 3 percent a year in unit sales, and there's others that are growing 1 or 2 percent. There's not dramatic changes taking place at all. I mean, Kraft Heinz is earning more money than Kraft and Heinz were earning six or seven years ago. I mean, and the products are being used in a huge way. Now it's true that certain — that there are always trends going to some degree, but they have not fallen apart, remotely. And they have widened the margin somewhat. But it is tougher, in terms of the margin and the price negotiations, probably to go through to the actual consumer. It's become a somewhat tougher passageway for all food companies, than it was ten years ago. It's still a terrific business. I mean, you know, you mentioned Jell-O or Velveeta. Charlie worked at my grandfather's grocery store in 1940, I worked there in '41. And they were buying those products then, and they buy the products now. The margins are still very good. They earn terrific returns on invested capital. But we paid too much in the case of Kraft. You can pay too much for a growing brand. I mean, you can pay way too much for a growing brand, probably be easier to be sucked into that. So, I basically don't worry about the brands. A certain number are very strong, and a certain number are declining a bit. But that was the case 10 years ago. It'll be the case 10 years from now. There's nothing dramatic happening in that.
