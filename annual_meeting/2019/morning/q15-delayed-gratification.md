## 问题

爸爸常放你们的视频、教我你们的人生课，但很多课都要求「延迟满足」这项技能。孩子们有没有什么办法培养延迟满足的能力？

## 回答要点

1. 芒格：延迟满足基本是天生的，「我们只能识别它，没法训练出来」
2. 巴菲特：低利率下持长期国债的延迟满足，等于 30 年后去迪士尼只能玩同样的项目
3. 「今天吃汉堡的人，以后也吃不上牛排」
4. 省下迪士尼的钱把两天变一周，未必是人生正解；钱花在家人快乐上有很多可说的
5. 拥有 5 万或 10 万美元都不快乐的人，拥有 5000 万或 1 亿也不会快乐；越过某个点后快乐与金钱没有相关性

## 关键引述

> **“you're not going to be happy if you have 50 million or a hundred million.”**
>
> 「拥有 5000 万或 1 亿你也不会快乐。」

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:58）：好，5 号站。

**Audience Member**（01:46:03）：尼尔·诺罗尼亚（13 岁，旧金山）：你们好，沃伦和查理。我叫尼尔·诺罗尼亚，13 岁，来自旧金山。我总觉得经常在自家客厅见到你们——我爸爸总在放你们股东会的视频，也教了我很多你们的人生课。但很多课都要求「延迟满足」这项技能。（笑声）我想知道，孩子们有没有什么办法培养延迟满足的能力？（笑声与掌声）

**Charlie Munger**（01:46:44）：如果你不介意，沃伦，这题我来。

**Warren Buffett**（01:46:47）：请。

**Charlie Munger**（01:46:49）：我来答，因为我是延迟满足的专家——我延迟它的时间够长了。（笑声）我的答案是：这种东西基本是天生的。有的人生下来就能延迟满足，有的人生下来什么都得马上得到。我从来没成功改变过后者。所以我们只能识别它，没法训练出来。

**Warren Buffett**（01:47:28）：查理有八个孩子，所以他越来越信「天性胜过教养」。（笑声）

**Charlie Munger**（01:47:36）：你们可能会看到一位 95 岁的老人家，衣衫褴褛，把延迟满足坚持到人生尽头——手里大概握着 4000 股 A 类股。（笑声）倒是一代代二代三代们，把珠宝买了个遍。

**Warren Buffett**（01:48:05）：有意思。说个更宽的视角：想想一只收益率 3% 的 30 年期国债：个人还要为这 3% 缴税，而美联储说目标就是 2% 的通胀。你就会明白，持有长期国债的「延迟满足」，意味着 30 年后去迪士尼乐园能玩的和今天一样多。对投资固定收益的人来说，低利率意味着今天吃汉堡的人，以后也吃不上牛排——这是我多年前对妻子、孩子和一切愿意听的人布道的说法。（笑）

所以我不认为对所有家庭、所有情境而言，省钱都是人生正解。如果你告诉孩子，为了 30 年后能把迪士尼之行从两天延长到一周，从此不去电影院、不去迪士尼——我觉得把钱花在给自己和家人带来快乐的事情上，有很多可说的。延迟满足并非在所有情境下都无条件正确。我一直信奉每挣 1 美元花两三美分、其余全存。（笑声）但我一直拥有我想要的一切。

要明白一点：如果你拥有 5 万或 10 万美元都不快乐，那拥有 5000 万或 1 亿你也不会快乐。一定量的钱确实能让人——和你身边的人——更有安全感。但堆积如山的钱——我认识的有钱人大概不比谁少——我不认为他们因为超级富有而更快乐。不缺钱的时候会更快乐。越过某个点之后，快乐与金钱没有相关性。所以，别在延迟满足上走火入魔。（掌声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:58）：OK, station 5.

**Audience Member**（01:46:03）：Hey, Warren and Charlie. I'm Neel Noronha. I'm 13 years old and from San Francisco. I feel like I see you in our living room a lot. My dad is constantly playing these videos of you at these meetings. And he teaches me a lot of lessons about you guys. But many of them require the delayed gratification skill. (Laughter) I want to know, is there any way that kids can develop the delayed gratification skill? (Laughter and applause)

**Charlie Munger**（01:46:44）：I'll take it, if you want me to, Warren.

**Warren Buffett**（01:46:47）：Go to it.

**Charlie Munger**（01:46:49）：I'll take that, because I'm a specialist in delayed gratification. I've had a lot of time to delay it. (Laughter) And my answer is that they sort of come out of the womb with the delayed gratification thing, or they come out of the womb where they have to have everything right now. And I've never been able to change them at all. So, we identify it. We don't train it in.

**Warren Buffett**（01:47:28）：Charlie's had eight children, so he's become more and more of a believer in nature versus nurture. (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（01:47:36）：You'll probably see some nice, old woman of about 95 out there, in threadbare clothing. And she's delaying gratification right to the end and probably has 4,000 A shares. (Laughter) It's just these second- and third-generation types that are buying all the jewelry.

**Warren Buffett**（01:48:05）：It's interesting. If you think about — we'll take it to a broader point. But if you think of a 30-year government bond paying 3 percent, and you allow for, as an individual, paying some taxes on the 3 percent you'll receive, and you'll have the Federal Reserve Board saying that their objective is to have 2 percent inflation, you'll really see that delayed gratification, if you own a long government bond, is that, you know, you get to go to Disneyland and ride the same number of rides 30 years from now that you would if you did it now. The low interest rates, for people who invest in fixed-dollar investments, really mean that you really aren't going to eat steak later on if you eat hamburgers now, which is what I used to preach to my wife and children and anybody else that would listen, many years ago. (Laughs) So, it's — I don't necessarily think that, for all families, in all circumstances, that saving money is necessarily the best thing to do in life. I mean, you know, if you really tell your kids they can —whatever it may be — they never go to the movies, or we'll never go to Disneyland or something of the sort, because if I save this money, 30 years from now, you know, well, we'll be able to stay a week instead of two days. I think there's a lot to be said for doing things that bring you and your family enjoyment, rather than trying to save every dime. So, I — delayed gratification is not necessarily an unqualified course of action under all circumstances. I always believed in spending two or three cents out of every dollar I earn and saving the rest. (Laughter) But I've always had everything I wanted. I mean, one thing you should understand, if you aren't happy having $50,000 or a hundred-thousand dollars, you're not going to be happy if you have 50 million or a hundred million. I mean, a certain amount of money does make you feel — and those around you — feel better, just in terms of being more secure, in some cases. But loads and loads of money — I probably know as many rich people as just about anybody. And I do not — I don't think they're happier because they get super rich. I think they are happier when they don't have to worry about money. But you don't see a correlation between happiness and money, beyond a certain place. So, don't go overboard on delayed gratification. (Applause)
