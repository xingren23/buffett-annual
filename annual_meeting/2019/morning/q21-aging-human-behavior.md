## 问题

您说过年纪越大越懂人性。能否展开讲讲您学到了什么，理解人性如何帮助做出更好的投资？

## 回答要点

1. 自嘲：用任何尺子量都在走下坡路，「现在考 SAT 恐怕很难堪」
2. 但年岁增长让人更懂人的行为——经验摆在那里，单靠读书成不了人性专家
3. 芒格引用李光耀的心法：「找出什么行得通，就去做」
4. 「弄清什么行得通，就意味着弄清别人会如何行动」

## 关键引述

> **“Figure out what works, and do it.”**
>
> 「找出什么行得通，就去做。」

**Warren Buffett**（02:17:49）：好，7 号站。

**Audience Member**（02:17:45）：JC（音，11 岁，来自中国）：早上好，巴菲特先生、芒格先生。我叫 JC，11 岁，来自中国，这是我第二年参会。芒格先生，二月在《每日日报》（Daily Journal）年会后再次见到您真高兴。巴菲特先生，您说过年纪越大越懂人性。能否展开讲讲您学到了什么，以及理解人性如何帮助做出更好的投资？也想请芒格先生谈谈。非常感谢。（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:18:37）：你该等查理回答，他更老。（笑声）论变老，他能讲的比我多得多。事实是，用任何尺子量，我都在走下坡路。现在让我考 SAT，和我二十岁出头的成绩比，恐怕很难堪。（笑）查理和我能举很多例子，还有些我们不会讲的例子，说明人老了哪些东西在衰退。

但我要说：随着年岁增长，你确实能够、也确实应该更懂人的行为——经验摆在那里。查理和我年轻时把感兴趣的书读了个遍，从研究他人的人生中学到了大量东西。但无论智商多高、老师多好，单靠读书成不了人性专家。很多时候你必须靠亲历去学。尽管有种种短板——心算没以前快，阅读没以前快——我确实认为自己比 25 岁、30 岁时更懂人的行为。

**Charlie Munger**（02:20:36）：给你一句心法吧。它来自一位刚刚去世的中国绅士——李光耀，大概是世界历史上最伟大的建国者。他一辈子反反复复只说一件事：「找出什么行得通，就去做。」只要带着这个简单的哲学去生活，你会发现它奇妙地有效。找出什么行得通，就去做。

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:08）：而弄清什么行得通，就意味着弄清别人……

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:12）：当然。

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:13）：……会如何行动。

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:14）：当然。

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:15）：查理和我见过人性在种种意想不到之处的极端表现。

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:23）：现在每晚都能看到人性的极端表现——打开电视就行。

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:39）：是啊，幸好他举的是这个例子。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:17:49）：OK, station 7.

**Audience Member**（02:17:45）：Good morning Mr. Buffett, Mr. Munger. My name is JC. (PH) I am 11 years old, and I came from China. This is my second year at the meeting. Mr. Munger, it's great to see you again after the Daily Journal meeting in February. Mr. Buffett, you mentioned that the older you get, the more you understood about human nature. Could you elaborate more about what you've learned, and how can the differences of human nature help you make a better investment? I would also like Mr. Munger to comment on that, please. Thank you very much. (Applause)

**Warren Buffett**（02:18:37）：You should wait for Charlie's answer, because he's even older. (Laughter) He can tell you more about being old than I can even. It's absolutely true that virtually any yardstick you use, I'm going downhill. And, you know, if I would take an SAT test now, and you could compare it to a score of what I was in my early 20s, I think it'd be quite embarrassing. (Laughs) And Charlie and I can give you a lot of examples, and there's others we won't tell you about how things decline as you get older. But I would say this. It's absolutely true in my view that you can and should understand human behavior better as you do get older. You just have more experience with it. And I don't think you can read — Charlie and I read every book we could on every subject we were interested in, you know, when we were very young. And we learned an enormous amount just from studying the lives of other people. And — but I don't think you can get to be an expert on human behavior at all by reading books, no matter what your I.Q. is, no matter who the teacher is. And I think that you really do learn a lot about human behavior. Sometimes you have to learn it by having multiple experiences. I actually think I, despite all the other shortcomings — and I can't do mental arithmetic as fast as I used to, and I can't read as fast as I used to. But I do think that I know a lot more about human behavior than I did when I was 25 or 30 —

**Charlie Munger**（02:20:36）：I'll give you — do you want one mantra? It comes from a Chinese gentleman who just died, Lee Kuan Yew, who was the greatest nation builder probably that ever lived in the history of the world. And he said one thing over and over and over again all his life. "Figure out what works, and do it." If you just go at life with that simple philosophy from your own national group, you will find it works wonderfully well. Figure out what works, and do it.

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:08）：And figuring out what works means figuring out how other people —

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:12）：Of course.

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:13）：— behave.

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:14）：Of course.

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:15）：And Charlie and I have seen the extremes in human behavior, in so many unexpected ways.

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:23）：Now we get it every night, extremes in human behavior. All you got to do is turn on the television.

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:39）：Yeah. I'm glad he used that example. (Laughter)
