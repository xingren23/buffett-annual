## 问题

《金融时报》引述巴菲特称公司可能回购高达 1000 亿美元股票（约当前市值 20%）。这个数字怎么来的？预计在什么时间区间内实现？

## 回答要点

1. 「三秒钟想出来的整数」，重点是「买得起」
2. 若真回购 1000 亿，市值大概率远低于 5000 亿，实际占比可能远超 20%
3. 回购的前提是股东掌握与巴菲特写给两个妹妹的年报同等的信息
4. 芒格：「等到一目了然的时候，我们会干得很漂亮」

## 关键引述

> **“we would certainly be willing to spend $100 billion.”**
>
> 「我们肯定愿意花上 1000 亿美元。」

> **“When it gets really obvious, we'll be very good at it.”**
>
> 「等到一目了然的时候，我们会干得很漂亮。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:08）：巴克莱刚经历了某种代理权之争吧？杰伊·盖尔布（巴克莱保险分析师）：没错，沃伦。（笑）我也有一个关于回购的问题。沃伦，在最近一篇《金融时报》的报道里，你被引述说公司未来可能回购高达 1000 亿美元的股票，约相当于伯克希尔当前市值的 20%。这个 1000 亿的数字怎么来的？你预计会在什么时间区间内实现？

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:29）：这个 1000 亿大概是我被问到之后三秒钟内想出来的。（笑声）它是个整数，而且我们买得起。如果股价合适，我们也愿意这么做。我们有钱买进 1000 亿美元的股票。而且注意，如果我们真的回购 1000 亿，公司市值很可能不止 5000 亿——不对，是远低于 5000 亿——所以实际买下的比例可能远超 20%。我们会花很多钱。我们经历过一些公司几十年里股本减少 70% 到 80% 的案例，我们喜欢折价买回股份的想法。

既然要从股东——也就是合伙人——手里回购，而且我们认为股票便宜，我们就必须非常确定他们掌握着评估自己持股所需的事实。这就像一个合伙企业：三个合伙人里有两个合谋孤立第三个，比如不告诉他一些他们知道、而他不知道的重要信息——那就很不地道。所以我们的信息披露，必须与我写给两个妹妹的那种披露完全一致。她们不是假想中的股东，是真实存在的——我每年都把年报写给她们。因为我觉得，如果你要卖股票，你应当掌握和我、和查理同样重要的信息。

只要我们的股价相对内在价值便宜，我们不会犹豫。短期内很可能买不了那么多，但我们肯定愿意花上 1000 亿美元。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:50）：等到一目了然的时候，我们会干得很漂亮。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:44:56）：让我确认一下。你刚才到底说什么来着？

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:58）：等到一目了然的时候，我们会干得很漂亮。

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:05）：哦，对。我就希望你说的是这个。（笑声）是的，我们会干得很漂亮。我们从不缺果断。我们不常点头，但只要事情一目了然——杰伊，假如你我合伙做生意，生意值 100 万，你说愿意把你的那一半以 30 万卖给我，你的 30 万马上到账。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:08）：OK. Jay Gelb from Barclays. Barclays just had a proxy contest of sorts, didn't it? JAY GELB (INSURANCE ANALYST, BARCLAYS): That's right, Warren. (Laughs) I also have a question on Berkshire Hathaway — I'm sorry — on share buybacks. Warren, in a recent Financial Times article, you were quoted as saying that the time may come when the company buys back as much as $100 billion of its shares, which equates to around 20 percent of Berkshire's current market cap. How did you arrive at that $100 billion figure? And over what time frame would you expect this to occur?

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:29）：Yeah. I probably arrived at that $100 billion figure in about three seconds when I got asked the question. (Laughter) It was a nice round figure and we could do it. And we would like to do it if the stock was — we've got the money to buy in $100 billion worth of stock. And bear in mind, if we're buying in $100 billion stock, it probably would be that the company wasn’t selling at 500 billion. So, it might buy well over 20 percent. We will spend a lot of money. We've been involved in companies where the number of shares has been reduced 70 or 80 percent over time. And we like the idea of buying shares at a discount. We do feel, if shareholders — if we're going to be repurchasing shares from shareholders who are partners, and we think it's cheap, we ought to be very sure that they have the facts available to evaluate what they own. I mean, just as if we had a partnership, it would not be good if there were three partners and two of them decided that they would sort of freeze out the third, maybe in terms of giving him material information that they knew that that third party didn't know. So, it's very important that our disclosure be the same sort of disclosure that I would give to my sisters who are the imaginary — they're not imaginary — but they're the shareholders to whom I address the annual report every year. Because I do feel that you, if you're going to sell your stock, should have the same information that's important, that's available to me and to Charlie. But we will — if our stock gets cheap, relative to intrinsic value, we would not hesitate. We wouldn't be able to buy that much in a very short period of time, in all likelihood. But we would certainly be willing to spend $100 billion. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:50）：I think when it gets really obvious, we'll be very good at it. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:44:56）：Let me get that straight. What'd you say, exactly?

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:58）：When it gets really obvious, we'll be very good at it.

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:05）：Oh, yeah. I was hoping that's what you said. (Laughter) Yeah, we will be good at it. We don't have any trouble being decisive. We don't say yes very often. But if it's something obvious — I mean, Jay, if you and I are partners, you know, and our business is worth a million dollars and you say you'll sell your half to me for 300,000, you'll have your 300,000 very quickly.
