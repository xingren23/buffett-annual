## 问题

「解决方案铁路项目」团队成员建议伯克希尔借助公私合营把 BNSF 电气化、开放输电走廊，并愿当面提交提案。

## 回答要点

1. 按吨英里计，铁路约占美国货运 40%；BNSF 承运全美 15% 以上的吨英里
2. 一加仑柴油可把一吨货运 500 英里以上，长途重载优于卡车；短途是卡车的天下
3. 铁路从业人数从二战后的 150 多万降至不到 20 万，运量却大增，效率与安全性大幅提升
4. 当面提案被婉拒，巴菲特请股东去找 BNSF CEO 卡尔·艾斯谈

## 关键引述

> **“we can move 500-plus ton miles of freight for one gallon of diesel.”**
>
> 「一加仑柴油，我们能把一吨货物运 500 英里以上。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:25:54）：好，1 号站，我右手边远处的股东小组。

**Audience Member**（00:25:55）：比尔·莫耶（华盛顿州瓦雄岛）：早上好。我叫比尔·莫耶，来自华盛顿州瓦雄岛，是「解决方案铁路项目」团队的一员。有意思的是，美国货运价值中只有 3.5% 走铁路。伯克希尔通过 BNSF 同时握有北部和南部跨大陆干线，完全有条件把更多货运从公路上拉下来，让柴油远离我们的社区，同时把这些通道与伯克希尔引以为豪的可再生能源资产结合起来。你们是否愿意和我们见个面，看一看一份利用你们资产、借助公私合作把铁路电气化的提案，为可再生能源的未来打开这些通道？

**Warren Buffett**（00:27:15）：不会。我们考察过很多东西，比如液化天然气。我们显然希望能源效率更高，也希望整体效率更高。你说的 3.5% 我不确定——我不清楚你用的分母是什么。如果按吨英里来看，铁路大约占美国货运的 40%——这还不算本地配送之类的业务。BNSF 运的吨英里比任何其他实体都多，我们承运了全美 15% 以上的吨英里。

比如在多式联运上和卡车竞争：长途我们非常有竞争力，但运输距离越短，卡车的灵活性越占优——卡车哪儿都能去，而我们只有轨道。这个等式取决于运输距离和其他因素，距离是很大的因素。一加仑柴油，我们能把一吨货物运 500 英里以上，远比卡车高效。所以长途、重载的货物会流向铁路，我们也在不断改进自己在等式中的那部分。但如果你要把货从发货人运到 10 英里、20 英里、30 英里外的收货人，那就不会走铁路。

我可以向你保证，我们一直在研究这些东西。卡尔·艾斯就在这里——他现在大概就在现场，在隔壁房间——铁路由他运营。你可以跟他谈，但我不觉得有你说的那种突破。我确实看到我们一年比一年更高效。当然，如果无人驾驶卡车成为变量，天平会向卡车倾斜。但在长途重载——这类货物量很大——你面对的是运力超过任何其他运输方式的铁路，而 BNSF 是领头羊。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:30:52）：长远看，我们的提问者站在天使这一边。铁路迟早会更电气化，我想什么时候做由格雷格（阿贝尔）决定。

**Warren Buffett**（00:31:12）：是的。我们都在研发这些技术。看数字就知道，我们比十年、二十年、三十年前高效多了。有个有意思的数字：二战刚结束时，美国人口大约 1.4 亿，现在是 3.3 亿，当年只有如今的 40%。那时铁路行业雇用了超过 150 万人，现在不到 20 万，运的货却多得多。当然，客运有变化。但与二战刚结束时相比，铁路的效率和安全性都大幅提高。查理，还有要补充的吗？

**Charlie Munger**（00:32:11）：没有了。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:25:54）：OK. Station number 1, from the shareholder group up on my far right.

**Audience Member**（00:25:55）：Good morning. My name is Bill Moyer and I'm from Vashon Island, Washington. And I'm part of a team called "The Solutionary Rail Project." Interestingly, only 3.5 percent of the value of freight in the U.S. moves on trains. Berkshire Hathaway is incredibly well positioned with its investments in the northern and southern trans-con through BNSF to grab far more of that freight traffic off of the roads and get diesel out of our communities, as well as harness transmission corridors for your Berkshire renewable energy assets, for which you're obviously very proud. Would you consider meeting with us to look at a proposal for utilizing your assets and leveraging a public/private partnership to electrify your railroads and open those corridors for a renewable energy future?

**Warren Buffett**（00:27:15）：No, I — we've examined a lot of things in terms of LNG. I mean, they're — obviously, we want to become more energy efficient, as well as just generally efficient. And I'm not sure about the value of freight. You mentioned 3 1/2 percent. I believe — I mean, I'm not sure what figure you're using as the denominator there. Because if you look at movement of traffic by ton miles, rails are around 40 percent of the U.S. — we're not talking local deliveries or all kinds of things like that — but they're 40 percent, roughly, by rail. And BNSF moves more ton miles than any other entity. We move 15 percent-plus of all the ton miles moved in the United States. But if you take trucking, for example, on intermodal freight, we're extremely competitive on the longer hauls, but the shorter the haul, the more likely it is that the flexibility of freight, where a truck can go anyplace and we have rails. So, the equation changes depending on distance hauled and other factors, but distance hauled is a huge factor. We can move a ton mile 500 — we can move 500-plus ton miles of freight for one gallon of diesel. And that is far more efficient than trucks. So, the long-haul traffic, and the heavy traffic, is going to go to the rails, and we try to improve our part of the equation on that all the time. But if you're going to transport something ten or 20 or 30 miles between a shipper and a receiver, and they're — you're not going to move that by rail. So, we look at things all the time, I can assure you. Carl Ice is in — well, he's probably here now, and he'll be in the other room — and he's running the railroad. You're free to talk to him, but I don't see any breakthrough like you're talking about. I do see us getting more efficient year-by-year-by-year. And obviously, if driverless trucks become part of the equation, that moves things toward trucking. But on long-haul, heavy stuff, and there's a lot of it, you're looking at the railroad that carries more than any other mode of transportation. And BNSF is the leader. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:30:52）：Well, over the long term, our questioner is on the side of the angels. Sooner or later, we'll have it more electrified. I think Greg (Abel) will decide when it happens.

**Warren Buffett**（00:31:12）：Yeah. But we're all working on the technology but — And we're considerably more efficient than ten, 20, 30 years ago, if you look at the numbers. But it — One interesting figure, I think right after World War II, when the country probably had about 140 million people against our 330 million now, so we had 40 percent of the population. We had over a million-and-a-half people employed in the railroad industry. Now there's less than 200,000 and we're carrying a whole lot more freight. Now, obviously there's some change in passengers. But the efficiency of the railroads compared to — and the safety — compared to what it was even immediately after World War II has improved dramatically. Charlie, anything more?

**Charlie Munger**（00:32:11）：No.
