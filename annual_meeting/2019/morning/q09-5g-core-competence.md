## 问题

5G 将挑战所有行业的模式，想在新时代抓住最好的投资机会，应该掌握什么核心能力？

## 回答要点

1. 伯克希尔顶端没有核心能力，靠子公司经理人预判各自行业的变化
2. 「我们不在总部层面集中管理这些」，经理人在很大程度上把公司当成自己的
3. 芒格：「我对 5G 知之甚少，但对中国略知一二——我们在中国买过东西，还会买更多」
4. 54 年里纺织、制鞋、百货、交易邮票等创始业务都被变化摧毁，伯克希尔的答案是调整与再配置资金

## 关键引述

> **“my guess is we'll buy more.”**
>
> 「我猜我们还会买更多。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:30）：好，3 号站。

**Audience Member**（01:01:35）：Terry（音，上海）：查理·芒格和沃伦·巴菲特，你们好。（听不清）我是来自上海的 Terry，我们的目标是抓住这个时代最好的投资机会。我的问题是：众所周知，5G 要来了。据说 5G 时代所有行业的模式都将受到挑战。如果想在新时代抓住最好的投资机会，我们应该掌握什么核心能力？谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:15）：伯克希尔的最顶端没有所谓的核心能力。（笑声）与 5G 相关的发展，或者这个世界上将发生的各种事——铁路上液化天然气的用途之类的问题——子公司里有比我们懂得多得多的专业人才。我们依靠经理人去预判各自行业的走向，他们有时会跟我们谈。但我们不在总部层面集中管理这些。查理，你要补充吗？你对 5G 有什么我不知道的了解？哦，你大概懂很多。

**Charlie Munger**（01:03:29）：不，我对 5G 知之甚少。但我对中国略知一二。我们在中国买过东西，我猜还会买更多。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:37）：是的。我们的想法是要有一批精通自己业务的经理人——我们确实有。你看到了，BNSF、伯克希尔·哈撒韦能源、路博润各自都有准备。这些人懂自己的行业，知道可能发生什么变化。有时他们还能在业务之间找到合作点。但我们不试图从总部指挥。这可能在某些时候有弱点，但净效应看，这对伯克希尔是巨大的好处。我们的经理人在很大程度上把公司当成自己的。我们要他们有主人翁感，不希望他们迷失在某个庞大的集团里——今天听这个小组的指示，明天听那个小组的。我可以讲几个我们收购的公司在这类体制下的恐怖故事。

世界会剧烈变化。想想伯克希尔这 54 年里世界变了多少。有些变化伤害过我们：纺织、制鞋、百货公司、交易邮票——这些是我们的创始业务。但我们确实会调整。整体上我们拥有相当一批好生意。有些生意确实会被时代的变迁摧毁。但我仍然是那个持证的资本主义者，我相信变化是好事，但你必须做出改变。1800 年 80% 的人务农。如果没有巨大的变化、还需要 80% 的人去生产粮食和棉花，我们就会是一个完全不同的社会。所以我们欢迎变化，也希望经理人有预判和适应的能力。但有时我们会错，那些生意会枯萎死亡，我们就该把钱用到别处。查理？好，卡罗尔。查理，你最近可没吃花生脆糖。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:30）：OK, station 3.

**Audience Member**（01:01:35）：Hello Charlie Munger and Warren Buffett, (unintelligible). I am Terry (PH) from Shanghai (unintelligible), which aims to catch the best investment opportunities in that era. So, my question is, as we all know, 5G is coming. It is said that the mode of all industry will be challenged in 5G era. So, what is the core competence that we should master, if (unintelligible) wants to catch the best investment opportunities in this era? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:15）：Well, there's no core competence at the very top of Berkshire. (Laughter) The subsidiaries that will be involved in developments relating to 5G, or any one of all kinds of things that are going to happen in this world, you know, the utility of LNG in the railroad, or all those kinds of questions, we have people in those businesses that know a lot more about them than we do. And we count on our managers to anticipate what is coming in their business. And sometimes they talk to us about it. But we do not run that on a centralized basis. And Charlie, do you want to have anything to add to that? Do you know anything about 5G I don't know? Well, you probably know a lot about 5G.

**Charlie Munger**（01:03:29）：No, I know very little about 5G. But I do know a little about China. And we have bought things in China. And my guess is we'll buy more. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:37）：Yeah. But I mean, we basically want to have a group of managers, and we do have a group of managers, who are on top of their businesses. I mean, you saw something that showed BNSF and Berkshire Hathaway Energy and Lubrizol all aware of that. Those people know their businesses. They know what changes are likely to be had. Sometimes, they find things that they can cooperate on between their businesses. But we don't try to run those from headquarters. And that may mean — that may have certain weaknesses at certain times. I think, net, it's been a terrific benefit for Berkshire. Our managers, to a great degree, own their businesses. And we want them to feel a sense of ownership. We don't want them to be lost in some massive conglomerate, where they get directions from this group, which is a subgroup of that group. And I could tell you a few horror stories from companies we bought, when they tell us about their experience under such an operation. The world is going to change in dramatic ways. Just think how much it's changed in the 54 years that we've had Berkshire. And some of those changes hurt us. They hurt us in textiles. They hurt us in shoes. They hurt us in the department store business. Hurt us in the trading stamp business. These were the founding businesses of this operation. But we do adjust. And we've got a group, overall, of very good businesses. We've got some that will be, actually, destroyed by what happens in this world. But that’s — I still am the card-carrying capitalist. And I believe that that's a good thing, but you have to make changes. We had 80 percent of the people working on farms in 1800. And if there hadn't been a lot of changes, and you needed 80 percent of the people in the country producing the food and cotton we needed, we would have a whole different society. So, we welcome change. And we certainly want to have managers that can anticipate and adapt to it. But sometimes, we'll be wrong. And those businesses will wither and die. And we'd better use the money someplace else. Charlie? OK, Carol. Charlie, you haven’t had any peanut brittle lately, you know. (Laughs)
