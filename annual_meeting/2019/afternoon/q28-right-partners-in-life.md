## 问题

你们是一对标志性组合。当你们之间出现分歧时，如何处理？

## 回答要点

1. 「60 年里我们从未争吵过」——有分歧，但从没有情绪或愤怒介入
2. 通常巴菲特说了算，因为芒格愿意如此；双方从不事后质疑对方
3. 芒格：问题不在相处，而在「我们走后公司会怎样——答案是：会很好」
4. 巴菲特 28 岁才认识芒格，全靠医生埃迪·戴维斯夫妇牵线
5. 「选对人生伙伴——尤其是选对配偶——极其重要」；秘书与 CFO 的继任者都更好，「也许股东们还会再幸运几次」

## 关键引述

> **“having the right partners in life is enormously important.”**
>
> 「选对人生伙伴极其重要。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:09:42）：好，7 号站。

**Audience Member**（02:09:48）：比尔·何（音，加拿大温哥华）：下午好，巴菲特先生、芒格先生。我叫比尔·何，来自加拿大温哥华。你们两位是一对标志性组合，我从小就很仰慕你们的投资策略。我的问题是：当你们之间出现分歧时，如何处理？

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:08）：你指的是个人层面的冲突——自己做还是让伯克希尔做——还是……哦，你们俩之间？查理和我，人们很难相信，60 年里我们从未争吵过。我们有分歧，大概还会偶尔在这件事那件事上分歧。但如果争吵的定义是情绪或愤怒开始介入，那从未发生。我认为查理比我聪明，但在某些事情上我花的功夫比他多。有时我们俩都认为自己对。通常是我说了算，因为查理愿意如此，而且事情做错时他从不质疑我。他做错事时我也绝不会质疑他。我们基本上不会有冲突。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:11:18）：问题不在我们相处得如何，而在我们走后公司会怎样。答案是：会很好。

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:33）：我们很幸运。我 28 岁时遇到他。我们曾在同一家杂货店打工，他在离我现在的住处不到一个街区的地方长大——但我 28 岁前根本不知道查理·芒格是谁。（笑）

显然，我们看世界的方式同频，商业决策也基本同频。查理愿意做的事比我少，那是因为我把时间花在这里，而他在设计宿舍之类。（笑声）我们各忙各的，也乐在其中。

选对人生伙伴——尤其是选对配偶——极其重要。有伙伴，个人生活和生意都更有趣，成就也可能更多，日子也更好过。独自在小屋里倒腾证券赚大钱、从不与人共事，那没什么乐趣。所以我的建议是——查理在电影里给过一条：去找愿意要你的最好的人。（笑）目标比较有限。

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:01）：但如果你是个收藏家又不缺钱，快乐并不难。收藏本身就有乐趣。想想你认识一辈子的人里，有几个收藏家缺钱花却收藏得不快乐？这就是我们收藏一辈子的原因。总有下一块石头等你翻开，有意思。

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:33）：是的。从某种意义上，我们收藏的是让生活更好、相处愉快的朋友。选择谁是你的英雄、谁是你的朋友，非常重要。我在这方面很幸运——查理和我能相识，全靠一位叫埃迪·戴维斯的医生和他的妻子。只要你做事够多，总会有些幸运的事发生，而最好的是那些让你与他人结下一生情谊的事。我们的董事里也有几位对我有类似的影响。所以我建议你去找比你强的人，然后努力变得像他们。

**Charlie Munger**（02:14:22）：有意思的是，一路走来我们也送别过一些人。失去沃伦的秘书格拉迪斯（凯泽）时我想：「天哪，她那么出色，我们再也找不到第二个了。」结果贝基（阿米克）更好。（笑声）然后是弗恩·麦肯齐，出色的首席财务官，他走了，现在的继任者（马克·汉堡）更好。我们非常幸运。也许股东们还会再幸运几次。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:09:42）：OK, station 7.

**Audience Member**（02:09:48）：Hi. Good afternoon, Mr. Buffett and Mr. Munger. My name is Bill He (PH), and I'm from Vancouver, Canada. You two make up an iconic duo. And growing up, I found your investment strategies very admirable. And so, my question is, how do you deal with conflicts when they arise between the two of you?

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:08）：Are you applying personal conflicts in terms of doing something ourselves versus having Berkshire, do it? Or — oh, between the two of you. I'm just in — Charlie and I literally, and people find this hard to believe, but in 60 years we've never had an argument. We have disagreed about things and we'll probably keep occasionally disagreeing about this or that. But if you define an argument as something where emotion starts entering into it, or anger or anything of the sort, it just doesn't happen. I think that Charlie is smarter than I am, but I also think that there are certain things where I've spent more on them than he has. And sometimes we both think we're right. And generally, I get my way because Charlie is willing to do it that way and he's never second-guessed me when things have been wrong. And I wouldn't dream of second-guessing him if he were doing something that was wrong — that turned out to be wrong. We will never have a conflict, basically. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:11:18）：Well, the issue isn't how long — how we get along, the issue is how is the company going to work when we're gone? And the answer is fine. It's going to work fine.

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:33）：We're lucky that, you know, I ran into him when I was, what, 28 years old. And — we both worked in the same grocery store and he grew up less than a block away from where I now live and everything. But I did not know who Charlie Munger was — (laughs) — until I was 28. But clearly, we're in sync in how we see the world. And we're in sync on business decisions, basically. Charlie would do fewer things than I would, but that's because I'm spending my time on this while he's designing dormitories or something. (Laughter) And we both keep busy in our own ways. And we have a lot of fun dividing the labor like we do. But you really want to work — I mean, having the right partners in life, particularly the right spouse, but having the right partners in life is enormously important. I mean, it's more fun with a partner, both in personal life and in business life. And you probably get more accomplished, too. But you just have a better time. It would not be any fun to do work in a little room and make a ton of money trading around securities but never working with another human being. So, I recommend finding — well, Charlie gave some advice in the movie, finding the best person that will have you or something like that. (Laughs) Sort of a limited objective.

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:01）：But it's not hard to be happy if you're a collector and don't run out of money. Collecting is intrinsically fun. Just think who — how many people who you've known your whole life who were collectors who didn't run out of money, who weren't happy collecting? That's why we've been collecting all our lives. You know, it's a very interesting thing. There's always a new rock to be turned over. And it's interesting.

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:33）：Yeah. And in a certain way we've collected friends that make our lives better and that we have a good time with. And it's very important, you know, who you select as your heroes or friends. And I've been lucky in this. I mean, it was only because of the doctor named Eddie Davis and his wife that Charlie and I even met. But if you keep doing enough things, some will work out very lucky. And the best ones are ones that involve lifelong involvement with other people. We've got some in our directors, a number of our directors, that have had similar impacts on me. So, I recommend that you look for somebody better than you are and then try to be like they are.

**Charlie Munger**（02:14:22）：It's funny, you know, we've lost people along the way. And when I lost Warren's secretary I thought, "Oh, my God. She was so wonderful. Gladys (Kaiser). We'll never get another one." Becki (Amick) is better. (Laughter) And then we had Verne McKenzie, who was a wonderful chief financial officer. He's gone and the current incumbent (Marc Hamburg) is better. We've been very lucky. And maybe the shareholders will be lucky a few more times. (Laughter)
