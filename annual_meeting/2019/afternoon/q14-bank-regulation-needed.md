## 问题

伯克希尔持有的大型金融机构受重监管、政治敏感度高。某些情况下，是不是监管者和政客、而不是 CEO 和董事会在管理这些大银行？

## 回答要点

1. 芒格：「当然。（笑声）但没太过分。」
2. GEICO 从年营收 700 万美元到 300 多亿，全程都在州级监管下
3. 保险天然容易招骗子——「钱先到你手里，承诺给了对方」，所以必须监管
4. 存款保险隐含契约：用政府信用就不能拿钱太放肆；房地产危机顶点的投行行为「只有一个词：恶心」
5. 「我本人觉得任何监管都烦人，但它对系统有益」——银行业早有过「狂野西部」

## 关键引述

> **“there's only word for the behavior — it was disgusting.”**
>
> 「那种行为只有一个词可以形容：恶心。」

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:18）：贝基？

**Becky Quick**（01:09:22）：贝基·奎克（CNBC）：问题来自威斯康星州格拉夫顿的布莱恩·古斯特，谈的是监管者与政客。「伯克希尔的投资组合里有几家受重监管、政治敏感度高的大型金融机构。你是否觉得，某些情况下是监管者和政客、而不是 CEO 和董事会在管理这些大银行？」

**Charlie Munger**（01:09:51）：当然。（笑声）但没太过分。

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:54）：保险一直在受监管——主要按州监管——从我们入行起就是这样。我看 GEICO 的时候，它一年做 700 万美元的生意；现在做 300 多亿。整段时间都有监管。监管通常烦人，但另一方面，我们不想要一帮江湖骗子经营保险。保险天然容易招骗子——钱先到你手里，承诺给了对方。我喜欢保险业和银行业有监管，这不意味着它不会偶尔把人逼疯，但这些行业就该被监管——它们太重要了。凡是你能拿走别人的钱、对方只带走一纸承诺的行业，我不介意一定程度的监管。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:11:06）：是。你用的是政府的信用——因为有存款保险，这里有个隐含契约：你不能拿那些钱太放肆。这完全合理。我绝对认为应该有一套监管体系来监督银行的冒险行为。房地产危机顶点时投资银行的问题尤其严重，那种行为只有一个词可以形容：恶心。而且几乎人人如此。沃伦，那场繁荣里能保持清醒的人屈指可数——他们都觉得别人在干蠢事，我不同干就落后了。多么疯狂的行为方式。所以，是的，有干预，但可能必须有。

**Warren Buffett**（01:12:09）：你需要食品药品管理局。身处行业里你会不满他们怎么管——我本人觉得任何监管都烦人——但它对系统有益。实际上不少监管者相当明智。可当你被人指挥怎么经营时，你不会有这种感觉。但如查理所讲，你不会想要一个谁都能进来乱来、闯了祸还要拖你下水的无监管银行体系。银行业很久以前就有过「狂野西部」，19 世纪制造了一堆问题。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:18）：Becky?

**Becky Quick**（01:09:22）：This question comes from Brian Gust of Grafton, Wisconsin. He's talking about regulators and politicians. It says, "The Berkshire Hathaway investment portfolio holds several large financial institutions that are heavily regulated and are politically charged. "Do you feel that, in some cases, the regulators and/or politicians are running the big banks instead of the CEO and the board of directors?"

**Charlie Munger**（01:09:51）：Sure. (Laughter) But not too much.

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:54）：No, insurance has been regulated — it happens to be regulated primarily on a state basis — but insurance has been regulated ever since we went into it, and it hasn't — you know, when I looked at GEICO, it was doing 7 million of business. And, you know, it will do 30-odd billion of — billion — of business now. And it's been regulated the whole time. And regulation can be a pain in the neck, generally, but on the other hand, we don't want a bunch of charlatans operating in the insurance business. And insurance actually lends itself to charlatans because you get handed money and you give the other guy a promise. And I like the fact that there is regulation in the insurance business, or the banking business. It doesn't mean it can't drive you crazy sometimes or anything of the sort, but those businesses should be regulated. It — they're too important. And anytime you can take other people's money, and they go home with a promise and you go home with the money, I don't mind a certain amount of regulation in those businesses. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:11:06）：Yeah. You're using the government's credit because you have deposit insurance, there's an implicit bargain. You can't be too crazy with what you do with the money. That's a perfectly reasonable — And I absolutely believe that we should have a regulation system that involves supervision of risk-taking by banks. It got particularly bad in the investment banks at the peak of the real estate crisis, and the behavior was — there's only word for the behavior — it was disgusting. And it was pretty much everybody. Warren, you — it's hard to think of anybody who stayed sane in that boom. They felt the other guy's doing dumb things, I've got to do it, too, or I'll be left out. What a crazy way to behave. And so, sure, there's some intervention, but there probably has to be.

**Warren Buffett**（01:12:09）：You want a Food and Drug Administration. Yeah. You'll be unhappy with how they do it, if you're in the business and all that, but — and, you know, I find any kind of regulation irritating. But nevertheless, it's good for the system. And actually, a number of regulators, you know, I would say that they've really been quite sensible about regulation. But you don't feel that way when you're being told how to run your business. But as Charlie says, you wouldn't want to be a bank that ran in an unregulated system where anybody could come in and do all kinds of things that would actually have consequences that drew you into the problems that they created themselves. We had the Wild West in banking long ago, and it produced a lot of problems in the 19th century.
