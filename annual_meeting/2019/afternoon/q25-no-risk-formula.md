## 问题

自律的风险评估方法，如何与「坚韧、决心、勇气常常是成功所必需」相平衡？

## 回答要点

1. 「伯克希尔没有任何评估风险的公式」，每笔交易自己算风险对收益
2. 第一道关：合理确信自己知道自己在做什么；再算收益损失与可承受的损失
3. 委员会与表格只会产出你想听的数字——「门槛 15%，所有项目都会算出 15.1」
4. 提案 stories：私人公司靠期权不入账把 1 亿盈利「变成」1.1 亿；私募基金让经理先加 15% 对冲巴菲特的砍价
5. 芒格收尾：「我们为什么希望我们的社会栋梁撒谎和行骗呢？」

## 关键引述

> **“we don't have any formula that evaluates risk”**
>
> 「我们没有任何评估风险的公式」

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:38）：好，6 号站。

**Audience Member**（01:57:43）：托尼·麦考尔（阿拉巴马州蒙哥马利）：下午好。我叫托尼·麦考尔，来自阿拉巴马州蒙哥马利。我的问题是关于你们自律的风险评估方法：你们如何把它与「坚韧、决心、勇气常常是成功所必需」这一事实相平衡？

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:12）：我当然喜欢与我们共事的人有决心和勇气。但我们没有任何评估风险的公式，我们只是在每一笔交易里自己做风险对收益的计算——无论是有价证券、私人投资，还是收购一门生意。有时我们会错，未来还会错。做这行，不做错很多决定是不可能的。但我们不认为一堆委员会、一堆表格能改善这个过程或结果。

如果我手下一个团队，他们会去猜我想要的答案，然后告诉我我想听的话。我在 20 家上市公司看过这种戏码。CEO 想做什么，投行家会兜圈子最终附和，内部委员会也会附和。

这和阿吉特（贾因）的保险业务一个道理：阿吉特接到保险询价，必须把这笔交易想透。关键的第一道关是：你是否合理地确信自己知道自己在做什么？过了这道关，再算收益对损失的数学，以及我们承受得起多大损失。只要我们认为收益的可能性更大、与风险相称，我们愿意接受听起来很大的损失。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:00:10）：我没什么可说的。

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:11）：很令人失望——伯克希尔没有任何公式。我们不会让人们加班到午夜做表格。如果门槛收益率定在 15%，所有项目都会算出 15.1 或 15.2——事情一定如此。你会得到你想听的数字，而且严重到极端的程度。

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:40）：投资界送来的提案很多，我必须讲一个，因为它说明了一切。前几天我们收到一份提案（数字我稍作伪装，免得被人认出），是一家私人公司，就说它年赚 1 亿美元。但卖方和投行建议我们把盈利看成 1.1 亿：理由是作为私人公司，他们必须用现金给高管付薪（计入费用），而我们可以用股票期权之类支付（不入费用，或被解释为「其实不算数」），于是只要送出一些我们本来不想送的东西，报表就能变成 1.1 亿。

换句话说，靠会计上撒点谎，就能多出 1000 万盈利，他们要我们为此付钱——因为他们自己做不到，我们做得到。到这一步，我当然已经完全失去兴趣。玩弄会计的花样令人瞠目：各种「调整」都在论证这个地方「其实」比以前赚得更多。这是一门生意。

还有一回，一家私募基金发来的邮件阴差阳错到了我们手里——那是基金方发给所投公司经理的预测制作指引。他们让经理把预测加上 15%，理由是「巴菲特反正会砍 15% 到 20%」。（笑）所以先加 15% 对冲他的保守。查理会告诉你，这行当不体面。（笑声）查理，你有没有更好的故事？（笑声）

**Charlie Munger**（02:02:42）：真的非常够糟了。

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（02:02:45）：安德鲁·罗斯·索尔金：谢谢沃伦。我想它是——

**Charlie Munger**（02:02:48）：我们为什么希望我们的社会栋梁撒谎和行骗呢？

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:38）：OK, station 6.

**Audience Member**（01:57:43）：Good afternoon, my name's Tony McCall and I'm from Montgomery, Alabama. And my question is about your disciplined risk evaluation approach and how you balance that with the fact that perseverance and determination and grit are often necessary for success.

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:12）：Well, I'm not — I certainly like determination and grit in the — with the people we work for. But we don't have any formula that evaluates risk, but we certainly make our own calculation of risk versus reward in every transaction we do. And that's true whether it's marketable securities, that's true whether it's private investments, that's true whether it's making an investment in a business. And sometimes we're wrong, and we're going to be wrong sometimes in the future. You can't make a lot of decisions in this business without being wrong. But we don't think the procedure — or the results — would be changed favorably by having lots of committees and lots of spreadsheets and that sort of thing. It just — you know — If I had a group under me, they would try and figure out what I wanted the answer to be, and they would tell me what I wanted to hear. And I've watched that approach at 20 public companies. And what the CEO wants to do, they may spend a lot of time getting there, but the investment banker gets there, and the internal committees get there, — or the internal operations — get there. The calculations are — it's the same as the insurance business with Ajit (Jain). Ajit gets calls on insurance deals, and you have to think through that deal. The main thing is you have — are you reasonably sure that you know what you're doing? And if it gets past that hurdle, then we go on to figure out the math of gain versus loss and how much loss we can afford to take in anything. And we're willing to take what sounds like large losses if we think that the rewards are more likely and proportional. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:00:10）：I've got nothing.

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:11）：It's very disappointing — we have no formulas around Berkshire. We don't sit down and write a bunch, you know — have people work till midnight calculating things and putting spreadsheets together. And if the hurdle rate is 15 percent or something, having them all come out at 15.1 or 15.2, because that's what's going to happen. I mean, you're going to get the numbers you want to hear and to an extreme degree.

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:40）：The proposals we see from the investment world — I've got to tell you about one because it illustrates what goes on. We received a proposition the other day — and I'll disguise the numbers a little bit so somebody can pick it out — but it was a private company, and we'll say it was earning a hundred million dollars a year. But the seller of the business and the investment banker suggested that we should look at the earnings as being 110 million dollars a year, because as a private company they had to pay their top people in cash, which was expensed. But we could pay them in stock options and things like that, which weren't expensed, or were explained as not really counting and therefore we could report 110 million dollars if we gave away something we didn't want to give away. But by essentially — by sort of lying about our accounting, we could add 10 million dollars in earnings, and they wanted us to pay them because they couldn't do it and we could do it. And therefore — at this point, they're losing me of course, totally. But it — it's just astounding the accounting games that are played. All the adjustments are why the place should really be — will be — earning more than before. It's a business. We also had one that came in from a private equity firm and by a mistake we got the email that was sent to the manager from the email from the private equity firm that owned it, to the manager, in terms of making projections for it. And they told them to add 15 percent because they said Buffett will discount it by 15 percent or 20 percent anyway. (Laughs) So just add 15 percent to offset his conservatism. You know, it's not an elegant business, as Charlie will tell you. (Laughter) You have any better stories, Charlie? (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（02:02:39）：It's bad enough.

**Warren Buffett**（02:02:41）：OK, Andrew —

**Charlie Munger**（02:02:42）：It's really very bad enough.

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（02:02:45）：Thank you, Warren. I think it's —

**Charlie Munger**（02:02:48）：Why do we want our leading citizens lying and cheating?
