## 问题

你是指数投资的鼓吹者，却让伯克希尔把巨额现金放在国库券里。若过去 15 年投入指数基金，2018 年底 1120 亿美元将变成约 1550 亿，机会成本超过账面价值的 12%。你怎么回应？

## 回答要点

1. 承认「完全正当的问题」，数字不争辩；接班人或许会采用
2. 2007—08 年若这么做，2008 年底、2009 年出手的能力可能就没了
3. 机会「成串而来」，且在别人不想配置资本的时候来；「一周前我们刚承诺了 100 亿美元」
4. 芒格引哈佛之鉴：最后一滴现金砸在错误时点、痛苦好几年；「我们不会改变」
5. 经营原则：不让以内在价值买入的股东永久性亏钱——他们是把身家押在伯克希尔上的人

## 关键引述

> **“there'll be two or three times when it'll be raining gold and all you have to do is go outside.”**
>
> 「会有两三次「天上掉金子」的时候，你要做的只是出门拿桶。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:52:26）：卡罗尔？

**Carol Loomis**（00:52:46）：「沃伦，你是指数投资和不做市场择时的鼓吹者。但伯克希尔持有巨额现金放在国库券里，在我看来你并没有践行自己的主张。一个不错的替代方案是：把伯克希尔多余的现金大部分投入一只分散良好的指数基金，直到找到有吸引力的收购或回购股票的机会。如果过去 15 年一直这么做——同时保留你想要的 200 亿美元现金垫——我估计到 2018 年底，公司 1120 亿美元的现金、现金等价物、短期投资与国库券余额将变成约 1550 亿美元。两个数字之差是机会成本，相当于伯克希尔当前账面价值的 12% 以上。你怎么回应？」——忘了说，提问者是佛罗里达州博卡拉顿 Colony Group 的迈克·埃尔扎尔。

**Warren Buffett**（00:53:45）：这是个完全正当的问题，数字我也不争辩。我可以说，这是我接班人可能希望采用的做法。因为总体上，我宁愿持有指数基金也不愿持有国库券。

但要说，如果我们在 2007、2008 年推行这个政策，2008 年底或 2009 年出手的能力可能就不同了。几千亿美元规模的执行难度和十几亿美元的类似政策不可同日而语。但这是完全理性的观察，回顾十年牛市它确实刺眼。我承认：如果数字小一些，这做法很有道理。数字大一些的话，未来伯克希尔或许也值得这么做。

一周前我们刚承诺了 100 亿美元。存在那样的情形——虽不常见，未必发生在任何给定的一周、一月或一年——让我们能非常迅速地花掉 1000 亿美元。如果那种情形出现，那笔资本将被极好地配置，远胜指数基金。所以我们一直按「会有配置资本的机会」来经营。机会大概率成串而来，而且会在别人不想配置资本的时候来。查理，你怎么看？

**Charlie Munger**（00:55:51）：我承认在现金上比别人保守一点，但我觉得没问题。事后诸葛亮地看，我们本可以把所有钱买进一堆跑赢标普的证券。别忘了，整个时期我们都握着额外的现金，就是为了机会来时用得上。像我们这么大的公司，现金多一点不是罪。

我看着哈佛把最后一滴现金——包括家长预付的学费——在恰恰错误的时点全数砸进市场，还做了大量私募股权的远期承诺，痛苦了好几年。我们不想变成哈佛。

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:42）：还有木材和一堆……

**Charlie Munger**（00:56:44）：什么？

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:45）：还有木材。我是说……

**Charlie Munger**（00:56:50）：对对。我们不会改变。（笑声与掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:55）：我们确实喜欢手里有足够的钱，能行动得又快又大。我们知道——也许会错过——这类机会不会常来。但我确信，未来二三十年里会有两三次「天上掉金子」的时候，你要做的只是出门拿桶。可我们不知道它们何时发生。而我们有大笔钱可以出手。

我承认：如果只能在短期国库券和指数基金之间二选一，把这笔钱投给美国经济，我选指数基金。但我们仍抱有希望。

关于伯克希尔你应当非常明确的一点是：我们经营这家公司的方式，始终考虑的是那些几乎全部净资产都在伯克希尔的股东。我自己恰好就在这个位置，但无论何种情况我都会这么做。按标准的投资建议衡量，很多人持有的伯克希尔占其净资产的比例高得不成比例。我们要为所有人赚钱，但我们要非常非常确保：以约等于内在企业价值的价格买入我们股票的人，不会永久性亏钱——只要他们愿意陪我们走一段。我们不愿看到上百万、也许两百万股东中有人真正亏钱。我们也知道世道动荡时人们如何行事——他们想要倚靠某种像直布罗陀巨岩一样的东西。我们对这批人有一份真实的责任感。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:52:26）：Carol?

**Carol Loomis**（00:52:46）："Warren, you are a big advocate of index investing, and of not trying to time the market. But by your having Berkshire hold such a large amount of cash in T-bills, it seems to me you don't practice what you preach. "I'm thinking that a good alternative would be for you to invest most of Berkshire's excess cash in a well-diversified index fund until you find an attractive acquisition or buy back stocks. "Had you done that over the past 15 years, all the time keeping the $20 billion cash cushion you want, I estimate that at the end of 2018, the company's 112 billion balance in cash, cash equivalents, in short-term investments and T-bills, would've instead been worth about 155 billion. The difference between the two figures is an opportunity cost equal to more than 12 percent of Berkshire's current book value. What is your response to what I say?" And I forgot to say the question is from Mike Elzahr, who is with the Colony Group, located in Boca Raton, Florida.

**Warren Buffett**（00:53:45）：That's a perfectly decent question, and I wouldn't quarrel with the numbers. And I would say that that is an alternative, for example, that my successor may wish to employ. Because, on balance, I would rather own an index fund than carry Treasury bills. I would say that if we'd instituted that policy in 2007 or '08, we might have been in a different position in terms of our ability to move late in 2008 or 2009. So, it has certain — it has certain execution problems with hundreds of billions of dollars than it does if you were having a similar policy with a billion or 2 billion or something of the sort. But it's a perfectly rational observation. And certainly, looking back on ten years of a bull market, it really jumps out at you. But I would argue that if you were working with smaller numbers, it would make a lot of sense. And if you're working with large numbers, it might well make sense in the future at Berkshire to operate that way. You know, we committed 10 billion a week ago. And there are conditions under which— and they're not remote, they're not likely in any given week or month or year — but there are conditions under which we could spend a hundred billion dollars very, very quickly. And if we did — if those conditions existed — it would be capital very well deployed, and much better than in an index fund. So, we've been — we're operating on the basis that we will get chances to deploy capital. They will come in clumps in all likelihood. And they will come when other people don't want to allocate capital. Charlie, what do you think about it?

**Charlie Munger**（00:55:51）：Well, I plead guilty to being a little more conservative with the cash than other people. But I think that's all right. We could have put all the money into a lot of securities that would've done better than the S&P with 20/20 hindsight. Remember, we had all that extra cash all that period, if something had come along in the way of opportunities and so on. I don't think it's a sin to be a little strong on cash when you're as a big a company as we are. We don't have to — I watched Harvard use the last ounce of their cash, including all their prepaid tuition from the parents, and plunge it into the market at exactly the wrong moment and make a lot of forward commitments to private equity. And they suffered, like, two or three years of absolute agony. We don't want to be like Harvard.

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:42）：Plus timber and a whole bunch of —

**Charlie Munger**（00:56:44）：What?

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:45）：Plus timber. And I mean —

**Charlie Munger**（00:56:50）：Yeah, yeah. We're not going to change. (Laughter and applause)

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:55）：We do like having a lot of money to be able to operate very fast and very big. And we know — and maybe we won't — we know we won't get those opportunities frequently. I don't think — certainly, you know, in the next 20 or 30 years there'll be two or three times when it'll be raining gold and all you have to do is go outside. But we don't know when they will happen. And we have a lot of money to commit. And I would say that if you told me I had to either carry short-term Treasury bills or have index funds and just let that money be invested in America generally, I would take the index funds. But we still have hopes. And the one thing you should very definitely understand about Berkshire is that we run the business in a way that we think is consistent with serving shareholders who have virtually all of their net worth in Berkshire. I happen to be in that position myself, but I would do it that way under any circumstances. We have a lot of people who trust us, who really have disproportionate amounts of Berkshire compared to their net worth, if you were to follow standard investment procedures. And we want to make money for everybody, but we want to make very, very sure that we don't lose permanently money for anybody that buys our stock somewhere around intrinsic business value to begin with. We just have an aversion to having a million-plus shareholders, maybe as many as two million, and having a lot of them ever really lose money, if they're willing to stay with us for a while. And we know how people behave when the world, generally, is upset. And they want to be with something — I think they want to be with something they feel is like the Rock of Gibraltar. And we have a real disposition toward that group.
