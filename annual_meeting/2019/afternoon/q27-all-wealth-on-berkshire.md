## 问题

巨额现金的机会成本一直由股东承担。等你不再掌舵，这一切还能延续吗？下一任会否不得不把伯克希尔转型为返还资本的工具？

## 回答要点

1. 「这有可能——在我治下就有可能」，大资金投得不如小资金好是毫无疑问的
2. 判断需要多年：有的年份机会遍地，有的年份颗粒无收
3. 遗嘱指示受托人豁免分散投资要求：捐赠过程中伯克希尔股票保持原样
4. 「我把全部净资产押在这上面，它丝毫不会让我迟疑」
5. 芒格：不持指数基金、只持两三只股票，「如果你知道自己在做什么，不需要 50 只股票的组合」

## 关键引述

> **“I'm betting my entire net worth on that.”**
>
> 「我把全部净资产押在这上面。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（02:05:50）：好。格雷格？

**Gregg Warren**（02:05:56）：格雷格·沃伦：沃伦，伯克希尔多年的成功，很大程度源于你和查理有耐心等待机会、哪怕资产负债表上现金堆积如山。这在历史上对股东有利：你们能充分利用股票与信贷市场的动荡或特殊机会（如西方石油交易），谈成最终利于股东的条件。但持有巨额现金有机会成本，投资者愿意承受——放弃返还资本、放弃分红回购、现金收益率偏低。展望未来，等你不再掌舵，我们有多大把握这一切仍将延续？尤其是伯克希尔回报预期放缓的情况下。下一任管理层是否更可能不得不把伯克希尔从收购与投资平台，逐步转型为向股东返还资本的工具？

**Warren Buffett**（02:06:59）：这当然有可能——在我治下就有可能，在接班人治下也有可能。问题在于：你能否把真正巨大的资金投得还不错。大资金投得不如小资金好，这毫无疑问。这需要许多年才能判断：有的年份机会遍地，有的年份颗粒无收。这不是一年或三年能下的判断，但拉长了肯定能下。

我个人——我的遗产在分批捐给慈善机构的过程中，基本只会是伯克希尔的股票。我在遗嘱里对受托人写道：你们豁免于「受托人应当分散投资」之类的常规法律要求。我希望他们持有的全部伯克希尔股票，在逐步捐赠的过程中保持原样。我完全不担心这一点——我希望有大笔的钱捐给慈善事业，而它几乎全部以伯克希尔的形式存在，我毫不担心。我愿意在生前就做这个决定，它在我身后还会延续若干年。

所以，我对伯克希尔文化的延续能力、对我们安排了正确的人，抱有极大的信心。我把全部净资产押在这上面，它丝毫不会让我迟疑。我每隔几年重写遗嘱，对伯克希尔持仓的表述始终相同。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:09:08）：我不持有任何指数基金。我一直愿意只持有两三只股票，也不介意法学院和商学院里教金融的人都说我错了。那不是错的，它运转得漂亮。如果你知道自己在做什么，不需要 50 只股票的组合。我希望我的继承人就是坐着不动。

**Warren Buffett**（02:09:35）：我的继承人希望我改遗嘱。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:05:50）：OK. Gregg?

**Gregg Warren**（02:05:56）：Warren, a lot of Berkshire's success over the years has come from the fact that you and Charlie have had the luxury of being patient, waiting for the right opportunities to come along to put excess capital to work, even if it has led to a buildup of large amounts of cash on the balance sheet. This has historically worked out well for shareholders, as you and Charlie have been able to take full advantage of the disruptions in equity and credit markets or special situations like we saw with the Oxy deal, to negotiate deals on terms that ultimately benefit Berkshire shareholders. That said, there is an opportunity cost attached to your decision to hold onto so much cash, one that investors have been willing to bear, primarily by forgoing a return of excess capital, dividends, and share repurchases, as well as seeing lower returns on cash holdings. As we look forward, how certain can we be that this will still be the case once you're no longer running the show, especially if Berkshire's returns are expected to be lower over time. And is it not more likely that the next managers at Berkshire will have to manage the eventual migration of Berkshire from an acquisition and investment platform to a returning capital to shareholders vehicle?

**Warren Buffett**（02:06:59）：Yeah. Well, that's certainly a possibility. I mean, that's a possibility under me. It's a possibility under the successor. I mean, it's a question of can you invest truly large sums reasonably well. You can't do it as well as you can do small sums. There's no question about that. But we will have to see how that works out over many years because certain years lots of opportunities, huge opportunities, present themselves and other years there are totally dry holes. So that's not a judgment you can make in a one-year period or a three-year period. It's certainly a judgment you can make over time though. And I personally — my estate will have basically nothing but Berkshire in it for some time as it gets disbursed to philanthropies. And I have a section in there which says to the trustees, in effect, to manage it — I have a section in there that says — ignore the — your exempt, from my standpoint, from the law that trustees normally should diversify and do all that sort of thing. And I want the entire amount that they have to be kept in Berkshire as they distribute it over time to the philanthropies. And I don't worry at all about the fact — I would like to have a very large sum go to the philanthropies, and I don't worry at about the fact that it essentially will all be in Berkshire. And I'm willing to make that decision while I'm alive, which will continue for some years after I die. So, I have a lot of confidence in the ability of the Berkshire culture to endure and that we have the right people to make sure that that happens. I'm betting my entire net worth on that. And that doesn't give me pause at all. I rewrite my will every few years, and write it the same way in respect to the Berkshire holdings. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:09:08）：I don't own any indexes. And I have always been willing to own just two or three stocks. And I have not minded that everybody who teaches finance in law school and business school teaches that what I'm doing is wrong. It isn't wrong. It's worked beautifully. I don't think you need a portfolio of 50 stocks if you know what you're doing. And I hope my heirs will just sit.

**Warren Buffett**（02:09:35）：My heirs hope that I'll change my will. (Laughter)
