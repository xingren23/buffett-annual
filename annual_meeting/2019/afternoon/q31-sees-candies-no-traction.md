## 问题

喜诗产品如此出色，为什么没有长成玛氏或好时的规模？能做些什么让它成为伯克希尔更大的一部分？

## 回答要点

1. 试过 10 到 12 个扩张想法，有些不止一次——「没有一个真正管用」
2. 盒装巧克力是礼物型产品：打开就抢光，但没人专程买来吃
3. 东部开店先人流如潮，最后销量只有体面回报所需的 2/3
4. 2500 万美元买入，累计带来超过 20 亿美元税前利润；留存收益投向别处而非糖果业
5. 钱是可替代的：在 X 做成了，就去 A、B、C、D、E、F 里找下一个机会
6. 芒格：没能变成玛氏或好时，「和我们没能得诺贝尔物理学奖一个道理——太难了」

## 关键引述

> **“we've had 10 or 12 ideas.”**
>
> 「我们有 10 到 12 个想法。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（02:20:28）：好，10 号站。

**Audience Member**（02:20:37）：路易斯·科博（音，巴拿马）：你们好，沃伦和查理。我是巴拿马的路易斯·科博，十年前起就是伯克希尔骄傲的股东。我一直在研究喜诗糖果，是它的忠实粉丝——我看查理整场会也是。为什么它没有长成玛氏或好时那样的规模？我们能做些什么让它成长、让它成为伯克希尔更大的一部分？产品如此出色。

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:24）：这些年我们大概有十几二十个想法。我们曾经真的很专注，因为它当时在我们业务里占比大得多——除保险外几乎是我们唯一的生意。我们有 10 到 12 个想法，有些试过不止一次，换了新经理又试。真相是：没有一个真正管用。这个生意在一个很小的利基里极其出色。

盒装巧克力是人人都乐意收、也乐意送的东西——两头都成立——但很少有人自己买来吃。我在办公室打开一盒——我们只有 25 个人——几乎立刻被抢光。作为礼物送人，对方高兴；宴会上盒子一开，一扫而空。可这些随手就抓的人，自己专程去糖果店买来吃的频率很低。它是非常典型的礼物型产品，无法全球扩张。

有意思的是：上次查证时，西部人偏爱牛奶巧克力，东部人偏爱黑巧；西部喜欢大块，东部接受迷你装。我们试图把它异地推广，试了无数次——因为一旦成功，回报美得惊人。但它走不远。在东部开店，起初人流如潮，人人说「我们盼你们来」。最后那家店的年销量是 X 磅，而同样的面积我们需要 1.5 倍 X 才能赚回体面的回报。我们什么都试过，因为这门数学太好了。总体上，巧克力消费本身增长不大。查理……

**Charlie Munger**（02:23:59）：是的。我们没能把小小的糖果公司变成玛氏或好时，原因和你们没能得诺贝尔物理学奖、没能永垂不朽一样。（笑声）对我们来说太难了。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:24:14）：但我们往里放了 2500 万美元，它给我们带来了超过 20 亿美元的税前利润——远超 20 亿。我们用这笔钱买了别的生意。如果我们是典型的公司，买下喜诗后拼命把所有留存收益再投回糖果生意，我猜我们会栽跟头。

这恰恰说明：你学到的那些公式——或者「把资本全部再投入本行业」的战略计划——有些生意只在相当有限的区域有效；有些生意能铺开。胡椒博士在达拉斯可能占 10% 或 12% 的市场份额——或者 8%，具体我不确定——但到了底特律或波士顿，还不到 1%。在一个流动的社会里，想想看：胡椒博士与可口可乐 1886 年诞生同期，有些东西能旅行，有些不能。糖果棒——你提到好时——吉百利在美国干得不怎么样，好时在英国也干得不怎么样，可我们这些人长得都差不多，却吃着不同的糖果棒。观察起来很有意思。

所谓「每家公司都该坚守原路线、在原产地继续寻找增长」的公式，我们并不接受：既然在 X 做成了，就该在 X 里找别的方式赚钱？我们完全乐意去 A、B、C、D、E、F 里找——钱是可替代的。我认为这个哲学对我们大有好处。查理，还有吗？

**Charlie Munger**（02:26:17）：我曾在晚宴上对一位刚写出伟大著作的伟人说：「你再也写不出那样伟大的书了。」他深受冒犯。我读了他后来的四本书——我完全正确。（笑声）一生写出一本伟大的书已经很了不起。人们没有更多产出时，并不是对你有所保留。难。

**Warren Buffett**（02:26:36）：是。但你应当把到手的第一本用到极致。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（02:26:44）：是。

**Warren Buffett**（02:26:45）：是，你已经很幸运了。

**Charlie Munger**（02:26:46）：是。

**Warren Buffett**（02:26:47）：我们非常幸运。喜诗那单我本会搞砸，是查理说「别那么小气」。我们仍以相当好的价格买到了它，而且学到了很多……

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:03）：这些年我们学到的东西多得惊人。

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:06）：是，我们学到了——

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:08）：要不然，成绩单会难看得多。

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:13）：是。

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:14）：任何时点，我们已经知道的都不足以带我们走下一步。这正是难处。想想你认识的那些想再进一步却跌下悬崖的人。

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:58）：好，在这个欢快的音符上——（笑声）——我们结束问答环节。（掌声与欢呼）谢谢。谢谢。谢谢。省着点掌声，留到明年——明年也许还需要。把剩下的给我们记账结转，谢谢你们。我们 3:45 回来开正式会议。委托书上没有需要表决的事项，但 15 分钟后我们回来。如果你喜欢流程，可以留下来看我们连选连任董事会。谢谢大家，感谢光临。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:20:28）：OK. Station 10.

**Audience Member**（02:20:37）：Hello, Warren and Charlie. I'm Luis Cobo (PH) from Panama. I'm a proud Berkshire Hathaway shareholder since 10 years ago. I've been looking at See's Candies, and I'm a pretty good fan of them. And I see Charlie is as well throughout our meeting. And even with all our consumption — and you know, the company has given us generous profits over the past decades. Why do you think the company has not grown to the scale of Mars or Hershey's, and what do you think we could do to make this company grow and become a bigger part of our company, being such an amazing product?

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:24）：Well, we've probably had a dozen or so ideas over the years. And we used to really focus on it because it was a much more sizable part of our business. In fact, it was practically our only business aside from insurance. And like I say, we've had 10 or 12 ideas. Some of them we tried more than once. And as we got a new manager they've tried them. And the truth is none of them really work. And the business is extraordinarily good in a very small niche. Boxed chocolates are something that everybody likes to receive, or maybe give it as a gift, both sides of it. And relatively few of the people go out and buy to consume themselves. If I leave a box of chocolates open at the office — we've only got 25 people — but it's gone, you know, almost immediately. If I take it as a gift to somebody, they're happy to get it. And if you leave the box open at a dinner party again, they're all gone. But those same people that so readily grab it when it's right there in front of them, do not walk out to a candy store very often and buy it just to eat themselves. They're not going to buy. It's very much a gift product. It does not grow worldwide. Very interesting thing. People in these — last time I checked, people in the west prefer milk chocolate, people in the east prefer dark chocolate. People in the west like big, chunky pieces. People in the east will take miniatures. We've tried to move it geographically many, many, many, many times. Because it would be so wonderful if it — when it works it works wonderfully. But it doesn't travel that well. If we open a store in the east, we get enormous traffic for a while and everybody says, "We've been waiting for you to come." And then it finally — we end up with a store that does X pounds per year when we need one and a half X in the same square footage to make terrific returns. And we've tried everything because the math is so good when it works. And overall, we have a business that doesn't — chocolate consumption generally doesn't grow that much. But yeah, go ahead Charlie —

**Charlie Munger**（02:23:59）：Yeah, well, we failed in turning our little candy company into Mars or Hershey's for the same reason that you fail to get the Nobel Prize in physics and achieve immortality. (Laughter) It's too tough for us. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（02:24:14）：But we put 25 million dollars into it. And it's given us over two billion dollars of pre-tax income, well over two billion. And we've used it to buy other businesses. And if we were the typical company and had bought that business and tried desperately to use all the retained earnings within the candy business I think we'd have fallen on our face. I think that it just illustrates that all these formulas, you know, you learn or that having a strategic plan to use all the capital or something — some businesses work in a fairly limited area. Others really play out over this — Dr. Pepper, you know, has — I don't know what the percentage is now, but it might be at 10 or 12 percent market share or something like that in Dallas or maybe it's eight. And then you go to Detroit or Boston and it's less than 1 percent. I'm not sure about the numbers currently. But you'd think in a mobile society, you know, with Dr. Pepper having been around since the time that Coke was founded in 1886 — it's amazing how certain things travel, certain things don't travel. You know, candy bars — you mentioned Hershey. I mean, Cadbury doesn't do that well here and Hershey doesn't do that well in the U.K. And here we are, we all look alike. But somehow, we eat different candy bars. It's very interesting to observe. And the idea that you have some formula for businesses that provide that each one should pursue the course they're on because they made it in X, they should try to find other ways to make it in X. We're quite willing to find it in A, B, C, D, E, or F — the money is fungible. And I think, actually, it has worked very much to our advantage to have that philosophy. So, anything further, Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:26:17）：I once told a very great man at dinner after he'd written a very great book, I said, "You know, you're never going to write another great book like that." And he was deeply offended. And I've read his four subsequent books and I'm totally right. (Laughter) To write one great book is a lot to do in one lifetime. And people aren't holding back on you when they don't do more. It's hard.

**Warren Buffett**（02:26:36）：Yeah. But you ought to make the most of the first one you got. (Laughs)

**Charlie Munger**（02:26:44）：Yeah.

**Warren Buffett**（02:26:45）：Yeah, you're lucky. You know.

**Charlie Munger**（02:26:46）：Yes.

**Warren Buffett**（02:26:47）：And we were very fortunate. I would've blown the chance to buy See's Candy, but Charlie said, "Don't be so cheap," basically. And we still got it at a pretty good price. And we learned a lot —

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:03）：It's amazing how much we've learned over the years.

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:06）：Yeah, we've learned —

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:08）：And if we hadn't, the record would be so much worse.

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:13）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:14）：At any given time, what we already knew was not going to be enough to take us to the next step. That's what makes it difficult. Think of all the people you know that have tried to take one extra step and have fallen off a cliff.

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:58）：Well, on that happy note — (laughter) — we will conclude the meeting. (Applause and cheering) Thank you. Thank you. Thank you. Thank you, but save of it for next year. We may need it then. Just give us a carry-forward on the rest of it, and thank you. We'll come back at 3:45. We will conduct the business meeting, and it doesn't — we have no — nothing on the proxy to vote on, but we will be back here in 15 minutes. And if you enjoy a process, you can stick around and watch us reelect our board. Thank you. Thanks for coming.
