## 问题

作为大型雇主和消费品生产商，如何看待自动化与临时雇佣兴起下优质全职岗位的前景？

## 回答要点

1. 两百年前农机化的前景同样「可怕」——90% 的务农者会失业，但今天有 1.6 亿个岗位
2. 「1776 年以来我们一直在想办法消灭岗位」，人均产出越来越多；就业人数超历史任何时期
3. 「不必担心美国的创造力枯竭」；系统有效，一定会有下一个大事
4. 芒格：把苦活转给机器人是过去 200 年一直在做的事；对未来的担忧部分来自左派，利率归零推高资产价格是意外
5. 杂货店的往事：爬梯子取豌豆的效率只有今天的三分之一到五分之一——「创造性破坏把我们解放出来去做投资」

## 关键引述

> **“there will be a next big thing.”**
>
> 「一定会有下一个大事。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:14）：好，2 号站。

**Audience Member**（01:03:29）：凯莉（旧金山）：你们好，沃伦、查理。我叫凯莉，这是我女儿克洛伊，她 11 周大，第一次参加伯克希尔股东会。（笑声）我们来自旧金山。有一个关于就业的问题：作为大型雇主和消费品生产商，随着自动化和临时雇佣的兴起，你们怎么看优质全职岗位前景的不确定性？

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:09）：两百年前问同样的问题：有人说「农业机械、拖拉机、联合收割机的前景，意味着 90% 的务农者将失去工作」——看起来很可怕，不是吗？但我们的经济、我们的人民、我们的制度极其善于变通，才有了今天的 1.6 亿个就业岗位——尽管 1776 年以来我们一直在想办法消灭岗位。这就是资本主义：人均产出越来越多。

我们从来不知道新岗位从哪里冒出来。如果你当年在客运铁路干，那行注定要变。但在这个经济体里，我们总能找到雇用更多人的办法。如今美国的就业人数超过历史上任何时期，尽管重工业公司一个个都在想办法提高生产率——用更少的人产同样的货，或用同样的人产更多的货。这就是资本主义。我认为不必担心美国的创造力枯竭。各行各业的人爱赚钱，但他们真正爱的是发明创造、是把事情做成。这个经济体有效，还会继续有效。

某些行业会很艰难，会有错位——汽车来了，马蹄铁就没那么多人打了。但我们如今雇着 1.55 亿人，养着 3.3 亿人口；而建国时只有 400 万人、80% 的劳动力在务农。系统有效，还会继续有效。我不知道下一个大事是什么，但我知道一定会有下一个大事。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:07:06）：我们要做的，就是尽量把苦活累活转给机器人。过去 200 年我们一直在这么干。没人想回到当铁匠、上街铲马粪的日子。那种活儿被消灭，我们高兴都来不及。

很多对未来的担忧来自左派：他们极度担心经济金字塔底层的人，在顶层的人跑得更快时过了几天紧日子。这是意外——我们当时陷入大麻烦，只好向世界灌钱、把利率压到零，于是资产价格被推高、富人受益。没人这么做是因为突然爱上了富人，纯属意外，而且很快会过去。我们要的就是生产率提升；某一个阶层在某一段跑得稍快一点，不值得过分担心。

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:14）：查理和我——（掌声）——都在杂货店干过。客人订一罐豌豆，我们得爬梯子取下来，装进折叠箱，再搬上卡车。看看从生产者到消费者之间实际转移的食物量、以及涉及的人数，效率大概只有现在最佳配送方式的三分之一、四分之一或五分之一。而……

**Charlie Munger**（01:08:48）：那时候的食品还更差。

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:50）：（笑）我祖父很难过这种赊账加配送的杂货店会被淘汰。它确实被淘汰了，但社会……

**Charlie Munger**（01:09:01）：它回来了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:02）：——消化了……你说什么？

**Charlie Munger**（01:09:03）：它回来了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:05）：回来了，而且效率更高。（笑声）总之，我们见过一点创造性破坏。坦白说，我们很高兴它把我们解放出来去做投资。对我们来说结果更好。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:14）：OK, station 2.

**Audience Member**（01:03:29）：Hi. Hi Warren, hi Charlie. My name is Carrie and this is my daughter, Chloe. She's 11 weeks. It's her very first Berkshire meeting. (Laughter) We're from San Francisco, and we have a question on employment for you. As both a major employer and a producer of consumer goods, what do you make of the uncertain outlook for good full-time jobs with the rise of automation and temporary employment?

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:09）：Well, if we'd asked that question 200 years ago, and somebody said, "With the outlook for development of farm machinery and tractors and combines and so on —" meaning that 90 percent of the people on farms were going to be — lose their job — it would look terrible, wouldn't it? But our economy and our people, our system, has been remarkably ingenious in achieving whatever we have now — 160 million jobs — when throughout the period ever since 1776, we've been figuring out ways to get rid of jobs. That's what capitalism does, and it produces more and more goods per person. And we never know exactly where they're going to come from. I mean, it — I don't know if you were whatever occupation — well, if you were in the passenger train business, I mean, you know, you were going to — that was going to change. But we find ways, in this economy, to employ more and more people. And we've got now more people employed than ever in the history of the country, even though company after company in heavy industry and that sort of thing, has been trying to figure out, naturally, how to get more productive all the time, which means turning out the same number of goods with fewer people, or turning out more goods with the same number. That is capitalism. I don't think you need to worry about American ingenuity running out. I mean, if you look at people in all kind of businesses, and they like to make money, but they really like to be inventive, you know. They like to do things. And this economy, it works. It will continue to work. And it will be very — it's very tough in certain industries, and there will be dislocations. You know, we won't be making as many horseshoes and that sort of thing when cars come along and all that. But we do find ways now to employ whatever we're employing — 155, whatever it is — million people, and supporting a population of 330 million people when we started with 4 million people, with 80 percent of the labor being employed on farms. So, the system works and it will continue to work. And I don't know what the next big thing will be. I do know there will be a next big thing. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:07:06）：Well, we want to shift the scut work to the robots to the extent we can. That's what we were doing, as Warren said, for 200 years. Nobody wants to go back to being a blacksmith, or scooping along the street, picking up the horse manure, or whatever the hell people used to do. We're glad to have that work eliminated. And a lot of this worry about the future comes from leftists who worry terribly that the people at the bottom of the economic pyramid have had a little stretch when the people at the top got ahead faster. That happened by accident because we were in so much trouble that we had to flood the world with money and drive interest rates down to zero. And, of course, that drove asset prices up and helped the rich. Nobody did that because they suddenly loved the rich, it was just an accident, and it will soon pass. We want to have all this productivity improvement, and we shouldn't worry a little about the fact that one class or another is a little ahead at one stretch.

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:14）：Charlie and I — (applause) — we worked in a grocery store. And when people ordered a can of peas, we had ladders that we climbed up to reach the can of peas, and then we placed it in a folding box, and then we put it on a truck. And if you looked at the amount of food actually transferred between the producer and the person who consumed it, and the number of people involved in the transaction, you know, it was — I don't know whether it was one-third or one-quarter or one-fifth as efficient as the best way now to get food delivered to you. And —

**Charlie Munger**（01:08:48）：And the food was worse.

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:50）：(Laughs) And my grandfather, you know, was distressed about the fact that this particular credit and delivery kind of store would be eliminated. And it was eliminated, but society —

**Charlie Munger**（01:09:01）：It's coming back.

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:02）：—addressed the — pardon?

**Charlie Munger**（01:09:03）：It's coming back.

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:05）：It's coming back, but more efficiently. (Laughter) Anyway, we've seen a little creative destruction. And frankly, we're glad that it freed us up to go into the investment business. Worked out better for us.
