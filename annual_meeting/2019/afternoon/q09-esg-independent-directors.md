## 问题

拉里·芬克预言所有投资者都将使用 ESG 指标。伯克希尔在气候、多元、包容等方面表现如何？这些指标有价值吗？

## 回答要点

1. 「实际上我们表现很好，但不为任何来要报告的人准备报告」
2. 艾奥瓦售电将 100% 来自风电；数据中心因低价清洁能源涌入，变成「移动的靶子」
3. 市值 5000 亿美元却没有月度合并利润表——「我们不需要」
4. 芒格：「最佳实践」的判断标准是「什么好卖」而不是「什么管用」
5. 独立董事年薪约 25 万美元、依赖 CEO 推荐，「恰恰最不独立」；蓝筹邮票董事会的故事：其余董事几乎全是客户而非所有者

## 关键引述

> **“the independent directors, in many cases, are the least independent.”**
>
> 「很多时候，所谓独立董事恰恰最不独立。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:04）：好，安德鲁？

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（00:37:08）：安德鲁·罗斯·索尔金：谢谢沃伦。一位股东提出的治理问题。贝莱德的拉里·芬克预言，不久后所有投资者都会用 ESG——环境、社会、治理——指标来评估公司价值。我担心伯克希尔在气候、多元、包容等方面得分不佳。你认为伯克希尔在这些指标上表现如何？这些指标有价值吗？

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:34）：实际上我认为我们表现很好，但我们不为任何来要报告的人准备报告。我们有个观念：股东虽然人人平等，但我们更偏爱个人股东，偏爱我们认识的合伙人。我们不想准备一堆报告、让 60 家子公司各成立一个团队往总部寄材料，再提供给某个——股价一涨就多半要卖掉的人。我们要经理人做正确的事，给他们巨大的自由度，我认为我们的平均战绩相当好。

你在电影里看到了：我们在艾奥瓦州卖出的电力将 100% 来自风电。这不是说 24 小时全靠风——我们卖出也买进——但大体上，我们创造的风电足以覆盖艾奥瓦全部客户的用电。艾奥瓦还有一家规模与我们相近的电力公司，几乎没有任何风电资源，虽然那边风一样吹。而且这是个移动的靶子：我们的风电干得太好，许多高科技公司想把数据中心设到艾奥瓦、以极低价格买清洁能源，于是靶子变成我们在当地的客户增长。

但我们不会让伯克希尔·哈撒韦能源的人花时间去填问卷、去讨好打分的人。我们信任经理人，我认为业绩至少体面。伯克希尔把开支和无谓的报表压到最低——年报里我提过——难以想象还有第二家公司：市值 5000 亿美元，我们竟然没有月度合并利润表。不需要。我想不出还有哪家机构这么干，但我们真的不需要。我们不会仅仅因为「美国公司都这么办」，就占用人力去做不能让生意经营得更好的事。

美国公司普遍担心维权投资者掀翻他们的苹果车，所以必须确保每位股东在这类议题上满意。幸运的是，我们不必为此操心，也就不必为这些事花大钱。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:41:12）：我认为在伯克希尔，环保这些事是我们下一层做的，而格雷格·阿贝尔干得极其出色，所以我认为我们得分很高。至于所谓最佳公司实践，谈这些的人并不真知道什么是最佳实践，只知道他们「认为」的最佳实践——他们的判断标准是「什么好卖」而不是「什么管用」。所以我更喜欢我们的做法，但愿上帝保佑我们永远别学他们的「最佳实践」。（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:50）：关于独立董事我想说一句。我当过 20 家上市公司的董事（不算伯克希尔子公司），见过形形色色的董事会运作。很多时候，所谓独立董事恰恰最不独立。担任董事的报酬通常一年 25 万美元左右。如果这是你收入的重要部分，而你还指望别的公司打电话问你的 CEO「某某当董事表现如何」，你现在的 CEO 回答「哦，他很好，从不提任何问题」，然后你再进下一家董事会拿 25 万，这怎么可能算独立？（掌声）

我特别想不起任何一位「董事报酬对其收入很重要」的独立董事，在董事会或委员会上做过任何真正违背 CEO 利益的事。他们被放进薪酬委员会。他们绝不会掀翻苹果车。换作我在那个位置，我多半也会一样。如果一年 25 万对你很重要，你干嘛要表现得让 CEO 对下一家公司说「这家伙有点太爱闹事，你们最好换人」？事情就是这么运作的。

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:37）：我想实际情况比沃伦说的还要糟一点。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:43）：是，查理和我——

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:44）：真的很糟。

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:46）：很糟。我们——

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:47）：不仅如此，沃伦和我在装腔作势这个行当里也占得很好，我们不需要再来更多了。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:56）：查理和我曾在同一个董事会。其实是我一个人进的——很久以前，我们在那家公司持有相当大的比例，董事会其余成员几乎全是公司的客户而非所有者，持股只是象征性的。有一次讨论一项涉及证券分配的税务决定，金额很大。另一位董事说：「我们就把税吞了吧。」他「吞」的份额大约 15 美元。（笑）我说：「咱们把这句话拆开——『我们』把税吞了。那么，谁愿意和我吞同等的一份？」你在餐桌上打嗝太频繁，就不会再受邀进董事会了。

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:07）：在蓝筹邮票公司，我们有位董事说：「我不明白为什么你们拥有全部股份，就可以这么重要。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:14）：是。（笑声）当年开完蓝筹邮票的董事会，查理和我得先冷静一下——我们和里克·加勒特持股多少来着？

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:18）：哦，50%。

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:19）：对，50%，而他们任命了那一批……

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:22）：全是扶轮社的会员。

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:24）：蓝筹邮票是从一项政府和解里来的什么安排。那不是理想的决策形式。他们心里的算法和我们不同。我可以理解，但我不打算复制。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:04）：OK, Andrew?

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（00:37:08）：Thanks, Warren. Governance question from a shareholder. Larry Fink of BlackRock has predicted that in the near future, all investors will be using ESG —environmental, social, governance — metrics to help determine the value of a company. I'm worried we don't score well on everything from climate to diversity to inclusion. How well do you think Berkshire measures up on those metrics, and are they valuable metrics?

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:34）：I think in reality we measure up well, but we don't participate in preparing reports for anybody that asks about it. And we have this idea that even though all shareholders are equal, we sort of — we prefer individual shareholders. We actually prefer people we know as co-owners. And we don't want to be preparing a lot of reports and asking 60 subsidiaries each to do something, where they'll set up a team, and they'll mail things to headquarters, and then we'll supply them to somebody who, if our stock goes up some, is probably going to sell it anyway. We want our managers to do the right things. We give them enormous latitude to do that. And I think that our batting average really is quite good. You saw that in the movie, we talked about having a hundred percent of the electricity we sell in Iowa come from, essentially, wind generation. Now that doesn't mean that we get to do it 24 hours a day. We sell some and we buy it. But essentially, we will be creating as much wind energy as all of our customers use in electricity. There's one competitive — there's one other utility — electric utility — that's about our size — roughly our size — in Iowa, and they have practically no wind resources. And the wind blows where they exist, too. But we have — we will have that hundred percent — and as a matter of fact, it's a moving target, because we do so well — partly — we do so well on wind generation that a number of the high-tech companies want to locate in Iowa and get clean energy from us at very low prices. And therefore, the moving target becomes our growth in customers in that area. But we are not going to put out a — we're not going to spend the time of the people at Berkshire Hathaway Energy responding to questionnaires or trying to score better with somebody that is working on that. It's just, we trust our managers and I think the performance is at least decent. And we keep expenses and needless reporting down to a minimum at Berkshire. We do not get — and I mentioned this in the annual report — I can't imagine another company like it — but here we are, with 500 billion of market capitalization — we do not have a consolidated P&L monthly. We don't need it. Now I can't imagine any other organization doing that, but we don't need it. And we're not going to tie up resources — people resources — doing things we don't need to do just because it's the sort of standard procedure in corporate America. And corporate America is very worried about, in general, they're very worried about whether somebody's going to upset their apple cart, you know, with activists and everything. So, they want to be very sure that every shareholder is happy on issues like that. And in the end, fortunately, we don't have to worry about that. So, we don't have to run up a lot of expenses doing things that don't actually let us run the business better. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:41:12）：Well, I think at Berkshire the environmental stuff is done one level down from us. And I think Greg Abel is just terrific at it. And so, I think we score very well. When it gets to so-called best corporate practices, I think the people that talk about them don't really know what the best practices are. They just know what they think are the best practices. And they determine that based on what will sell, not what will work. And so, I like our way of doing things better than theirs, and I hope to God we never follow their best practices. (Applause)

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:50）：I'd like to point out one thing on independent directors. I mean, I have been on 20 public company corporate boards, not counting any Berkshire subsidiaries. So, I've seen a lot of corporate boards operate. And the independent directors, in many cases, are the least independent. I mean, if the income you receive as a corporate director — which typically may be around $250,000 a year — now, if that's an important part of your income, and you hope that some other corporation calls the CEO and says, "How's so-and-so as a director?" and the current CEO — your CEO — says, "Oh, he's fine and never raises any problems," and then you get on another board at 250,000 and that's an important part, how in the world is that independent? I mean, I really, just an observation. (Applause) I can't recall, particularly, any independent director — where their income from the board was important to them — I can't recall them ever doing anything in board meetings or committee meetings that actually was counter to the interest — you know, they put them on the comp committee. They're just not going to upset the apple cart, because what they're — and I'd probably behave the same way in the same position. I mean, if $250,000 a year is important to you, why in the hell would you behave in a way that's going to cause your CEO to say to the next CEO, "This guy acts up a little bit too much. You really better get somebody else." It's the way it works. But they've —

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:37）：I think it works a little worse than Warren's telling you. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:43）：Yeah, Charlie and I —

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:44）：It's really awful.

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:46）：It's awful. I mean, we —

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:47）：And not only that, Warren and I are — we occupy the niche for pomposity very well ourselves. We don't need any more of it. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:56）：Charlie and I were on one board. Well, I was on one board, actually, a long time ago where we owned a very significant percentage of the company. And the rest of the board was almost exclusively customers of the company. But not owners. They had absolutely token holdings. And at one point we were looking at something where a tax decision was being made in terms of the distribution of some securities. And it was a lot of money that was involved. And one of the other directors said, "Well, let's just swallow the tax." Well, his swallowing amounted to about $15 or something — (Laughs) I said, "Let's parse this sentence out. Let's swallow the tax. That's let us swallow the tax. So, who wants to swallow an equal amount, you know, to me?" It's — you know, it's — you don't get invited to be on boards if you belch too often at the dinner table —

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:07）：Well, at Blue Chip Stamps we had a director who said, "I don't see why you guys get to be so important just because you own all the shares."

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:14）：Yeah. (Laughter) Charlie and I used to have to cool off after the Blue Chip Stamps meetings, because we and Rick Garrett owned what percent, probably?

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:18）：Oh, 50 percent.

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:19）：Yeah, 50 percent, and they'd appointed all these —

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:22）：They were all members of the Rotary Club.

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:24）：It came out of a government settlement or something. And it was not an ideal form of decision making. And they just had a different calculus in their mind than we did. And I can understand it, but I'm not going to replicate it. (Laughs)
