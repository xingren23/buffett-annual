## 问题

如今竞争更激烈，构建能力圈时会怎么做？仍是宽泛的通才框架，还是更深更窄地聚焦某些行业、市场甚至国家？

## 回答要点

1. 起步时靠翻穆迪手册逐页筛选，大量阅读、区分「懂与不懂」
2. 把圈子做大，同时对能力圈边界保持现实
3. 「对哪怕相对狭小的一块比别人懂得多」，等赔率强烈偏向自己时再出手
4. 芒格：对绝大多数人，伟大的策略是专业化——「没人要找一半肛肠科一半牙科的医生」；他俩的路线不是标准路线，多少靠运气
5. 老沃森：「我不是天才，但我在某些点上聪明，而且我守在这些点上」

## 关键引述

> **“I'm no genius, but I'm smart in spots and I stay around those spots.”**
>
> 「我不是天才，但我在某些点上聪明，而且我守在这些点上。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:00）：好，11 号站。

**Audience Member**（00:32:06）：费罗兹·卡尤姆（加拿大密西沙加，现居纽约）：巴菲特先生、芒格先生，感谢接受提问。我的问题是如何最好地效仿你们构建能力圈的成功。如今的投资环境比你们起步时竞争激烈得多。构建能力圈时你们会有什么不同的做法吗？还是会搭建一个非常宽的通才框架？还是会更深但更窄地聚焦，比如聚焦某些行业、市场甚至某个国家？如果是后者，哪些会让你们感兴趣？谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:49）：是的，你说得对，现在竞争激烈得多。我起步时，真的可以拿着穆迪工业手册和穆迪银行金融手册一页页翻过去——至少把每家公司过一眼——想想哪些值得再琢磨。我会做大量阅读，尽可能多学各行各业的生意，然后弄清哪些我有真正重要、与绝大多数竞争对手不同的知识与理解。同时我也会弄清哪些自己不懂。我会尽力把圈子做大，同时尽力对能力圈的边界保持现实。

1951 年 1 月见到 GEICO 的洛里默·戴维森时，我就知道我能吃透保险——那个周六共处的三四个小时里，他讲的一切在我看来如此言之成理。于是我深钻进去，能懂，我的脑子在这方面好使。但零售我自认搞不懂——我只在查理待过的那家杂货店干过，我们俩都没从中学到多少零售，只知道它比我们喜欢的活儿辛苦。

你也得做同样的事，而且竞争者多得多。但只要你对哪怕相对狭小的一块比别人懂得多，又没有频繁出手的冲动——就等到赔率强烈偏向你的时候。这仍是很有意思的游戏，只是比从前难了。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:34:55）：我认为对绝大多数人来说，伟大的策略是专业化。没人想找一个一半干肛肠科、一半干牙科的医生。（笑声）所以普通人成功的路是窄而专。沃伦和我没走那条路，因为我们偏爱另一种活法。但我不认为我们该把它推荐给别人。

**Warren Buffett**（00:35:28）：我们那个年代寻宝的机会多一些，宝也比较容易辨认……

**Charlie Munger**（00:35:31）：我们做成了，但多少有点运气。

**Warren Buffett**（00:35:37）：是。

**Charlie Munger**（00:35:38）：那不是标准路线。

**Warren Buffett**（00:35:41）：我最懂得的生意其实是保险，而且当时竞争者寥寥。我去过宾州哈里斯堡的保险监管部门——记得有一回专门开车去查一家宾夕法尼亚公司，那时互联网上查不到这些信息。进门问起那家公司，对方说：「你是第一个来打听它的。」竞争真不多。我还常去休斯敦街的标准普尔图书馆，要各种冷门资料，里面根本没什么人，倒有一大堆桌子任你坐下来翻阅。所以竞争更少。

但只要你把哪怕一件事弄得很透，它总会在某个时刻给你优势。IBM 的老沃森说过：「我不是天才，但我在某些点上聪明，而且我守在这些点上。」查理和我基本就是这么做的。我想你也能做到。不过你会在……

**Charlie Munger**（00:36:58）：对，我们在好几个领域做到了。那很难。

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:00）：我们也有几次被揍得头破血流。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:00）：OK, station 11.

**Audience Member**（00:32:06）：Mr. Buffett and Mr. Munger, thank you for taking my question. My name is Feroz Qayyum and I'm from Mississauga in Canada, and now live in New York. My question is how to best emulate your success in building your circle of competence. Given the environment today in investing is a lot more competitive than when you started out, what would you do differently, if anything at all, when building your circle? Would you still build a very broad, generalist framework? Or would you build a much deeper but narrower focus, say on industries, markets, or even a country? And if so, which ones would interest you? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:49）：Yeah, well, you're right. It's much more competitive now than when I started. And you would — when I started, I literally could take the Moody's industrial manual and the Moody's banks and financial manual and I could go through page by page, at least run my eyes over every company and think about which ones I might think more about. It's — it's important — I would just do a whole lot of reading. I'd try and learn as much as I could about as many businesses, and I would try to figure out which ones I really had some important knowledge and understanding that was probably different than, overwhelmingly, most of my competitors. And I would also try and figure out which ones I didn't understand, and I would focus on having as big a circle as I could have, and also focus on being as realistic as I could about where the perimeters of my circle of competence were. I knew when I met (GEICO executive) Lorimer Davidson in January of 1951, I could get insurance. I mean, what he said made so much sense to me in the three or four hours I spent with him on that Saturday. So, I dug into it and I could understand it. My mind worked well in that respect. I didn't think I could understand retailing. All I'd done is work for the same grocery store that Charlie had, and neither one of us learned that much about retailing, except it was harder work than we liked. And you've got to do the same thing, and you've got way more competition now. But if you get to know even about a relatively small area more than the other people do, and you don't feel the compulsion to act too often, you just wait till the odds are strongly in your favor. It's still a very interesting game. It's harder than it used to be. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:34:55）：Well, I think the great strategy, for the great mass of humanity, is to specialize. Nobody wants to go to a doctor that half-proctologist and half-dentist, you know? (Laughter) And so, the ordinary way to succeed is to narrowly specialize. Warren and I really didn't do that. And that — and we didn't because we prefer the other type of activity. But I don't think we could recommend it to other people.

**Warren Buffett**（00:35:28）：Yeah, a little more treasure hunting in our day, and it was easy to spot the treasures —

**Charlie Munger**（00:35:31）：We made it work, but it was kind of a lucky thing.

**Warren Buffett**（00:35:37）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（00:35:38）：It's not the standard way to go.

**Warren Buffett**（00:35:41）：The business, at least I best understood, actually was insurance. I mean — and I had very little competition. You know, I went to the insurance department in Harrisburg, Pennsylvania. I remember one time I drove there just to check on some Pennsylvania company. And this is when you couldn't get all this information on the internet. And I went in and I asked about some company, and the guy said, "You're the first one that's ever asked about that company." And there wasn't a lot. I went over to the Standard and Poor's library on Houston — Houston — Street, I guess they call it. And I would go up there and ask for all this obscure information. And there wasn't anybody sitting around there. They had a whole bunch of tables that you could set and examine things through. So, there was less competition. But if you know even one thing very well, it'll give you an edge at some point. You know, it's what Tom Watson Sr. said at IBM, you know. "I'm no genius, but I'm smart in spots and I stay around those spots." And that's basically what Charlie and I try and do. And I think that's probably what you can do. But you'll find those spots in —

**Charlie Munger**（00:36:58）：Yeah, we did it in several fields. That's hard.

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:00）：And we got our head handed to us a few times, too.
