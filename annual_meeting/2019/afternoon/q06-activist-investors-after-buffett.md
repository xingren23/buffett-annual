## 问题

在你们的持股完全分散之后，伯克希尔会否更容易被维权投资者盯上、被要求拆分？

## 回答要点

1. 芒格：「那会发生在我死后几十年」——「我想我不会被它烦扰太多」
2. 以持股分散方式看，很多年内都不可能，概率很低
3. 伯克希尔有很多只有在一个实体内才能最大化的属性，人们并未充分理解
4. 「拆分解锁价值」是彻头彻尾的胡扯——价值早已内嵌
5. 若模式长期不奏效，「那就应该发生点别的什么」

## 关键引述

> **“it deserves to be continued in its present form.”**
>
> 「它值得以现在的形态延续下去。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:23:29）：贝基？（掌声）

**Becky Quick**（00:23:35）：贝基·奎克（CNBC）：问题来自澳大利亚化学工程师斯图尔特·博伊德（音）。他说：「目前伯克希尔对维权投资者几乎无法下手：第一，沃伦你的持股很大；第二，股东认可业务整体在伯克希尔伞下运营比拆卖更有价值；第三，伯克希尔的市值规模本身就是大多数维权投资者的门槛。沃伦、查理，在你们的持股完全分散之后，伯克希尔会否更容易被维权投资者盯上？我猜这不是让你们失眠的事，但值得一问。」

**Charlie Munger**（00:24:20）：那会发生在我死后几十年。

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:24）：是——

**Charlie Munger**（00:24:25）：我想我不会被它烦扰太多。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:31）：什么事都可能发生，但概率很低。以我的股票分散方式和他人持股方式看，很多年内都不可能。但归根结底，伯克希尔应当用时间证明自己。世上没有永恒的企业。

它值得以现在的形态延续下去。它有很多只有在一个实体内才能最大化的属性，人们并未充分理解。如果有人拆分出一块「能享受高市盈率、因而价值被解锁」的业务，那是彻头彻尾的胡扯——价值早已内嵌。拆出当天股价也许多 3% 到 5%，但长期看我们想留住那些绝妙的生意。

我最终相信文化会是独一无二的，我们将能做别人做不到的事。把生意放在一个屋檐下的优势，长期看可能非常显著。若果真如此，没有维权投资者能拿下它。而如果这个模式长期不奏效，那就应该发生点别的什么。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:26:01）：没有了。

**Warren Buffett**（00:26:02）：好。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:23:29）：Becky? (Applause)

**Becky Quick**（00:23:35）：This question comes from Stuart Boyd (PH), who's a chemical engineer from Australia. He says, "Currently Berkshire would be incredibly difficult for an activist investor to target, because number one, Warren, your ownership stake is large. Number two, shareholders appreciate the business is more valuable operating under the Berkshire umbrella rather than being sold off in pieces. "And number three, the sheer size or market capitalization of Berkshire is an entry barrier for most activist investors. "Warren and Charlie, after your ownership has been completely distributed, will Berkshire be more vulnerable to activist investors? I'm guessing this isn't something that keeps you up at night, but thought it was worth asking."

**Charlie Munger**（00:24:20）：No, it's going to happen quite a few decades after my death.

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:24）：Yeah. It —

**Charlie Munger**（00:24:25）：I don't think I'll be bothered much by it. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:31）：No, anything could happen. It's a low probability. It can't happen for a lot of years, in terms of the way my stock gets distributed and in terms of the way other stock is held. But in the end, Berkshire should prove itself over time. I mean, there are no perpetuities. And it deserves to be continued in its present form. It has a lot of attributes that are maximized by being in one entity, which people don't fully understand. I think if you spin off something that would command a high PE that therefore value has been unlocked, which is totally nonsense. I mean, it's already built in. One day out, you know, you might have an extra 3 percent or 5 percent in price, but over the years, we want to keep the wonderful businesses. But eventually I think the culture will remain one of a kind. I think that we will be able to do things other people can't do. I think that the advantages of having them in one spot will likely be significant over time. And if that happens, then no activist is going to take it over. And if the model doesn't work for some reason over a long period of time, then something else should happen. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:26:01）：Nothing more.

**Warren Buffett**（00:26:02）：OK.
