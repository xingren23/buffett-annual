## 问题

伯克希尔持有约 2000 亿美元上市股票，却缺少具体的组合业绩信息。为什么不每年告诉我们持仓的表现？

## 回答要点

1. 股票约占伯克希尔价值 40%，经营企业占 60%；前十之外合计不到 10%
2. 约 2000 亿持仓中至少 1500 亿的发行公司每年回购、变相提高伯克希尔持股
3. 「我们不做解释为什么持有某只股票的生意」，不吸引他人抢买抬价
4. 「如果它们某些时期跌得很难看、回购得更多，我们的结果反而好得多」
5. 取前十大或十二大持仓，任何人都能自己算出业绩

## 关键引述

> **“we're not in the business of explaining why we own a stock.”**
>
> 「我们不做「解释为什么持有某只股票」的生意。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:07:57）：好，卡罗尔？

**Carol Loomis**（00:08:02）：这个问题来自布莱恩·尼尔（音），写在梅奥医学教育网站。「伯克希尔持有约 2000 亿美元上市股票。我感谢持仓披露，但遗憾缺少具体的业绩信息。既然投资上市股票是伯克希尔业务的重要部分，为什么不每年告诉我们组合的表现？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:08:36）：显然这个数可以很容易算出来。股票约占伯克希尔价值的 40%，其余 60% 是经营企业。看前十大的话，我估计十名开外的持仓加起来不到伯克希尔价值的 10%。

再说一遍：我们不做「解释为什么持有某只股票」的生意，也不想吸引人来和我们抢买。在我们约 2000 亿的持仓里，我估计至少 1500 亿的发行公司每年都在回购股票、变相提高我们的持股。凭什么我们要告诉一大群人去买那些股票，结果公司要为他们花更多钱？股票上涨时人们很兴奋，但如果我们要继续持有美国银行、苹果或任何大持仓，未来十年里它们在某些时期跌得很难看、回购得更多，我们的结果反而好得多。这就像我们自己回购，只不过花的是他们的钱。道理如此简单。凭什么要向全世界宣扬这些股票该涨，让对方高兴卖出或怎样，反而让我们多花钱？我们也不可能在这些股票上高抛低吸获利。

所以，国内持仓按季度申报（前面已经说过）。但我们不愿把持仓告诉全世界，正如不愿透露 NetJets 的策略、路博润添加剂的研发、家具城新店的选址——那是专有信息。我们必须披露一定数量，但绝不会向别人荐股。

至于计算业绩：取前十大或十二大持仓，任何人都能算——年底《华尔街日报》和各家报纸都会刊登年初至今表现之类的数据，简单的算术。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:11:37）：我没有要补充的。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:07:57）：OK, Carol?

**Carol Loomis**（00:08:02）：This question is from Brian Neal (PH), who writes from the Mayo Clinic Education Site. "Berkshire owns approximately 200 billion in publicly traded stocks. I appreciate the disclosure of Berkshire's holdings, but I am disappointed by the lack of specific performance information. "Since investing in publicly owned stocks is so much a part of Berkshire's business, why do you not tell us every year how our portfolio performed?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:08:36）：Well, obviously it could be calculated fairly easily, and it's about 40 percent of Berkshire's value. But 60 percent is the businesses. And if you look at the top ten stocks I would guess, you know, you're down to where beyond those ten stocks you're talking about less than — probably less than 10 percent of Berkshire's value. So, I — again — we're not in the business of explaining why we own a stock. We're not looking for people to compete to buy it. We have a portfolio of companies where I would say that, of that 200 billion or so, at least 150 billion of them are buying in their stock and increasing our interest every year. And why in the world should we want to tell a whole bunch of people to go out and buy those stocks so that we end up paying — or the company on our behalf — ends up paying more money for them? I mean, people get very happy when their stocks go up. But if we're going to own whatever, whether it's Bank of America, whether it's Apple, whether it's any of the big holdings, we will do considerably better in the next ten years if their stocks do terribly during certain periods and that they buy lots of stock in. It's just exactly like buying it ourselves, except we're using their — they're using our money. But it's so elementary. And why in the world would we want to go out and tell the world that these stocks should go up so that maybe they can sell or something when it costs us money? And we're not going to be able to move in and out of the stocks to our advantage. So, our holdings are filed quarterly — our domestic holdings, as it was pointed out earlier — filed quarterly. But we would rather not tell the world what we own, any more than we'd like to tell them what our strategy is at NetJets or what we're going to do with Lubrizol and what we're working on in the way of better advances in additives or whatever it may be, or where we plan to build a new store for the Furniture Mart or something. That's proprietary information. And we have to disclose a certain amount, but we're certainly not going to be touting the stocks to other people. In terms of calculating our performance, you can take the top ten or 12 stocks, and anybody could make the calculation. I mean, at the end of the year the Wall Street Journal runs — all the papers run something — where it says a year-to-date performance or something of the sort. So that's a simple calculation. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:11:37）：I've got nothing to add to that.
