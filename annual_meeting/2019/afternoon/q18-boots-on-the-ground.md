## 问题

伯克希尔从「派人到现场找机会」变成「卖家主动上门」的拉模式。机会成本会否是错失大量海外业务？要不要在海外派驻更多人手？

## 回答要点

1. 买 ISCAR 后伊坦·韦特海默协助增加欧洲曝光，但标的必须足够大
2. 「10 亿美元的收购给 250 亿的盘子添 8000 万是好事，但不值得花大量时间」
3. 要找的是传了 50 年、100 年且想与伯克希尔做交易的家族企业；拍卖我们不参加
4. 芒格：「我们的问题不是缺少人手，而是现场那些人出的价格我们不愿付」
5. 「对手用别人的钱买东西，赚了归自己、亏了没份」——这不是伯克希尔的算式

## 关键引述

> **“our problem is the people on the ground are paying prices that we don't want to pay.”**
>
> 「现场那些人出的价格，我们不愿付。」

**Warren Buffett**（01:24:18）：好，格雷格。（笑声）

**Gregg Warren**（01:24:22）：格雷格·沃伦：沃伦，想顺着前面两问说下去，因为有个共同主题。纵观伯克希尔历史上的收购与投资，确实存在明显的母国偏好。坚持做自己最熟悉的事当然有价值，但你们似乎从最初「派人到现场找投资机会」的模式，变成了如今更满足于卖家或其代表主动来电、登门——从推的模式变成拉的模式。这没什么不对，但我想问：这种模式的机会成本，会不会是伯克希尔错过大量海外业务——那里的企业主不知道你们愿意整体收购并让他们继续经营；同时因为不够熟悉当地市场而错过股票投资机会。伯克希尔是否需要在海外市场派驻更多人手？

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:30）：是的。买下 ISCAR 之后，伊坦·韦特海默认为我们应该在欧洲增加曝光，他帮了不少忙。我飞过去，他安排了各种会面，接触不少。不是对方不知道我们，我们也确实接到一些消息。但麻烦在于标的必须足够大。如果我们做一笔 10 亿美元的收购，赚 1 亿左右税前、8000 万税后——前提是我们真懂这门生意、确信经营团队未来依然有动力、像自有资金时一样卖力——给 250 亿的盘子添 8000 万是好事，但不值得花大量时间。让托德（康布斯）和泰德（韦施勒）帮忙看项目、做筛选，我们能多得到一些。

但归根结底，你需要的是：某个在欧洲或英国把企业传了 50 年、100 年的家族，能做成交易，而且想和伯克希尔做。如果他们想卖最高价、想搞拍卖，我们不会赢也不会参加——我们不浪费时间。

如果我们组建一支收购团队，他们就一定会收购点什么。我见过太多机构：如果你的工作就是每天上班筛选项目，你身为战略部或并购部，就总想做点什么。我也想做点什么，但除非伯克希尔真正受益——而且是足够大的体量——否则我不做。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:27:46）：沃伦，我们的问题不是缺少人手，而是现场那些人出的价格我们不愿付。

**Warren Buffett**（01:27:55）：是。

**Charlie Munger**（01:27:56）：这才是我们的问题。这个问题派再多的人也治不好。

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:00）：我们明年可以花出 1000 亿美元。这不是问题。

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:06）：不是。

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:07）：花得聪明才是大问题。

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:10）：我们的对手用别人的钱买东西，赚了归自己、亏了没份。

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:15）：而且很多……

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:17）：和这样的人竞争很难。

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:20）：是。他们会加杠杆，失败了照样赚大钱，成功了赚得更多。这不是我们的算式。

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:30）：不是。

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:31）：不总是这样，但现在确实是。

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:35）：变得和所有人一样，并不符合股东的利益。

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:44）：是。（掌声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:24:18）：OK, Gregg. (Laughter)

**Gregg Warren**（01:24:22）：Yeah, Warren, just wanted to kind of maybe follow up on those past two questions, because there is sort of a theme there. It seems to me that there's definitely more of a home country bias when we look at the acquisitions and investments that Berkshire's done historically. And while there's definitely value in sticking with what you know and feel the most comfortable with, it seems like you've gone from a model that was originally focused on putting boots on the ground to find investment and acquisition opportunities to one where you're seemingly more content to have sellers or their representatives call you or drop by the office, basically more of a pull model than a push model. There's nothing wrong with this, but I just wonder, if the opportunity cost that comes with this type of model is that Berkshire misses out on a lot of overseas business where owners are unaware of your willingness to step up and buy them outright and allow them to run their companies under the Berkshire umbrella, and missing stock investment opportunities because Berkshire if not necessarily familiar enough with the local market. At this point, do you think Berkshire would benefit from putting more boots on the ground in these overseas markets?

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:30）：Yeah, we — actually, it must have been after we bought ISCAR, Eitan Wertheimer convinced me that we should get more exposure in Europe. And he helped out in doing that. I went over, he arranged various meetings. We've had a lot of contact. It isn't that they're not aware, and we do hear about some. But we do have the problem they've got to be sizable. I mean, if we do a billion-dollar acquisition, and it makes $100 million, or thereabouts, pretax, $80 million after tax, you know, it's — if we really know the business and we're sure we're not going to have a problem with the people running it being motivated in the future, and doing a similar job as to when they had their money in and everything, it's nice to add 80 million to 25 billion. But you can't afford to spend lots of time doing that. And we gain something by having Todd (Combs) and Ted (Weschler) do some looking at things, screening them and that sort of thing. But in the end, you want somebody that — you want some family that's held their business in Europe or in the U.K. for 50 or 100 years that can make a deal, and that wants to do it with Berkshire. I mean, if they're looking to get the most money, if they want to have an auction, we're not going to win and we're not going to participate because we're not going to waste our time on it. If we form an acquisition crew, they'll acquire something. I mean, I've watched so many institutions in operation that, you know, if your job every day is to go to work and screen a bunch of things with the idea that you're the strategic department or acquisition department, you're going to want to do something. I want to do something, but I don't want to do something unless — (laughs) — Berkshire benefits by it — truly benefits by it — and generally it's of a size.

**Charlie Munger**（01:27:46）：Warren, our problem is not a lack of boots on the ground, our problem is the people on the ground are paying prices that we don't want to pay.

**Warren Buffett**（01:27:55）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（01:27:56）：That's our problem. And that problem is not going to be cured by boots.

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:00）：We can spend $100 billion in the next year. That is not a problem.

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:06）：No.

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:07）：Spending it intelligently is a huge problem. And the —

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:10）：Our competitors are buying with somebody else's money, and they get part of the upside and take none of the downside.

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:15）：And a lot of them —

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:17）：It is hard to compete with people like that.

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:20）：Yeah. They'll leverage it up, they'll make a lot of money if it fails, and they'll make even more money if it succeeds. And that's not our equation.

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:30）：No.

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:31）：And that isn't always that way, but it's certainly that way now. It's probably —

**Charlie Munger**（01:28:35）：And it's not in the shareholders' interest that we get to be like everybody else.

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:44）：Yeah. (Applause)
