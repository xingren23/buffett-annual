## 问题

亚马逊、谷歌、Facebook、微软、阿里巴巴、腾讯都拥有宽护城河与强品牌。伯克希尔要不要调整「宽护城河＋强品牌」的模型，去拥抱而非回避科技？

## 回答要点

1. 芒格抢答：「我想答案是：也许。」
2. 在科技世界，护城河若能建成可以价值连城，但巴菲特没有信心自己是最佳判断者
3. 「我们不想在自己不懂的棋局里争胜负」，也不把股东的钱分包给别人的判断
4. 原则没变，仍会留在自认为知悉的领域；目标是提高击球率而非不错过最大的机会
5. 芒格：伯克希尔仍有大量护城河宽阔的公司，包括强大的工业品牌；「但我们在新领域里没建下什么功勋」

## 关键引述

> **“I think the answer is maybe.”**
>
> 「我想答案是：也许。」

> **“we won't go into something because somebody else tells us it's a good thing to do.”**
>
> 「我们不会因为别人说「这是好事」就一头扎进去。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:16:47）：10 号站。

**Audience Member**（00:16:58）：达芙妮（9 岁，纽约）：巴菲特先生、芒格先生，你们好。我叫达芙妮，来自纽约，今年 9 岁。我很兴奋能来伯克希尔股东会，这是第三年。

**Warren Buffett**（00:17:15）：哇。（掌声）你现在应该已经发财了吧。（笑声）

**Auience Member**（00:17:24）：达芙妮：你们常说，投资者应当寻找有宽阔护城河的企业，护城河后的城堡由值得信赖、能做正确决策的国王或女王掌管。过去你们把这些建议用在拥有世界级品牌的公司上——可口可乐、美国运通、喜诗糖果——也用在帮助品牌守护和拓宽护城河的媒体公司上，如首府广播、ABC、华盛顿邮报。你们过去一般回避科技公司，理由是技术变化太快、很难在科技领域建立能力圈。今天，世界上一些最具统治力的公司是科技公司——美国的亚马逊、谷歌、Facebook、微软，中国的阿里巴巴和腾讯——它们都拥有宽阔的护城河、强大的品牌，由杰出的企业家掌舵。

**Warren Buffett**（00:19:01）：说得好。（笑声）

**Audience Member**（00:19:05）：我的问题是：如果伯克希尔要坚持投资宽阔护城河与强大品牌的传统——尤其是资本效率高的公司——是否需要拓宽投资视野，把更多领先的科技平台纳入进来？换句话说，你们是否需要调整「宽护城河＋强品牌」的模型，去拥抱而非回避科技？（掌声）

**Charlie Munger**（00:19:46）：我想答案是：也许。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:20:03）：我想答案是把她请进董事会，能大幅拉低平均年龄，我们挨的批评也会少些。

你说得完全对：我们喜欢护城河。过去在「一城一报」的报纸里、在主导地位稳固、广告定价偏低的地方电视台里，在品牌里，我们都能识别它。在科技世界，如果你能建起护城河，它可以价值连城。但很多情形下我没有信心自己是判断护城河的最佳人选。搞清楚谁在赢、生意是什么，这不难；难的是懂这局棋的人远比我多。我们不想在自己不懂的棋局里争胜负。我们可以雇人——比如泰德（韦施勒）和托德（康姆斯）——在某些投资领域比我甚至查理懂得更多。但原则没有变。

你说得对，一些老牌企业的护城河消失了；你说得对，未来会有护城河宽阔、价值巨大的公司，我们希望时不时识别出一家。但我们仍会留在自认为知悉的领域里，即便在这里也会犯错。我们不会因为别人说「这是好事」就一头扎进去。我们不会把你的钱分包给别人的判断。你可以拿自己的钱去跟别人的判断，但我们不相信雇十个某个领域的专家就能带来卓越的投资结果，而且我们担心那样会亏很多钱。

所以我们会尽力扩大伯克希尔人的能力圈，减少错失。但未来仍会错失很多，过去也错失很多。关键是找到我们击球率高的地方。错过最大的那几个并不要紧，只要我们投出去的钱结果尚可。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:22:47）：我认为我们仍然拥有大量护城河宽阔的公司，其中不少是我们所在利基里极其强大的工业品牌。所以伯克希尔股东不必担心我们变成一滩不赚钱的烂泥。但我们在新领域里确实没建下什么功勋。

**Warren Buffett**（00:23:15）：是的，我们最后不会全押在马鞭这种生意上。这是个非常好的问题，也是我们始终关注的焦点。我希望……

**Charlie Munger**（00:23:25）：我们在努力进步。

**Warren Buffett**（00:23:26）：希望明年能看到你第四次来这里。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:16:47）：Station 10.

**Audience Member**（00:16:58）：Mr. Buffett and Mr. Munger, hi. My name is Daphne. I'm from New York and I'm nine years old. And I'm excited to be at the Berkshire meeting, and this is my third year.

**Warren Buffett**（00:17:15）：Wow. (Applause) You should be rich by now. (Laughter)

**Auience Member**（00:17:24）：You have often said that investors are well-served by identifying businesses with a wide moat, where the castle behind the moat is run by a king or queen who can be trusted to make good decisions. In the past, you have applied this advice by investing in businesses with world class strong brands, such as Coke, American Express, and See's, as well as media companies that has helped these brands protect and widen their moats, such as Cap Cities, ABC, and the Washington Post. In the past, you have also generally avoided investing in technology companies, pointing out how quickly technology changes and how hard it is to build a circle of competence in it. Today, we seem to be in a world where some of the most dominant companies in the world are technology companies. And we have built powerful platforms, such as Amazon, Google, Facebook, and Microsoft in America, and Alibaba and Tencent in China. These companies all have wide moats, strong brands, and are led by brilliant — entrepreneurs.

**Warren Buffett**（00:19:01）：That's good. (Laughter)

**Audience Member**（00:19:05）：My question to you is this: if Berkshire is to honor its tradition of investing in wide moats and strong brands, and especially in companies that are also capital efficient, do you think that Berkshire needs to expand its investing lens to include more of these leading technology platforms? In other words, do you believe that you need to adapt your model of wide moats and strong brands to embrace, not avoid, technology? (Applause)

**Charlie Munger**（00:19:46）：I think the answer is maybe. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:20:03）：I think the answer is to put her on the board and it'll bring down the average age enormously. We won't get criticized as much. You're exactly right, in that we do like moats, and we used to be able to identify them in a newspaper that was the only newspaper in town, or in TV stations where we felt the dominant position, we felt the product was underpriced in terms of advertising. We saw it in brands, sometimes. And it is true that in the tech world, if you can build a moat, it can be incredibly valuable. I've not felt the confidence that I was the best one to judge that in many cases. It wasn't hard to figure out who was winning at any given time or what their business was about, but there were a huge number of people that knew more about the game than I did. And we don't want to try and win at a game we don't understand. We may hire people, such as Ted (Weschler) and Todd (Combs), that are better at understanding certain areas of investing than I am, or maybe even Charlie is. But the principles haven't changed. You're right that some of the old ones have lost their moat and you're right that there are going to be companies in the future that have them that will be enormously valuable. And we hope we can identify one every now and then. But we won't — we'll still stay within where we think we know what we're doing. And obviously, we'll make mistakes even within that area. But we won't go into something because somebody else tells us it's a good thing to do. I mean, we are not going to subcontract your money to somebody else's judgment. You can take your money and follow somebody else's judgment, but we're not in the business of thinking that if we hire ten people with specialties in this area or that, that it will lead to superior investment results. And we do worry that we could blow a lot of money that way. So, we'll do our best to enlarge the circle of competence of the people at Berkshire so that we don't miss so many. But we'll miss a lot in the future. We missed a lot in the past. The main thing to do is to find things where our batting average is going to be high. And if we miss the biggest ones, that really doesn't bother us, as long as the things we do with money work out OK. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:22:47）：Well, I think we've still got an awful lot of companies with big moats, and a lot of them are very — and some are industrial brands that are just incredibly strong in the niches we're in. So, Berkshire shareholders don't need to worry about we're just one big morass of unprofitability or anything like that. But we have not covered ourselves with glory in the new fields.

**Warren Buffett**（00:23:15）：Yeah. We won't end up all in buggy whips, though, or anything. But it's a very good question, and it's what we focus on all the time. And I hope —

**Charlie Munger**（00:23:25）：We're trying to improve.

**Warren Buffett**（00:23:26）：And we hope we see you back here for your fourth next year.
