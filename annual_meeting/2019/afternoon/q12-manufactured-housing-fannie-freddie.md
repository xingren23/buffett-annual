## 问题

房利美、房地美推出装配式住房贷款新计划，会带来多大需求？如何影响克莱顿的贷款利润？利差收窄时还受益吗？

## 回答要点

1. 「可能不利于利润，但对美国是好事」——装配式住房是普通人获得体面住所的合理途径
2. 克莱顿已每月向两房出售约 1000 万美元贷款
3. 芒格：两房会越来越多通过克莱顿放贷，因为「克莱顿非常可靠」
4. 已收购现场建造建筑商，现拥有九家现场建造业务（几年前为零）
5. 2008—09 年衰退中，借款人 FICO 分很低但只要保住工作就还贷——「房子对人们真的意味着什么」

## 关键引述

> **“a home really means something to people.”**
>
> 「房子对人们真的意味着什么。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:07）：乔纳森？

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:59:12）：乔纳森·勃兰特：房利美和房地美推出了针对装配式住房贷款的新融资计划，我猜终于能让需要按揭的装配式住房买家与购买现场建造房屋的人多少站上同一条起跑线。你预计这些新计划对装配式住房需求有多大正面作用？会如何影响克莱顿（Clayton）可观的贷款利润？克莱顿会向两房卖出更多贷款吗？利差收窄时利润还能受益吗？

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:46）：可能不利于利润，但两房获准对装配式住房做更多贷款，肯定是一件好事。装配式住房是普通人获得体面住所的合理途径，只是某种程度上融资很难。本地银行常做，大放贷机构不想做。两房扩大业务的可能性很大。我们已经在卖——每月大概 1000 万美元的贷款——给两房。在我看来，两房在这个领域做得更多，对美国是好事。显然我们会卖出更多房子，但会损失融资收入，最终可能吃亏也可能占便宜。但我认为这是该做的事。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:01:00）：我认为两房会为越来越多的装配式住房提供融资，而且会越来越多地通过克莱顿来做。因为克莱顿非常可靠，能为人们以便宜的价格造出好房子。我看得见的未来里，克莱顿会越做越大。那家伙还年轻——他和沃伦跟我长得完全不一样。

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:26）：克莱顿的管理团队完美，我们正在扩张现场建造房屋业务。几天前刚完成对一家建筑商的收购，现在有九家——我记得是九家——现场建造业务，几年前一家都没有。我们对凯文·克莱顿和他的团队评价极高。去年董事会在诺克斯维尔开会，第二次考察了克莱顿。我们乐见克莱顿扩张，也乐见更多人住上可负担的住房。

2008—09 年衰退中，我们的借款人平均 FICO 分很低——和典型购房者相比——但只要保住了工作，他们就还贷。他们想要那个家，房子对他们极为重要。我们还有各种帮扶计划。所以在当时的压力之下，我们的贷款表现远好于外界预期。原因就是：房子对人们真的意味着什么。除非失业或生病——再说我们有帮扶计划——他们就会还贷，过相当体面的日子。而如果两房扩大计划，他们还能更便宜。我希望如此。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:07）：Jonathan?

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:59:12）：Freddie Mac and Fannie Mae have new financing programs for manufactured home loans that I'm guessing could finally put purchasers of those homes who need mortgages on a somewhat more level playing field with those buying site-built homes. How positive an effect do you expect these new programs to have on manufactured home demand? And how might the programs affect Clayton's sizable profits from lending? Will Clayton sell more loans to Freddie and Fannie, and does that help profits even if spreads compress?

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:46）：Well, it may not help profits, but it would — it definitely is good if the Freddie and Fannie are authorized to do more lending against manufactured homes. Manufactured homes are a very reasonable for people to get decent housing and have a home. And they are hard to finance to some degree. The local banks frequently do it, but the big lenders haven't wanted to do it. They are — There is the possibility, or the likelihood, that Freddie and Fannie are going to expand. We already sell — I don't know whether it's 10 million a month of loans or something like that — to Freddie and Fannie. But it would be very good for America, in my view, if Freddie and Fannie did more in that area. Obviously, we would sell some more homes, but we would lose financing, and we might come out behind, we might come out ahead. But I think it would a good thing to do. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:01:00）：Well, I think Freddie and Fannie will finance more and more homes, and I think they'll do it more and more of it through Clayton. And they'll do it because Clayton is very trustworthy, and will do a very good job at making good housing at cheap prices for people. And I think Clayton will get bigger and bigger and bigger as far ahead as you can see. And the guy's young, he doesn't look like Warren and me. Not at all.

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:26）：We've got a perfect managerial group at Clayton, and we're expanding our site-built homes. We just closed on a builder a couple — a few days ago. And we now have nine different — I believe — nine different site-built home operations, and we didn't have any a few years ago. And we think extraordinarily well of Kevin Clayton and his group. Our directors met last year in Knoxville and viewed the Clayton operation for the second time. So, we like the idea of Clayton expanding, and we like the idea of more people having very affordable housing. During the 2008 and '09 recession, our borrowers — who had very low FICO scores on average, I mean compared to typical home buyers, and they — if they kept their jobs, they made the payments. I mean, they wanted that home, and the home was an enormously important item to them. And we had various programs that helped them as well. But our loan experience was far better than people anticipated under the stress that existed then. But it was because a home really means something to people. And absent losing jobs or sickness — and, like I say, we have some programs to help people — they make the payments, and they have very decent living. But they would get that even cheaper if Freddie and Fannie expanded their programs. And, like I say, I hope they do.
