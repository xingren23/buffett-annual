## 问题

指数基金有低成本优势，伯克希尔有浮存金杠杆与资本配置优势。若两者十年总回报持平，伯克希尔的内在价值增长是否会跑赢标普 500？高出多少？

## 回答要点

1. 「我什么也不估」，但先算税：公司层面资本利得税 21%，股息实际税率约 10.5%—13%
2. 指数基金公司层面不纳税、直接穿透股东，伯克希尔天生处于公司税劣势
3. 强牛市里大概率跑输，市场回到当前水平或更低时大概率跑赢
4. 芒格：即便只与标普打平，税后也遥遥领先
5. 「这个国家对我们好得不可思议」——双重税不抱怨，年报里的「顺风」别国给不了

## 关键引述

> **“we will probably overperform.”**
>
> 「我们大概率跑赢。（指市场回到当前水平或更低时）」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:52）：卡罗尔？

**Carol Loomis**（01:33:58）：这个问题来自加州丹维尔的斯蒂芬·德博德，角度我从未听过。「巴菲特先生，您一直推荐低成本的标普 500 指数基金——今天也再次推荐——作为可靠的长期投资工具。这些基金有内在的结构性优势：低成本、自动换仓。但伯克希尔也有结构性优势：浮存金带来的财务杠杆、多元的资本配置机会。我认为伯克希尔在这场竞赛中领先。举例说，如果伯克希尔的经营业务与投资业绩在十年里恰好与标普指数的总回报持平，我认为伯克希尔内在价值的增长仍将跑赢标普 500。如果您同意，能否估算高出多少个百分点？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:11）：（笑）答案是我什么也不估。但如果我们只持有股票、持有标普，我们的表现会显著差于标普，因为我们要承担公司层面的税：现在资本利得税 21%，外加可能的州所得税；股息的实际税率取决于持有结构，大约在 10.5%—13% 之间。所以单凭税法，伯克希尔相对于指数基金就处于公司层面的劣势——指数基金在公司层面不纳税，直接穿透给股东。

我不知道我们能否跑赢标普 500。我知道的是：我们对待股东的钱，与对待自己的钱完全一样。我们把自己的身世与这门生意绑在一起，并且对任何可能显著毁灭价值的事高度警觉。

如果从现在起是很强的牛市，我们大概率跑输；如果五年、十年后市场回到当前水平或更低，我们大概率跑赢。至于十年总回报与内在价值增长的比较——我不知道会不会发生。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:37:19）：对缴税的股东来说，伯克希尔有一个大优势：即便我们只与标普打平，税后我们也遥遥领先。我们在人生中已有相当体面的角色和位置，不该太失望。

**Warren Buffett**（01:37:41）：不。回到合伙企业时代，如果我们把结构限定在最初的合伙人群里，做同样的事，股东税后结果会更优一些。但现在这种形式也并不差，尽管公司层面的资本利得税曾有一两年高达 39%，长期是 35%，有的州还要加所得税。持有穿透式基金就不会有这样的双重征税。当然，股票永远不卖就不用交；一旦卖出，就有双重税效应。

我们毫无抱怨。这个国家对我们好得不可思议——年报里写的「顺风」——这在任何别的国家都不可能发生。能在此时此地在这个国家经营，我们非常幸运。查理，还有吗？

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:04）：没有。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:52）：Carol?

**Carol Loomis**（01:33:58）：This question comes from Stephen De Bode of Danville, California, and it raises a question I've certainly never heard before. "Mr. Buffett, in the past, you have recommended low-cost S&P — and again today — the S&P 500 Index funds as reliable, long-term investment vehicles. These funds have certain inherent structural advantages such as low costs, and automatic reshuffling of their holding. "But Berkshire also has certain structural advantages, such as financial leverage from the float, and diverse capital allocation opportunities. I think of Berkshire as being ahead in this game. "For example, it seems to me that if Berkshire's overall operating business and investment performance were to exactly match the total return of the S&P Index over a 10-year period, Berkshire's growth in intrinsic value would outperform the S&P 500. "If you agree, could you estimate by how many percentage points?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:11）：(Laughs) Well, the answer is, I won't estimate anything. But the — if we just owned stocks, and we owned the S&P, our performance would be significantly worse than the S&P because we would be incurring a corporate tax, which would now be 21 percent on capital gains, plus possibly some state income taxes. And effectively, our tax rate on dividends is — depends where they're held — but somewhere between 10 1/2 or 11 and 13 percent. So, Berkshire is a mistake — or it's at a corporate disadvantage simply by the way the tax law runs, compared to owning an index fund, which has no tax at the corporate level, but just passes through to shareholders. So, I wouldn't — I don't know whether we'll outperform the S&P 500 or not. I know that we'll behave with our shareholders' money exactly as we would behave with our own money. And we will have — we'll basically tie our fortunes in life to this business, and we will be very cognizant of doing anything that can destroy value in any significant way. But we will probably — If there were to be a very strong bull market from this point forward, we would probably underperform during that period. If the market five years from now or 10 years from now is at this level or below, we will probably overperform. But I don't think that I want to — I don't quite understand the question in terms of when it said the total return of the S&P over a 10-year period and Berkshire's growth in intrinsic value would outperform. I don't know whether that will happen or not. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:37:19）：Well, there would be one big advantage for the shareholders that pay taxes, and that is that the Berkshire shareholders, even if we just matched the S&P, we'd be way ahead after taxes. We all have a pretty decent role in life and a pretty good position. We shouldn't be too disappointed.

**Warren Buffett**（01:37:41）：No. If we — we could have structured — going back to partnership days — we could have structured things so that actually, over a period of time, doing the same things we did, would have actually come out somewhat more favorably for shareholders if we had kept it to the original partnership group. But the present form hasn't worked badly, although we have had periods when our corporate capital gains tax, as opposed to the individual, I think it got up to 39 percent a couple of years or one year, and certainly was 35 percent for a long time. And then, on top of that, we had the state income taxes in some cases. And they exceeded — well, I mean, if you owned a pass-through fund, you did not have that level of possible double taxation. Now, if you hold your stock forever, you don't pay it. But if you actually sell your stock, you've had a double tax effect. We're not complaining in any way, shape, or form. This country has treated us incredibly well, and we've added this huge tailwind, which I wrote about in the annual report. And it wouldn't have happened in any other country. So, we've been very lucky that we've been operating in this country at this time. Charlie, anything?

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:04）：No.
