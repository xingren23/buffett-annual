# 第 07 问 · 是否应缩减长尾保险风险

## 问题

股东担心超出预期的保险赔付会拖垮原本优秀的业务，认为这一风险在巴菲特、芒格与贾因之后会更难把控。他问，长期看伯克希尔是否应聚焦 GEICO 这类简单的短尾业务，并缩减长尾风险敞口。

## 回答要点

1. 阿吉特认为合同确定性是核心风险，长尾与短尾财产险都受合同措辞被曲解和监管环境的影响，营业中断险是最近的例子。
2. 伯克希尔愿意在单一事件中承受 100 亿美元损失，但要求相称的定价，不愿在自估只会亏 5000 万的业务上亏 100 亿。
3. 美国童子军的索赔从约 1100—2000 件升到接近 10 万件，多个州单方面延长诉讼时效、原告律师资金充裕是主因。
4. 敞口按主要风险类别汇总，定价中为「未知的未知」留出空间，以给敞口划定边界。
5. 巴菲特说保险里的意外几乎全是不愉快的，但仍认为这是世界上最好的保险业务，阿吉特是它的创造者。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:13:38）：这个问题是给沃伦和阿吉特的。来自 Fernando Lewis，一位来自巴拿马的长期伯克希尔股东，他说：「作为一个打算继续持有几十年的股东，我最大的担忧是超出预期的保险损失可能带来的亏损。「我们在其他伟大的公司身上见过这种情况：承保错误最终使此前被视为典范的业务遭受重创。「虽然我理解伯克希尔的文化独一无二，保险部门也充满才华出众的人，但这一风险令我不安。「我们许多股东现在感到安心，因为有幸由巴菲特先生、芒格先生和贾因先生来把关这些交易。然而，终有一天情况不再如此。认为从长期看，伯克希尔应聚焦 GEICO 这类简单、普通的短尾保险业务，并缩减某些长尾风险的规模，这样想是否合理？「我想说明，我对所有打造出世界上最好保险集团的伯克希尔员工怀有最大的敬意和感激。

我相信我们拥有这样的才华，能在未来几十年保持这一领域的领先。这里关注的是某些保险业务线固有的不透明与风险。」」

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:41）：阿吉特，你要不要先开始，还是——？

**Ajit Jain**（01:14:45）：我的意思是，显然，合同确定性是我们在保险业面临的一个问题。这个问题不仅涉及你提到的长尾业务线，甚至涉及短尾的财产险业务线。最近的例子是营业中断险，它是企业买卖的任何财产险保单的组成部分。每次我们签发一份合同，都存在一种风险：或者因为合同写法上的马虎，或者因为我们都必须身处的监管环境，合同里的字句可能被曲解。而通常当它们被曲解时，结果都对保险业不利，而不是有利。所以，这是一种风险。就它可能有多糟而言，这是一种未知的风险。我希望我们在给产品定价时把这算进去了。可以说，我们为未知的未知留了一点东西。我们尝试按主要风险类别汇总我们的敞口。希望这能让我们安心一些，对敞口究竟能有多大有一些界限。但毫无疑问，监管机构在这门生意的经济性上扮演着非常重要的角色，尤其在美国，我们要面对 50 个州的监管机构，在定价方面、在合同方面、在——

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:15）：保险里那些意外，大多数、几乎全部，都是不愉快的。我的意思是，（笑），你一开始就拿到保费。那是愉快的。从那以后，你会遇到一些非常有想象力的损失。还有一些是你自己承担的。我们愿意承受，就外部上限而言，我们想，嗯，我们愿意在单一事件中损失 100 亿美元。我们要求为此得到非常恰当的报酬。但我们有资源这么做。而且如果，但我们不想在自认为最多只会（笑）损失 5000 万之类的业务上损失 100 亿。而且，你知道，比如美国童子军现在的情况，你知道，他们，我想已经提交了 1100 件索赔，大致如此，而现在，光是在，就有 17000 件，

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:11）：不，不。现在已经接近 10 万件了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:13）：你知道，而且——

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:14）：增加了 50 倍。

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:17）：而这些可以追溯到 1950 或 1960 年。而且有人在为索赔做广告。所以，突然间，你就有了一大堆索赔。我确信很多索赔是有效的。我也确信很多是无效的。而且——

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:25）：嗯——

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:26）：——你到底怎么把两者区分开？（笑）

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:28）：对，这回到你刚提出的问题。索赔数量从不到 2000 件飙升到接近 10 万件，原因是诉讼时效本来已经过了，但在好几个州，如果不是大多数州的话，他们单方面延长了可以提出索赔的截止期限，往后推了几年。结果是出现了多得多的索赔，由如今资金非常充裕的原告律师出资支持。这就导致索赔数量一路飙升。

**Warren Buffett**（01:18:08）：对。在保险里你会遇到很多不愉快的意外。但我们有一个非常，我在这上面很有偏见。但我不会，（笑），我认为我们拥有世界上最好的保险业务。而阿吉特是创造它的人。GEICO 的人，我们买下了它，他们随着时间做了了不起的事，也贡献了他们那一份，其他人也是。但阿吉特是它的交响乐指挥。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（01:13:38）：This question is for Warren and Ajit. It comes from Fernando Lewis, a long-time Berkshire shareholder from Panama, who says, "As a shareholder that intends to remain so for many decades, my biggest concern is around possible losses arising from higher-than-expected insurance losses. "We've seen this in other great companies where underwriting mistakes end up crippling businesses previously considered exemplar. "While I understand that Berkshire's culture is unique and the insurance division is full of talented individuals, this is a risk that concerns me. "Many of us shareholders feel comfortable now, given the privilege of having Mr. Buffett, Mr. Munger, and Mr. Jain looking at these deals. However, there will be a day when this is no longer the case. Is it reasonable to think that over the long term, Berkshire should focus on plain, vanilla short-tail insurance businesses like GEICO and reduce the size of some long-tail risk? "I want to clarify that I have the most respect and gratitude for all of Berkshire employees that have built the best insurance group in the world. I'm confident we have this talent to remain leaders in this field for decades to come. This is focused on the inherent opaqueness and risk of some insurance lines."

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:41）：Ajit, do you want to lead off, or —?

**Ajit Jain**（01:14:45）：I mean, clearly contract certainty is an issue for us in the insurance industry. It is an issue that cuts across not only the long-tail line that you mentioned, but even short-tail property focus lines. The most recent example is business interruption, which is an integral part of any property insurance policy that is bought and sold by corporations. It is a risk every time we issue a contract that, either because of sloppiness in terms of how that contract is written, or because of the regulatory environment we all have to live in, that the words in the contract may be tortured. And normally when they are tortured, they end up going against the insurance industry, not in their favor. So, it is a risk. It's an unknown risk, in terms of how bad it can be. I hope we price for it when we price for the product. We throw something for the unknown unknowns, if you will. And we try and aggregate our exposures by major risk categories. Hopefully, that'll give us some comfort, in terms of having some boundaries on what the exposure really can be. But there's no question the regulators play a very important role, in terms of the economics of the business, especially in the U.S., where the 50 state regulators who we have to deal with, in terms of pricing, in terms of contracts, in terms of —

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:15）：Most of those surprises in insurance, practically all of them, are unpleasant. I mean, (laughs), you get the premium up front. That's pleasant. And then from there on, you get some very imaginative losses that come through. And you get some that you've taken on. We are willing to lose, in terms of, sort of, the outside limit, we think, well, we're willing to lose $10 billion in a single event. And we want to get paid very appropriately for that. But we've got the resources to do it. And if — But we don't want to lose 10 billion in something where we only thought we could (laughs) lose 50 million or something like that. And, you know, the current situation, for example, with The Boy Scouts of America, you know, they — I think there were 11-hundred claims, or something like that, that have been filed and now, there's 17,000 just in —

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:11）：No, no. They're close to a hundred thousand now.

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:13）：You know, and —

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:14）：Up by 50 times.

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:17）：And these go back to 1950 or 1960. And you've got people advertising for claims. And so, all of a sudden, you get a lot of claims. I'm sure a lot of the claims are valid. I'm sure a lot of them are invalid. And —

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:25）：Well —

**Warren Buffett**（01:17:26）：— and how in the world do you pick out the difference? (Laughs)

**Ajit Jain**（01:17:28）：Yeah, and it goes back to the issue that you just raised. The reason why this number of claims have skyrocketed from less than 2,000 to close to a hundred thousand is because the statute of limitations had expired, but in several states, if not in most states, they have unilaterally extended the deadline by when you can make claims, and expanded it by a few years. As a result of which, a lot more claims have appeared, funded by plaintiff lawyers who are now very well-funded. And that results in claims just skyrocketing.

**Warren Buffett**（01:18:08）：Yeah. You get a lot of unpleasant surprises in insurance. But we've got a very — I'm very biased on this. But I wouldn't — (laughs) — I think we've got the best insurance operation in the world. And Ajit is the guy that created it. And the people at GEICO — we bought that — and they did wonderful things over time to contribute their part of it, too, and other people have. But Ajit is the symphony conductor of it. (Laughs)
