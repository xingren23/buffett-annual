## 问题

股东援引巴菲特 1997 年谈烟草股时划道德线的表态，问买入雪佛龙是否与此矛盾。他还问，气候议题上的警报是否已变得不理性，转型节奏的共识是否高估了现实，从而成为年轻一代的沉重税负。最后他问，这是否意味着未来十年碳氢化合物和雪佛龙的前景不会受损。

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特称两边极端的人「都有点疯」，不愿看到所有碳氢化合物在三年内被禁，认为那样的世界行不通。
2. 伯克希尔持有 Costco 与沃尔玛，两家大量门店销售香烟，他并不因此回避这些公司。
3. 1997 年放弃孟菲斯嚼烟生意，理由是危害小于吸烟草、经营者正派且做的是合法生意，但最终仍然拒绝。
4. 他认为雪佛龙「一点都不是邪恶公司」，长期仍需要大量碳氢化合物，但世界正在远离它们。
5. 他自称不喜欢就股票做道德判断；持有指数基金的人同样会持有雪佛龙。
6. 芒格说若在斯沃斯莫尔的英语教授与雪佛龙员工之间素未谋面地选女婿，他会选雪佛龙那位。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Becky Quick**（00:49:05）：这个问题来自英国的 Andrew Dixon。他说：「我的问题关于石油天然气行业，以及你们买入雪佛龙股票。「1997 年被问到烟草股时，你提到个人和公司偶尔必须就自己愿意做什么划出道德底线。「你当时说，你对在烟草股上做重大投入感到不安，对它们的前景也感到不安。查理也提到过放弃一桩你们都知道十拿九稳的私募烟草交易，但你们俩对拒绝那笔交易都毫不后悔。「我并不是说石油天然气行业有着和香烟一样已知的负外部性。它们没有。就烟草而言，产品与癌症之间的因果关系直接、明显、可衡量。就碳氢化合物而言，社会成本与收益的评估要复杂得多。「然而，社会上越来越大的一部分人划的线，使他们的画里根本不包含碳氢化合物，就是这样。

「我的问题是：气候界的危言耸听现在是否已经弥漫整个社会，到了不理性的程度？「关于向更绿色能源转型所能达到的变化速度，我们是否已经建立了一种不切实际的共识，以至于它正在变成当前年轻一代背负的过于昂贵的税？「我们能否从你买入雪佛龙股票推断出，你不认为来自社会、监管机构和政客的呼号会在未来十年损害碳氢化合物——以及就此而言的雪佛龙——的前景？「投资者是否仍然可以假定，一家能以低成本每桶发现并生产石油的石油天然气企业，能在未来很长一段时间里产生足够的资本回报？」」

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:43）：嗯，（笑），我给你一个十个字的回答。（笑）我记不全那里都有哪些问题了。但我要说，站在两边极端的人都有点疯。（笑）我会很不愿意看到所有碳氢化合物在三年内被禁。你知道，你不会想要那样一个世界，那行不通。另一方面，你知道，正在发生的事会随着时间被适应，就像我们适应了各种各样的事情一样。我不认为，我对 1997 年那段话很感兴趣，因为，你知道，我们以前谈过这个。我们持有 Costco 或沃尔玛毫无问题，你知道，它们相当多的门店卖香烟。那是大宗品类。你知道，那是能把人吸引进来的东西。它们知道香烟的价格。而且，你知道，它们把香烟摆在前面。所以我们不，这是个非常棘手的情况。很久以前我们去孟菲斯时做过那个决定。

我们看了一门非常非常好的生意。它的危害小得多，至少从我能查到的一切来看，嚼烟的危害比吸烟草小得多。那些人是正派的人。他们经营的是合法生意。而且他们自己都嚼烟。（笑）所以他们还告诉我，他们的母亲活到 100 岁还在嚼烟，诸如此类。但查理和我确实走到了那家酒店的大堂。我们只是对自己说：「这大概是我们见过的最好的生意。」然后我打给我当时的女婿 Allen Greenberg。他在为一个与纳德尔相关的组织（Public Citizen，公共公民）工作时研究过嚼烟及其影响。我们决定不做。但是，你知道，我们会，你知道，我看到，我过去常在我们的报纸上看到金融公司的广告，而我知道它们很糟糕，你知道？决定进入还是退出一门生意，是非常难的事。

决定哪家公司对社会贡献更大，也是非常难的时候。我的意思是，我不知道是否，我认为雪佛龙以各种方式造福了社会。我认为它还在继续这样做。我认为我们在很长一段时间里会需要大量碳氢化合物，我们会很高兴拥有它们。但我也确实认为世界正在远离它们。而那可能会改变。我不喜欢就股票做道德判断，就实际经营企业而言。但每门生意都有某些东西，如果你知道了，你就不会喜欢。你知道，肉类加工厂，你进过肉类加工厂吗？（笑）你知道，那里有，如果你期望完美，在你配偶身上、在朋友身上、在公司身上，你都找不到。而你自己选择怎么做，如果你持有指数基金，你就会持有雪佛龙。相信我，雪佛龙一点都不是邪恶的公司。（笑）而且我持有雪佛龙，一点都没有不安。

如果我们拥有整家公司，我也不会，我不会对进入那门生意感到不安。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:54:14）：嗯，我同意。你知道，你可以想象两种情况。一个年轻人娶进你们家。他是比如斯沃斯莫尔的英语教授，或者他在雪佛龙工作。素未谋面的情况下，你挑哪个？我承认我会选雪佛龙那位。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:54:34）：希望你女儿们同意你的看法。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（00:49:05）：This question comes from Andrew Dixon in the UK. He says, "My question is in relation to the oil and gas business and your purchase of Chevron stock. "When being asked a question on tobacco stocks in 1997, you mentioned that individuals and companies occasionally have to draw moral lines about what they're willing to do. "You stated at the time that you were not comfortable in making a big commitment in tobacco stocks, and that you were uncomfortable about their prospects. Charlie has also referenced passing up on a private tobacco deal that you both knew was a cinch, yet you both have no regrets in saying no to the transaction. "I'm not suggesting that the oil and gas business has the same known negative externalities as cigarettes. They do not. With tobacco, the cause and effect relationship between the products and career is direct, obvious, and measurable. With hydrocarbons, the societal costs and benefits are far more complex to evaluate. "However, an increasing portion of society is drawing their lines in such a way that their painting does not include hydrocarbons, period. "My question is: has the alarmism from the climate community now become pervasive across society to the extent it has become irrational? "Have we built our own unrealistic consensus on the pace of change achievable with regards to the transition to greener energy sources to the extent that this is becoming an overly expensive tax worn by the current younger generation?” "Can we gather from your purchase of Chevron stock that you do not believe the howling from society, regulators, and politicians will impair the prospects of hydrocarbons, and Chevron for that matter, in the next ten years? "Can investors still assume an oil and gas business that finds and produces oil at low cost per barrel can generate a sufficient return on capital for a long time to come?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:43）：Well, (laughs), I'll give you a ten-word answer to that. (Laughs) I can't remember all the questions there were there. But I would say that people that are on the extremes of both sides are a little nuts. (Laughs) I would hate to have all the hydrocarbons banned in three years. You know, you wouldn't want a world that — it wouldn't work. And on the other hand, you know, what's happening will be adapted to over time, just as we've adapted to all kinds of things. I do not think — I'm interested in that quote from 1997, because, you know, we've talked about this before. We have no problem owning Costco or Walmart, you know, and a substantial number of their stores, you know, they sell cigarettes. It's big item. You know, it's something that brings people in. They know the price of cigarettes. And, you know, they put them up front. And so, we don't — it's a very tough situation. We made that decision a long time ago when we went to Memphis. And we looked at a business that was a very, very good business. And it was much less harmful — at least from everything I could find out — it was much less harmful than smoking tobacco — chewing tobacco was. And these were decent people. And they were running a legal business. And they all chewed tobacco themselves. (Laughs) So, they — and they told me that their mother was 100 and chewing tobacco, and all these things. But Charlie and I did go down in the lobby of that hotel. And we just said to ourselves, "This is probably the best business we've ever seen." And I called my then-son-in-law, Allen Greenberg. And he'd studied chewing tobacco and its effects when he was working for a Nader-related organization (Public Citizen). And we decided not to do it. But, you know, would we — you know, I see — I used to see ads in our paper from financial companies where I knew they were terrible, you know? And it's a very tough thing to decide whether you get in or out of a business. And it's a very tough time to decide what — would companies benefit society more than others. I mean it's — I don't know whether — I think Chevron's benefited society in all kinds of ways. And I think it continues to do so. And I think we're going to need a lot of hydrocarbon for a long time, and we'll be very glad we've got them. But I do think that the world's moving away from them, too. And that could change. I don't like making the moral judgments on stocks in terms of actually running the businesses. But there's something about every business that, if you knew it, you wouldn't like. And, you know, meatpackers — have you ever gone through a meatpacking plant? (Laughs) You know, there's — if you expect perfection, you know, in your spouse or in your friends or in companies, you're not going to find it. And what you elect to do yourself — if you own an index fund, you're going the own Chevron. And believe me, Chevron is not an evil company in the least. (Laughs) And I have no compunction about owning — in the least — about owning Chevron. And if we owned the entire business, I wouldn't — I would not feel uncomfortable about being in that business. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:54:14）：Well, I agree. You know, you can imagine two things. A young man marries into your family. He's a English professor at, say, Swarthmore, or he works for Chevron. Which would you pick, sight unseen? I want to admit I'd take the guy from Chevron. (LAUGHTER)

**Warren Buffett**（00:54:34）：Hope your daughters agree with you. (LAUGHTER)
