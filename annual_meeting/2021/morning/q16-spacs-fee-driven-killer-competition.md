## 问题

股东问：大量新 SPAC 的兴起，对伯克希尔寻找并完成新收购的能力有什么影响？巴菲特答，SPAC 必须在两年内把钱投出去，用别人的钱抬价，伯克希尔在这种竞购里没有优势。芒格把这类行为称为「费用驱动的买入」，认为它不只是愚蠢，而是可耻。

## 回答要点

1. SPAC 一般须在两年内把资金投出去，这种时间压力下的抢购被巴菲特称为「致命的」。
2. 用别人的钱管钱、只享上行不担下行，必然「总得买点什么」，伯克希尔无法与之竞争。
3. 华尔街跟着钱走，挂上名人名字几乎什么都能卖出去，这是赌场式市场的夸张版本。
4. 巴菲特引用凯恩斯 1936 年《通论》：当一国的资本发展成为赌场活动的副产品，这件事很可能做不好。
5. 芒格称 SPAC 与总收益衍生品赚的是费用驱动的轻松钱，推到过度会给文明带来可怕问题，监管者与选民也不光彩。
6. 巴菲特不怪小赌的普通人，说赌博是人性；芒格说他不喜欢「宰割傻瓜的专业人士」。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:48:07）：这个问题来自 Sam Butler。他说自己多年来一直是股东，他问：「这么多新 SPAC 的兴起，对伯克希尔寻找并完成新收购的能力有什么影响？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:06）：嗯，这是致命的。据我理解，SPAC 一般必须在两年内把钱花出去，所以它们必须在两年内买下一门生意。如果你拿枪指着我的头说：「你必须在两年内买下一家大企业」，你知道，我会买一家。但（笑），那不会是什么好企业。你知道，我们看了又看。而现在有，我不知道有多少，是不是几百家，来自私募股权基金的压力一直存在。我是说，如果你在为别人管钱，你收取费用，你享受上行收益，却不承担下行风险，那你总会买点什么。我可以给你讲一个，很多年前我接到过一位非常著名的人物的电话，他参与其中，想了解再保险。我说：「嗯，我并不真的认为这是一门很好的生意。」他说：「是啊。」（笑）「但是，」他说，「如果我六个月内不把这笔钱花出去，我就得把它还给投资人。

」所以，你知道，如果你用的是别人的钱，你面对的方程式就不一样：你享受上行收益，而如果你不做事，你就得把钱还给他们。（笑）而且，坦白说，我们在这方面没有竞争力，你知道吧？而这不会永远持续下去。但钱现在就在那里，而华尔街跟着钱走。而且它什么都做，你知道，基本上是只要能行得通就做。SPAC 已经行之有效地运转了一段时间，你挂上一个名人的名字，几乎什么都能卖出去。这是，但它是我们在那种，嗯，赌博（听不清）类型市场里所见情况的夸张版本。其实，我确实有一段（经济学家约翰·梅纳德）凯恩斯的话，我们可能会放到，让我看看有没有，对，这大概是历史上最著名的引语之一，因为它确实概括了这个问题：我们拥有世界所能想象的最伟大的市场。

我是说，想象一下，你能够拥有世界上最大企业的部分股权，投入数十亿美元，然后在，你知道，两天后就能撤出来。我是说，相比农场、公寓楼或写字楼，那些要花几个月才能完成一笔交易，市场提供了一个机会，让你以非常、非常低的成本、瞬间地参与并投资于能产生收益的资产，规模巨大，各种好处都有。但如果他们能把赌徒吸引进来，它才真正赚到钱，因为赌徒提供更多交易活动，他们愿意支付更愚蠢的费用，还有各种各样的情况。所以，你拥有这个对人类而言不可思议的巨大资产，但它真正赚钱的时候，是人们在做蠢事的时候。我是说，钱真正就在那里。凯恩斯 1936 年写下这段话，幻灯片上写的是 1939 年，但他 1936 年在《通论》里写的，你知道：「投机者或许无害，如同企业稳定流水上的泡沫。

但当企业成为投机漩涡上的泡沫时，情况就严重了。当一个国家的资本发展成为赌场活动的副产品时，这件事很可能做不好。」嗯，股市，过去一年我们有大量的人进了赌场。你有成千上万、数以百万计的人开了账户做日内交易，他们在卖出看跌和看涨期权，他们，我会说，赌徒数量的增长是史上最大的，基本上，而且，你知道，赌博本身没有错。他们的赔率比玩州彩票要好。但他们口袋里有钱。他们有交易刺激。而且他们实际上，你知道，有很多不错的结果。如果他们只是买股票并持有，他们会做得不错。但赌博冲动在全世界的人身上都很强，偶尔它会得到巨大的推动。而各种条件导致这样一种局面：每天进赌场的人比离开的人多，它在一段时间内会创造出自己的现实。

而没有人告诉你，什么时候钟会敲十二下，一切都会变成南瓜和老鼠。当竞争者用别人的钱玩，或者，不管，如果他们愚蠢地用自己的钱玩，但大手笔都是用别人的钱做的（笑），他们会打败我们，我是说，我们不是，那是另一种游戏，而且他们有更多，他们有很多钱。所以，在这类时期持续的时候，我们在收购上不会有多少运气。但这种事以前发生过。这大概是我们所见过的最极端的情况了，不是吗，查理？或者，

**Charlie Munger**（01:53:30）：是的。当然，我称之为费用驱动的买入。换句话说，不是因为这是一笔好投资才买。他们买它，是因为顾问能拿到费用。当然，这种事越多，你的文明就越愚蠢。而在某种程度上，这也是一种道德失败。因为像 SPAC 和总收益衍生品之类的东西所赚的轻松钱，等等等等——你把它推到过度，就会给文明带来可怕的问题。这对做这些事的人不光彩，对允许它发生的监管者和选民也不光彩。所以，我认为在当前的状况下，我们有很多该感到羞耻的地方。

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:14）：但钱就在那里。

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:16）：是啊，但我们仍然有——（笑）——正在发生的事是可耻的，你知道吧？不只是愚蠢，是可耻的。

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:28）：这不是——我不认为很多赌博的人可耻。我是说，赌博是非常人性的本能，他们口袋里有钱，他们知道别人赚了钱，而他们并不觉得那人比自己聪明，而且——

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:40）：不，不，我不介意那些赌博的可怜虫。我不喜欢那些宰割傻瓜的专业人士。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（01:48:07）：This question comes from Sam Butler. And it says he's been a shareholder for many years, and asks, "What impact does the rise of so many new SPACs have on Berkshire's ability to find and close new acquisitions?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:06）：Well, it's a killer. The SPACs generally have to spend their money in two years, as I understand it, so they have to buy a business in two years. If you put a gun to my head and said, "You’ve got to buy a big business in two years," you know, I'd buy one. But (laughs), it wouldn't be much of one. You know, we look and look. And now there are, I don't know how many, whether it's hundreds —there's always been the pressure from private equity funds. I mean, if you're running money for somebody else, and you're getting paid a fee, and you get the upside, and you don't have a downside, you're going to buy something. I could tell you about a — I had a very famous — I had a call from a very famous figure many years ago who was involved in it and wanted to learn about reinsurance. And I said, "Well, I don't really think it's a very good business." And he said, "Yeah." (Laughs) "But," he says, "if I don't spend this money in six months, I've got to give it back to the investors." So, you know, it's a different equation that you have if you're working with other people's money, where you get the upside and you have to give it back to them if you don't do something. (Laughs) And, frankly, we're not competitive with that, you know? And that won't go on forever. But it's where the money is now, and Wall Street goes where the money is. And it does anything, you know, basically that works. And SPACs have been working for a while, and you stick your — a famous name on it and you can sell almost anything. It's — but it's an exaggerated version of what we've seen in kind of, well, gambling (unintelligible) type market. In fact, I did have a quote from (economist John Maynard) Keynes that we might put up on the — let's see if I've got — yeah, this is probably the most famous — one of the most famous quotes in history, because it really sums up the problem of the fact we've got the greatest markets the world could ever imagine. I mean, imagine being able to own parts of the biggest businesses in the world, and putting billions of dollars in them, then take it out of — you know, two days later. I mean, compared to farms or apartment houses or office buildings, where it takes months to close a deal, I mean, the markets offer a chance to participate and invest in earning assets on a basis that's very, very low cost and instantaneous, huge, all kinds of good things. But it makes its real money if they can get the gamblers to come in, because they provide more action and they're willing to pay sillier fees and all kinds of things. So, you have this incredible, huge asset to humanity, but it really makes its money when people are doing stupid things. I mean, that's where the money really is. And Keynes wrote this 1936 — it says 1939 on the slide, but he wrote it in 1936 in "The General Theory" — that, you know: "Speculators may do no harm as bubbles on the steady stream of enterprise. But the position is serious when enterprise becomes the bubble on a whirlpool of speculation. When the capital development of a country becomes a by-product of the activities of a casino, the job is likely to be ill-done." Well, the stock market — we've had a lot of people in the casino in the last year. You have millions and millions of people have set up accounts where they day-trade, where they — where they're selling puts and calls, where they — I would say that you had the greatest increase in the number of gamblers, essentially, that — and there's — you know, there's nothing wrong with gambling. They got better odds than they've got if play the state lottery. But they have cash in their pocket. They've had action. And they actually, you know, have a lot of good results. And if they just bought stocks, they'd do fine, and held them. But the gambling impulse is very strong in people worldwide, and occasionally it gets an enormous shove. And conditions lead to this place where more people are entering the casino than are leaving every day, and it creates its own reality for a while. And nobody tells you when the clock's going to strike twelve and it all turns to pumpkins and mice. The — when the competition is playing with other people's money — or — whether — and if they're playing foolishly with their own money — but the big stuff is done with other people's money (laughs) — they're going to beat us, I mean — We're not — that's a different game, and they've got more – they've got a lot of money. So, we're not going to have much luck on acquisitions while this sort of a period continues. But it's happened before. This is about as extreme as we've seen it, isn't it, Charlie? Or —

**Charlie Munger**（01:53:30）：Yes. Of course, I call it fee-driven buying. In other words, it's not buying because it's a good investment. They're buying it because the advisor gets a fee. And, of course, the more of that you get, the sillier your civilization is getting. And to some extent, it's a moral failing, too. Because the easy money made by things like SPACs and total return derivatives, and so on and so on — you push that to excess, it causes horrible problems for the civilization. And it reflects no credit on the people who are doing it, and no credit on the regulators and voters that allow it. So, I think we have a lot to be ashamed of in the current conditions.

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:14）：But it's where the money is.

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:16）：Yeah, but we still have — (laughter) — it's shameful what's going on, you know? It's not just stupid, it's shameful.

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:28）：It's not — I don't regard it as shameful on a lot of the people that gamble. I mean, gambling is a very human instinct, and they've got money in their pocket, and they know somebody else has made money who they don't think's any smarter than they are, and —

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:40）：No, no, I don't mind the poor fish that gamble. I don't like the professionals that take the suckers.
