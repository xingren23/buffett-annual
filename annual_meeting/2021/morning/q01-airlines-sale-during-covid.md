## 问题

伯克希尔一向主张「别人恐惧时贪婪」，但在 2020 年初疫情最恐慌时却在低点附近抛售航空股，也没有趁大幅折价买入上市公司股票或大举回购自身股份。股东想知道当时伯克希尔的决策过程。他还问，在《CARES 法案》确认政府会为金融市场兜底之后，公司为何仍未出手。

## 回答要点

1. 疫情初期的净卖出规模很小，约为当时所持全部业务价值的 1%—1.5%；公司业务总值约 7000 亿美元。
2. 航空股是例外：四大航合计市值不到 1000 亿美元，行业最初获得的 250 亿美元援助大部分流向四大，其中一部分是赠款而非贷款。
3. 若伯克希尔仍持有各航 8%—10%，航空公司申请援助时会被要求「去找伯克希尔要」，结果可能很不一样。
4. 巴菲特称这不是伯克希尔历史上的伟大时刻，回头看当时买入会更好。
5. 他仍不愿买入航空业，但通过美国运通 19% 的持股和 Precision Castparts 保留了对航空旅行的敞口。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（00:30:06）：谢谢，沃伦。大家好。这第一个问题来自 Andy Cies。他说自己持有的 B 股「远远不够多」。他说：「巴菲特先生，您以『别人贪婪时恐惧，别人恐惧时贪婪』这句话闻名。但从各方面看，伯克希尔在新冠疫情最初的几个月里，却在别人最恐惧的时候恐惧了——在低点或接近低点抛售航空股，没有利用笼罩市场的恐惧以极低折扣买入上市公司的股票，而且在伯克希尔股价非常有吸引力时，迟疑于大规模回购。「我很想听听您对那段时间的看法，以及伯克希尔是如何做决策的，特别是在《CARES 法案》让公司确信政府会为金融市场提供有力后盾之后。」」

**Warren Buffett**（00:30:59）：嗯，直到很晚，直到货币政策和财政政策双双出手，嗯，你知道我们遇到了一个难以置信的问题。就像查理是首席文化官一样，我是伯克希尔的首席风险官。这就是我的工作。我们希望做得好，但我们更要确保不会做得极糟。但我们并没有卖出很大的量。我的意思是，我们是一家拥有大约 7000 亿美元业务的公司，有些我们全资拥有，有些只持有一部分。我不清楚我们在那个时期卖出的规模是不是相当于当时全部业务价值的 1%。但航空这门生意尤其有意思。我待会儿会讲到财政和货币政策做了什么。但我们旗下几家子公司有几个人想向政府申请援助。有些情况下，它们有持股几个百分点的少数股东，他们说，这个，你知道，正在出台、正在让经济停摆的监管规定会把我们搞死。

他们说，大家都在申请，我们为什么不申请？我说，你知道，（笑），伯克希尔扛得住。这是给那些扛不住眼下局面的人准备的。所以我们不申请。但航空公司是紧接着发生的事情里最显眼的受益者。它们最初拿到 250 亿美元，其中大部分流向四大航空公司，还有一部分是赠款而非贷款。你知道，我认为那是恰当的公共政策。我希望这笔钱能发到每一家餐馆、洗衣店，每一家真正倒闭、毫无，我的意思是，它们基本上被彻底毁掉了。但航空公司，显然，发生的事情在任何方面都不是它们的错。这和 2008、2009 年不一样，那时人们责怪银行，不愿看到它们得到救助。所以，那是，不过，航空公司是在破产状态下运营的。所以不像那样，如我们所知，四大里有三家在过去 10 到 15 年内走过破产程序，所以这些多少习惯了在破产中运营的航空公司，本来也会继续运营下去。

但航空公司得到帮助是完全正当的。整个航空业，你知道，你看看苹果 2 万亿之类的数字，四大航空公司全部加起来，市值大约 1000 亿美元，差不多，我的意思是这非常非常小，合在一起，它们连门槛都够不上。我是说，它们进不了前 50 名。总之，它们去找政府了。它们需要政府的帮助，或者说它们需要，否则其中一些就会破产。真正是国会，不过（财政部长）史蒂夫·姆努钦也是，他们认定航空公司值得帮助，我对此毫无异议。但设想一下，如果伯克希尔是每一家航空公司 10% 的股东，它曾经就是，他们会说，「去找伯克希尔要。」（笑）那会，那就好比我们的一家，他们本来会，如果他们有一个非常非常非常富有的股东持股 8% 或 9%，结果很可能非常非常非常不一样。

而他们去申请的时候，并没有这样的股东。所以，我们的，结果可能就不一样了。事实上，我认为很可能不一样。我的意思是，我现在就能想象出那些头条。因为你也见过一些公司拿了一两亿美元的头条，而那些公司其实并不需要。有些还了回去，大多数都还了回去。但你会看到，实际上你看到的结果，很可能和我们保留股票时不一样。但无论如何，一个市值确实不到 1000 亿美元的行业损失了相当多的钱。它们失去了未来的盈利能力。我的意思是，现在它们，你知道，国际旅行还没有恢复。但我总体上要说，经济复苏的程度远远好于你当时能有把握说的。所以我们不喜欢当时在银行上有那么多钱，我削减了一些银行投资。但基本上，我们的净卖出约为 1% 或 1.5%。

回头看，你知道，当时买入会更好。但，我不认为那是伯克希尔历史上的一个伟大时刻。但同时，按会计准则计算，我们的净资产超过美国任何一家公司。我们拥有六七千亿美元总体上不错的业务。而且我想，我想，我想航空业因为我们卖出而表现得更好了，我祝他们顺利。但我还是不想买航空这门生意（笑）。国际，人们真的很想，他们想因私人原因旅行，而商务旅行是另一回事。当然，我们在商务旅行上有很大的敞口，因为我们持有美国运通 19% 的股份，还拥有 Precision Castparts，它为航空业提供服务。所以我们仍然在航空旅行上有很大的投资，有很大的投入。但我们祝四大一切顺利。我认为它们的管理层在这段时期做得非常好。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（00:30:06）：Thanks, Warren. And hello to everybody. This first question that came in, came in from Andy Cies. He says he's the owner of not nearly enough B shares. He says, "Mr. Buffett, you're well known for saying to be fearful when others are greedy and be greedy when others are fearful. But, by all appearances, Berkshire was fearful when others were most fearful in the early months of COVID, dumping airline stocks at or near the low, not taking advantage of the fear gripping the market to buy shares of public companies at exceptional discounts, and being hesitant to buy back significant amounts of Berkshire stock at very attractive prices. "I'd appreciate hearing your thoughts surrounding this time and how Berkshire approached its decision making, specifically after it was assured, through the CARES Act, that the government would provide a robust backstop to the financial markets."

**Warren Buffett**（00:30:59）：Well, it wasn't until late — until both monetary and fiscal policy kicked in — well, you knew we had an incredible problem. And I am, just as Charlie is the chief culture officer, I'm the chief risk officer of Berkshire. That's my job. We hope we do well, but we want to be sure we don't do terribly. But we didn't sell a substantial amount. I mean we're a company with six, probably $700 billion worth of businesses, some we own in their entirety, some we own a piece of. And I don't know whether we were sellers of maybe 1% of the value of all the businesses we had at that period. But the airline — it's kind of interesting with the airline businesses, in particular. And I'll get to what was done in fiscal and monetary policy. But we had a few people at various subsidiaries of Berkshire that wanted to go in for help from the government. And, in some cases, they had minority shareholders who owned a few percent, and they said, well, this — you know, we're going to get killed by what's happening with the regulations that are being put out and that were stopping the economy. And they said everybody's going in for them, and why don't we go in? I said, you know — (laughs) — Berkshire can handle it. This is for people that can't handle what's happening. And so, we're not applying. But the airlines were the most prominent beneficiaries of what took place immediately. They got 25 billion, initially, most of which went to the big four airlines, and some of which went is as grants, not loans. And, you know, I think that was fine public policy. I think it — I wished it could go to every restaurant and dry cleaner and every small business that really was out of business and had no — I mean they were made toast of, you know, basically. But the airlines — clearly, what happened was not their fault in any way, shape, or form. It wasn't like 2008 and '09 when people blamed the banks and hated to see them helped. So, it was — Now, airlines operate in bankruptcy. So, it isn't like that — three of the four big ones, as we know, went through bankruptcy within the previous 10 or 15 — so the airlines that were kind of used to operating in bankruptcy, they would have kept operating. But it was perfectly proper for the airlines to be helped. The entire airline business, you know — you look at these figures of 2 trillion for Apple and so on — the entire big four airlines, they sold for about $100 billion, almost — I mean it's a very, very small — combined, they wouldn't come close to making the cut. I mean, they wouldn't be in the top 50. So anyway, they went into the government. They needed the government help, or they needed — or they would go bankrupt, some of them. And really the Congress, but (Treasury Secretary) Steve Mnuchin, too, they decided they deserved the help, which I do not quarrel with at all. But imagine if Berkshire was the 10% holder, which they had been, of everyone in the airlines, they said, "Take it up in Berkshire." (Laughs) It’s — it would be like one of our — they would have had — they might have very well had a very, very, very different result if they'd had a very, very, very rich shareholder that owned 8 or 9%. And they didn't have that. You know, when they went in. So, our — you might not have gotten the same result. In fact, I would think you probably wouldn't. I mean, I can just see the headlines now. I mean, you know, because you've seen the headlines on some companies that took a hundred million or two, you know, and really didn't need it. And some of them gave it back — most of them gave it back. But you were looking — you’re actually looking at probably a different result than if we'd kept our stock. But in any event, an industry that was really selling for less than a hundred billion dollars lost a significant amount of money. They lost prospective earning power. I mean right now, they're — you know, international travel's not come back. But I would say, overall, to — the economic recovery has gone far better than you could say with any assurance. So, we didn't like having as much money as we had in banks at that time. So I cut back some of the bank investment. But basically, our net sales were about 1% or one and a half percent. And looking back, you know, it would have been better to be buying. But what — I do not consider it a great moment in Berkshire's history. But also, we've got more net worth than any company in the United States, under accounting principles. And we've got six or 700 billion of generally good businesses. And I think, as I think, I think the airline business has done better because we've sold, and I wish them well. But I still wouldn't want to buy the airline business (laughs). International — people really want to — they want to travel for personal reasons, and business travel is another thing. And we've got a big exposure to business travel, of course, through the fact that we own 19% of American Express and we own Precision Castparts, which services the air business. So, we've still got a big investment in air travel, a big commitment to it. But we wish the big four the best. And I think their managements have done a very good job during this period.
