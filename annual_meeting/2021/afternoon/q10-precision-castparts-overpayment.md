## 问题

2016 年计算 Precision Castparts 未来平均盈利时犯了什么错，导致伯克希尔出价过高？2020 年其盈利因疫情和航空业大幅下滑，当时本可以做哪些不同的计算？Precision 当前的问题是否比疫情本身更大？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特承认犯错的是自己而非伯克希尔，问题出在对平均盈利能力的判断，不在管理层。
2. 收购时未充分估计波音 737 Max 的麻烦、GE 发动机需求下降，以及这些业务陷入接近萧条的情形。
3. 巴菲特仍称 Precision 是极好的公司、对管理层满意，但相对于平均盈利付价过高。
4. 失望的业务会因不再增长而在伯克希尔占比越来越小；GEICO 如今的业务量是收购控股时的 15 倍，占比自然变大。
5. 伯克希尔最初的三块业务——纺织、百货、交易印花——全部失败，公司却并未因此被淘汰。
6. 巴菲特说自己会继续犯错，芒格插话说「我们其他人会帮忙」。

**Becky Quick**（00:28:36）：这个问题来自布鲁克林的 Asher Haft，他说——Asher 从 2006 年起就是股东。他说他很感激你们在解释自己犯下的代价高昂的错误时的那份诚实和坦率。「在今年的董事长信里，你谈到 2016 年你在计算 Precision Castparts 未来平均盈利时犯了一个错误，导致伯克希尔为收购它付了过高的价格。看起来 Precision 的盈利在 2020 年因为疫情以及航空和旅行行业的影响而大幅下滑。你在 2016 年本可以做哪些计算，从而可能改变你收购它的决定？第二，Precision 目前面临的问题是否比疫情更大？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:29:37）：嗯，顺便说一句，犯错的不是伯克希尔，是我。（笑）不，每当我们考虑收购一家企业时，我们都在评估这门生意的竞争优势、我们必须支付的价格、我们得到的管理层，以及所有一切。我们在管理层上没有犯错。但就平均盈利能力而言，而且，你知道，当波音在 Max 上出问题时，那是一种概率。我是说，任何时候，大公司的任何客户，我是说，各种事情都可能发生。而我们已经看到其中一些事情发生了。因此，相对于平均盈利，我付得太多了。这是一家极好的公司。而且，你知道，它，我对管理层和一切都满意，而且，但 GE 需要的发动机没有我们以为的那么多。（笑）而且他们进入了电力业务，还有各种各样的东西。我们知道他们在这些业务里。但我们没有想到，这些业务会陷入近乎萧条的状态，而我们收购的其他一些业务最终有时表现比我们想的更好。

但我们会继续犯错。我是说，我不该说我们会，是我会。但即便是这些其他，

**Charlie Munger**（00:30:49）：我们其他人会帮忙。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:30:55）：而且我们会，你知道，我们有过一些很棒的交易，也有过一些很糟的交易。而好的一点是，正如我指出的，这一点其实并不完全适用于 Precision 这个案例，但当我们对一门生意失望时，它通常会在我们的业务中占越来越小的比例，就事物的自然规律而言，因为它哪儿也去不了。而当我们得到一门成功的生意，比如 GEICO 之类，GEICO 现在的业务量是我们在 19，年收购控股权时的 15 倍，它们在我们的组合中按比例变成了重要得多的部分。所以，你真的会，仅仅通过自然的力量，把更多的钱放在那些发展比你预想更好的东西上。你实际上最终会在那些成功的东西上获得更高的集中度。这不像，正如查理会说的，这不像养孩子。我是说，（笑）那个，那些不好的会给你带来更多麻烦。

（笑）但，在这件事上，在企业的孩子里，小的那些有点，顺便说一句，查理和我起步时有三家企业。伯克希尔是纺织业，Diversified Retailing 是一家百货商店，而交易印花是 Blue Chip 的业务。那就是我们拼起来的三家公司。而最初的三门生意全都失败了。这让我有点感慨，那些担心「我们难道不知道煤早晚会被淘汰吗」的人。（笑）我们当然知道煤会，你知道，但这不意味着我们早晚会被淘汰。我是说，每门生意都有一些这样需要思考的事情。

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:27）：最大的危险——招股书里有那一节，他们叫什么来着？关于——某些——

**Greg Abel**（00:32:43）：风险因素。

**Ajit Jain**（00:32:44）：风险因素。

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:45）：风险因素，对。头号风险因素，你从来不会看到它，头号风险因素是这家公司得到了错误的管理层。你让一个男人或女人来掌管它，他们很有亲和力，董事们喜欢他们。他们不知道自己在做什么，但他们知道怎么装样子。那是一家企业最大的单一危险，而且那个人留下来经营它 10 年或 15 年，还留在纺织业务或百货业务里并且扩张。（笑）而且，你知道，我看过很多企业。这就是造成头号问题的原因。而这并不是那种律师让他们列出来、他们就全列出来的东西。

**Becky Quick**（00:28:36）：This question comes from Asher Haft in Brooklyn, who says — Asher's been a shareholder since 2006. Says that he appreciates your honestly and candidness when it comes to explaining costly errors you made. "In this year's chairman letter, you discussed that you made a mistake in 2016 when calculating Precision Castpart's average amount of future earnings, which resulted in Berkshire overpaying to acquire it. "It appears that Precision's earnings declined substantially in 2020 because of the pandemic and the effect of airline and travel industry. What calculations could you have made in 2016 that might have altered your decision to acquire it? And secondly, are the problems Precision is currently facing larger than the pandemic?

**Warren Buffett**（00:29:37）：Well, Berkshire didn't make the mistake, I made the mistake, incidentally. (Laughs) No, any time we look at buying a business, we're evaluating the competitive strengths of the business, the price we have to pay, the management we get and everything. And we didn't make a mistake on the management. But in terms of the earning power, on average, and, you know — when Boeing has troubles with the Max, well, that's a probability. I mean, any time, any customers of big — I mean, all kinds of things can happen. And we have seen some of those things happen. And therefore, I paid too much, in relation to average earnings. It's a terrific company. And, you know, it's — I'm happy with the management and everything, and — But GE doesn't need as many engines as we thought they would need. (Laughs) And they get into the power business, and a variety of things. And we knew they were in the businesses. But we did not think those businesses would necessarily be in something close to a depression when other businesses are — that we bought end up, sometimes, doing better than we think. But we'll continue making mistakes. I mean — and I shouldn't say we will — I will. But even these other —

**Charlie Munger**（00:30:49）：The rest of us will help. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:30:55）：And we'll — you know, we've got some wonderful deals, and some terrible deals. And the nice thing about it is — as I pointed out, this doesn't really apply in the case of Precision, precisely — but when we're disappointed in a business, it usually becomes a smaller and smaller percentage of our business, just by the nature of things, because it isn't going anyplace. And when we get a successful business, like a GEICO or something of the sort — GEICO's doing — they're doing 15 times as much business as when we bought control in 19 — they become a proportionally much more important part of our mix. So, you really get, through just natural forces, you get more of your money in the things that have developed more favorably than you thought. You actually end up getting a greater concentration in the ones that work out. It's not like — as Charlie would say, it's not like having children. I mean — (Laughs) The one that — the bad ones cause you more problems. (Laughs) But the — in this — in the children of businesses, the small ones kind of — by the way, we started with three businesses, Charlie and I. And Berkshire was textiles, Diversified Retailing was a department store, and trading stamps were Blue Chip's business. And those were the three companies we put together. And all three of the original businesses failed. Which sort of gets me, in terms of the people that are worried about, don't we know that coal is going to be phased out over time? (Laughs) Of course, we know coal's going to be — you know, but that doesn't mean we're going to be phased out over time. I mean that — Every business has some things to think about, about that way.

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:27）：The biggest danger — they have that section in the prospectus called, what do they call that? About — certain —

**Greg Abel**（00:32:43）：Risk factors.

**Ajit Jain**（00:32:44）：Risk factors.

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:45）：Risk factors, yeah. The number one risk factor — you never see it — the number one risk factor is that this business gets the wrong management. And you get a guy or a woman in charge of it that are — they're personable, the directors like them. They don't know what they're doing, but they know how to put on an appearance. That's the biggest single danger that a business — and that that person stays and runs it for 10 or 15 years, and either stays in the textile business or department store business and expands. (Laughs) And, you know, I've looked at a lot of businesses. And that's what's caused the number one problem. And it isn't the kind of thing where they list them all because the lawyers tell them to list them.
