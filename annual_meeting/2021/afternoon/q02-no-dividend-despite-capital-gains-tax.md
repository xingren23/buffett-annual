## 问题

若资本利得税率升至 43.4%，伯克希尔会不会把资本回报方式从回购改为派息？在那种情形下，股息对股东的税务待遇会好得多。

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特明确表示，即使资本利得税率升到 43.4%，伯克希尔也不会因此改派股息。
2. 伯克希尔曾就股息提案投票，约 97% 的股份反对派息。
3. 股东群体与 REIT、MLP 不同，是 55 年里聚集起来的终身投资者，买入时并无变现打算。
4. 可口可乐式固定股息不能对几百万抱着既定预期的股东突然改变，两家公司也不会互相变成对方。
5. 股东更在意伯克希尔能否为约 1000 亿美元找到去处，其次是回购能提高他们的持股比例。
6. 税法怎么改与这一决定无关，因为绝大多数股东要的是把钱再投资。

**Becky Quick**（00:03:32）：好，这个问题来自加里·甘比诺（Gary Gambino）。他想知道：「如果资本利得税率升到 43.4%，伯克希尔会不会把资本回报政策从回购转向股息？在那种情形下，股息对股东的税务待遇会好得多。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:03:54）：对。我们确实让股东投过一次票。不过我们的股东群体和 REIT、MLP 的股东不一样。人们会选择自己进入什么样的载体。买 SPAC 的人是指望股票下周就涨，基本上是这样。我们这群人是 55 年里聚集起来的，但起点是一批把持股当作终身投资的人。如果他们想变现，他们认为到时能拿到公平的价格，但他们买的时候并没有那种打算。所以我们投了票，我记得大约 97% 的股份表示不要股息。这在其他公司不会成立。像可口可乐那样多年派发固定股息，然后突然改变政策，面对几百万抱着一种预期买入的人，试图把公司变成另一种动物，那是发疯。但可口可乐不会变成伯克希尔，伯克希尔也不会变成可口可乐。我们有不同的所有者群体，而且它会持续自我筛选，因为人们每天都有选择：你想要哪一种？在这方面伯克希尔是特定的一种动物。

所以，我们不会——即使他们调整税法，那跟这个决定其实毫无关系。我们有非常多数量的股东希望我们把钱再投资。他们更关心的是我们能不能找到用钱的地方，那 1000 亿美元左右。回购对他们有帮助——随着时间推移，他们持有伯克希尔的百分比会上升。他们当然乐见我们再买一家企业，但他们也不介意我们提高他们在现有生意中的权益比例。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（00:03:32）：OK, this comes from Gary Gambino. He wants to know "if Berkshire would switch it's capital return policy to dividends from buybacks if the capital gains rate goes up to 43.4%. Dividends would be far more taxed-advertised for shareholders under that scenario."

**Warren Buffett**（00:03:54）：Yeah. We literally did have a vote by our shareholders. Now, we've got a different group of shareholders than a REIT would have or, you know, an MLP might have. I mean there's different — people select what they go into. And people that go into SPACs are hoping the stock goes up next week, you know, I mean, basically, and — We've got a bunch of people that were assembled over 55 years, but they started with a base of people that — it was a lifetime investment. And if they wanted to cash out, they thought they'd get a fair price at that time. But they really didn't — they bought it with no intentions like that. So, we had a vote, and I think it was something like 97% or something of the shares said they don't want a dividend. And now, that wouldn't be true at other companies. And it would be crazy to be paying a regular dividend, like Coca-Cola has done for many years, and then, all of a sudden, change the policy on millions of people who had bought it with one expectation in mind, and try and change it into a different animal. But Coca-Cola isn't going to change to Berkshire, and Berkshire isn't going to change to Coca-Cola. We've got a different group of owners. And it will keep self-selecting, because people have a choice every day: which do you want — sort of thing do you want to be in? And Berkshire is a certain kind of animal in that respect. So, we will not — if they jig around the tax laws, I mean, that's really got nothing to do with the decision. I mean we've got a very substantial majority of people that want us to reinvest the money. And what they're more concerned about is whether we find something to do with the money, the hundred billion or something. And repurchasing shares is something that helps them in their — they own a larger percentage of Berkshire as they go along. And they'd love to see us buy another business. But they don't mind us intensifying their interest in the present business.
