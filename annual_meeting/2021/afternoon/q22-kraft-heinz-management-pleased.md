## 问题

一位苏格兰股东想知道，Kraft Heinz 过去 12 个月的表现与疫情前那段令人失望的业绩相比如何，以及伯克希尔对这家公司的短期和长期前景怎么看。问题同时问巴菲特、芒格和阿贝尔三人。

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特称伯克希尔与 3G 当年从 Heinz 这一笔交易起步，建立了「半正式」的合伙关系，后来才与伙伴共同收购 Kraft；伯克希尔按约定只做财务合伙人，运营由对方主导。
2. 阿贝尔同时是 Kraft Heinz 董事，他表示公司请到米格尔·帕特里西奥担任 CEO，也搭好了管理团队，管理层正专注执行、理顺资本结构并压降债务。
3. 巴菲特强调 Kraft Heinz 的股票值多少要由别人评估，伯克希尔不提供建议，只补了一句「我们对……感觉好一些了」。
4. 芒格引英国演员塞德里克·哈德威克的话——「我当大演员当得太久了，久到不知道自己真正想的是什么」——说明 CEO 反复对外讲同一套说法，久而久之连自己都信了。
5. 巴菲特认为这类「公司神话」由一任 CEO 传给下一任，下属不会反驳，会酿成巨大错误，他打算将来在年报里专门写这个题目。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:01:10）：这个问题来自一位苏格兰股东，他想知道沃伦、查理和格雷格怎么看 Kraft Heinz 过去 12 个月的表现——和疫情前那段令人失望的业绩相比。另外，你们对 Kraft Heinz 的前景，眼下和更长期分别怎么看？

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:38）：嗯，格雷格是董事会成员。所以他——（笑）我不确定我们有资格对 Kraft Heinz 提建议。你知道，很多年前，那还只是 Heinz 这一笔交易的时候，我们和 3G 实际上建立了一种半正式的合伙关系，后来又和伙伴们一起收购了 Kraft。他们该担的那份责任都担起来了。我们也做了当初说好要做的事，就是当财务合伙人。他们更像是运营合伙人，或者说我们在重大决策上有一定程度的参与，他们会听我们的意见。但就 Kraft Heinz 的股票而言，我们不做任何——那得由别人去评估。

**Greg Abel**（01:02:26）：是的。沃伦，我唯一想补充的是，他们安排了米格尔（帕特里西奥）这样一位强有力的管理者担任 CEO，这一点让我们很放心。他也在 Kraft Heinz 组建了一支很好的团队。所以，我们对现有的领导层和管理团队很满意。他们非常专注于执行，专注于把资本结构理顺、把债务结构降下来。所以，对现有团队带领下的前进路径，我们非常满意——

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:55）：是的，我们对——感觉好一些了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:59）：其中一点，这是个更宽泛的话题，不过，其中一个话题，我甚至可能在将来的某份年报里写一写，就是人们对自己所在组织的种种神话所造成的问题。我在各种形态下见过太多次了。某种程度上，这个问题在过去二三十年里变得更突出了，因为 CEO 常常，和投资者关系部门一起，他们说，嗯，我们必须和分析师群体保持持续沟通。于是，他们每隔两个月就出来一次，重复一些关于自己公司的说法，这些说法就变成了某种教义问答。而 CEO 说了某件事两个月之后，没人会站出来说，其实根本不是那么回事。（笑）他们不会自我否定，也不会改变方向。所以，一旦这些神话形成，它可能以很多不同方式出现。我可以举很多例子，但我不会举。就像我跟美国企业界的朋友们说的，我真的不会去揭发他们。

（笑）但有很多神话是从一任 CEO 传给下一任的。接任的 CEO 能说，选他的那个人走错了路，或者说，他一直在跟我们讲的东西其实并不完全真实吗？他说不出口。然后他就开始重复这些话。这会带来巨大的错误。但不举例子就很难把这件事讲清楚，而我不喜欢举例子。（笑）所以，等我哪天写出来再看吧。查理，你大概有些想法。我们，他在很多场合都有前排座位，他待过一些我没待过的董事会，我是说。而且这不只限于商业，还延伸到教育，延伸到，嗯，很多领域。

**Charlie Munger**（01:04:55）：真正有意思的是，你老是这样喋喋不休，就算说错了也照样往里灌。所以，人类言论史上我最喜欢的一句话之一，出自塞德里克·哈德威克爵士，他是位了不起的英国演员。他说：「我当大演员当得太久了，久到我已经不知道自己对着任何题目真正想的是什么。」我觉得很多人都这样。几乎每个政客都这样。

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:31）：而且它会嵌进公司的——

**Charlie Munger**（01:05:32）：嵌进去，诸如此类——

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:34）：而现在麻烦的是，CEO 们讲话太频繁了。所以，如果他们对自家公司说了什么离谱的话，还反复重复，下属是不会去反驳的。而且——就像查理刚说的，过一阵子他们自己就信了。这很危险。

**Charlie Munger**（01:05:52）：是啊。当然，年轻人受了通识教育之后就有了这些想法，还以为上帝直接给了他们洞见。他们和政客一样疯。

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:57）：是啊，有些老人也这样。

**Charlie Munger**（01:06:01）：是啊，嗯——（笑声）不过老人已经疯了。可是——

**Warren Buffett**（01:06:12）：他们死得早一些，所以——

**Charlie Munger**（01:06:14）：我们有我们那些老的疯病。新的疯病，归年轻人。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（01:01:10）：This question comes from a shareholder in Scotland who wants to know Warren, Charlie, and Greg's views on how Kraft Heinz has performed over the last 12 months compared to the disappointing performance pre-COVID. And what are your current and longer-term views on Kraft Heinz' prospects?

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:38）：Well, I think that Greg's on the board. So, he — (laughs) I don't know that we're in a position to give advice on Kraft Heinz. You know, we entered a, in effect, a semi-formal partnership with 3G many years ago when it was just the Heinz deal, and then went on to acquire Kraft with our partners. And they've done more than — they hold up their share of things, you know. And we do what we said we'd do going in, which is to be a financial partner. And they're more of the operating partner, or we participate, to a degree, in any big decisions, and that they would listen to us. But we're not making any — in terms of Kraft Heinz stock — that's up to somebody else to evaluate.

**Greg Abel**（01:02:26）：Yeah. The only thing I would add, Warren, is I think we're very comfortable with the fact that they put a strong manager in place in (CEO) Miguel (Patricio). And he's put a very good team in place at Kraft Heinz. So, we're pleased with the leadership and management team in place. They're very focused on how they're executing as they've gone forward and rationalizing their capital structure and managing down their debt structure. So, very pleased with the path forward with the existing team —

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:55）：Yeah, we feel better about the —

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:59）：One of the — this is a more general subject, but — One of the subjects— I might even write about it in one of the future annual reports — is the problems caused by the myths that people have about their own organization. And I've seen that so many times in various forms. And to some extent, the problem has become accentuated in the last 20 or 30 years because the CEO often — and works with the investor relations (unintelligible) — and they say well, we have to have constant contact with the analyst community. And of course — so they go on every couple of months, and they repeat certain things about their company, and it becomes part of, sort of, the catechism. And nobody's going to go on two months after the CEO has said one thing and say, well, actually that really isn't the way. (Laughs) They're not going to contradict themselves or change course. And so, if you get these myths — and they can occur in a lot of different ways. I could give a lot of examples, which I won't do. As I tell my friends in corporate America, I'm really not going to squeal on them. (Laughs) But there's a lot of mythology that gets handed down from one CEO to the next. Can the succeeding CEO say the guy that picked him, you know, was on the wrong course, or, he's been telling us something that isn't really quite true. He can't do it. You know, and then he starts repeating it. And it leads to enormous errors. But it's hard to tell the story without giving examples, and I don't like to give examples. (Laughs) So, we'll see when I write about it sometime. Charlie, you've probably got some thoughts. We've — He's been – he's had a ringside seat at a lot of — he's been on boards that I haven't been on, I mean. And it doesn't just extend to business. It goes beyond that into education, into — well, a lot of areas.

**Charlie Munger**（01:04:55）：What's really interesting, is the way you prattle out all the time, you're pounding back in even if it's wrong. And so, one of my favorite remarks in the history of human remarks was by Sir Cedrick Hardwicke who was a great British actor. And he said, "I have been a great actor for so long, that I no longer know what I truly think on any subject." And I think that happens to a lot of people. And it happens to virtually every politician.

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:31）：And it gets embedded in corporate —

**Charlie Munger**（01:05:32）：Gets embedded and so on —

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:34）：And the trouble is now, the CEOs speak out so often. So, if they've got some crazy thing that they're saying about their company and they keep repeating it, the subordinates aren't going to contradict it. The — and as Charlie just said, they just believe it after a while. And it's dangerous.

**Charlie Munger**（01:05:52）：Yeah. And of course, the young people get these ideas after their liberal educations and think that God has given them direct insights. And they're just as crazy as the politicians.

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:57）：Yeah, there's some old people that have them, too.

**Charlie Munger**（01:06:01）：Yeah, well — (Laughter) But the old people are already crazy. But —

**Warren Buffett**（01:06:12）：They're going to die sooner, so —

**Charlie Munger**（01:06:14）：We have our old insanities. The new insanities, the young get. (Laughter)
