## 问题

你在《所有者手册》里写，去世时无需卖出股票就能支付现金遗赠和税款。拜登提出未实现收益在死亡时视同出售、按 43.4% 征税，这会改变届时必须卖出的股票数量吗？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特承诺，去世时名下 99.7% 的财产要么流向慈善，要么流向联邦政府，规则由政府定。
2. 他宁愿钱去慈善：由聪明的公益人士使用，比把联邦债务减少 1000 亿美元能产生更大效用。
3. 他认为国债高低 1000 亿美元对世界毫无区别，当下政府借款也几乎不花成本。
4. 芒格打趣「我保证那不会让你不安」，暗指届时巴菲特已无从感知。
5. 即便美国民主决定全部征走，巴菲特说也不会让他不安，但他认为不该这么做。
6. 他强调这是个人看法而非公共政策主张，最终应由美国人民通过代表决定。

## 关键引述

> **“99.7% of what I have when I die will either go to philanthropy or to the federal government”**

**Becky Quick**（00:12:11）：最后一个税务问题。来自威廉·巴纳德（William Barnard），他说：「沃伦，在年报的《所有者手册》部分，你写道：『我去世时，无需卖出任何股票，就能支付我已经做出的现金遗赠和税款。』」他问：「拜登最近的提案主张，未实现收益在死亡时视同出售、按 43.4% 的税率征税，这会改变你去世时为缴税而必须卖出的股票数量吗？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:12:49）：对。税法明天就可能改，而且可以有很多种改法，过去也确实用过很多种改法。我可以告诉你，我实际上可以向社会作出承诺：我去世时，我所拥有的 99.7% 要么流向慈善，要么流向联邦政府。规则由联邦政府来定。不，我宁愿它流向慈善。我认为，如果它由慈善领域一些聪明人来运用，比它仅仅在我去世时把联邦债务减少个 1000 亿美元左右，能产生更大的效用。我不认为联邦债务高 1000 亿还是低 1000 亿有任何一点区别（笑）。它不会改变世界上的任何事。而且现在这其实也帮不了他们什么，因为他们可以借 1000 亿美元，反正也不花他们什么成本。但那种债务状况不会一直持续。不过我不会，我不认为，我只是在谈个人看法，并不是在倡导这项公共政策。

但我不会，如果他们全部拿走，那也不会让我不安。我是说，

**Charlie Munger**（00:13:43）：我保证那不会让你不安。

**Warren Buffett**（00:13:45）：对！（笑声）查理是说：「你根本不会知道了。」（笑声）你知道，如果美国民主决定全部拿走更好，我不认为他们会这么做，我也不认为他们应该这么做，但即便如此，那又怎么样呢？（笑）我希望这笔钱被用来为人类做出最大的贡献。也就是说，由聪明、动机正当的人，更重要的是，动机不正当的人不要，来分配它，方式嘛，谁知道 10 年、20 年、30 年或 40 年后会是什么样？我确实知道，如果交给政府，基本上就是把这笔国债减掉相应的数额。我不认为这会改变他们是否修改最低工资法，或者做别的事（笑）。我只是觉得那个小数字变了：某一天预算里会显示「收到巴菲特款项 X」，然后（笑）下面出现一个巨大的数字。我不认为它真的，所以我宁愿它被私人使用。

但这最终要由美国人民通过他们的代表来决定。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（00:12:11）：One last tax question. This one comes from William Barnard, who says, "In the Owner's Manual, a portion of your annual report, Warren, you state, 'On my death, none of my stock will have to be sold to take care of the cash bequests I've made or for taxes.' "Would the recent Biden proposal to treat unrealized gains as sold and taxable at death at a 43.4% rate change the amount of stock required to be sold for payment of taxes upon your death?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:12:49）：Yeah. Well, the tax law can be changed tomorrow. And I don't — you know, it can be done a lot of different ways. And it's been done a lot of different ways in the past. I can tell you — I can actually make a promise to society, that 99.7% of what I have when I die will either go to philanthropy or to the federal government. And the federal government can actually determine the rules on that. And no, I would prefer that it would go to philanthropy. I think it actually will accomplish more utility if it goes to be used by some smart people in philanthropy than if it simply reduces the federal debt by a hundred billion dollars or something when I die. I don't think it makes a damn bit of difference (laughs) you know, if the federal debt is 100 billion higher or lower. It won't change anything in the world. And in present days, it doesn't really save them anything, because they can borrow a hundred billion, and it doesn't cost them anything anyway. But that debt condition won't prevail. But I don't — I would not regard — I'm just talking personally, I'm not really advocating as this public policy. But I wouldn't — if they took it all, you know, it would not bother me. I mean it —

**Charlie Munger**（00:13:43）：I guarantee it won't bother you.

**Warren Buffett**（00:13:45）：Yeah! (Laughter) Charlie says, "You won't know." (Laughter) You know, if you decide — if American democracy decides that it's better to take it all — which I don't think they will, and I don't think they should — but nevertheless, you know, so what, you know? (Laughs) I would like to see it used to accomplish the most for humanity. I mean, and that means having smart people, properly motivated — and more importantly, not improperly motivated — distribute it in a way — and who knows what the hell it would be, 10, 20, 30, or 40 years from now? I do know, if it goes to government, it basically reduces the national debt by that amount. I don't think it changes whether they change the minimum wage laws or does anything else. (Laughs) I just think that little figure changes. It'll be — you know, it'll show up in the budget one day, you know, received from Buffett, you know, X, and then (laughs) some huge figure appears down below. I don't think it really — so I would prefer it be used privately. But that's really up to the people in the United States to decide through their representatives.
