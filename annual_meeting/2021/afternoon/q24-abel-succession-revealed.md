## 问题

股东提到，讨论伯克希尔时大家总聚焦保险业务和 2019 年被称为「红杉」的大型非保险企业，而大量子公司从未被提及。他问伯克希尔是否会大到难以管理，以及投资者是否该为多数子公司信息不足而担心。

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特承认伯克希尔确实大到做不了某些事，比如无法把时间花在寻找 1 亿美元的收购上；但他说 TTI、Forest River 这类公司自己从没实地去过，也照样理解。
2. 他以 TTI 的保罗·安德鲁斯为例：15 年前买下，期间盈利增至原来的八倍，本人却从不要求加薪，只说「明年再谈」。
3. 芒格说伯克希尔与美国几乎所有大公司都不同，分权得「太过头」，子公司权限极大，只要这套办法有效就能长期维持，可偏偏没人效仿。
4. 巴菲特强调分权必须搭配正确文化：若高层只想在未来五年为自己捞一大笔钱，这套体系就行不通。
5. 芒格在答问中顺口说出「格雷格会把这种文化保持下去」，等于提前透露格雷格·阿贝尔是接班人；巴菲特随后打趣「你不会知道的」。
6. 芒格还以罗马帝国因分权而维持长久作类比。

## 关键引述

> **“we are so excessively decentralized”**
>
> 「我们分权得太过头了。」

> **“and Greg will keep the culture”**
>
> 「格雷格会把这种文化保持下去。」

> **“the Roman Empire worked as long as it did because it was so decentralized”**
>
> 「罗马帝国能维持那么久，就是因为它分权得厉害。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:11:49）：这个问题来自塔尔萨的 Mark Blakley。他说：「这是问沃伦和查理的。我们讨论伯克希尔时，常常聚焦于保险业务和最大的那几家非保险企业，也就是你们 2019 年提到的那些「红杉」。然而，伯克希尔拥有大量子公司，其中大多数从未被提及。是否存在某个节点，伯克希尔会大到难以管理？对于伯克希尔大多数公司信息匮乏，我们是否应该有所担心？会不会有一天，伯克希尔变得过于庞大和复杂？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:12:28）：嗯，要说做某些事，它确实太大了，这一点毫无疑问。你知道，我们不能把时间花在寻找 1 亿美元的收购上，不过，我们有两家，福特沃斯有一家很棒的公司（TTI）。当时有一位了不起的人（保罗·安德鲁斯）在经营它，他前不久去世了。他经营着它，15 年前他把公司卖给我，基本上还是他在管。我到现在都找不到去那家公司的路。我们还有一家很棒的房车制造公司（Forest River），在印第安纳州的埃尔克哈特。我们 15 年前买下它。我从没去过，你知道。说不定有个家伙躲在柜子里，每个月编些数字发给我。但我觉得自己对这门生意理解得挺好。可我从来没亲眼见过它。经营它的那位（彼得·利格尔）喜欢经营它。他也喜欢我别去插手。

他让格雷格（阿贝尔）插手得比我多一点，因为，（笑）但这，我们这套体系，配上出色的企业和出色的管理者，是行得通的。找到他们是我们的责任。找到之后把它们培育好，同样是我们的责任。如果你遇到像保罗·安德鲁斯这样的人，他经营 TTI，把它从一无所有、完完全全的零做起，却没人听说过他，而在替我们经营的这段时间里，盈利增至原来的八倍。他很开心。员工很开心。他是个了不起的人。我们也很开心。我年底会给他打电话，说，保罗，你知道，这地方，你干得风生水起，你该加薪了，或者说奖金，他说：「这个我们明年再谈吧，沃伦。」我是说，他就是热爱，他热爱那门生意。我爱伯克希尔。他爱那门生意。我让他填一堆报表，报告他用了多少碳之类（笑），那也不会给他增加任何价值。

你知道，想想保罗·安德鲁斯这样的人会做出反社会的事（笑）或者诸如此类，那才荒唐。我们很想再多一些这样的人。显然，我们规模越大，这样的公司就越难买到。但我们已经有了不少。而且我认为，长期来看，我们买的最后一家还没出现。不过近期我确实看不到任何机会。我们通过回购股份，对已有的公司兴趣稍微更浓了一些。所以，股东们每年都拥有这些公司更多一点，只要我们，假设我们在回购股份，而这取决于价格。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:18）：是啊。我不认为我们大到管不了了，因为我们和美国几乎所有其他大公司都不同：我们分权得太过头了。我们放权放到这种程度，子公司握有这么多权限，所以只要这套办法还有效，我们就能这么干很久很久。而且我要说，到目前为止，我们的分权带来的好处多于缺陷。但好像没人学我们。

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:51）：嗯，这完全属实。不过我要说一句：除非有相应的正确文化相配，分权是行不通的（笑）。

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:57）：是啊，可我们有。

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:58）：对，我们有。不过——

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:59）：但格雷格——

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:00）：——而且它依赖于此。我是说——

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:03）：而格雷格会——格雷格会把这种文化保持下去。

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:06）：如果我们有——如果我们的文化是高层的人想在未来五年里给自己赚一大笔钱，那这套就行不通。

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:19）：不，当然不行。文化是其中一部分。但假设我们保持住这种文化，它还能走很远。

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:25）：很长很长一段时间。

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:27）：很长很长一段时间。我想这会让所有人大吃一惊。顺便说一句——

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:25）：查理（听不清）——

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:29）：——罗马帝国能维持那么久，就是因为它分权得厉害。

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:35）：就像查理对我说的，你不会知道的。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（01:11:49）：This question comes in from Mark Blakley in Tulsa. "This is for Warren and Charlie. When we discuss Berkshire, we often focus on the insurance operations and the largest non-insurance businesses, the "Redwoods," as you mentioned in 2019. "However, Berkshire owns a large number of subsidiary businesses, most of which are never mentioned. Is there a point at which Berkshire becomes too large to manage? And should we have any concern over the lack of information for most of Berkshire's companies? Is there a time that could come when Berkshire's too large and complex?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:12:28）：Well, it's too large to do certain things, that's for sure. I mean, you know, it's not — we can't spend our time looking for a hundred million-dollar acquisitions, but — We have a couple — a wonderful company (TTI) in Fort Worth. And we had a marvelous man running it (Paul Andrews), and he died recently. But he ran it — he sold it to me 15 years ago, and he just basically ran it, you know. And I couldn't find my way to the company. We've got this terrific company that makes recreational vehicles (Forest River), the Elkhart — based in Elkhart, Indiana. And we bought it 15 years ago. I've never been there, you know. Maybe there's some guy in a closet just making up numbers to send to me every month. But I feel I understand the business pretty well. But I've never seen it. And the fellow that runs it (Peter Liegl) likes running it. And he likes me keeping my nose out of it. And he'll let Greg (Abel) in a little more than he'll let me because — (laughs) But it's — we've got a system that will work with wonderful businesses and wonderful managers. And it's up to us to find them. But it's also (up to) us to nurture them when we find them. And if you get somebody like Paul Andrews, who ran TTI, and who built it from nothing, absolutely nothing, and nobody ever heard of him, and the earnings have octupled during the period that he ran it for us. And he was happy. The employees were happy. He was a wonderful man. We were happy. And I would call him at the end of the year, and I'd say, Paul, you know, this place is — you're shooting the lights out and everything, and you should take a raise — and he said — or bonus — he's say, "Well, we'll talk about that next year, Warren." I mean, he just loved — he loved the business. I love Berkshire. He loved the business. And I wasn't going to add anything by having him fill out a bunch of reports about (laughs) how much he's using in the way of carbon or anything. You know, it's just — it's ridiculous to think of a guy like Paul Andrews behaving in an antisocial manner (laughs) or anything of the sort. And we'd love to have more of those. And obviously, if we get bigger, they get harder to buy. But we've got a number in the place. And I don't think we've bought our last one, over time. But I certainly don't see anything in the near future at all. But we're intensifying our interest a little bit in the ones we have by repurchasing shares. So, our shareholders own more of those companies every year while we're — assuming we're repurchasing shares, which is price-sensitive. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:18）：Yeah. I don't think we're getting too big to manage, because we're different from practically every other big corporation in the United States, in that we are so excessively decentralized. We have decentralized so much, and we have so much authority in the subsidiaries, that we can keep doing it for a long, long time, as long as it keeps working. And I would say, so far, that our decentralization has caused more benefits than defects. But nobody seems to copy us.

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:51）：Well, but that's absolutely true. But I would say this. Decentralization won't work (laughs) unless you have the right kind of culture accompanying it.

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:57）：Yeah, but we do.

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:58）：Yeah, we do. But —

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:59）：But Greg is —

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:00）：— and it's dependent on it. I mean —

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:03）：And Greg will — and Greg will keep the culture.

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:06）：If we'd had the — if we had a culture of people who were trying to make a lot of money for themselves in the next five years at the top, it would not have worked.

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:19）：No, of course not. And the culture is part of it. But assuming we keep the culture, it can go on quite a ways.

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:25）：For a long, long time.

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:27）：Long, long time. I think it may amaze everybody. And by the way —

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:25）：Charlie (unintelligible) —

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:29）：—the Roman Empire worked as long as it did because it was so decentralized.

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:35）：As Charlie says to me, you won't know. (Laughs)
