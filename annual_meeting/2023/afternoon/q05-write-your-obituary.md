## 问题

这位第四次现场参会的 15 岁股东引述今年的致股东信：伯克希尔的历程由「持续的储蓄、掌控的力量、美国顺风，以及对重大错误的回避」构成。他说两位过去都谦逊地承认犯过许多错误，但「重大错误」这个说法第一次让他留意。他问：在投资和生活中，应该避免哪些重大错误？他也想听芒格的看法。

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特的第一条建议不是投资而是人生：先写下自己的讣告，再想办法活成配得上它的样子，并说这件事会随年岁增长越想越明白。
2. 生意上的唯一硬要求是别犯把自己淘汰出局的错误——不该有哪个晚上为投资担心（前提是还有钱可投）。
3. 理财上花得略少于挣得：房贷可作例外，但信用卡 12%—14% 的利息意味着你必须稳定赚到同样高的回报，他直言「何必让自己陷进去」。
4. 他引汤姆·墨菲两句忠告：「你总可以等到明天再叫某人下地狱」——在今天，30 秒的冲动无法抹去；以及「赞美我的名字，批评我的类别」。
5. 他说从未见过本质上善良的人死时没有朋友，却见过不少有钱人死时连家人朋友都没有；墨菲 50 年间没做过一件不友善的事。
6. 芒格把处方压缩成：花得比挣得少、精明投资、远离有毒的人和有毒的活动、终生学习、大量延迟满足；做到几乎必然成功，做不到就需要很多运气。

## 关键引述

> **“you should write your obituary and then try and figure out how to live up to it”**

**Warren Buffett**（00:38:37）：好，第四号提问站？

**Audience Member**（00:38:38）：下午好，巴菲特先生、芒格先生。我叫 J.C.，15 岁，来自俄亥俄州。这是我第四次现场参加伯克希尔股东大会。我非常热衷于从你们的演讲、访谈和文章中学习。感谢你们一直分享智慧。巴菲特先生，在今年的年度致股东信中，您说伯克希尔的历程由「持续的储蓄、掌控的力量、美国顺风，以及对重大错误的回避」构成。您过去也谦逊地承认自己犯过很多错误。但这是「重大错误」这个说法第一次让我注意到。能否请您给我们一些建议：在投资和生活中，我们应该避免哪些重大错误？我也很想听听芒格先生的想法，谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（00:39:37）：嗯，主要的，（掌声）查理说过，你能犯的重大错误，你知道，如果你在美国，你是幸运的。你走遍世界，有些地方你没有多少选择。但你应该先写好你的讣告，然后再想办法怎样活才配得上它。你知道，这一点你会随着年岁增长越来越明白。生意上的错误，你只要确保不要犯任何会让你出局、或者几乎让你出局的错误。你不应该有一个晚上为投资而担心，我的意思是，前提是你还有钱可投。你应该花得比挣得少一点。你也可以花得比挣得多一点，然后你就有了债务，而多半你永远走不出债务。我会把你的房子按揭作为例外。但信用卡债务，我们在大规模做信用卡生意，而且会继续做，何必让自己陷进去？如果你实际上在为一笔信用卡付 12% 或 14% 或不管多少的利息，你知道，你等于在说：「我要让自己的钱赚到超过 12% 或 14% 的回报。

」如果你能做到，来伯克希尔·哈撒韦吧。所以，查理在旁边我不好意思这么说，但这直接出自本·富兰克林。（笑）也没那么复杂。不过，嗯，我给你们讲几课。你知道，汤姆·墨菲，我第一次见他时，他对我说了两件事。他说：「你总可以等到明天再叫某人下地狱。」嗯，这在当时是很好的建议。你想想，当你可以坐下来对着电脑，30 秒内告诉某人下地狱或者别的什么，就把自己的人生永久毁掉，这建议有多好。而你无法抹去。你知道，那个选项并没有失去。他还说：「赞美我的名字，批评我的类别。」还有什么比这更有道理？我的意思是，你喜欢那种整天批评你的人吗？而且在讨论问题时，你不需要为了说明观点去污蔑任何人。还有另一条普遍的建议：我从未见过一个本质上善良的人死时没有朋友。

而我见过很多有钱人死时没有朋友，包括没有家人。但我从没见过，你知道，我见过一些人，包括老汤姆·墨菲，也许小汤姆也在这儿，（笑）但肯定是他父亲，我观察了他 50 年，我从没见过他做过一件不友善的事。我也没见他做过多少蠢事。我的意思是，并不是他没有辨别力，他只是认定没有理由那么做。哇，这在人生中带来多大的不同。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:03）：嗯，花得比挣得少、精明地投资、远离有毒的人和有毒的活动、终生坚持学习，等等等等，这些简单得很，并且大量延迟满足，因为你自己更喜欢那样的人生。如果你做到所有这些，你几乎必然会成功。如果你不做，你就需要很多运气。而你不想需要很多运气。你想进入一场你很有可能赢、而且不需要任何异常运气的游戏。

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:44）：我还要再加一点：你需要知道别人是怎么操纵他人的，同时你需要抵制自己这么做的诱惑。

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:55）：哦对，那些试图骗你、对你说谎、或者在履行承诺上不可靠的有毒的人。人生一大教训就是：把他们赶出你的生活。

**Warren Buffett**（00:44:10）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:11）：而且要快。（掌声）要快。

**Warren Buffett**（00:44:17）：我还要补充，查理会完全同意我：如果可能，也要做得圆融一些。（笑）但一定要把他们从你的生活里清出去。

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:30）：是的。对。我不介意一点圆融。（笑声）甚至不介意一点财务上的代价。但问题是把他们赶出你的生活。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:38:37）：OK, station four?

**Audience Member**（00:38:38）：Good afternoon Mr. Buffett, Mr. Munger. My name is J.C. I am 15 years old and I'm from Ohio. This is my fourth in-person Berkshire meeting. I have a lot of passion learning from your speeches, interviews, and articles. Thank you for sharing your wisdom all the time. Mr. Buffett, in your annual shareholder letter this year, you said that Berkshire's journey consisted of "continuous savings, the power of commanding, the American tailwind, and avoidance of major mistakes". You have humbly admitted in the past that you have made many mistakes. But this is the first time that major mistakes stood out to me. Could you please advise us on what major mistakes we should avoid in both investing and in life? I would also like to have Mr. Munger's thoughts too, please. Thank you very much.

**Warren Buffett**（00:39:37）：Well, the main, (APPLAUSE) Charlie said the major mistake you could make, you know, you're lucky if you're in the United States. If you go around the world you don't have a lot of choices in some places. But you should write your obituary and then try and figure out how to live up to it. And, you know, that's something you get wiser on as you go along. The business mistakes, you just want to make sure you don't make any mistakes that take you out of the game or come close to taking you out of your game. You should never have a night when you're worried about investing, I mean, assuming you have any money to invest at all. And you should just spend a little bit less than you earn. And you can spend a little bit more than you earn, and then you've got debt, and the chances are you'll never get out of debt. I'll make an exception in terms of a mortgage on your house. But credit card debt, and we're in the credit card business big time, and we'll stay in the credit card business, but why get behind the game? And if you're effectively paying 12% or 14% or whatever percent you're paying on a credit card, you know, you're saying, "I'm going to earn more than 12% or 14% of my money." And if you can do that, come to Berkshire Hathaway. So, it's, I hate to say this when Charlie's around me, but it's straight out of Ben Franklin. I mean, (LAUGH) and it's not that complicated. But you, well, I'll give you a couple lessons. You know, Tom Murphy, the first time I met him, said two things to me. He said, "You can always tell someone to go to hell tomorrow." Well, that was great advice then. And think of what great advice it is when you can sit down at a computer and screw your life up forever by telling somebody to go to hell, or something else, in 30 seconds. And you can't erase it. And, you know, haven't lost the option. And he said, you know, "Praise my name, criticize my category." Well, what makes more sense than that? I mean, who do you like that criticizes you all the time? And you don't need to vilify anybody to make your point on subjects of discussion. And then the other general piece of advice, I've never known anybody that was basically kind that died without friends. And I've known plenty of people with money that have died without friends, including their family. But I've never known anybody, and you know, I've seen a few people, including Tom Murphy Sr. and maybe Jr., who's here, (LAUGH) but certainly his dad, I never saw him, I watched him for 50 years, I never saw him do an unkind act. I didn't see him do very many stupid acts either. I mean, it wasn't that he was non-discriminating, he just decided that there was no reason to do it. And wow, what a difference that makes in life. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:03）：Well, it's so simple to spend less than you earn, and invest shrewdly, and avoid toxic people and toxic activities, and try and keep learning all your life, et cetera, et cetera, and do a lot of deferred gratification because you prefer life that way. And if you do all those things, you are almost certain to succeed. And if you don't, you're going to need a lot of luck. And you don't want to need a lot of luck. You want to go into a game where you're very likely to win without having any unusual luck.

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:44）：I'd add one more thought too, you need to know how people can manipulate other people, and you need to resist the temptation to do it yourself.

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:55）：Oh yes, the toxic people who are trying to fool you or lie to you or aren't reliable in meeting their commitments. A great lesson of life is get them the hell out of your life.

**Warren Buffett**（00:44:10）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:11）：And do it fast. (APPLAUSE) Do it fast.

**Warren Buffett**（00:44:17）：And I would add, Charlie would totally agree with me, do it tactfully, if possible, too. (LAUGH) But do get them out of your life.

**Charlie Munger**（00:44:30）：Yes. Yeah, I don't mind a little tact. (LAUGHTER) Or even a little financial cost. But the question is getting them the hell out of your life.
