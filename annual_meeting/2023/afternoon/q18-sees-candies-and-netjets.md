## 问题

这位股东说，已经很久没有听到 See's Candy 和 NetJets 的消息了。他请两位介绍 See's 的业绩，并预测它在美国还有多久会找不到可以开新店的地方。他同时请两位概述 NetJets 自被收购以来的表现，以及它是否实现了收购当时你们看到的潜力。

## 回答要点

1. See's 的问题从来不是开店，而是巴菲特所说的「这个了不起的品牌不走远」：它的魔力基本只覆盖加州与邻近的西部，像引力一样，一进入东部就衰减。
2. 他给出消费者的地理差异：东部人偏好黑巧克力、西部人偏好牛奶巧克力；东部能卖迷你装，西部不行。这类现象他称「疯狂」，只能持续观察，因为总能学到一点东西。
3. 加州的经济性太好，使伯克希尔反复做试验——试验成本不高，每位新来的经理也都想试；结果总是头一周以为对了，随后发现魔力无法移植。See's 如今 101 岁，而糖果店这一业态本身已所剩无几。
4. NetJets 服务的是必须非常富有才用得起的人，实际上花的是继承人的钱；巴菲特用 1968 或 1969 年劝独身的 Alice 姑妈买皮草大衣作类比——「买的不是你，是你的侄甥」。
5. 规模上，机队约 600 架，计入欧洲约 650 架，今年将买入 100 架而一架不卖，因为订单已经积压；他把 NetJets 比作法拉利——法拉利年销 1.1 万至 1.2 万辆，却全球闻名，而美国去年销量约 1400 万辆。
6. 他提到竞争对手 Wheels Up：两年前以 10 美元上市，日前只卖 48 美分，1.26 万名客户在预付卡上投入逾 10 亿美元，他认为其中一些人日后会非常失望。芒格则说，NetJets 现在可以说与任何一家航空公司等值。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:38）：好，贝基？

**Becky Quick**（01:49:39）：这里有来自西澳大利亚州珀斯的 Simon Withers 的一个问题。「我们已经很久没有听到 See's Candy 和 NetJets 的消息了。能否请你们介绍一下 See's 的业绩，并预测它在美国还有多久会找不到可以开新店的地方？另外，能否也请概述一下 NetJets 自被收购以来的表现，以及它是否实现了你们在收购当时看到的潜力？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:16）：嗯，就 See's 而言，问题从来不是开店。我们发现，我们拥有这个了不起的品牌，但它不走远。你知道？那种神秘感、实际的产品、人们对某些东西的感受，就像我们之前说过的，有时候它局限于特定的市场。Dr Pepper 在达拉斯—沃斯堡卖得极多，人均渗透率也许是底特律或波士顿的 10 倍。你会说：「一个已经存在了一个世纪的产品，怎么会这样？」而人是会流动的。你有全国性的广告。我也不确定。但我在观察不同品牌的过程中，不断学到更多。查理和我，我们在加州的经济性太好了，以至于我们在许多情况下一次又一次地做同样的实验。做实验花不了多少钱。我们试过世上的一切办法，想让一个品牌走远。头一周我们总以为自己是对的。然后我们发现那种魔力——我们几乎可以打败任何一家糖果店。

但世界已经变了，如今已经没有糖果店可谈了。所以，See's 现在 101 岁了。它有魔力。而它的魔力在邻近的西部是有限的。几乎像引力一样。然后你到了东部。顺便说，在东部，人们喜欢黑巧克力胜过牛奶巧克力。在西部，人们喜欢牛奶巧克力胜过黑巧克力。在东部，你可以卖迷你装，还有黑巧——在西部——我是说，这世上有各种各样消费者做出的疯狂事情。但你要持续观察，因为你确实能学到一点东西。而对查理和我来说，因为一旦成功经济性太好，所以总有一种不断尝试的诱惑，我们试过各种东西。当然，每一位新来的经理也都想试。因为他们学到的是这应该行得通。但它行不通。所以，但这正是它非常有意思的地方。

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:38）：至于 NetJets，我们确实学会了如何区分、并且有理由地区分一项只有非常富的人才用得起的服务。但如果你非常富，实际上你花的是继承人的钱。我是说，这就是我在 Alice 姑妈从教书变成身家数百万美元之后跟她说的。她来看我。她从未结婚。她在 1968 或 1969 年说：「我买得起这件皮草大衣吗？」我说：「Alice，买的不是你，是你的侄甥们，因为你的钱就是要留给他们的。代表你的侄甥们说话，我要说我们希望你去买。」同样道理。你的继承人希望你坐着那架喷气机到处飞吗？还是希望你多留一点钱给基金会或孩子？解决这个问题的办法，是给你孩子自己几个小时的飞行额度。这样他们的态度就会变。所以，它自成一类。它以另一种方式做到了法拉利在汽车上做到的事。

法拉利一年卖 1.1 万辆车。我是说，也许 1.2 万辆。而它们全世界闻名。我们在波士顿之类的地方会有一家雪佛兰经销店。我们卖的车一样多。但我们不是法拉利。NetJets 有 600 架——嗯，算上欧洲，我是说，也许 650 架，但我们今年要买 100 架飞机。而且我们一架都不卖，因为订单已经积压。我们坐 NetJet 的航班去了东京。我们到达时状态很好。我们在那里待了几天。然后飞回来。根本没什么可挑剔的。现在你可以说：「嗯，你老了，有点骄奢了」之类。但这笔钱会流向慈善。这笔钱大概会有一千亿美元或更多。我想慈善机构希望我在自己身上花几个钱。它只要比各家慈善机构最后花出去的那些美元更有效率就行——那些机构在寻找真正有意义的事情上问题不少。

所以，NetJets 没有任何竞争者。前几天我们看过 Wheels Up。股票两年前以 10 美元发行。前几天卖 48 美分。他们有 1.26 万人交出了 10 亿美元、略多于 10 亿美元，买预付卡，先付钱，以后拿到一定的小时数。而他们并不——我认为很有可能一些人日后会非常失望。当他们有钱去做 NetJets 时，他们知道自己会坐上同样的飞机、由同样的飞行员驾驶，就像我和我家人自我们买下这家公司之前就一直坐的那样。所以，这个决定并不是由某种商业目标塑造的。在那之前几年，我从未听说过 NetJets。是 Frank Rooney 向我提起的。我立刻买了股票。然后我们买下了这家公司。嗯，我的孩子们有时也得坐商业航班。

但有时他们也能用我们的。但我从没坐过别的。我为什么要坐别的？我是说，它是黄金标准。没有人能比得上我们的机队。我是说，如果你有 600 架飞机，你在美国能让它们出现在比任何其他人的飞机都多得多的地方。我想我们是第二大机队（听不清）商业客机。而我们的机队还在增长，就像我说的，一年 100 架的速度。所以，这是一家了不起的公司。Adam Johnson 干得很出色。他在这门生意上做到的事，你简直无法相信。很长一段时间里，这是个艰难的模型。但他把它带到了今天的位置。我们应当永远拥有一家了不起的公司。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:57:38）：嗯，NetJets 一直很了不起。你可以说，它现在与任何一家航空公司等值。

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:45）：这太不一样了。而查理——我们当年费了很大劲才把 NetJets 的会员资格卖给查理。后来我们想出办法，让他买会员资格，就是在机身里装一个经济舱座位。（笑声）那真的把他拿下了。我想我们是唯一一个靠这个理由卖出去的。

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:11）：我过去常坐经济舱从洛杉矶来参加伯克希尔股东大会。机舱里满是有钱的股东。我走进经济舱时他们会鼓掌。我真的很喜欢那样。（笑声）（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:32）：但我得告诉你，我们半把他带坏了。他觉得自己有点非得解释一下，但他自己还是坐 NetJet 飞，所以。不坐才是不理智的。你知道，是你的继承人在付钱。我是说，你给我找一个人，他的遗产因为他在 NetJets 上的开销而变成负数、因此陷入那种境地，告诉我。但我至今没见过一例。巴菲特家族也不会是。付你会员费的，是最终那个剩余受益人。不过，当你像查理和我大半辈子那样生活时，要习惯花那么多钱还是有点难。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:38）：OK. Becky?

**Becky Quick**（01:49:39）：Here's a question from Simon Withers (PH), in Perth, in Western Australia. "It's been a long time since we've heard about See's Candy and NetJets. Could you please give us an update on See's performance, and when you project it will run out of places to open stores in the United States? "And could you also give us an overview of how NetJets has performed since its acquisition, and whether it's achieved the potential you saw at the time of that acquisition?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:16）：Well, with See's it hasn't been a question of opening stores. We found out that we have this wonderful brand that doesn't travel. You know? The mystique, the actual product, the feelings people have about some things, as we said before, I mean, it sometimes it's limited to given markets. Dr. Pepper sells at a huge rate in Dallas-Fort Worth, and maybe at 10 times the percentage per capita, maybe that it has in Detroit, or Boston. And you say, "Well, how can that be with a product that's been around for a century?" And people travel. You have national advertising. And I'm not sure. But I keep learning more, as I watch different brands. And Charlie and I, our economics were so good in California, that we tried in many cases the same experiment over and over again. It doesn't cost much to experiment. And we've tried everything in the world to cause a brand to travel. And we always think we were right for the first week. And then we find out that the magic — we can beat any other candy store, pretty much. But there aren't any candy stores anymore to speak of, as the world has changed. So, See's is 101 years now. It has magic. And it has limited magic in sort of the adjacent West. It's gravitational, almost. And then you get to the East. And incidentally, in the East, people prefer dark chocolate to milk chocolate. In the West, people prefer milk chocolate to dark. In the East, you can sell miniatures, and dark — in the West — I mean, there's all kinds of crazy things in the world that consumers do. But you want to keep observing it, because you do learn a little. And with Charlie and I, the temptation to keep trying things, because the economics were so good if we succeeded, so we tried various things. And, of course, every manager wants to try that comes along. Because they've learned that it should work. But it doesn't work. So, but that's what makes it very interesting.

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:38）：And NetJets, we have really learned how to distinguish, and justifiably distinguish a service to people that you have to be very well to do to use it. But if you're very well to do, in effect, you're spending your heir's money. I mean, it's what I told my Aunt Alice after she went from teaching to be worth millions and millions of dollars. And she came to see me. She'd never been married. And she said, "Can I afford to buy this fur coat?" in 1968 or '69. And I said, "Alice, you aren't buying it. Your nieces and nephews are buying it, because that's who you're leaving your money to." "Speaking on behalf of your nieces and nephews I would say we want you to buy it." And it's the same way. Do your heirs want you to fly around in that jet? Or do they want you to leave a little more money to your foundation or your kids? And the way to solve that one is to offer your kids a few hours themselves. Then their attitude can change. So, it's in a class by itself. It's done what Ferrari has done in a different sort of way in cars. Ferrari sells 11,000 cars a year. I mean, maybe 12,000. And they're known throughout the world. And we'll have a Chevy dealership in Boston or something. We'll sell as many cars. But we're not Ferrari. And NetJets has 600 — well, counting Europe, I mean, it's maybe 650, but we're going to buy 100 planes this year. And we won't sell any, because we've got a backlog. And we took a NetJet flight over to Tokyo. And we arrived in good shape. And we spent a couple days there. And we flew back. And there's just nothing to it. Now, you can say, "Well, you're getting sort of decadent and all that in your old age." But the money will go to philanthropy. And the money will probably be a hundred billion or more. And I figured the philanthropies want me to spend a few bucks on myself. All it has to do is be better than the last dollars that are spent by various philanthropies, which have plenty of problems finding things to do that make lots of sense. So, NetJets, there isn't any competitor. We had looked the other day at Wheels Up. Stock came out at $10 a couple years ago. It was selling at 48 cents the other day. And they've got 12,600 people that have given a billion dollars, a little over a billion dollars on prepaid cards where they've given them money, and they get a certain number of hours later on. And they don't — I think there's a good chance that some people are going to be very disappointed later on. When they have money to do the NetJets, they know they'll get on the same planes, with the same pilots as I and my family have flown on since before we bought the company. So, the decision wasn't shaped by some commercial objective. A couple years before, I'd never heard of NetJets. And Frank Rooney mentioned it to me. And I bought share immediately. And we bought the company. And, well, my kids have to fly commercial sometimes. But sometimes they get to use ours, too. But I've never flown anything else. And why would I? I mean, it's the gold standard. And nobody will match our fleet. I mean, if you got 600 planes, you've got them a lot more places in the United States than anybody else will have theirs. I think we're the second largest fleet (UNINTEL) commercial airliners. And our fleet's growing, like I said, at the rate of a hundred planes a year. So, it's a marvelous company. And Adam Johnson has performed. You just can't believe what he's done with the business. It was a tough model for a long time. But he's brought it where it is. And we should have a wonderful company forever. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:57:38）：Well, NetJets has been remarkable. You can argue that it's worth as much as any airline now.

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:45）：It's so different. And Charlie — we had a hard time selling Charlie a NetJets membership. And then we figured the way to get him to buy a membership was to put a coach seat in the fuselage. (LAUGHTER) And that really knocked him off. I think he's the only one we sold on the basis of that.

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:11）：I used to come to the Berkshire annual meetings on coach from Los Angeles. And it was full of rich stockholders. And they would clap when I came into the coach section. I really liked that. (LAUGHTER) (APPLAUSE)

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:32）：But I've got to tell you, we semi-corrupted him. He feels he kind of has to explain it, but he still flies in NetJet himself, so. And it'd be crazy not to. You know, your heirs are paying for it. I mean, find me anybody whose estate came in at less than zero because of what they spend on NetJets, threw them into that position, let me know. But I've never seen a case yet. And it won't be the case for the Buffett family. And it's the residual bottom beneficiary that's paying for your membership. And it's a little hard to get used to paying that much money though, when you've lived like Charlie and I have most of our lives.
