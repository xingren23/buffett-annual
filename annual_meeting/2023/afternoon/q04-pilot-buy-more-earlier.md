## 问题

这位 2004 年入股的股东算了一笔账：今年为 Pilot 41.4% 股权支付的金额，把整家公司估到约 190 亿美元，约为 BP 收购 TravelCenters of America 所付价格的六倍，而 Pilot 的市场份额只是对方的三倍。他问，把最终价格建立在 2022 年盈利之上——而那一年的燃油利润率异常之高——是不是一个大错误？

## 回答要点

1. 交易分三阶段：首阶段价格事后看「非常有吸引力」，第二阶段恰逢柴油业务的好年景，卖方因此拿到好价；第三阶段 20% 的选择权在卖方手上。
2. 巴菲特直言把选择权交给对方「从来都不是明智的结构安排」，但同样的安排在 1983 年 8 月 30 日买下内布拉斯加家具商城 80% 时结果「非常完美」。
3. 他的结论是：以现有价格拥有这 80% 已令人满意，但若一开始就把 80% 全部买下会更好；芒格补了一句「沃伦，它当时不卖」。
4. 他称与 TravelCenters of America 的比较「毫无道理」：对方规模小得多，且物业全部是租来的；Pilot 在州际公路沿线有数百处被规划为商业用途、约 15 英亩的网点。
5. 对 BP 的交易他不下判断，只说读过招股书、能理解那是 BP 的一大产出来源；伯克希尔的首选仍是「开一张支票、拥有整个公司、让管理层留任」，如 TTI。
6. 他花大段篇幅推介即将接任 CEO 的 Adam Wright：奥马哈 North High 公立学校毕业、内布拉斯加大学橄榄球冲球约 3,600 码、读书时打三份工；Pilot 去年销售额接近 800 亿美元，正常价格下约为一半。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:40）：贝基？

**Becky Quick**（00:32:40）：这个问题来自俄亥俄州帕尔马的 Gary Gambino，他说自己从 2004 年起就是伯克希尔的股东。他说：「今年为 Pilot 41.4% 股权支付的金额，把整家公司估值到大约 190 亿美元。这大约是 BP 收购 TravelCenters of America 所付价格的六倍，而 Pilot 的市场份额只是 TravelCenters of America 的三倍。把最终价格建立在 2022 年盈利之上——而那一年的燃油利润率异常之高——是不是一个大错误？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:33:19）：是的。嗯，这是个很好的问题。答案是，我们安排的是分三阶段购买，第三阶段由持有 20% 的那一方选择。第一阶段我们买下的价格，事后看非常有吸引力。第二阶段恰好赶上柴油业务非常好的一年，也就是说卖方拿到了很好的价格。我要说，总体而言，我们对自己以这个价格拥有这 80% 感到非常满意，但如果我们一开始就把这 80% 全部买下，会更好。最后 20% 的选择权在卖方手里，这种结构安排从来都不明智。我们和其他公司也有过这种安排——嗯，我们在家具商城身上就有过，1983 年 8 月 30 日我们买下了（内布拉斯加）家具商城 80%，差不多 40 年前，结果非常完美。但当你把选择权给对方，他们就占了一些优势。

我们现在拥有一家我们非常喜欢的公司 80% 的股权，而拿它和 Travel America 相比其实毫无道理，因为 Travel America 不仅规模小得多，而且他们的物业全是租来的。而我们在州际公路沿线有几百上千个网点，土地用途被规划为商业，也许 15 英亩，没有别的东西能比。他们也不可能把州际公路往右挪两英里之类，你知道，诸如此类。所以我们占据的位置是——BP 也许做了一笔好交易，也许没有。我读过招股书，我能理解。我的意思是，这对 BP 来说是一个很大的产出来源。但我喜欢 Pilot 一直以来的管理层。我非常喜欢即将上任的那位新 CEO。我得跟你们讲讲 Adam Wright，他接下了这份工作。

他来自奥马哈。他不是因为来自奥马哈才被选中的。他来自奥马哈。他毕业于 North High，一所公立学校，我妻子就是从那儿毕业的。我有孙辈也从那儿毕业。他上了内布拉斯加大学，在橄榄球上差点创下冲球纪录，那个纪录永远不会被打破了，因为他们已经放弃了橄榄球，不过我想他大概冲了 3,600 码。（笑声）他读书期间同时打三份工。20 年前他在 MidAmerica 实习，他母亲做工供他上学。这简直就是一个霍雷肖·阿尔杰故事的平方。现在由他管理 Pilot。哈斯拉姆家族给了我们一门很棒的业务，「大吉姆」，现在他（听不清）了不起的人。事情就是这样。我很高兴我们拥有 Pilot，我只是希望我当初做这笔交易时就把 100% 都买下来（笑），但当初提供给我们的并不是那样的交易——

**Charlie Munger**（00:36:33）：沃伦，它当时不卖。

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:35）：它当时不卖。是的。而且我们买家具商城时，最后 20% 也不卖，所以我们就买了 80%。那笔结果也不错。我们做过各种结构不同的交易。最好的方式就是给人开一张支票，把股票拿过来。我们在 TTI 就是这么做的，但你不能总是做成同样的交易。如果我们足够喜欢这门生意、足够喜欢这些人，我们会用不同的方式量身定制，但我们的偏好是开一张支票、拥有整个公司，同时让管理层留任。查理，有什么要补充的吗？

**Charlie Munger**（00:37:21）：没有。

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:22）：是啊。看着像 Adam Wright 这样的人成长起来，真的很美妙。我的意思是，我不知道他母亲当年挣多少钱，但他上了 North High，那所学校大概离这儿四五英里。公立学校毕业生。他一步一步走上来。在太平洋燃气电力公司待过很短一段时间，然后我们把他带回了 Pilot。而 Pilot——嗯，Pilot，去年柴油价格完全不同。Pilot 去年销售额接近 800 亿美元，但今年价格更正常，这个，是很大的变化，你知道，只有那个数字的一半左右，也许稍多一点。而他，我不知道 Adam 多大年纪，但他在 40 多岁。他是从格雷格·阿贝尔参与的那个组织体系里成长起来的。现在他在这里，经营一门非常重要的大生意。

伯克希尔能这样做很好。我不认为——你知道，别人也许上过更有名的商学院，那又怎样？你知道，我们已经看到了 Adam 能做什么。

**Warren Buffett**（00:32:40）：Becky?

**Becky Quick**（00:32:40）：This question comes from Gary Gambino in Parma, Ohio, who says he's been a Berkshire shareholder since 2004. He says, "The amount paid this year for the 41.4% stake in Pilot values the entire company at around $19 billion. That's about six times what BP is paying for Travel Centers of America, but Pilot's market share is just three times Travel Centers of America's. "Was it a big mistake to base the final price in 2022 earnings, which has unusually high fuel margins?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:33:19）：Yeah. Well, that's a very good question. And the answer is that we arranged to buy it in three stages, with the third stage being at the option of the owner of 20%. The first stage we bought at what turned out to be a very attractive price. The second stage turned out to be a very good year for the diesel business, which means that the seller got a very good price. And I would say that overall, we feel very good about the fact that we own the 80% at the price that we do, but we would've been better if we just bought the 80% to start with. And the last 20% the seller has the option, and that's always an unintelligent way of structuring something. We've had that arrangement with other, well, we had it with the Furniture Mart, we bought 80% of the [Nebraska] Furniture Mart on August 30th, 1983, almost 40 years ago, and it's worked out perfectly. But when you give the other person the option, they've got some advantage. We have 80% now of a business we like very much, and the comparison to Travel America is really spurious, because Travel America is not only much smaller, but they rent all their properties. And we have hundreds and hundreds of locations on the interstate that are zoned for what we, commercial, maybe 15 acres or, there's nothing like it. And they're not going to move the interstate two miles to the right or something, you know, or anything of the sort. So, we've got a position that, you know, BP may or may not have made a fine deal. I've read the prospectus and I can understand. I mean, it's a big source of output for BP. But I like the management we have had at Pilot. I like very much the fellow who's coming in that's the new CEO. I just have to tell you a little bit about Adam Wright, who's taking that job on. He came from Omaha. He wasn't selected because he came from Omaha. He came from Omaha. He came from North High, a public school that my wife graduated from. I've got grandchildren that graduated from there. He went to the University of Nebraska and almost set the rushing record in football, which will never be beaten because they've given up football, but I think he rushed for maybe (LAUGHTER) 3,600 yards. He held three jobs while he went through there. He interned at MidAmerica 20 years ago, his mother worked to put him through school. I mean, it's just, it's Horatio Alger-squared. And we have him to manage Pilot. And the Haslams have given us a wonderful business, "Big Jim," and now he (UNINTEL) great people. And here we are. And I'm very glad we own Pilot, I just wish we bought 100% (LAUGH) of it when I first made the deal, but that was not the deal that was offered to us —

**Charlie Munger**（00:36:33）：Warren, it wasn't for sale.

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:35）：It wasn't for sale. Yeah. And the last 20% of the Furniture Mart wasn't for sale when we bought it, so we bought 80% of it. And that's worked out well. And we've done various deals various ways. The best way to do it is just write people the check and get the stock. But we did that with TTI, but you can't always make the same deal. If we like the business well enough and the people well enough, we will tailor it differently, but our preference is to write a check and own the whole place and keep the management in place. Charlie, anything to add on?

**Charlie Munger**（00:37:21）：No.

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:22）：Yeah. It's really wonderful to watch something like Adam Wright work out. I mean, basically, you know, I don't know what his mother was earning, but he went to North High, which is probably four or five miles from here. Public school graduate. And worked his way up. Went through a short period at Pacific Gas and Electric, and we brought him home there on Pilot. And Pilot, well, Pilot, the prices on diesel were way different last year. Pilot was close to $80 billion of sales last year, but more normal prices this, it's significant, you know, it's half that or thereabouts, maybe a little more. But he is, I don't know how old Adam would be, but he's in his 40s. And he came up through the organization that Greg Abel was involved with. And now here he is, running a very major business. It's good at Berkshire to be able to do that. And I don't, you know, somebody else may have gone to more prestigious business schools, and I think so what? You know, we've seen what Adam can do.
