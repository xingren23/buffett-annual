## 问题

这位股东指出，会计规则改变后，伯克希尔须把股权投资公允价值变动计入利润表，巴菲特多次让股东忽略这些变动。而近期巴菲特又批评银行用持有至到期会计，把投资组合的公允价值变动挡在利润表和股东权益之外，损害了利益相关者。他问：为什么对银行和对伯克希尔的市值计价会计看法不同？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特说两种情况他都主张把市值放在资产负债表、而不经过利润表；资产负债表告诉你存款能否兑付，利润表却每五秒就变一次。
2. 他以苹果为例：周五上涨七八个点就相当于 70 亿美元，这样的利润表「简直是疯了」，而且它只反映某一时点的状况。
3. 对银行，他指出房贷对消费者是好工具、对银行却是糟糕的资产，必须关注其持有资产价值与次日清晨可能被要求支付的金额是否已经失衡。
4. 他说这正是几年前一直采用的做法，建议股东照此理解，但伯克希尔仍会遵守 SEC 与州监管机构要求的所有规则。
5. 芒格称这项会计变更「完全是发疯」，认为任何懂企业实际如何运作的人都不会做出这种改变，会计师只是「一时兴起」。
6. 巴菲特回忆 25 年前曾建议审计委员会向审计师提四个问题，但因可能加重审计师责任、且管理层不愿回答而未能推行；他重申会一贯做合法的事，也一贯说出自己认为对的话。

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:24）：好。现在问我们一个简单的问题吧，贝基。（笑）（掌声）

**Becky Quick**（02:21:33）：这个问题来自 Doug DeShiel。他说：「自从会计规则改变、要求伯克希尔把股权投资的公允价值变动计入利润表以来，巴菲特先生多次告诉股东忽略这些变动，因为它们并不反映这些投资将产生的长期回报。最近，巴菲特先生提出，银行使用的持有至到期会计——以避免把银行投资组合的公允价值变动反映在利润表和股东权益中——对其各类利益相关者是一种损害。巴菲特先生能否详细说明，为什么他对银行和对伯克希尔的市值计价会计看法不同？」

**Warren Buffett**（02:22:24）：嗯，我认为在两种情况下，都应该放在资产负债表上，而不是利润表上。审计师面临一个非常棘手的问题：显然利润表会流入资产负债表。但资产负债表告诉你存款能否被支付，它告诉你很多事情。我们把它放在资产负债表上。我们相信在资产负债表上列示市场价值。我们只是不认为应该让它经过利润账户。要做到这一点，我们会把它放进「其他综合收益」，很长一段时间都是这么做的。所以，在某种程度上，很大程度上，我同情审计行业。但他们必须真正决定：是希望资产负债表代表价值，只不过它不反映上行，比如我们买下时思糖果，它现在值钱得多，所以从这个意义上说是保守的；还是希望有一个对人们来说变得毫无意义的利润账户。因为它真的每五秒就变一次，你知道？我是说，嗯，今天市场休市，但你知道，我猜苹果周五涨了七八个点吧。

那是 70 亿美元：我是说，这个利润账户简直是疯了。它反映的是我们在那一刻的位置。当然，如果你是银行，你把钱投出去，主要是房贷，对银行来说是糟糕的资产，对消费者来说却是很好的工具；而且它现在以一种与过去完全不同的方式嵌入整个社会，你必须关注他们持有的资产价值与明天早上可能被要求支付的金额之间是否已经失衡。（笑）如果我们所有的钱明天早上都可能被要求偿付，我们的行为方式就得和伯克希尔大不相同。所以，我真的认为正确的做法就是我们建议的做法。这也正是几年前一直在做的。我建议股东这样看，但我们会遵守规则，显然是 SEC、州监管机构和其他所有人要求我们的规则。但我仍然会向股东解释，就像我会向我妹妹解释一样，伯克希尔真正重要的是什么。

我认为每位管理层实际上都对这一点负有义务，而他们反而往另一个方向走，给出一大堆完全无意义的数字。你无法想象其中一些，你知道，EBITDA，我原以为那已经是最糟的了。但他们还在继续。你知道，扣除一切之前的利润：EBE。（笑声）但这不会改变我会告诉我妹妹的话，她今天就在观众席上，我希望。我应该对所有股东说：「我们会一贯做合法的事，也会一贯说出我们认为对的话。」查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:25:38）：对。（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:25:42）：我们希望拥有者理解他们拥有的是什么。你知道？这不意味着他们必须理解细节。但这就是为什么我们有跟随了 50 年、60 年的人。这不意味着他们读 10-Q 之类的东西。但他们觉得我们是在像邻居一样对他们说实话。

**Charlie Munger**（02:26:09）：对，我不知道会计师们在做那项改动时在想什么。在我看来那完全是发疯，绝对是发疯。我不明白任何理解企业实际如何运作、以及拥有企业的管理者应如何经营企业的人，怎么会做出那样的会计变更。会计师们这么做只是因为他们一时兴起。

**Warren Buffett**（02:26:32）：25 年前，我向审计行业建议，让审计委员会向审计师提四个问题，如果问了这些问题，股东会对公司了解得多得多。但让审计师被问这些问题对他们并不好，因为回答可能增加他们的责任。客户也不想让他们回答，因为管理层不想让他们回答。

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:01）：对，他们想要一个制度：只要遵守某些规则，他们就安全。这可以理解。但我不认为过去这一年——这条要求有价证券的变动每季度经过利润账户的规则——他们那么做是为了保护自己免于——

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:20）：不是。

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:21）：——责任。他们只是出于自己某种疯狂的理由那么做的。

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:26）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（02:27:27）：你有一群人，他们都会从一个庞大而复杂的体系里领取高额薪酬，并在其中步步高升，就像我们军队里的许多军官一样；天知道，如果你把他们关在一个小房间里，让他们发明新的会计准则，他们会干出什么来。

**Warren Buffett**（02:27:50）：嗯，对我们的审计师说一句：记住那是查理在说话，他其实并不——（笑声）但我同意他，（笑）百分之百。他 99 岁了，他能说一些我不能说的话。（笑声）好。二号提问站——但他说的非常重要。我是说，你必须对到底发生了什么有一些洞察。

**Charlie Munger**（02:28:25）：即使你是（听不清）会计师。

**Warren Buffett**（02:28:30）：对。

**Warren Buffett**（02:21:24）：OK. Now ask us an easy question, Becky. (LAUGH) (APPLAUSE)

**Becky Quick**（02:21:33）：This question comes from Doug DeShiel (PH). "Since the accounting rules changed requiring Berkshire to report the change in fair value of its equity investments through the income statement, Mr. Buffett has repeatedly told shareholders to ignore those changes as they're not reflective of the long-term returns that those investments will produce. "Recently, Mr. Buffett has argued that hold-to-maturity accounting used by the banks to avoid reflecting the changes in fair value of bank investment portfolios in the income statement and the shareholder equity account do a disservice to its various stakeholders. Can Mr. Buffett elaborate on why he views mark-to-market accounting differently for banks in comparison to Berkshire?"

**Warren Buffett**（02:22:24）：Well, I believe, in both cases, in doing it on the balance sheet and not in the income statement. And it's a very tough problem the auditors face is that obviously the income statement feeds into the balance sheet. But the balance sheet tells you whether deposits can be paid, it tells you a lot of things. And we show it on our balance sheet. We believe in showing market values on our balance sheet. We just don't believe in running it through the income account. And in getting there we would put in "other comprehensive income," like it was for a long time. So, I sympathize, to some extent — a far extent, with the audit group. But they have to really decide whether they want the balance sheet to represent values — except it doesn't reflect them on the upside if we buy a See's Candy and it's worth way more money, so it's conservative in that sense; or whether they want to have an income account that becomes meaningless to people. Because it really changes every five seconds, you know? I mean — well, the market's closed today, but, you know, I guess Apple was up, what, seven or eight points or something on Friday. I mean, that's $7 billion: I mean, that's a crazy income account. It is a reflection of where we stand at that point. And, of course, if you're a bank, where you're putting out money really at things — mortgages, I mean, primarily, that are terrible instruments for a bank to own, but a great instrument for a consumer to buy; and built-in to the whole society now in a way that is entirely different than in the past, you've got to pay attention to whether they've gotten out of whack in terms of the value of what they own and what can be demanded of them tomorrow morning. (LAUGH) And if we had all of our money that could be demanded from us tomorrow morning, we'd have to behave a lot differently than Berkshire does. So, I really think the way to do it is the way we recommend doing. Which is exactly what was being done until a few years ago. I recommend the shareholders look at it that way, but we're going to follow the rules, obviously that the SEC, you know, the state authorities and everybody require of us. But I'll still explain to the shareholders exactly what I would explain to my sister about what really counts at Berkshire. And I think every management actually has an obligation to that, and instead of it, they go in the other direction and give them a lot of figures that are total nonsense. You can't imagine some of the — you know, EBITDA, I thought, was about as bad as you could get. But they've kept going. You know, earnings before everything: EBE. (LAUGHTER) But that doesn't change what I would tell my sister, who's here in the audience, I hope. And I should tell this to all the shareholders: "We'll consistently do what is legal, and we'll consistently say what we think is right." Charlie?

But they feel we're telling it to them like, you know, they lived next door to us.
