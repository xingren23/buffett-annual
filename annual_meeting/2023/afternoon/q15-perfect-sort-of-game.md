## 问题

这位第四次来「资本主义的伍德斯托克」的股东先向两位致谢，然后问了两件事：你们彼此之间有什么从未讲过的、最好笑的故事？另外，你们生意中最难的部分是什么？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特先答第二问：我们没有「难的生意」，我们热爱自己的生意；每天早上起床心情都很好，不知道那天会发生什么，也许什么都不会发生。
2. 他描述理想的工作条件：共事的是能想象到的最好的一群人，彼此喜欢，没有人盯着别人的位置；办公室离家约五分钟，因此一生没把时间花在通勤上。
3. 芒格说两人本身就够荒唐，不需要多少笑话，各自做的怪事足以让彼此一直觉得有趣。
4. 巴菲特讲了 1966 年在巴尔的摩收购百货公司 Hochschild Kohn 的旧事：需要一位在附近、且完全照吩咐办事的律师，芒格从 Wilmer Cutler 找来一位素未谋面的人。
5. 芒格给那位律师的指示是「把沃伦当成任何一位 90 岁的客户来对待」——当时巴菲特 35 或 36 岁；交易很快谈成，随后到 Maryland National Bank 借 600 万美元，收购价 1200 万美元。
6. 银行家 Cammy Slack 一句「你们要为小小的 Hochschild-Kohn 借 600 万？」，让两人立刻开始怀疑这是不是一笔好买卖，此后一直在想怎么把它卖掉；巴菲特以高尔夫作比：若每洞都能一杆进洞就没人会打球，把球打进树林、有时救得出有时救不出正是乐趣所在。

## 关键引述

> **“Treat Warren like any other 90-year-old client.”**
>
> 「把沃伦当成任何一位 90 岁的客户来对待。」

> **“we don't have a hard business”**
>
> 「我们没有难的生意。」

> **“part of the fun of the game is the fact that you hit them to the woods”**
>
> 「这项游戏的乐趣之一，就在于你会把球打进树林。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:52）：九号台。我没弄错吧？好。

**Audience Member**（01:33:56）：我叫 Avlon Gross，来自加利福尼亚州洛杉矶。我是股东，这是我第四年来「资本主义的伍德斯托克」。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:13）：很高兴你来。

**Audience Member**（01:34:15）：非常感谢你们所做的一切，沃伦·巴菲特、查理·芒格。你们彼此之间有什么从没讲过的、最好笑的故事？另外，你们生意中最难的部分是什么？（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:33）：我来答第二个。你问题的第二部分——我们没有难的生意。我们热爱我们的生意。每天早上起床，我心情都很好。我不知道那天会发生什么。也许什么都不会发生，但也可能会有点事。就算什么都没有，我也会去买点短期国债之类的东西。和我共事的是你能想象到的最好的一群人。我是说，我们彼此喜欢。没有人盯着别人的位置，或者类似的事。这是理想的工作条件。而且离我家只有五分钟，差不多吧。所以我这辈子没把时间花在通勤上。我实在想不出还有什么比这更好的。查理有很多你没听过的笑话。我们看看他能想出哪一个。

**Charlie Munger**（01:35:29）：嗯，我觉得沃伦和我本身就够荒唐了，不需要多少笑话。我们各自做的事都够古怪，足以让彼此一直觉得有趣。

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:43）：跟他们讲讲买 Hochschild Kohn 的时候你跟律师说的那句话。

**Charlie Munger**（01:35:49）：我不记得了。你讲吧。

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:52）：你记得吗？（笑声）那是 1966 年，我们在巴尔的摩，收购一家百货公司。我们需要一位律师。我们需要一位就在附近、而且完全照吩咐办事的律师。查理从 Wilmer Cutler 找了一位很好的律师，我想是这家。我不知道查理还记不记得他当时给那位律师的指示。

**Charlie Munger**（01:36:23）：不，我不记得了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:25）：是这样，查理对那位我们此前从未见过的律师说——他说：「把沃伦当成……」我当时 35 还是 36 岁。他说：「把沃伦当成任何一位 90 岁的客户来对待。」（笑声）那家伙完全明白他的意思。我们很快就做成了交易。然后我们去了银行，First National Bank，我想是这家——其实应该是 Maryland National Bank。那儿有个叫 Cammy Slack 的人，是吧，查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:36:45）：是的。

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:46）：我们想以 1200 万美元的收购价借 600 万美元。Cammy 一脸困惑地看着我们。他说：「你们要为小小的 Hochschild-Kohn 借 600 万？」查理和我就说了句大意是：「马里兰国民银行是我们第一个找上门的。要是他们不想做，我们还有另一家银行可以去。」总之，他们把钱借给了我们。但他说「小小的 Hochschild-Kohn」的时候，我们立刻开始想：「也许这不是我们这辈子见过的最好的一笔交易。」从那一刻起，我们就在琢磨怎么把它卖掉。Sandy Gottesman 当时也参与其中。从某种意义上说，那些没成的交易给我们的乐趣，和成了的一样多。我是说，如果你知道自己去打高尔夫，每一洞都能一杆进洞，你只要把球打出去，球就进了 300 码、400 码外的洞，那就没人会打高尔夫了。

这项游戏的乐趣之一，就在于你会把球打进树林。有时能救出来，有时救不出来。所以我们身处一场完美的游戏。我们两个都很享受。我们一起玩得很开心。而且我们不必做任何自己并不真正相信该做的事。我是说，我们不受任何集团指使。所以我们可以塑造自己的命运，从某种意义上说，也塑造我们经营公司的原则。这是人生中极大的奢侈。我们不想当世界上任何其他公司的董事长或 CEO，或者别的什么。那样我们就得顺从一些我们真的不想顺从的东西。这个描述公平吗，查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:05）：是的，公平。

**Warren Buffett**（01:39:07）：好。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:52）：Station nine. Am I right on this? Yeah.

**Audience Member**（01:33:56）：My name is Avlon Gross (PH). And I am from Los Angeles, California. I am a shareholder. And it's my fourth year coming to the "Woodstock of Capitalism." (LAUGHTER)

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:13）：We're glad you came.

**Audience Member**（01:34:15）：Thank you so much for everything Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger. What is the funniest story that you have never told about each other? And also, what is the hardest part of your business? (APPLAUSE)

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:33）：I'll answer the second. The second part of your question is that we don't have a hard business. We love our business. Every morning, when I get up, I feel good. I don't know what's going to happen that day. Maybe nothing will happen. But maybe something will happen. And if nothing else, I'll roll some T-bills or something. But I work with the greatest group of people you can imagine. I mean, we like each other. And nobody is after anybody else's job, or anything of the sort. It's ideal working conditions. And it's five minutes from my home, or thereabouts. So, I haven't spent my life commuting. I just can't imagine having anything better. And Charlie's got a lot of funny stories you haven't heard. But we'll see which one he comes up with.

**Charlie Munger**（01:35:29）：Well, I think Warren and I are naturally so ridiculous that we don't need very many funny stories. We each do things that are peculiar enough so that we can keep one another amused.

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:43）：Tell them what you told the lawyer when we were buying Hochschild Kohn.

**Charlie Munger**（01:35:49）：I don't remember. You tell them.

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:52）：Well, you remember? (LAUGHTER) It was 1966, and we were down in Baltimore, buying a department store. And we needed a lawyer. And we needed a lawyer who was nearby and would do exactly as told. And Charlie came up with a very good lawyer from Wilmer Cutler's, I believe. And I don't know whether Charlie remembers the instructions he gave the lawyer or not.

**Charlie Munger**（01:36:23）：No. I don't.

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:25）：Well, Charlie told the lawyer, who we never met before. And he said, "Treat Warren like" — I was 36 at the time or 35. He said, "Treat Warren like any other 90-year-old client." (LAUGHTER) This guy knew exactly what he meant. And we made the deal in a hurry. And then we went to the bank, First National Bank, I believe it was — or Maryland National Bank, actually. And there was a fellow named Cammy Slack there, wasn't there, Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:36:45）：Yeah.

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:46）：We wanted to borrow $6,000,000 bucks against a $12,000,000 purchase. And Cammy looked at us with bewilderment. And he says, "You want you borrow $6,000,000 for this little old Hochschild-Kohn?" And Charlie and I said something to the effect, "Well, the Maryland National was our first call. And if they didn't want to do it, we had another bank we'd go to." And, anyway, they lent us the money. But when he said little old Hochschild-Kohn, we immediately started thinking, "Maybe this isn't the best deal we've ever seen in our lives." And from that point on, we were trying to figure out how to sell it. Sandy Gottesman was involved with us then too. And we had as much fun out of deals that didn't work in a certain sense as the ones that did work. I mean, if you knew you were going to play golf and you were going to hit a hole in one on every hole, you just hit the ball, and it went in the hole that was 300 yards away, or 400 yards away, nobody would play golf. I mean, part of the fun of the game is the fact that you hit them to the woods. And sometimes you get them out, and sometimes you don't. So, we are in the perfect sort of game. And we both enjoy it. And we have a lot of fun together. And we don't have to do anything we don't really believe in doing. I mean, we are not dictated to by any group. And so, we get to forge our own destiny. And in a sense, forge the principle by which we can run the company. And that's a huge luxury in life. And we don't want to be president of any other company in the world, or CEO, or anything else. We'd have to conform to certain things that we really don't want to conform to. Is that a fair description, Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:05）：Yeah, it is.

**Warren Buffett**（01:39:07）：Yeah.
