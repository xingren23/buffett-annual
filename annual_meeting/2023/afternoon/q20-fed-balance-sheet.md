## 问题

这位股东问，美联储资产负债表目前的规模是否令人担心，尤其是量化宽松的结果：美联储凭空扩张资产负债表，净效果实质上是一种单式记账，仅凭一系列账面分录就凭空造出有价值的东西。她还想知道芒格对此怎么看。

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特直接表态不担心美联储，认为它正在履行设立时赋予的职能；美联储有就业与通胀两个目标，但它并不是制造赤字的一方，也解决不了财政问题。
2. 他并不认同把通胀目标从 0 改为每年 2%，理由是这等于公开告诉人们要让本国货币每年贬值 2%，会带来很多后果。
3. 他指出流通中货币已从 8000 亿美元增至 2.2 万亿美元，且以百元钞为主；按全美人口（含婴儿）折算约人均 50 张百元钞，他并不知道这些钱在哪里。
4. 芒格称当前局面「非常极端」，若只是一味印钞花钱，最终会造成糟糕的麻烦，并以拉美货币长期失控、经济成就远落后于美国为反例。
5. 巴菲特以二战储蓄债券为例：1942—1945 年付出 18.75 美元、收回 25 美元，战后国债占 GDP 从 30%—40% 升至 120%，随后出现大量通胀，购债者的一部分购买力被拿走。
6. 他用芒格「跳楼者在落地前都还好」的比喻收尾，并引参议院财政委员会前主席 Russell Long 的话——「别征你的税，别征我的税，去征树后面那个人」，说明政治上拨款容易、征税难。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:04:38）：好。贝基？

**Becky Quick**（02:04:39）：这个问题来自艾奥瓦州锡达拉皮兹的 Lindsay Peter Schumacher。她问：「美联储资产负债表目前的规模是否让你担心？特别是量化宽松的结果——美联储凭空扩张了资产负债表。其净效果实质上是一种单式记账，除了制造一系列账面分录之外，凭空创造出有价值的东西。也想知道芒格先生对此怎么看。」

**Warren Buffett**（02:05:11）：嗯，我不认为美联储是问题所在，我也认为他们解决不了财政问题。我不担心美联储。我认为它正在履行设立时赋予它的职能。他们有两个目标，而我大概不会是主张把通胀目标从 0 改成每年 2% 的人。你知道，我认为，如果你告诉人们你的目标是让本国货币每年贬值 2%，那会有很多含义。尽管（笑）对很多人来说这感觉不错。很多人想要一点通胀，但没人想要高通胀，除了那些欠了很多债的人。所以我并不担心美联储的资产负债表。我喜欢看它，数字很大，我一向喜欢大数字。但有意思的是：对我来说最有趣的数字之一是流通中的货币。它已经，2007、2008 年人们说「现金是垃圾」，「所有这些现金都会消失」。可如果你看美联储的资产负债表，它从 8000 亿美元变成 2.2 万亿美元，其中绝大部分是百元钞，压倒性地是百元钞。

我算过，美国平均每个人，包括婴儿、所有人，大约有 50 张百元钞，我真的很想知道这些钱都在哪儿。我是说，没人在南美或非洲之类的地方囤积欧洲美元。而现在对现金的需求，你知道，其中一部分可能是隐蔽的毒品交易之类的活动。但任何认为「现金是垃圾」的人都该看看美联储的资产负债表。事实上，你可以看 5 美元、2 美元、1 美元钞票各有多少，而真正活跃的是百元钞。百元钞扩散的方式简直令人震惊。当然，我不知道它们在哪里，我也不认为美联储能确切知道，但他们大概比我猜得准得多。但这事确实发生了，你每周都能看到，你会看到流通中货币大概还会略有增长。相信我，「现金」不是垃圾。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:08:00）：嗯，我不知道这一切会把我们带向何方。情况非常极端。我认为在应对它时也可以相当极端：大萧条之类的时候，如果之后你回到某个有纪律的时期。但如果你只是——但如果你打算就这么一直印钱、一直花钱，我认为最终会造成糟糕的麻烦。你在拉美能看到这一点。拉美一直让本国货币失控，当然，它在经济成就上远远落后于美国。所以我认为，如果我们彻底放弃旧的方式，进入一个只想印钱、只想让日子更容易过下去的新世界，我们会付出代价。

**Warren Buffett**（02:09:17）：我们在二战中付了代价，我是说每个人：学童和其他所有人，包括我自己。我们买的是最初叫「战争债券」、后来叫「国防债券储蓄债券」之类的东西。但从 1942 到 1944 或 1945 年，你付出 18.75 美元，拿回 25.00 美元，每个孩子都在攒储蓄邮票之类的。可等这一切结束，国债占 GDP 的比例是 120%，而不是 30% 或 40%。随后我们经历了大量通胀，非常多。所以，那些真正买了这些债券、支持了战争的人，一部分购买力被拿走了。嗯，这其实也没什么特别不对的。但当国家养成这种习惯时，我认为很难判断社会的临界点在哪里。而我不认为你会想靠近它。

**Charlie Munger**（02:10:35）：是啊，但让大批人失业同样很难。这就是张力所在。

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:41）：对。所以美联储才有就业和通胀两个目标。但他们不是能创造赤字的那一方。到目前为止，这套体系运转得相当不错，尽管就像我说的，它一直——

**Charlie Munger**（02:10:58）：到目前为止，那个刚从高楼上跳下来的人——

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:03）：对，没错。

**Charlie Munger**（02:11:04）：——在落地之前都还好。

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:08）：对。（笑声）不过，也许我们可以在三楼或六楼就停下来。我们不知道是哪一层，但我们知道它还没落地。可是，你知道，在政治上，投票通过拨款非常、非常、非常有诱惑力，而投票征税可不有趣。参议院财政委员会主席 Russell Long——现在都有一座以他命名的楼了——他说过：「别征你的税，别征我的税，去征树后面那个人。」（笑）基本上这就是那种态度——我是说，这就是政治上管用的现实。到目前为止，这个国家在有很多理论上可能造成大问题的东西的情况下，运转得非常好。但这不保证我们的未来；储备货币地位让我们能做很多事，但如果我们搞砸了，它也会带来很多后果。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:19）：嗯，我们是在把这个话题说死了，但它确实是个问题。我希望我们有一个解决方案。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:04:38）：OK. Becky?

**Becky Quick**（02:04:39）：This question comes from Lindsay Peter Schumacher in Cedar Rapids, Iowa. "Does the current size of the Federal Reserve balance sheet concern you? In particular, the result of quantitative easing — the Federal Reserve expanded its balance sheet out of nothing. The net effect in essence is a form of single-entry accounting creating something of value out of nothing other than a series of book entries. And wondering what Mr. Munger thinks about this as well."

**Warren Buffett**（02:05:11）：Well, I don't think the Federal Reserve is the problem, and I think they can't solve the fiscal problem. I do not worry about the Federal Reserve. I think it's fulfilling the functions for which it was established. They have two objectives, and I would not have been one probably that would have changed the inflation objective: to 2% a year from 0. You know, I think that if you tell people that you're shooting to depreciate your currency at 2% a year, that has a lot of implications. Although it feels good to a (LAUGH) lot of people. A lot of people want a little inflation, but nobody wants a lot of inflation except somebody's that got a lot of debts. And I do not worry about the Federal Reserve balance sheet. I enjoy looking at it, and the numbers are big — I always like big numbers. But it's interesting: One of the most interesting figures, to me, is currency in circulation. I mean, it has gone — they were saying, "Cash is trash," back in 2007 and '08, "and all that cash is going to disappear." Well, if you look at the Federal Reserve balance sheet, it's gone from $800 billion to $2.2 trillion, and most of that's in hundred-dollar bills, overwhelmingly. And I figured out I think there's about 50 $100 bills per person — babies, everybody, in the United States, and I would really like to know where all of that is. I mean, nobody's hoarding eurodollars, you know, in South America, or Africa, or wherever. And the demand for currency now — you know, some of it may be subtle drug dealers' activities and of that. But anybody who thinks "cash is trash" ought to look at the Federal Reserve balance sheet. And, actually, you can look at how many $5 bills and $2 bills, and ones and all that, and the action has been in $100 bills. I mean, it is just astounding the way that hundred-dollar bills have spread. And, of course, I don't know where they are, and I don't think the Fed can know exactly, but they'd probably make a lot better guess than I could. But I do it's happened, and you can watch it every week, and you'll watch currency in circulation probably grow a little bit. And, believe me, "cash" is not trash. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:08:00）：Well, I don't know where we're headed with all of this. It's been very extreme. I think you could be pretty extreme in fighting it: depressions and so forth, if you reverted afterwards to a period of some discipline. But if you just — But if you're going to just keep printing money and spending it, I think eventually it causes bad trouble. And you can see it Latin America. Latin America let its currency get out of control all the time, and, of course, it lagged the United States in economic achievement greatly. So, I think we pay a price if we ever give up our old ways entirely and go into a new world where we just try and print money, just make it easier to get through the year.

**Warren Buffett**（02:09:17）：We paid a price in World War II, I mean, everybody: school kids and everybody else, myself included. And we bought what were originally called "War Bonds," and "Defense Bonds," and "Savings Bonds," and all that. But from 1942 to 1944 or '45, you paid out $18.75 and you got $25.00 back, and every kid saved savings stamps and all that. But when you got all through, you know, you had 120% of GDP in the national debt instead of 30% or 40%. And we had a lot of inflation subsequently, a lot. So, the people that really bought those bonds and supported the war had a portion of their purchasing power taken away from them. Well, there wasn't anything wrong with that, particularly. But when the country gets in the habit of doing that, I think it's tough to figure out where the breaking point is with society. But I don't think you want to come anywhere close to it.

**Charlie Munger**（02:10:35）：Yeah, but it's also tough to have a mass of people unemployed. That's the tension.

**Warren Buffett**（02:10:41）：Yep. Well, that's why the Fed has two objectives, in terms of employment and inflation. But they are not the ones that could create the deficits. And so far, the system has worked pretty well, although, like I say, it's been —

**Charlie Munger**（02:10:58）：So, far, the man who just jumped off a tall building —

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:03）：Yeah, right.

**Charlie Munger**（02:11:04）：— is all right until he hits the ground.

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:08）：Yeah. (LAUGHTER) Well, but there could be ways we can stop now at the third floor, or the sixth floor. We don't know what floor it is, but we know it hasn't hit the ground. But, you know, politically it's very, very, very tempting to vote appropriations, and it's not fun to vote taxes. And Russell Long, head of the Senate Finance Committee, they've got a building named after him now, he said, "You know, don't tax you, don't tax me, tax that guy behind the tree." And (LAUGH) basically that is the attitude — I mean, it's the reality of what is useful in politics. And so far, this country's managed to work very well with lot of things that could theoretically cause a lot of problems. But it doesn't guarantee us the future on it and being the reserve currency lets us do a lot of things, but it also creates a lot of consequences if we screw it up. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:19）：Well, we're beating a subject to death, but it is a problem. I wish we had a solution.
