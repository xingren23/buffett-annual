# 第 21 问 · 被资本主义替代的人需要照顾

## 问题

这位学生指出，疫情期间出现了供应链短缺，尤其是来自亚洲的短缺，于是一些企业带着政治意图把生产迁走。他问：企业应该做这类决定吗？政府又应该支持它们吗？

## 回答要点

1. 芒格认为，如果在墨西哥生产便宜得多，去那里开厂、买更便宜的零件是自然的，很多汽车厂商已经这么做；但没人愿意让整个国家空心化、制造业岗位全在别处。
2. 巴菲特以伯克希尔自身为例：纺织业务先因南方相对北方变得可行而失去竞争力，南方成本上升后又转到中国；1964 年接手时，一半工人只会说葡萄牙语。
3. 芒格补充了南方成本条件的变化：田纳西河谷管理局（TVA）提供廉价电力，空调则改变了炎热地区无法生产的问题。
4. 巴菲特说，国家整体因生产外移而更富裕，但被替代的人——如 55、60 岁、只会葡萄牙语的新贝德福德工人——无法靠「再培训」解决，不能轻飘飘地谈。
5. 芒格引亚当·斯密：自由市场资本主义会自动提高人均 GDP、惠及包括底层在内的所有人，但过程内生出大量痛苦；把痛苦全部拿走，也就把收益全部拿走。
6. 巴菲特承认其他制度并没有更好，但资本主义也造成财富差距扩大；长期看，保住资本主义最好的部分又不让只会说葡萄牙语的人受损，在政治上并不天然兼容。
7. 他以美国以世界 0.5% 的人口产出 25% 的 GDP 作结，称之为「奇迹」；芒格则警告，把进步当成理所当然而非需要争取之物，这种态度是「毒药」。

## 关键引述

> **“nobody wants to hollow out the whole country”**
>
> 「没有人想把整个国家掏空。」

> **“if you try and take all the pain out, you'll also take all the gains out of it”**
>
> 「如果你想拿走全部痛苦，你也会拿走全部收益。」

> **“It's man's nature to take the progress as a right.”**
>
> 「人的本性是把进步当成一种权利。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:12:27）：好。接下来我们去一号提问站，看看能不能——

**Audience Member**（02:12:32）：巴菲特先生、芒格先生，你们好。我叫 Connor，是诺丁汉大学的经济学学生。今天我想问的是：疫情期间我们目睹了供应链短缺，尤其是来自亚洲的短缺。结果是，企业带着政治意图选择把生产迁走。企业应该做这类决定吗？政府应该支持它们吗？

**Warren Buffett**（02:12:58）：查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:59）：嗯，这是个好问题。

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:01）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:02）：显然这是合乎逻辑的——如果你在做生意，而你能在墨西哥以便宜得多的成本生产，那在墨西哥开厂、拿到更便宜的零件是很自然的。很多汽车制造商正是这么做的。另一方面，没有人想把整个国家掏空，让所有制造业岗位都在别处，而我们都像一群农民那样生活：你知道，就像 1820 年前后的英国殖民地那样。而这些想法当然存在巨大的张力。我们的海外生产并没有那么多，对吧，沃伦？

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:43）：对。不过我们已经失去了——嗯，最初的伯克希尔·哈撒韦作为一家纺织制造商，是因为南方相对北方变得可行而失去了竞争力。然后当然，最终——

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:54）：南方变贵了——

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:56）：南方变——

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:58）：——就转到了中国。

**Warren Buffett**（02:14:00）：当然。社会因此受益，而在这种情况下有些人被淘汰了。一个富裕的社会应该以某种方式照顾好那些在我们的鞋厂工作过的人、在我们纺织公司工作过的人。我是说，如果你在我们 1964 年接手时的纺织业务里工作，我们一半的工人只会说葡萄牙语。你知道，他们的工资一点也不高。但那时你可以在南方做，我们注定要倒闭。这完全不是工人的错，任何意义上都不是。也不是我们的错：我们一直在努力竞争。

**Charlie Munger**（02:14:48）：嗯，那边的田纳西河谷管理局有廉价电力。

**Warren Buffett**（02:14:52）：当然。

**Charlie Munger**（02:14:53）：而税收真的能转化为电力。

**Warren Buffett**（02:14:56）：还有空调改变了一切，因为那些鬼地方热得没法待。所以，是很多因素。但后来它在很多方面转移到海外，总体上国家因此变得更好。但它替代了很多人，而这些人确实无法在生活中做别的事。你不能对一个 55 岁或 60 岁、只会说葡萄牙语的人谈「再培训」，还真告诉他们，他们在马萨诸塞州新贝德福德会有「光明的未来」。你知道，所以你不能轻飘飘地谈这件事。而我们负担得起照顾这些人。我们有一些运转得还算合理的制度，但这里存在张力——你知道，那个什么都不做的人怎么办，诸如此类。所以，这些不是容易解决的问题。但我要说，总的来说，我们希望整个世界繁荣，我们不希望美国是一个极度繁荣的国家，而世界其他地方——

**Charlie Munger**（02:16:10）：——在挨饿。

**Warren Buffett**（02:16:10）：不。那行不通。在一个核世界里尤其行不通。所以——作为一个人道的人，你可以有自己的感受，但这件事可以做得更好。我们有资源去做。我是说，这个国家的产出，可以用少得多的人、做更多专业化的事情来完成。当然，事实也正是如此。在我有生之年，美国的工作周已经大幅下降。而人们仍然觉得很忙。人类的命运大概就是问：「我怎么才能把这些事都做完？」但我母亲不会开车送三个孩子去任何地方，我是说，如果你想去哪儿，如果你运气好、年纪够大，你有一辆自行车。而世界总是把一切向上走看成某种基准，反而让人有些不满。以我们的繁荣，我们可以做很多 1930 年做不到的事，包括照顾那些因为别人能做得更好、自身境遇得到改善而被替代的人。我们必须确保我们有最好的制度来照顾那些因此被替代的人。

但在我们现有的政治体制下做这件事，一路上会犯很多错误。但我们必须继续朝这个方向走。我要说——

**Charlie Munger**（02:18:11）：这件事真正有意思的地方在于，亚当·斯密是对的：自由市场资本主义会自动把财产留在私人手中。自由贸易等等会自动创造出不断增长的人均 GDP，帮助所有人，包括最底层的人：对所有人都帮助很大。但这个过程内生的，是自由市场资本主义中的大量痛苦：比如新贝德福德那家纺织公司里的葡萄牙工人。从来没有人想出办法把痛苦全部拿走。我们确实有政府安全网拿掉一部分痛苦，而且随着时间推移我们把安全网做得大一点。但除此之外，如果你想拿走全部痛苦，你也会拿走全部收益。我们不会有人均 GDP 的增长。你会得到一个像俄罗斯那样的经济——有人这样形容它：「他们假装付我们钱，我们假装工作。」（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（02:19:25）：对。其他制度并没有运转得更好，但它也确实造成了越来越多的财富差距。那些什么都不做、只是管理资产、并没有额外创造什么的人，发了大财。政府的职责是保留资本主义最好的部分，同时不让只会说葡萄牙语的人在这个过程中受苦。我是说，这两者在政治上长期并不兼容。我们在社会保障之类的事情上跌跌撞撞地取得进展。我们比我出生时富裕得多。

**Charlie Munger**（02:20:05）：净来看，美国在资本主义增长与不断扩大的社会安全网之间的这种张力上做得非常好。回望过去，我们可以为我们的国家感到相当自豪。

**Warren Buffett**（02:20:19）：对。这也许就是为什么我们以世界 0.5% 的人口，拥有世界 25% 的 GDP，而且是在几个世纪里做到的。我是说，这是个奇迹。这不是因为我们更聪明，但我们必须承认，这套制度一定有什么东西运转得相当好，尽管它曾产生过一场内战和种种事情，你知道？还有女性，你知道，即使在通过第十九修正案之后，也得不到任何机会。但这是一项进行中的工作。我认为实际上是有进步的，但人的本性就是看到其中的问题，如果你——

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:06）：更糟的是：人的本性是把进步当成一种权利。不是需要争取或努力的东西，而是应该自动从横梁上流进来的东西。这种态度是毒药。它对任何人都有害无益。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:12:27）：OK. With that we'll go to station one and see if we —

**Audience Member**（02:12:32）：Hello, Mr. Buffett and Mr. Munger. My name's Connor, I'm an economics student at the University of Nottingham. My question for you today is during the pandemic we witnessed supply chain shortages, especially from Asia. As a result, companies have chosen, with political intentions, to move production away. Should companies make these decisions? And should the government support them?

**Warren Buffett**（02:12:58）：Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:59）：Well, that's a good question.

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:01）：Yep.

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:02）：Obviously, it's logical — if you're in business and you can make the thing in Mexico way cheaper, it's natural to open a factory in Mexico and get your parts cheaper. And a lot of the auto manufacturers have done exactly that. On the other hand, nobody wants to hollow out the whole country, so all the manufacturing jobs are elsewhere and we're all living like a bunch of farmers: You know, like English colonies in 1820 or something. And these ideas are, of course, in big tension. We don't have that much foreign production, right, Warren?

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:43）：Yeah. Well, but we've lost — well, originally Berkshire Hathaway, as a textile manufacturer, lost because the South became feasible versus the North. And, of course, then eventually —

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:54）：The South got expensive —

**Warren Buffett**（02:13:56）：South got —

**Charlie Munger**（02:13:58）：— and moved to China.

**Warren Buffett**（02:14:00）：Sure. And society benefits and some people get killed in that sort of a situation. And a rich society should take care, one way or another, of people that worked in our shoe factories, people who worked in our textile companies. I mean, if you worked in our textile operation, in 1964 when we took it over, half our workers only spoke Portuguese. And, you know, they weren't getting great wages at all. But now you could do it in the South, and we were doomed to go out of business. And it wasn't the fault of the worker in any way, shape, or form. It wasn't our fault: We kept trying to compete.

**Charlie Munger**（02:14:48）：Well, TVA had cheap power down there.

**Warren Buffett**（02:14:52）：Sure.

**Charlie Munger**（02:14:53）：And tax dollars really can yield power.

**Warren Buffett**（02:14:56）：And air conditioning changed everything because the heat in those damn places was impossible. And so, a lot of things. But then it moves offshore in many ways, and net the country is better off because of it. But it displaces a lot of people who really can't do something else in life. You can't talk about "retraining" somebody that's 55 or 60, and speaks only Portuguese, and really tell them they're going to have "a great future" in New Bedford, Mass. You know, and so you don't want to be glib about it. And we can afford to take care of those people. And we've got some systems that work reasonably well, but there's a tension between — you know, what about the person that doesn't do anything and all that kind of stuff. So, these are not easy problems to solve. But I would say that, by and large, we want the whole world to prosper, we do not want the United States to be a country of extraordinary prosperity, and have the rest of the world —

**Charlie Munger**（02:16:10）：— starving.

**Warren Buffett**（02:16:10）：No. It isn't going to work. And it particularly isn't going to work in a nuclear world. So — and you can have your own feelings about it as a humane person, but it can be done better. And we've got the resources to do it. I mean, the output of this country, one, can be done with a lot fewer people, and doing more specialized things. And, of course, it has been. The workweek in the United States, you know, in my lifetime, has dropped dramatically. And people still feel busy. And it will be the human lot to say, you know, "How can I get all these things done?" But my mother didn't drive three kids anyplace, I mean, if you wanted to go anyplace, if you were lucky and you got old enough, you had a bicycle. And the world just keeps looking at everything moving up as becoming sort of a base that leaves them somewhat dissatisfied. And with our prosperity, we can do a lot of things we couldn't do in 1930, including taking care of people that got displaced by the fact that somebody else can do that work and improve their lot in life. And we've got to make sure that we have the best system that takes care of the people who get displaced by that. But doing that in the political system we have and everything, we'll make a lot of mistakes along the way. But we've got to keep moving in that direction. And I'd say —

**Charlie Munger**（02:18:11）：The really interesting thing about it is that Adam Smith was right: that free market capitalism automatically leaves a property in private hands. And free trade and all that automatically creates GDP per capita that grows and helps everybody, including the people at the bottom: helps everybody a lot. But inherent in the process, there's a lot of pain in that free market capitalism: for instance, for the Portuguese workers in a textile company in New Bedford. And nobody's ever figured out how to take all the pain out of it. We do have government safety nets that take some of the pain out, and we make those safety nets a little bigger as time goes by. But, apart from that, if you try and take all the pain out, you'll also take all the gains out of it. We won't have a growing GDP per capita. You'll have an economy like Russia's, which has been characterized as saying: "They pretend to pay us, and we pretend to work." (LAUGHTER)

**Warren Buffett**（02:19:25）：Yeah. The other systems haven't worked better, but it also produces more and more disparities in wealth. And people that do nothing but get assets under management without actually performing anything extra make fortunes. And, I mean, it's a job of government to keep the best aspects of capitalism, while not causing people that only speak Portuguese to suffer in the process. I mean, the two aren't compatible politically over time. And we stumble along making progress on things like Social Security and all that. And we are a lot better off than we are when I was born.

**Charlie Munger**（02:20:05）：Net, the United States has done a very good job of this tension between capitalistic growth and a growing social safety net. We can be pretty proud of our country, looking backward.

**Warren Buffett**（02:20:19）：Yeah. That may be why we have 25% of the world's GDP starting with a half-a-percent of the population and did it in a few centuries. I mean, it's a miracle. And it wasn't because we're smarter, but we've got to say there must be something to the system that's worked pretty well even though it's produced a civil war and all kinds of things, you know? And women, you know, not getting a shot at anything, you know, even after they passed the 19th Amendment. But it's a work in progress. I think actually there's been progress, but it's mankind's nature to see the things wrong with it if you —

**Charlie Munger**（02:21:06）：It's even worse: It's man's nature to take the progress as a right. Not something to be earned or strive for, but something that should automatically just flow in over the transom. And that attitude is poison. It doesn't do anybody any good.
