## 问题

提问者用橄榄球赛作比喻：四分卫是父母，橄榄球是遗产或家族企业，接球的孩子从没参加过训练，接住球并达阵的概率大约只有 10%。他问，父母该如何为下一代的继承做准备，家族企业又该如何安排创始人离开后的经营权。巴菲特怎么看这个普遍问题。

## 回答要点

1. 橄榄球比喻：四分卫是父母，球是遗产或家族企业，接球的孩子从未参加训练，接球达阵的概率约为 10%
2. 巴菲特家的做法：三个孩子读过遗嘱、理解内容并提出建议之后，他才签字；此事宜在子女成年后进行，他的孩子如今都已 60 多岁
3. 若子女在宣读遗嘱时才第一次听到逝者对各种事情的看法，父母就犯了无法纠正的错误
4. 芒格称伯克希尔的遗产规划很简单——「就拿着那该死的股票」，并称这套办法只适合 95% 的人
5. 巴菲特：想让子女拥有某种价值观，先要自己按那种价值观生活，起草巧妙的遗嘱替代不了言传身教
6. TTI 的保罗·安德鲁斯 61 岁时为企业和员工寻找买家，不愿卖给竞争对手或私募股权公司，最终卖给伯克希尔

## 关键引述

> **“in my family, I do not sign a will until my three children have read it, understand it, and made suggestions.”**
>
> 「在我们家，我的三个孩子没有读过遗嘱、理解它并提出建议之前，我不会签署遗嘱。」

> **“Just hold the goddamn stock.”**
>
> 「就拿着那该死的股票别动。」

> **“if you want to figure out how you want to live your life, you write your obituary and reverse engineer it.”**
>
> 「如果你想弄清楚自己想过怎样的一生，就先写好讣告，然后倒推。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:23）：好，四号提问站？

**Audience Member**（00:56:24）：你好。我是马文·布鲁姆，得克萨斯州沃斯堡的遗产规划律师，你们的很多公司都在那里。事实上，沃伦，我是在我们敬爱的保罗·安德鲁斯的追悼会上认识你的，他是 TTI 的经理。我想听听你对遗产规划领域一个普遍问题的看法。那就是，大多数父母没能让下一代为即将到来的继承做好准备。尤其是，如果遗产中包含家族企业，大多数父母没有做企业传承规划，没有去安排：当创始人不再经营企业的那一天，不是「如果」，而是「何时」，由谁来经营。孩子们没有准备好，不像另一位查尔斯国王，不是查理·芒格国王，（笑声）他为英格兰国王这份工作已经准备了 70 多年。我有时这样描述这种情况：想象一场橄榄球赛。球场一端是四分卫。他技术很好，把一记漂亮的传球扔向球场另一端。

球场另一端是接球手。他们从没参加过训练，不懂比赛规则，不知道怎么作为一个团队配合。他们一无所知。所以，四分卫就是父亲和母亲。橄榄球就是遗产或家族企业，接球手就是孩子们。那么，他们接住球并达阵得分的概率有多大？大概只有 10% 左右。

**Warren Buffett**（00:58:13）：我明白这个画面了。（笑声）（掌声）仅仅因为我的年纪，也在某种程度上因为捐赠承诺这类事情，我大概观察过很多特别富有的家庭，观察过那些问题，而它们都非常具体地取决于那个家庭。在我们家，我的三个孩子没有读过遗嘱、理解它并提出建议之前，我不会签署遗嘱。当然，我的孩子现在都 60 多岁了，如果我在他们 20 多岁时就这么做，效果不会好。这取决于家庭；取决于孩子们彼此的感情。有各种各样的情况。取决于你拥有的是什么类型的企业。所以有上千个变量。但我确实认为，如果孩子已经成年，而宣读遗嘱时他们才第一次听到逝者对各种事情的看法，那父母就犯了一个可怕的错误。我遇到过各种各样的情况，有些人对孩子什么都不说，有些人则想用自己的遗嘱让孩子服从自己的意愿。

他们犯了上百万个错误。而这是你没有机会纠正的那一个。嗯，查理也有很多经验，关于——

**Charlie Munger**（00:59:46）：嗯，在伯克希尔，我们的遗产规划问题很简单。就拿着那该死的股票别动。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:56）：嗯，（掌声）但这不适合所有人，查理。

**Charlie Munger**（01:00:03）：不，它只适合 95% 的人。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:00:09）：我不确定，如果你有几十亿美元，你是否想把它留给所有的孩子。我是说，那是另一个问题——

**Charlie Munger**（01:00:19）：嗯，那是另一个问题。但如果你要把它放在某个地方，我宁愿是伯克希尔股票。

**Warren Buffett**（01:00:27）：是的，哦，你解决的是投资那一半问题。但你还有个人层面的问题：他们四岁的时候，一个孩子拽了另一个孩子的猫尾巴，或者类似的事。我是说，你面对的是人。你最想要的是孩子们能和睦相处。你希望这一辈子都如此，而遗产并不是唯一可能把这件事搞砸的地方。我是说，我知道不少案例，当事人不知道遗嘱里写了什么，而涉及金额巨大。你知道，大约 15 分钟内，他们每个人都找了律师，而且从此不相往来。把这件事处理好很重要。如果你希望孩子拥有某些价值观，重要的是你自己按那些价值观生活。重要的是你和他们谈论这些。（笑）从他们出生那天起，他们就在向你学习，学习你真实的样子。不要以为一份起草巧妙的遗嘱可以替代你自己的行为，替代你在教导孩子你希望他们拥有的价值观上的作用。

然后你的遗嘱应当与此配合。他们长大以后，也会学着结合遗产的规模把价值观传下去。如果是家庭农场，那是一回事；如果是一堆可流通证券，那是另一回事。但我知道一个例子，一个特别富有的人每年把孩子聚在一起吃顿饭，想尽办法让他们在空白的所得税申报表上签字，因为他不愿让他们知道家里有多少钱等等。那样行不通。我是说，我也不确定什么方法对他会行得通。但查理和我说过，如果你想弄清楚你想怎样度过一生，你就先写好讣告，然后倒推。你知道，顺便说一句，你提到的 TTI 的保罗·安德鲁斯，他的一生和我认识的任何人一样精彩。他思考过这些问题，他来找我。他当时 61 岁，我想，钱远远超过他所需。他喜欢把钱给人。他有各种各样想做的善事。

他说：「一年来我一直在为我的生意担心，TTI。」他说：「我需要的钱都有了。家里需要的钱也都有了。但这家企业我该怎么办？这些人一辈子都在帮我。」他说：「我可以把它卖给竞争对手。如果我卖给竞争对手，他们合并时会把我的员工裁掉，留下他们自己的员工。如果我卖给私募股权公司之类，他们签文件的时候就在盘算退出策略了。」他说：「不是因为你是什么了不起的人，只是因为你是唯一剩下的那个。」（笑声）我们买下了它，从此幸福地生活下去。那是一个懂得人生是什么的人。

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:23）：OK, station four?

**Audience Member**（00:56:24）：Hello. I'm Marvin Blum, an estate planning lawyer from Fort Worth, Texas, home to many of your companies. In fact, Warren, I met you at the memorial for our beloved Paul Andrews, who was manager of TTI. I'd like to get your thoughts on a widespread problem in the world of estate planning. And that's the failure of most parents to prepare the next generation for the inheritance coming their way. In particular, if the estate includes a family business, most parents fail to do business succession planning to plan for who'll run the business on the day when, not if, the founder is no longer there to run it. The kids aren't prepared, unlike the other King Charles, not King Charlie Munger, (Laughter) who has been preparing for his job as King of England now for more than 70 years. I sometimes describe the situation like this: Picture a football game. At one end of the field is a quarterback. He has great skills; he throws a beautiful pass to the other end of the field. And at the other end of the field are the receivers. They've never been to a practice, they don't know the rules of the game, they don't know how to work together as a team. They're clueless. So, the quarterback is the patriarch and the matriarch. The football is the inheritance or the family business, and the receivers are the kids. So, what are the odds that they're going to catch the football and go score a touchdown? Probably only around 10%.

**Warren Buffett**（00:58:13）：I have the picture. (Laughter) (Applause) Just because of my age, and to some extent because of things like the giving pledges, I probably observed as many particularly wealthy families, the problems, and they all are very particular to the family. And in my family, I do not sign a will until my three children have read it, understand it, and made suggestions. Now, my children are in their 60s and that would not have been a great success if I'd done the same thing in their 20s. It depends on the family; it depends on how the kids feel about each other. There's all kinds of things. Depends on the kind of business you have. So, there's a thousand variables. But I do think that if the children are grown, and when the will is read to them, it's the first they've heard about what the deceased thought about things, the parents have made a terrible mistake. And I've run into all kinds of situations, and some people don't tell their children anything, and some of them try and get them to bend to their will by using their own personal will. They make a million mistakes. And that's one you don't get to correct. Well, Charlie's had a lot of experience too with the —

**Charlie Munger**（00:59:46）：Well, at Berkshire we have a simple problem of estate planning. Just hold the goddamn stock. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:56）：Well, (Applause) but that doesn't fit everybody, Charlie.

**Charlie Munger**（01:00:03）：No, it only fits 95%. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:00:09）：I don't know necessarily whether if you have billions of dollars, you want to leave it to all of your children. I mean, that's something —

**Charlie Munger**（01:00:19）：Well, that's another question. But if you're going to place it somewhere, I'd just as soon have Berkshire stock.

**Warren Buffett**（01:00:27）：Yeah, oh, you're solving the investment problems one. But you have the personal problem of the fact that when they were four, one of the kids pulled the other kid's cat's tail or something like that. I mean, you're dealing with human beings. And the biggest thing you want is you want your children to get along. And you want that all through your life, and the estate isn't the only place where you can mess that up. But I mean, I know a number of cases where the people did not know what was in the will where there were huge sums involved. And you know, within about 15 minutes each one of them had a lawyer, and you know, they don't get along since. It's important to handle it target. And if you want your kids to have certain values, it's important that you live those values. It's important that you talk about it to them. (Laugh) They're learning from you from the day they're born, what you're really like. And don't think that a cleverly drawn will substitute for your own behavior in teaching your kids the values you hope that they will have. And then your will should be in conjunction with that. And they grow older, and then they learn to pass along their values in connection with the size of the estate. If there's family farms it's one thing, if it's a bunch of marketable securities it's something else. But I know one instance by a particularly rich fellow that once a year he'd get his kids together, and have a dinner, and do all kinds of things to get them to sign their income tax returns in blank, because he didn't want them to know how much money they had and everything. Well, that isn't going to work. I mean, I don't know necessarily what would've worked with him. But Charlie and I have said, if you want to figure out how you want to live your life, you write your obituary and reverse engineer it. You know, and Paul Andrews incidentally, who you mentioned at TTI, lived as great a life as anybody I've known. And he thought about these problems, and he came to me. He was 61, I think, had all the money way beyond what he needed. He liked to give it to people. He had all kinds of good things he wanted to do. And he said, "For a year I've been worried about my business, TTI." And he said, "I have all the money I need. The family has all the money they need. But what do I do with the business? These people have helped me throughout my life." And he said, "I can sell it to a competitor. And if I sold it to a competitor, they'd fire my people and keep their people when they put it together. "And if I sell it to a private equity firm or something, they'll be figuring out their exit strategy as they sign the papers." And he said, "It isn't that you're such a great guy, it's just that you're the only one left." (Laughter) And we bought it, and we lived happily ever after. And that was a man that knew the life was about.
