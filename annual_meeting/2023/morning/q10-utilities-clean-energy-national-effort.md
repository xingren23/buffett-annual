## 问题

提问者来自中国，注意到越来越多的人关注环境保护，政府也在支持新能源产业。他问巴菲特和芒格如何看待新能源的持续发展，新能源企业未来怎样才能发展得更好。

## 回答要点

1. BHE 目标：2030 年碳排放较 2005 年下降 50%，对标《巴黎协定》对电力行业的要求
2. PacifiCorp 跨六州；原估 60 亿美元的输电项目建成略超三分之一，实际支出接近 70 亿美元
3. BHE 未来十年有 700 亿美元已知项目，用于服务客户和能源转型
4. 美国没有全国统一方案，实际逐州制定计划，再跨州整合
5. 巴菲特：美国输电建设落后于应有水平，跨州面临不同管辖机构
6. 巴菲特以二战为例：以每年 1 美元请企业人士进华盛顿，重排工业产能造船只、坦克和飞机；和平时期 50 个州难以复制这套机制

## 关键引述

> **“At the time it was a $6 billion transmission project. Today we have a little more than a third of it built. And we've spent probably closer to $7 billion.”**
>
> 「当时这是一个 60 亿美元的输电项目。今天我们建成了略多于三分之一。而我们大概已经花了接近 70 亿美元。」

> **“So, that's the Paris Accord and the standard they want to hold the utility industry or the utility companies to.”**
>
> 「这就是《巴黎协定》，也是他们希望用来要求电力行业或电力公司的标准。」

> **“But we haven't created the unity of purpose and the machinery that worked in World War II”**
>
> 「但我们没有创造出二战时那种目标的一致性和那套机制。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:18）：好，五号提问站。

**Audience Member**（01:09:19）：沃伦先生、芒格先生，你们好。我叫 Soo Jing Hua（音），来自中国，来自一家公司。首先，我今天来到这里非常激动，也非常荣幸。我的问题是：现在越来越多的人关注环境保护，政府也在支持新能源产业，那么你们对新能源的持续发展有什么看法？新能源企业未来怎样才能实现更好的发展？

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:58）：是的，嗯，我想格雷格最适合回答，因为自从我们买下一家叫 MidAmerican、现在叫伯克希尔哈撒韦能源的公司以来。他每年都在谈这件事，准备报告，希望我们能帮助解决一些相关问题。我猜，我们在这上面花的钱比美国任何一家公用事业公司都多。

**Greg Abel**（01:10:23）：绝对是。

**Warren Buffett**（01:10:24）：而且我们才刚刚触及表面。但当你跨越州界时，这并不容易。我是说，你有不同的管辖区域。这个国家在输电方面本该比现在更超前。我们一直是推动这件事的最大因素。不过，你何不给他们讲一点？

**Greg Abel**（01:10:47）：好的，谢谢沃伦。毫无疑问，全球正在发生一场能源转型，正如沃伦提到的，美国也是。在某些方面，我希望在美国我们至少能有一个全国性的清晰方案，来应对这件事。但现实是，除了少数例外，这是逐州进行的。所以，当你想到伯克希尔哈撒韦能源，我们在美国拥有三家公用事业公司。它们都参与多个州。但它们是逐州制定计划，然后再试图在各州之间整合。机会是巨大的，因为转型正在发生。我们已经列出了 BHE 在碳排放方面的目标：到 2030 年，碳排放量相对 2005 年减少 50%。这就是《巴黎协定》，也是他们希望用来要求电力行业或电力公司的标准。我们正走在这条路上。但要实现它，是一段真正的旅程。我经常和沃伦谈。我们在 2000 年代中期买下 PacifiCorp 时，立刻意识到要建大量可再生能源，就像我们在中西部和艾奥瓦一直在做的那样。

但那基本上是在一个州内。而现在 PacifiCorp，我们在六个州。我们从 2000 年代中期开始做这件事。现在到了这一步。我们制定了一份很好的输电计划。这里是我们打算怎么建。这里是我们打算怎么落实，以及这段时间里给客户带来的所有好处。现在是 2023 年，我们建成了略多于三分之一。当时这是一个 60 亿美元的输电项目。今天我们建成了略多于三分之一。而我们大概已经花了接近 70 亿美元。结果是对的。对我们的客户来说，这仍然是一个很好的结果。但作为转型的一部分，你绝对必须建它，才能输送所有这些可再生能源。这就是沃伦所强调的那种复杂性。你不可能某天早上醒来就把这个问题解决。你先从输电开始，然后再建电源。

但在同一家公司，如果我们看整个 BHE 能源正在做的事和那场能源转型，我们有 700 亿美元的已知项目，这些项目确实是为恰当服务客户、并在那些公用事业中实现那种能源转型所必需的。而这发生在未来十年。所以，我们有一支绝对能应对挑战的团队。他们在兑现承诺。对我们的每一家公司、对我们的股东来说，这都是一个很好的商业机会。因为当我们投入这些资本时，我们显然能赚到股权回报。但这将是一段漫长的旅程。它会在很长一段时间里发生。而且你走得越远，就越依赖于各种技术的演进——这些技术在进步，但还没有到位。

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:37）：你提出了一个问题。我想多用一分钟，因为这件事太重要了。我真的不知道我们的政府形式在解决你描述的问题上是否理想。我们曾经解决过一次。二战时，我们带着一个半瘸半拐的国家，发现自己身处一场世界大战。我们在世界大战中所做的，是把一群人请到华盛顿，一年 1 美元。我不知道是西德尼·温伯格还是高盛的谁。你随便点名。我们给了他们巨大的权力，去重新配置美国的资源，来面对他们面对的问题，也就是造出一台战争机器。他们所做的，是找到亨利·凯泽，让他造船。他们去找福特汽车公司，说：「你们造坦克，也造一些飞机。」他们以一种令人难以置信的方式重新安排了美国的工业体系。因为他们拥有联邦政府的权力。他们拥有美国企业的才智。他们拥有美国企业的设施。

这带来了非常成功的结果。但在和平时期，我们有 50 个州，你必须让他们合作，我们能这样做吗？你可以下命令。但你无法指定资本流向哪里。那是另一端的事。你知道，我们试图用税收优惠之类的手段来做。但我们没有创造出二战时那种目标的一致性和那套机制——当时基本上每个人都觉得自己的唯一工作就是赢下战争。我们想办法用我们的工业能力，实际上击败了轴心国。而你要如何在如今的民主制度下重现这一点？我不确定我知道答案。但我确实知道问题在哪。我认为，如果你手上有一场紧急状态，我是说，你真的需要重新设计美国的能源系统。我不认为你能在没有某种类似那套机制、那种紧迫感的情况下做到。资本在那里。人在那里。目标显而易见。我们似乎就是无法在和平时期做到——在和平时期，我们习惯于遵循既定的一套程序。

你知道，中国是一个国家。我们有 50 个州。我们的政府体制完全不同。我们应该经得起这个考验，但到目前为止还没有成功。所以，谢谢你的提问。

**Greg Abel**（01:10:47）：Sure, thanks, Warren. So, there's no question there's an energy transformation going on around the globe and as Warren touched on, in the U.S. And in some ways, I would hope here in the U.S. we'd at least have a clear plan across the nation as to how to approach that. But the reality is it is state by state with some exceptions. So, as a result, when you think of Berkshire Hathaway Energy, we own three U.S. utilities there. And they all participate in multiple states. But they're developing plans state by state and then trying to integrate them across the various states. The opportunities are significant because there is a transformation going on. We've outlined our goal on where we're going relative to carbon at BHE where they'll by 2030 reduce their carbon footprint by 50% relative to 2005. So, that's the Paris Accord and the standard they want to hold the utility industry or the utility companies to. And we're well on that path. But to achieve it is a true journey. I've often talked to Warren. When we bought Pacific back in the mid-2000s, we immediately recognized to build a lot of renewable energy like we've been doing in the Midwest and Iowa. But that was basically in a single state. Now PacifiCorp, we're in six states. We started that back in the mid-2000s. Here we are. And we laid out a great transmission plan. Here's how we're going to build it. Here's how we're going effectuate it, and all the benefits for our customers over that period of time. Here we are in 2023, and we have a little more than a third of that. At the time it was a $6 billion transmission project. Today we have a little more than a third of it built. And we've spent probably closer to $7 billion. And it's the right outcome. It's still a great outcome for our customers. But as part of the transformation, you absolutely have to build it to move all that renewable energy. And that's sort of the complexity Warren was highlighting. You can't just wake up one day and solve this problem. You start with transmission, and then you build the resources. But at that same company, and if we look at what we're doing across BHE Energy and that energy transformation, we have $70 billion of known projects that are really required to properly serve our customers and achieve that type of energy transformation across those utilities. And that's in the coming ten years. So, we have a team that's absolutely up to the challenge. They're delivering on their commitments. And it's a very good business opportunity for each of our companies and for our shareholders. Because as we deploy that capital, we obviously earn a return on equity of it. But it will be a long journey. It'll happen over an extended period of time. And the further you get out there, the more dependent upon the evolution of a variety of technologies that are progressing, but not there yet.

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:37）：You've raised a question. I want to just take an extra minute now because it's so important. And I don't really know whether our form of government is ideal at all in terms of solving the problem you describe. We have solved it one time. In World War II, we took a country that was semi-limping along. And we found ourselves in a world war. And what we did in a world war is we brought a bunch of people to Washington at a $1 a year. I don't know whether it was Sidney Weinberg or Goldman Sachs. You just name them. And we gave them enormous power to reorient the resources of the United States to face the problem that they faced, which was to create a war machine. And what they did was they found Henry Kaiser, you know, and told him to build ships. And they went to the Ford Motor Company and said, "You build tanks and some airplanes." And they reordered the industrial enterprise of the United States in a way that was unbelievable. Because they had the power of the federal government. And they had the ingenuity of American business. And they had the facilities of American business. And it led to a very successful outcome. But can we do that in a peace time where you've got 50 states and you have to get them to cooperate? And you can issue orders. But you can't designate where the capital goes. It's the other end. And, you know, we try and do it with tax incentives and all of that sort of thing. But we haven't created the unity of purpose and the machinery that worked in World War II where essentially everybody felt their one job was to win the war. And we figured out how to use our industrial capacity to in effect defeat the Axis powers. And how do you recreate that with, you know, the present democratic system? I'm not sure I know the answer. But I sure know the problem. And I think that if you've got an emergency on your hands, I mean, you really need to re-engineer the energy system of the United States. I don't think you can do it without something resembling the machinery, the urgency, whatever. The capital's there. The people are there. The objective is obvious. And we just don't seem to be able to do it in a peace time where we're used to following a given set of procedures. And, you know, China's one country. And we've got 50 states. And we got a whole different system of government. We should be up to the test, but so far it hasn't worked. So, thank you for the question.
