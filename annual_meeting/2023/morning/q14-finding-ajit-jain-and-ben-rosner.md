## 问题

这位股东说，大家来到现场的原因之一，是两位很会讲故事，股东们会把这些故事带回家。他请求巴菲特和芒格分享几个此前可能没听过的故事，讲讲格雷格·阿贝尔和阿吉特·贾因，以展现他们的品格和作为领导者的水准。

## 回答要点

1. 贾因 1986 年在一个周六走进办公室，由迈克·戈德堡引荐；巴菲特自 1969 年起就想做再保险，已经摸索了 17 年而无进展。
2. 贾因此前毫无保险经验，但做过管理咨询，了解美国企业如何运作；谈过之后巴菲特说自己「知道挖到了金矿」。
3. 巴菲特称，即使把全球最顶尖的十位保险经理人全部要来，也换不了一个贾因；他并强调招聘时从不看对方上过什么学校。
4. 芒格回忆路易斯·文森蒂体重仅 155 磅却在斯坦福打上主力橄榄球，问他缘故，回答是「我跑得挺快」。
5. 本·罗森纳念到四年级就辍学、完全自学，比任何人都懂老社区的零售业，像鹰一样盯着生意；他去世后，那种能力也随之消失。
6. 罗森纳与合伙人利奥·西蒙在 1933 年芝加哥世博会前几乎白捡一艘一战潜艇，拖着它穿过芝加哥街头造成堵塞，警察询问时他说「你得跟我合伙人卡彭先生谈」；西蒙 1967 年前后去世后，他仍把一半利润交给其遗孀，直到对方开始指责他，才以 600 万美元把生意卖给巴菲特。

## 关键引述

> **“if I had the top pick of ten insurance managers in the world, I could take all ten and you can't replace Ajit.”**
>
> 「如果让我在全球挑选十位最好的保险经理人，我可以把十个都要来，也换不了一个阿吉特。」

> **“But he isn't Ajit because he went to those schools.”**
>
> 「但他成为阿吉特，并不是因为他上了那些学校。」

>
> 「你得跟我合伙人卡彭先生谈。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:08）：好。（掌声）第七号提问站。

**Audience Member**（01:35:12）：巴菲特先生、芒格先生，感谢你们这个周末接待我们。我叫 Beau Clayton（音），来自北卡罗来纳州达勒姆。我们大家来到这里的原因之一，是你们很会讲故事，我们把这些故事带回家。能否请你们分享几个也许我们此前没听过的故事（笑声），讲讲阿贝尔先生和贾因先生，展现他们的品格和作为领导者的水准？（掌声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:54）：好，我先从阿吉特讲起。（笑声）他 1986 年走进办公室。我大概是在 1969 年有了进入再保险业务的好主意。所以我已经摸索了 17 年。当时有位很出色的人负责这块业务，但他也偏爱某些经纪人。我的意思是，他按传统方式经营，质量一流等等，但他没有试图改变这套体系，只是在某种程度上改进它。我们毫无进展，在荒野里游荡了 17 年。我认为我们本可以做出好东西。后来阿吉特在一个周六来了，我想是迈克·戈德堡（音）把他引来的。迈克因为这个举动值得被永久铭记。（笑声）我和他聊了一会儿。我记得那个周六我可能一边和他说话一边在拆邮件。他完全没有保险经验。但他对美国的公司如何运作有不少了解，因为他做过管理咨询。和他谈过之后，我知道我挖到了金矿。

于是我雇用了他，给了他一些资金支持。我们几乎立刻就赶上了市场上一段非常好的时期，让他得以施展。阿吉特，你知道，如果让我在全球范围内挑选十位最好的保险经理人，我可以把十个都要来，也换不了一个阿吉特。我们现在仍然享受交谈。我们不像以前那么频繁，但以前几乎每天都聊。他是独一无二的。你知道，如果你活得够久，你只需要一个独一无二的人。（笑声）保罗·安德鲁斯（音）留在 TTI，他拥有世上所有的钱。每次我跟他谈加薪之类的事，他都说：「我们明年再谈这个。」他不是那种你从顶尖商学院挑状元会得到的人。我要说这一点：在招聘时，我从来不看任何人上过什么学校。我的意思是，有人给我寄简历之类，我不管他们上的是哪所学校。阿吉特碰巧上了几所相当好的学校，但他成为阿吉特，并不是因为他上了那些学校。

查理，你可以讲一两个故事。你是怎么发现路易斯·文森蒂的？（笑声）

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:08）：嗯，他就在那里。但你得能认出他来。我曾问过路易斯，他体重只有 155 磅，是怎么在——我想是斯坦福——打上主力橄榄球的。他说：「嗯，」他说，「我跑得挺快。」他确实跑得挺快。但（笑声）我们在自己的公司里发现了很多跑得挺快的人。

**Warren Buffett**（01:39:37）：是啊，我们有个家伙念到四年级就退学了，对吧？本·罗森纳。我没记错吧？

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:44）：哦，对。完全自学。在那些老社区里，本·罗森纳比任何人都更懂零售业。他像鹰一样盯着自己生意里的一切，他很了不起。这就是一个例子。我们再也没找到能做成他所做之事的人——本去世后，那种能力就离开了我们。

**Warren Buffett**（01:40:05）：是啊，你想听个挺有意思的故事，因为本·罗森纳有个合伙人，利奥·西蒙。利奥·西蒙是莫·安嫩伯格的女儿女婿。因此利奥非常、非常、非常富有。而本起步时一无所有。但他们彼此喜欢。很久以前，在他们涉足我们后来买下的那门生意之前，他们想到一个主意：买一艘一战时期的潜艇，运到「进步世纪」博览会，实际上就是世博会，我想是 1933 年的芝加哥。他们几乎没花钱就买下了那艘潜艇。他们盘算，一个从奥马哈来、第一次逛世博会的普通人，花 25 美分什么的就能看一艘潜艇，应该会掏钱。于是他们把它从佛罗里达，不管是从哪儿弄到的，拖到了芝加哥。然后他们进了芝加哥，拖着潜艇在芝加哥街头走。（笑声）你知道，那造成的交通问题没人能想象。

一个警察走过来，对本说：「你们打算把这艘潜艇弄到哪儿去？」（笑声）本说：「你得跟我合伙人卡彭先生谈。」（笑声）警察说：「行吧，你们继续走。」（笑声）这就是本·罗森纳。后来利奥·西蒙去世了，大概是 1967 年，本·罗森纳继续把一半利润交给他的遗孀。她当然极其富有，是莫·安嫩伯格的长女。我想莫一连生了九个女儿，之后才有沃尔特，第十个。我可能差一个。总之，我去过她那套豪华公寓。不管怎样，本一直让她占这笔交易的一半。他还让她签租金支票，好让她看起来在这门生意里做了点什么。她显然不需要这笔钱，但他就是觉得，合伙人利奥一死，自己就有义务。后来她开始指责他。那时本去找他的律师，其实也是她的律师，威尔·萨尔斯泰纳（音）。

我不知道威尔后来怎么样了。但他给我打了电话，因为本想让我给他打电话，因为本想让我买下这门生意。他想，如果我买下，他就能摆脱前合伙人的妻子。他让我和查理过去，我们去了威尔·萨尔斯泰纳的办公室。本说：「我干到年底就结束，就这样。但我把这东西卖给你，600 万美元。」而当时我有 200 万美元现金、几百万房地产、几百万经营收益。这简直是疯了。但他觉得，如果自己拿的是个烂价格，她也就只能从这烂价格里分一半，而且只出了一半的钱。他当时看了我一眼。查理，剩下的你来讲。（笑声）

**Charlie Munger**（01:43:46）：他说：「我听说你是西部拔枪最快的人。」他说：「拔枪吧。」（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:43:55）：我们当时在纽约一位律师的办公室里。（笑声）而这个家伙正在卖掉他的宝贝，还告诉我们他要走了。我把查理拉到一边，说：「如果这家伙年底真的走了，你可以把所有写过的心理学书都扔掉。我的意思是，那不可能发生。」于是我们买下了它，之后和本一直过得很快乐。有一次他带我去看我们在布鲁克林的一处房产。路上我说：「本，你知道，刚开始时我答应过你，不干预这门生意。」他知道后面要跟一个「但是」。他只是说：「谢谢你，沃伦。」然后就把我堵住了。（笑声）他很有意思。我们有很多本·罗森纳的故事，但你们现在听到的这个，是此前没有发表过的。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:08）：OK. (Applause) Station seven.

**Audience Member**（01:35:12）：Mr. Buffett and Mr. Munger, thank you for having us this weekend. My name is Beau Clayton (PH), and I'm from Durham, North Carolina. One of the reasons that we are all here is that you're great storytellers. And we carry those stories back home with us. Can you please share a couple stories that maybe we haven't heard before (Laughter) about Mr. Abel and Mr. Jain that capture their character and their caliber as leaders? (Applause)

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:54）：Well, I'll start out with Ajit. (Laugh) He walked into the office in 1986, and I'd gotten the bright idea of going into the reinsurance business I think in maybe 1969. So, I'd stumbled along for 17 years. And I had a wonderful guy that ran it. But he also liked certain brokers. I mean, he was running it the traditional way. Top quality and everything else, but he didn't try and change the system. He tried to improve the system to some degree. And we went nowhere. Seventeen years wandering around in the wilderness. And I thought we could have something good. And then Ajit came in on a Saturday and Mike Goldberg (PH) had steered him in, I think. And Mike deserves to be enshrined (Laugh) in perpetuity for that act. And I talked with him a while. I think maybe I was opening the mail on Saturday while I talked with him. And he had absolutely zero experience with insurance. But he'd actually seen a good bit about how corporate America operated, because he'd been in management consulting. And after talking with him, I knew I'd struck gold. And so, I hired him and gave him the backing of some money. And we hit a very good period in the market almost right away for him to act. And Ajit, you know, if I had the top pick of ten insurance managers in the world, I could take all ten and you can't replace Ajit. And we still enjoy talking. We don't talk as frequently as we used to, but we used to talk about every day. But he's one of a kind. And, you know, if you're going to stick around long enough, you only need one of a kind. (Laugh) Paul Andrews (PH) stuck around at TTI, had all the money in the world. Every time I talked to him about getting a raise or something of the sort, he said, "We'll talk about that next year." He was not what you get when you get the top draft picks from the leading business schools. And I will say this, I have never looked at where anybody went to school in terms of hiring. I mean, somebody mails me a resume or something, I don't care where they went to school. And it just so happens that Ajit went to some pretty good schools. But he isn't Ajit because he went to those schools. And Charlie, you can tell a story or two. How'd you find Louis Vincenti? (Laugh)

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:08）：Well, he was there. But you've got to recognize him. I asked Louis once how he managed to play first-string football at, I think, Stanford when he only weighed 155 pounds. And he said, "Well," he said, "I was pretty quick." And he was pretty quick. But (Laugh) we have found a lot of people within our companies who are pretty quick.

**Warren Buffett**（01:39:37）：Yeah, we had one guy that quit at fourth grade, didn't we? Ben Rosner. Am I wrong?

**Charlie Munger**（01:39:44）：Oh yeah. Totally self-educated. Ben Rosner knew more about retailing in those old neighborhoods than anybody. And he watched everything in his business like a hawk, and he was amazing. Now there was an example. We never found anybody who could do what — when Ben died, that ability left us.

**Warren Buffett**（01:40:05）：Yeah, and you want a story that's kind of interesting, because Ben Rosner had a partner, Leo Simon. And Leo Simon was Mo Annenberg's son-in-law. And Leo, therefore, was very, very, very wealthy. And Ben started with nothing. But they liked each other. And one time well before they got involved in the business we bought, they got the idea of buying a submarine from World War I and taking it to the Century of Progress, which was the World's Fair, in effect, in Chicago I think in 1933. So, they bought the submarine for practically nothing. And they figured the average guy from Omaha who's going to his first World's Fair could get a submarine for a quarter or something, that they'd pay it. So, they hauled it from Florida, wherever they got it, hauled it to Chicago. And then they got into Chicago, and they were hauling a submarine down the streets of Chicago. (Laugh) And, you know, it was creating traffic problems like nobody could imagine. So, a cop came over and he said to Ben, he says, "Where do you think you guys are going with that submarine?" (Laugh) And Ben says, "You'll have to talk to my partner, Mr. Capone." (Laugh) And the cop says, "You're on. You know, just keep going." And (Laughter) that was Ben Rosner. And then Leo Simon died, and when he died in 1967 or so, Ben Rosner kept delivering half the profits to his widow, who was incredibly rich, of course, being Mo Annenberg's first-born daughter. I think Mo had nine girls in a row before Walter came along, the tenth. I may be off by one. But anyway, I went to his fancy apartment. And anyway, Ben kept her in for half the deal. And he had her sign the rent checks just so she would look like she was doing something in this business. And she didn't need the money, obviously, but he just felt he was obligated once his partner, Leo, died. And then she started criticizing him. And at that point, Ben went to his lawyer, who was her lawyer, actually, Will Salsteiner (PH). I don't know whatever happened to Will. But he gave me a call because Ben wanted to call me because he wanted me to buy it. And he wanted me, if I bought it, he'd be rid of the ex-partner's wife. And he had Charlie and me come back, and we went to Will Salsteiner's office. And Ben says, "I'll work until the end of the year, and that's all. But I'll sell you this thing for $6 million bucks." And I had $2 million in cash, and a couple of million in real estate, and a couple million of operating earnings. It was just crazy. But he felt if he was getting a lousy price, she was taking a half of the lousy price for half the money. So, he looked at me at some point. Charlie, you describe the rest of it. (Laugh)

**Charlie Munger**（01:43:46）：He said, "I hear you're the fastest draw in the West." He says, "Draw." (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:43:55）：We're in a New York lawyer's office. (Laugh) And this guy is selling his baby. And he told us he's leaving. I got Charlie on the side, I said, "If this guy leaves at the end of the year, you can throw away every psychology book that's ever been written. I mean, it isn't going to happen." And so, we bought it and we lived happily ever after with Ben. And one time he was taking me over to see a property we had in Brooklyn. And along the way I said, "Ben, you know, I promised you I wouldn't interfere in the business when we started." And he knew a "but" was coming. And he just said, "Thank you, Warren," and then he shut me up. (Laughter) He was a lot of fun. We have so many Ben Rosner stories, but now you've heard one that hasn't been published before.
