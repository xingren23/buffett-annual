## 问题

提问者引述 2022 年年报：伯克希尔将始终持有大量现金和美国短期国债，并避免在不利时点出现被迫的现金需求。他推算，巴菲特去世后 A 股转为 B 股捐给各家基金会，基金会将在 12—15 年内陆续卖出。他担心像卡尔·伊坎这样的公司掠夺者或某个团体趁机吸筹控股，抛弃持现哲学，转向贪婪、鲁莽、高度投机，破坏伯克希尔金融堡垒的地位，也担心子公司经营方式被改变。巴菲特和芒格是否担心？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特估算，若 12 年或 15 年不分红，收购伯克希尔大约需要 1.5 万亿美元，这会限制住任何觊觎者，也没有人能靠自己凑到接近这个数目。
2. 他说格雷格·阿贝尔和董事会会有一段很长的「蜜月期」，因为其余投票权仍然存在；但最终他们仍要按经营表现被评判。
3. 他强调伯克希尔应被视为「国家资产」而非「国家负债」，只要配得上赢就会赢，并称这发生的概率「非常、非常、非常高」。
4. 芒格表示不花时间担心自己死后 50 年才发生的事，「每一天的责任其实很轻」，并认为这种担心没有必要。
5. 巴菲特回顾 58 年来商业模式被多次修改，最初很快确定不该做纺织公司；公司不会做致命的决策，不会有大额债务集中到期，也不会有可被大规模兑现的保单。
6. 他认为伯克希尔拥有无人能及的股东基础，这是 58 年把股东当作公司主人对待的结果。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（01:29:00）：好，贝基。

**Becky Quick**（01:29:04）：这个问题来自 Dennis DeJaniero。正如沃伦在 2022 年年报中所说，伯克希尔将始终持有大量现金和美国短期国债。它也会避免可能导致在不方便的时候出现令人不安的现金需求的行为，包括金融恐慌和史无前例的保险损失。沃伦去世后，他的 A 股将转换为 B 股，分配给多家基金会。这些基金会随后会卖出股份，为其事业提供资金。沃伦估计，他的全部股份需要 12—15 年才能卖完。我担心，像卡尔·伊坎这样的公司掠夺者，或者某个团体，会买下足够多的股份来控制伯克希尔，完全无视沃伦持有大量现金和美国短期国债的理念，转而变得贪婪、鲁莽、高度投机，毁掉伯克希尔作为坚如磐石的金融堡垒的地位。我还担心，伯克希尔子公司的经营方式可能会被改变。

沃伦和查理是否担心这些情况会发生？

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:17）：嗯，我认为可以说我们考虑得很多。但我并不特别担心。确实，格雷格和董事会会在一段很长的时间里拥有蜜月期，因为其他投票权仍然存在。但也确实，最终他们会根据我们经营得比其他人好多少来被评判。如果我们 12 年或 15 年不分红，那么收购它需要大约 1.5 万亿美元。我认为这会限制住那些团体。他们喜欢盘算能拿它借多少钱。（笑声）那行不通。也没有人能靠自己凑到接近这个数目。我认为重要的一点是，伯克希尔被视为一项国家资产，而不是国家负债。以我们的经营方式，我们必须对这个国家是加分的。而我们肯定会有这样一份记录：到那时又长了 12 年或 15 年，用多得多的资本、更多的公司做成。会有更多事情发生，我们数千亿美元的资金可以以大量就业、大量产品、大量行为的方式进入经济。

而且它可以和其他东西相比较。所以，如果我们配得上赢，我认为我们会赢。我认为这发生的概率非常、非常、非常高。查理。

**Charlie Munger**（01:31:58）：嗯，我不会花太多时间去担心我死后 50 年才发生的事。我觉得，如果你多少留心一些，每一天的责任其实很轻。尽量往前想，然后结果怎样就接受怎样。所以我是顺其自然的。但我不——我认为他是在不必要地焦虑。

**Warren Buffett**（01:32:17）：好。基本上我们两个人都不担心。（笑声）但我们会做规划。我们确实做规划。你知道，我心里对伯克希尔一直是怎样的模式有一个模型。58 年里它被修改过很多次。我最初、或者说很快就知道的一件事是：它不该是一家纺织公司。（笑声）那是个重要的决定。（笑声）我的意思是，我们只是随到手的牌去打。我们做过几个非常好的决定。我们永远不会做一个会杀死我们的决定。只有那些威胁到地球的事情，我们无能为力。但我们让自己保持在比任何人都好的状态。我们不会有大额债务集中到期，不会有可以被大规模兑现的保单。我们会持有看起来规模巨大的资本。它确实是巨大的资本。但同时也存在巨大的盈利能力，巨大的多样性，一切。所以，我们的商业模式会被打分，而且是在很多我们乐意与之比较的对象中被打分。

所以我认为，我们交给未来的东西非常稳固。我还认为，我们拥有无人能比的股东基础。我的意思是，据我所知，全国没有哪家公司能这样，除非是在上市前就已经是员工持股的公司之类。但这是 58 年把股东当作公司主人来对待的产物。那意味着什么？意味着让客户满意，意味着被你的社区（掌声）欢迎，而不是被拒之门外。意味着在发生金融危机时，政府觉得有你在更好。因为在某些情况下，你能提供这个国家实际提供不了的东西，而你会守在那里。同时，这对生意也是好事。我们总会遇到这样那样的危机。但只要它们不是挑战地球的那种——那类威胁才让人担心——我们就会是对美国加分的力量。如果我们对美国是加分的，我们就能活下来。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:29:00）：OK, Becky. (Laughter)

**Becky Quick**（01:29:04）：This question comes from Dennis DeJaniero. As Warren stated in the 2022 annual report, Berkshire will always hold a boat load of cash in U.S. Treasury bills. It will also avoid behavior that could result in any uncomfortable cash needs at inconvenient times, including financial panics and unprecedented insurance losses. After Warren passes away, his A shares will be converted into B shares and distributed to various foundations. These foundations will then sell the shares to fund their causes. Warren estimates it will take 12-15 years for all his shares to be sold. I worry that a corporate raider like Carl Icahn or a group will buy up enough of these shares to take control of Berkshire and completely disregard Warren's philosophy of holding a lot of cash and U.S. Treasury bills, and instead be greedy, reckless, and highly speculative and ruin Berkshire's position as a rock-solid financial fortress. I also worry that changes might be made in how Berkshire's subsidiaries are run. Do Warren and Charlie worry that these things could happen?

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:17）：Well, I think it's fair to say we think about it plenty. But I don't worry enormously. It is true that Greg and the directors will have a honeymoon period for a long time simply because other votes that will still remain. But it's true that eventually they will get judged based on how well our operation fares versus others. Now if we don't pay any dividends in 12 or 15 years, you're talking a trillion and a half that it would take to take over. And I think that limits the group. They like to think about how much they can borrow against it. (Laugh) It doesn't work. And there's nobody that can come close to doing it themselves. And I think that the important thing is that Berkshire be regarded as a national asset rather than a national liability. We've got to be a plus to the country with our form of operation. And we certainly have got a record which will then be 12 or 15 years longer, done with much more capital, more companies. More things will have happened where our hundreds of billions can work its way into the economy in terms of lots of jobs, lots of products, lots of behavior. And it can be compared with other things. So, I think we win out if we deserve to win out. And I think the odds of that happening are very, very, very high. Charlie.

**Charlie Munger**（01:31:58）：Well, I don't spend much time worrying about something that happened 50 years ago after I'm dead. I think if you sort of take care. Each day's responsibility is pretty low. And think ahead as well as you can. Then you just take the results as they fall. So, I'm philosophical. But I'm not — I think he's fretting unnecessarily.

**Warren Buffett**（01:32:17）：OK. Neither one of us are worried, basically. (Laughter) But we plan. We do plan. And, you know, I've got a model in my mind of what Berkshire has been the model. It's been modified plenty of times over 58 years. The one thing I knew initially or very quickly was it shouldn't be a textile company. (Laugh) That was an important decision. (Laughter) And, I mean, we've just played the hand as it came along. And we made a few really good decisions. We'll never make a decision that kills us. Only things that are a threat to the planet, we don't have any answer for those. But we keep ourselves in better shape than anybody else. And we just aren't going to have big maturities of debt that come along. We aren't going to have insurance policies that can be cashed in en masse. And we will sit with what looks a huge amount of capital. And it is a huge amount of capital. But there's a huge amount of earning power. There's a huge amount of diversity, everything. So, our business model will be graded, and it'll be graded against a lot of people that we like to be graded against. So, I think we're handing something very secure over to the future. And I think we've got a shareholder base like nobody has. I mean, there isn't anybody in the country that I know of unless they've had a employee-owned company prior to going public or something of the sort. But this is the product of, you know, 58 years of regarding the shareholder as the owner of the company. But what does that mean? That means having happy customers. It means being (Applause) welcomed by your community rather than having them turn you away. It means that the government feels better with you if there's a financial crisis. Because you can provide something that actually the country can't under some circumstances. And you'll be there. And at the same time, it'll be good for the business. And we will have crises of one sort or another. But if they aren't challenging the planet, which worries you in terms of some of the threats that we have, we'll be a plus to the United States. And if we're a plus to the United States, we'll survive.
