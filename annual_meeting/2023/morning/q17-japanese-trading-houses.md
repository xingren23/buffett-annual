## 问题

把日本那笔投资的故事再讲一遍，那个故事值得再讲。

## 回答要点

1. 五家商社体量极大、业务可理解，伯克希尔与其中多数以十几种方式打过交道；本地最后一座燃煤电厂就由其中一家建造。
2. 按买入价计算，这个组合的回报率约 14%，同时派发不错的股息，有些还回购股票。
3. 以日元发债融资对冲汇率风险，成本约 0.5%，两端一夹，利差相当可观，且资金是永久的。
4. 持股超过 5% 是在巴菲特 90 岁生日当天宣布的；目前持股 7.4%，未经对方同意不会超过 9.9%。
5. 原计划发行 560 亿日元债券，最终规模约 1644 亿日元。
6. 预计每年为伯克希尔增加约 4 亿—5 亿美元价值；带阿贝尔同行，是为未来 10—40 年的合作铺路。

## 关键引述

> **“They were sitting as a group where they were earning, we'll say 14% on what we were going to pay to buy them.”**
>
> 「作为一个组合，按我们要付的买价计算，它们能赚大约 14%。」

> **“if you get 14% on one side and a half a percent on the other side, and you've got money forever”**
>
> 「一边是 14%，另一边是 0.5%，钱又是永久的。」

> **“We won't go over 9.9% without their agreeing”**
>
> 「未经他们同意，我们不会超过 9.9%。」

**Charlie Munger**（01:55:26）：好，把日本那笔投资的故事讲给他们听。那个故事值得再讲一遍。是个好故事。

**Warren Buffett**（01:55:33）：嗯，其实很简单。我的意思是，当年我开始的时候，别人在翻《花花公子》，我在翻穆迪手册，基本上就是这样。有部片子叫《Turn Every Page》，我前几天看了第二遍，是莉齐·戈特利布拍的。我建议世上每个人都去看，因为我过去就是一页一页翻的。我在穆迪翻过成千上万页，在波士顿公用事业局翻过，在保险监管部门翻过。我就是一直翻页。那样翻能持续一阵子。但现在要找到东西，我们需要大想法。查理，你刚才问什么来着？

**Charlie Munger**（01:56:25）：跟他们讲讲日本那件事。

**Warren Buffett**（01:56:28）：嗯，日本那件事很简单。我的意思是，本和我喜欢看公司。我喜欢看公司的数字。这里有五家非常、非常庞大的公司，业务都能看懂。它们中的大多数，也许是全部，我们都以十几种不同的方式打过交道。你从这个地方往外开几英里，我们最后一座燃煤电厂就是其中一家建的。所以它们就在那儿。作为一个组合，按我们要付的买价计算，它们能赚大约 14%。它们派发不错的股息。有些还打算回购股票。它们旗下有一大堆我们能看懂的生意，当然这不代表我们对任何一块有深入了解。但我们见过它们怎么运营，诸如此类。没什么复杂的。同时，我们可以通过融资把汇率风险对冲掉。成本是 0.5%。一边是 14%，另一边是 0.5%，钱又是永久的，它们还在做聪明的事，体量又够大，那我们就开始买。

我大概在动手半年之后才告诉格雷格。等我们在五家都超过 5%，我在我 90 岁生日那天宣布，我们持股超过 5%。最近我第一次过去拜访他们。结果比我们预期的好得多，我们非常高兴。现在我们持有它们 7.4%。未经他们同意，我们不会超过 9.9%；到最后我们另外卖出去了 1640 亿左右——具体是多少，我也说不准。

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:35）：要是我们只有 50 亿美元之类的，他们也会为我们做这件事。光是这样就赚了 100 亿美元。那我们看起来就像英雄。现在这 100 亿美元就那么消失了，像伯克希尔报表上的一个小点。

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:48）：但很好玩。

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:50）：是很好玩，而且那是 100 亿美元。

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:54）：查理说，跟他聊这件事，能让我不去酒吧（笑声）。我大概是在动手一年之后才跟查理讲的。谁知道呢。我的意思是，我显然知道他喜欢——这很明显。

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:14）：我们想把每一块钱都用上。可我们能做的只有大约 100 亿美元。

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:21）：其实还不到那么多。但你知道，我们已经浮盈 40 亿或 50 亿美元，还不算股息。而且我们拿到一个极好的 carry。他们欢迎我们，他们也应该欢迎我们。但我们很喜欢现在的做法。我们完全不是去告诉他们该怎么做的。但我们确实说过，我们永远不会超过 9.9%，这话算数。他们知道我们说话算话。我过去一趟，部分是为了把格雷格介绍给这些人，因为未来 10 年、20 年、30 年、40 年我们都要和他们在一起。他们偶尔也许会发现一些我们可以一起做的事。他们期待，我们也期待。另外我们在日本还有一些经营业务。那么，格雷格，你有什么要说的吗？

**Greg Abel**（02:00:22）：没有，我只想补一点：第一，沃伦，你过去那一趟，是为了和这些日本公司建立信任。因为我们确实希望有长期机会。但根本上，正如你强调的，这已经是一笔很好的投资。我还要说，我们那五场会面确实很了不起。我的意思是，这些公司，围绕它们的文化和历史，以及它们的那种自豪感——就是有一些向它们学习的时刻。所以，和五家公司实实在在待了两天，是很好的经历。

**Warren Buffett**（02:01:02）：而且我们原本打算发行并卖出 560 亿日元，结果变成了 1644 亿左右。一切都运转得很好。正如查理说的，这对 5000 亿美元的净资产来说不算什么。但这一笔，会在未来多年里持续为伯克希尔增加价值，大概一年 4 亿到 5 亿美元。我们会继续找更多机会。在日本，伯克希尔是日本以外最大的借款方——企业借款人之外最大的那个。我们并没有打算做到那样。但（笑）结果就成了这样。我们还没完。就那边可能出现的局面而言。我在日本也有一些直接经营的业务，刚才提过。我们有很出色的伙伴在为我们工作。我什么都不用做。

**Charlie Munger**（01:55:26）：Well, tell them the story of the Japanese investment. That should be told again. That's a nice story.

**Warren Buffett**（01:55:33）：Well, it was pretty simple. I mean, back when I started other people were going through Playboy and I was going through Moody's, I mean, basically. And there's a movie out called "Turn Every Page," which I saw again for the second time a couple of days ago, Lizzie Gottlieb. And I recommend everybody in this world watch that, because I turned every page in the past. And I did it for thousands and thousands of pages at Moody's, and I did it at the Department of Public Utilities in Boston. I did it in the insurance department. I just kept turning pages. Well, that goes on for a while. But now we need big ideas in order to find things. And what was your question, Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:56:25）：Tell them about the Japanese.

**Warren Buffett**（01:56:28）：Well, the Japanese thing was simple. I mean, Ben and I liked looking at companies. I mean, I like looking at figures about companies. And here were five very, very substantial companies, understandable companies. Most of them, maybe all of them we'd done business with in a dozen different ways. If you go a couple miles from where this place is, our last coal generating plant was built by one of the companies. So here they were. They were sitting as a group where they were earning, we'll say 14% on what we were going to pay to buy them. They were paying decent dividends. They were going to repurchase shares in some cases. They owned a whole bunch of businesses that we could understand as a group, although it didn't mean we had deep understanding on any. But we'd seen them operate and everything. There wasn't anything to it. And at the same time, we could take out the currency risk by financing in the end. And that was going to cost a half of 1%. Well, if you get 14% on one side and a half a percent on the other side, and you've got money forever, and they're doing intelligent things, and they're sizable, so we just started buying them. I didn't even probably tell Greg until maybe six months after we'd gotten going. And then when we hit 5% in all of them, we announced on my birthday, 90th, that we owned over 5%. And recently went over for the first time to visit with them. And we were more than pleasantly surprised, delighted, with what we find there. And now we own 7.4% of them. We won't go over 9.9% without their agreeing, and we sold another $164, whatever it is billion by the end.

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:35）：They would've done it for us if we only had $5 billion or something. And it made $10 billion simply in that way. We would look like heroes. Now $10 billion just sort of disappears as if it's a little dot in Berkshire's reports.

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:48）：But it's fun.

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:50）：It is fun, and it is $10 billion.

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:54）：And Charlie says it keeps (Laughter) me out of bars when I talk to him about it. And I probably talked to Charlie about those the year after I started. But who knows? I mean, I knew he'd like it, I mean, obviously.

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:14）：We tried to do every dollar. We would do — we could only do about $10 billion.

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:21）：Well, not even quite that much. But, you know, we are $4 or $5 billion ahead, plus dividends. And we've got a carry that's terrific. And they welcome us, and they should welcome us. But we love it the way we're operating. We're not there to tell them what to do in the least. But we did say we'd never go over 9.9%, and we mean it. And they know that we'll be true to our word. And I went over there, partly to introduce Greg to those people, because we're going to be with them 10, 20, 30, 40 years from now. And they may occasionally find something that we can do jointly. And they look forward to doing that, and we look forward to it, and in addition we have some other operating businesses in Japan. So, Greg, do you have anything?

**Greg Abel**（02:00:22）：No, the only thing I would add is that 1) as, Warren, you went over there, it was to build the trust with these Japanese companies. Because we do hope there's long-term opportunities. But fundamentally, as you highlighted, they've been a very good investment. I'd also highlight the five meetings we had were really quite remarkable. I mean, these companies, the culture and the history around it, and how proud they are, you know, there's just moments of learning from them. So, it was just a great experience to spend really two days with the five companies.

**Warren Buffett**（02:01:02）：And an issue that we intended to be 56 billion of yen that we were issuing and selling turned out to be 164.4 or something like that. And everything's worked so well. And as Charlie says, you know, it doesn't move $500 billion of net worth that much. But this one, you know, will keep adding over the years to Berkshire's value with this very widespread, probably $4 or $500 million a year. And, you know, we'll just keep looking for more opportunities. And Japan, Berkshire is the largest borrower, outside of corporate borrowers, outside of Japan that exists. And we didn't set out to be that. But (Laugh) it's turned out that way. And we're not done. I mean, you know, in terms of what may come along there. And we have some direct operations there, as I mentioned. And we've got some really wonderful partners working for us. And I don't have to do anything.
