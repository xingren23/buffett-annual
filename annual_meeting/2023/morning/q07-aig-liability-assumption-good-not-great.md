## 问题

2016 年伯克希尔与 AIG 做了一笔独特的交易：承担最多 200 亿美元负债，换取约 100 亿美元前期付款。利率上升之后，这笔交易进展如何？几周前在东京，你谈到资产对利率上升敏感的银行所面临的风险，伯克希尔的负债对久期有多敏感？

## 回答要点

1. 2016 年与 AIG 的交易：承担最多 200 亿美元负债，收取约 100 亿美元前期款，这笔钱没有被限定买债，而是进入统一管理的资产池。
2. 伯克希尔流动资产现为 1300 多亿美元，占美国所有财产意外险公司净资产约 26%，别家保险公司没有这样的资产负债表。
3. 阿吉特：实际赔付是成交时点预测金额的 96%，「算好，但不算很好」，仍领先于曲线。
4. 若最终赔付低于预期，等于以明显低于 4% 的利率借到钱，另赚 100 万美元费用（按 2015 年美元计）；但侵权责任不断出现，他只是「谨慎乐观」。
5. 芒格：伯克希尔旗下财产意外险公司每 1 美元保费背后有四倍于正常水平的股东资本。
6. 巴菲特：每年有 250 亿美元以上来自保险以外、与保险不相关的收益，且不分红；债务在铁路和能源层面，其余业务不靠举债。

## 关键引述

> **“the actual payouts are 96% of what we had projected to pay out at this point in time, which is good but not great.”**
>
> 「实际赔付是我们此刻预计应赔付金额的 96%，这算好，但不算很好。」

> **“We account for 26% or something like that of the net worth of all property casualty companies in the United States.”**
>
> 「我们占到了美国所有财产意外险公司净资产的大约 26%。」

> **“I'm cautiously optimistic that the deal will work out better than how we expected it to work out.”**
>
> 「我谨慎乐观地认为，这笔交易的结果会好于我们当初的预期。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:49:32）：贝基？

**Becky Quick**（00:49:32）：这个问题提给沃伦和阿吉特。来自新泽西州利文斯顿的杰森·普隆纳（音）。他说：「2016 年你们跟 AIG 做了一笔非常独特的交易，承担最多 200 亿美元的负债，换取约 100 亿美元的前期付款。考虑到利率上升，能否介绍一下这笔交易的进展？几周前在东京，你谈到那些资产对利率上升敏感的银行所面临的风险。伯克希尔的负债对久期有多敏感，希望能听到一些见解。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:20）：这是问阿吉特，还是问我，还是谁？

**Becky Quick**（00:50:24）：两位都问。

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:25）：好。我先说一点，但关键在阿吉特。这笔交易是他搭起来的。我们拿到了 100 亿美元，就这么说吧。但我们并没有被限制只能拿去买债券。所以，就 AIG 这笔交易、或者我们跟任何人做类似交易而言，利率在某种程度上会影响我们。但我们不必把它放进期限匹配的债券之类的东西里。它进入我们管理的一个资产总池，现在流动资产是 1300 多亿美元。它并不像人们喜欢想象的那样，被单独划在一个小格子里。没有其他保险公司能做到这一点。他们连这样想都做不到，甚至不习惯这样想。他们不能这样想，是因为他们没有我们这样的资产负债表。我们占到了美国所有财产意外险公司净资产的大约 26%。到目前为止，我们必须支付的款项一直不算大。如果我哪里说错了，阿吉特会纠正我，因为他盯着这些数字。

就我们分担的损失而言，我们已经支付的金额略低于我们预期要支付的金额。但它满足了 AIG 的目的。它找上我们，因为我们处在独一无二的位置。没有别人能承接下来，就像我们接手劳合社那次一样。劳合社当时说，除了伯克希尔·哈撒韦别无选择——他们实际上是通过把大量负债卸给伯克希尔·哈撒韦，才让自己的市场复活。这样的交易不会经常出现。如果规模是 5 亿美元之类，就会有别人进场出更高的价。华尔街人人都在找钱。但如果他们开始谈的是 AIG 那样的交易，现在就没有别的去处。阿吉特，把我说的数字全纠正一遍。（笑声）

**Ajit Jain**（00:52:53）：不用纠正。看这笔交易做得怎么样，一个角度是：成交的时候，我们对每年要赔付多少做过预测。之后我们一直监控交易开始以来的实际赔付，看它跟预期要赔付的金额相比如何。正如沃伦所说，这两个数字非常接近。更具体地说，实际赔付是我们此刻预计应赔付金额的 96%，这算好，但不算很好。我们仍然领先于曲线。如果我们最终赔付少于预期，那我们不仅以非常有吸引力的利率借到了钱——明显低于 4%——还赚了一笔费用，按 2015 年美元计是 100 万美元。所以我们是以低于 4% 的利率借到钱，还赚了 100 万美元费用，比我们原本预期略好一些。所以净下来，我们对这笔交易很满意，很高兴做了它。但这场游戏还没结束。侵权责任每隔一天就会冒出来。

所以我谨慎乐观地认为，这笔交易的结果会好于我们当初的预期。

**Charlie Munger**（00:54:08）：真正有意思的是，在伯克希尔内部，财产意外险公司每 1 美元保费背后，有四倍于常规的股东资本。四倍于正常水平。当然，我们能看到那些大交易。如果你有一大笔负债想甩给别人，你会信任谁？

**Warren Buffett**（00:54:30）：而且我们每年有 250 亿美元甚至更多来自保险以外的业务，与保险不相关，且没有任何义务。我们不分红。你知道，如果分红，然后你削减股息，第二天再想出去写保险试试。这个行业里，人们指望着你赔付。我们拿到那 100 亿美元时，并不承诺把它投进 5 年期、10 年期债券。我们根本不那样想。跟我们做生意的人知道，他们面对的是一个独一无二的对象，不管经济发生什么，都能付得出 100 亿美元。所以，不只是保险公司本身无比强健，还在于我们每个月都有大量盈利进来，债务不多。我是说，我们在铁路和能源层面有债务。但就其余业务而言——我们并不为那些债务提供担保，不过（听不清）。就是没有第二个伯克希尔，阿吉特在谈判时清楚这一点。

只要金额足够大，对方也清楚。有很多人乐意拿到 5 亿或 3 亿美元，他们可能会想着把这笔钱贷出去，因为他们的保险公司能在某个更高的利率上做到。但这不是我们玩的游戏，我们也没兴趣玩。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:49:32）：Becky?

**Becky Quick**（00:49:32）：This question is for both Warren and Ajit. Comes from Jason Plonner (PH) in Livingston, New Jersey. He says, "In 2016, you entered into a very unique transition with AIG where you assumed up to $20 billion of liabilities in exchange for about $10 billion upfront. Can you please provide us with an update on this transition in light of the increase in interest rates? And then in Tokyo just a few weeks ago, you talked about the risks of banks with assets that were susceptible to rising interest rates. Any insight as to how Berkshire liabilities are susceptible to duration would be appreciated."

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:20）：Is that directed to Ajit, or me, or what?

**Becky Quick**（00:50:24）：Both.

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:25）：OK. Let me introduce my one thing, but Ajit is the key to this. He's the one that put the deal together. But we got handed $10 billion, we'll say. But we weren't restricted to putting that into bonds. So, interest rates affect us to some degree maybe, in terms of the deal we did with AIG, or anybody we would do a similar deal with like that. But we don't have to put it in matching bonds or anything of the sort. It goes into a general pool of assets, which we manage, and the liquid assets now are $130 plus. But it is not set aside in some little compartment like people like to think. Now, no other insurance company could do it. But they can't think that way. They aren't even used to thinking that way. But they can't think that way because they don't have our balance sheet. We account for 26% or something like that of the net worth of all property casualty companies in the United States. So far, the payments that we have had to make have run modestly. And Ajit will correct me on this if I'm wrong, because he paid attention. The amount we have had to pay has run slightly below the amount we anticipated having to pay in terms of our share of the losses. But it served AIG's purposes. It came to us where we are in a unique position. There's nobody else that was able to write that, just like when we took on Lloyd's. I mean, Lloyd's said there was no choice other than Berkshire Hathaway when they essentially resuscitated their market by laying off a lot of liabilities on Berkshire Hathaway. So, we won't see those deals very often. If they're for $500 million or something like that, somebody else will go in there and offer more money. And everybody's looking for money on Wall Street. But if they start talking with the deal like the AIG deal, there isn't any other stop now. Correct me on all my numbers there, Ajit. (Laughter)

**Ajit Jain**（00:52:53）：No. One way to look at how the deal is performing since we did the deal is at the point in time when we did the deal, we had made certain projections of how much we will pay out each year. And what we do is monitor what the action payments are since the inception of the deal, and how does that compare with what we expected to pay out. As Warren mentioned, these two numbers are very close to each other. More specifically, the actual payouts are 96% of what we had projected to pay out at this point in time, which is good but not great. We are still ahead of the curve. If we do end up paying out less than what we projected, not only would we have borrowed money at a very attractive rate, meaning significantly less than 4%, in addition to that we would have made a fee, which in 2015 dollars would be $1 million. So, we would've borrowed money at less than 4% and we would've made $1 million fee, which is slightly more than what we were expecting to do. So, net/net, we're very happy with the deal, we're happy we did it. But the game is not over. There are tort liabilities that are coming down the pike every second day. So, I'm cautiously optimistic that the deal will work out better than how we expected it to work out.

**Charlie Munger**（00:54:08）：Well, the really interesting thing is that within Berkshire, the casualty insurance companies have four times as much stockholder capital behind each dollar of premium value. Four times normal. And of course, we see the big deals. Who would you trust if you had a big liability you wanted to dump on somebody?

**Warren Buffett**（00:54:30）：And we have $25 billion or more coming in from things other than insurance, uncorrelated to insurance, every year with no obligations. We don't pay dividends. If we pay dividends, you know, and you cut your dividend, try going around trying to write insurance the next day. I mean, and it's a business where people are counting on you to pay. And when we take that $10 billion, we don't agree to put it in five-year bonds and ten-year bonds. We don't even think that way. And the people that do business with us know that they have somebody like nobody else on those that's going to be able to pay $10 billion no matter what happens to the economy. So, it's not only the presence of enormous strength in the insurance companies, it's the fact we have all these earnings that essentially come in every month, we don't have a lot of debt. I mean, we have debt at the railroad and the energy level. But in terms of the rest of the operation — And we don't guarantee that debt, but it's (UNINTEL). And there just isn't another Berkshire, and Ajit recognizes that when he's negotiating. So does the other party if the sums are big enough. There's all kinds of people that love to get $500 million or $300 million, and they may think in terms of lending it out because that's what their insurance companies can do at a somewhere higher rate. But that is not a game we play, and we don't have any interest in playing in.
