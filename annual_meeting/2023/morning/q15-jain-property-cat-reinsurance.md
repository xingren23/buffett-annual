## 问题

提问者指出，再保险业正经历过去 15 年来最艰难的定价环境之一，而伯克希尔历史上会在这种压力时期、经济回报非常有吸引力时出手。但今年公司似乎无意把资源投入巨灾财险再保险。他想请阿吉特解释在回报可观的情况下为何不参与，并谈谈收购 Alleghany 之后如何规划再保险业务。

## 回答要点

1. 贾因表示 Alleghany 以 TransAtlantic Re 品牌经营再保险，战略与管理层都不会改变，会继续按原有方式运作。
2. 过去 15 年巨灾财险价格不具吸引力，伯克希尔虽有参与但「非常有限」；12 月 31 日续保前他曾乐观，认为能记入数十亿美元保费。
3. 12 月最后十天大量产能涌入，价格未达到公司的定价要求，1 月 1 日续保「令人大失所望」，承保量低于预期。
4. 因为留住了「弹药」，4 月 1 日续保时价格猛涨、高于 1 月 1 日，公司大举承保；如今巨灾暴露比五六个月前多近 50%。
5. 组合非常不均衡：若佛罗里达遭遇大飓风，所有单元合计可能损失高达 150 亿美元；若没有损失，则赚取数十亿美元利润。
6. 以公司接近 3000 亿美元的资本计，可承受的暴露约为 5%；几周前全球暴露约 130 亿美元，他电话请示提到 150 亿，通话不到 30 秒。

>
> 「如果飓风发生在佛罗里达，我们在所有单元合计可能损失高达 150 亿美元。」

>
> 「我们把它看作愿意承担的 5% 暴露。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:02）：好，贝基？

**Becky Quick**（01:45:03）：这个问题来自 Chai Gohill（音）。他写道，这是给阿吉特的。「再保险业正经历过去 15 年来最艰难的定价环境之一。伯克希尔历史上会在这种压力时期、经济回报非常有吸引力时参与其中。今年，尽管回报如此强劲，伯克希尔似乎没有兴趣把资源投入巨灾财险再保险。能否详细说明在回报如此可观的情况下不参与的原因，以及您对收购 Alleghany 之后如何规划再保险业务的更宏观看法？」

**Ajit Jain**（01:45:44）：好。关于 Alleghany，这个回答很简单。我们让各个运营单元彼此独立。就 Alleghany 而言，他们以 TransAtlantic Re 这一品牌在再保险业务中有重要地位。那家公司会按过去的方式经营。战略或管理层都不会有变化。他们会继续做他们在做的事。他们一直非常成功，希望会继续成功。至于我过去几年一直活跃的巨灾财险业务，你说得对，过去 15 年是艰难时期。价格没有吸引力。尽管过去 15 年我们在巨灾财险业务中有一些存在，但实际上非常有限。12 月 31 日是巨灾再保险的一个大续保日，我们原本希望能在阳光下晒几天，能够部署资本，承保一些相当有吸引力的业务。实际情况是，直到 12 月第三周左右，我还非常乐观，认为我们会有机会把几十亿美元记入账上。

但在 12 月的最后十天，不幸的是大量产能冒了出来。我们期望实现的价格并没有真正达到我们的定价要求，结果 1 月 1 日令人大失所望。我们没有写到希望写的那么多。现在快进到 4 月 1 日，那是另一个大续保日，我们手上留了很多弹药。我们很幸运留住了弹药。因为 4 月 1 日价格突然又猛涨，远高于 1 月 1 日的水平，开始对我们有吸引力。所以现在我们的组合对巨灾财险的暴露非常重。打个比方，我们今天的暴露比五六个月前多了近 50%。所以我认为，我们已经写到了产能允许的上限。我们对已承保的业务非常满意，利润率一直健康。我唯一想提的是，尽管利润率健康，我们的组合非常不均衡。这意味着，如果佛罗里达遭遇一场大飓风，我们会有非常重大的损失。

反过来，如果重大损失发生在佛罗里达以外的任何地方，相对于我们的竞争对手，我们的损失会小得多。净下来，我对这个组合非常满意。它比过去好得多。我不知道这能持续多久，当然，如果飓风发生在佛罗里达，我们在所有单元合计可能损失高达 150 亿美元。如果没有损失，我们会赚几十亿美元利润。

**Warren Buffett**（01:48:55）：还有，阿吉特，你打电话给我，说想让我们承担某个数——再多几十亿的暴露——我用了多久才说同意。（笑声）

**Ajit Jain**（01:49:11）：是的，我们考虑暴露的方式是，把整个公司所有保险业务合在一起看。鉴于我们有接近 3000 亿美元的资本——略低于这个数——我们把它看作我们愿意承担的 5% 暴露。所以，把沃伦的故事讲完：几周前，我们在全球的暴露大约是 130 亿美元。我打电话给沃伦，说：「我们已经到 130 亿了。如果能到 150 亿会不错，那是个整数。」那通电话不到 30 秒。（笑声）我想沃伦根本没听数字就说了同意。（笑声）

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:07）：我希望他再打给我。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:02）：OK, Becky?

**Becky Quick**（01:45:03）：This question comes from Chai Gohill (PH). This he writes, this is for Ajit. "Reinsurance industry is going through one of the hardest pricing environments in the last 15 years. Berkshire historically has participated during these stressed times when economic returns are very attractive. "This year, it appears Berkshire has not been interested in deploying its resources towards property cat reinsurance despite such strong returns. Can you elaborate on reasons for not participating despite these returns, and your broader view on how you're planning to shape your reinsurance business post-acquisition of Alleghany?"

**Ajit Jain**（01:45:44）：OK. In terms of Alleghany, that's an easy response. We treat our operating units independent of each other. And as far as Alleghany is concerned, they have a major presence in the reinsurance business under the brand name of TransAtlantic Re. That company will operate the way it's been operating in the past. There will be no change in terms of strategy or management. And they will keep doing what they're doing. They've been very successful, and hopefully will keep being successful. Now, in terms of the property cat business that I have been active in over these last several years, you're right that the last 15 years has been a difficult time. Prices have not been attractive. And even though we have had some presence in the property cat business in the last 15 years, it really has been minimal. This December 31st, which is a big renewal date for cat reinsurance, we were hoping that we would get a few days in the sun, and we'd be able to deploy our capital, and be able to write some fairly attractive business. As it happened towards the end of December, until about the third week of December, I was very optimistic that we would get a chance to put several billion dollars on the books. But in the last ten days of December, unfortunately a lot of capacity came out of the woodworks. Pricing that we were expecting to realize didn't really come and meet our pricing requirements, as a result of which January 1 was a big disappointment. We did not write as much as we were hoping to write. Now, fast forward to April 1, which is another big renewal date, we had a lot of powder dry. And we were lucky that we kept the powder dry. Because April 1, suddenly prices zoomed up again, a lot higher than what they were on January 1, and started to look attractive to us. So, now we have a portfolio that is very heavily exposed to property catastrophe. To put that in perspective, our exposure today is almost 50% more than what it was five, six months ago. So, I think we have written as much as our capacity will allow us to write. We are very happy with what we've written. The margins have been healthy. The only thing that I want to mention to you is that, while the mentions have been healthy, we have a very unbalanced portfolio. What that means is if there's a big hurricane in Florida, we will have a very substantial loss. As opposed to that if we have a very big loss anywhere other than Florida, relative to our competition, we will have a much smaller loss. Net-net, I'm very happy with the portfolio. It is a lot better than what it's been in the past. I don't know how long it'll last, and of course if the hurricane happens in Florida, we could lose, across all the units, as much as $15 billion. And if there isn't a loss, we'll make several billion dollars as profit.

**Warren Buffett**（01:48:55）：And, Ajit, when you called me and said you'd like to expose us to whatever it was, a couple billion more of exposure, how long I took to say yes. (Laugh)

**Ajit Jain**（01:49:11）：Yeah, so the way we think about our exposure is, you know, in the insurance operations collectively across the entire company. Given that we have about a little less than $300 billion of capital, we think of that as a 5% exposure that we're willing to take on. So, to complete Warren's story, a few weeks ago we had about $13 billion of exposure all across the globe. And I called up Warren and I said, "We're up to $13. It'll be nice if we can go up to $15. That's a good round number." And that was less than a 30-second phone call. (Laugh) I think Warren said yes without even listening to what the numbers were. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:07）：I hope he calls me again. (Laughter)
