## 问题

达莫达兰教授在《Investing the Templeton Way》播客里说，他不习惯单一持仓在组合中占到 25%—35%，那已接近危险区。他指出苹果现在占伯克希尔组合的 35%。想请沃伦和查理评论。

## 回答要点

1. 芒格对「占比过高」的说法只回一句：「我看他是脑子坏了。」
2. 巴菲特：苹果并不占伯克希尔组合的 35%，因为组合里还有 BNSF、能源业务、Garanimals、喜诗糖果等实体生意。
3. 苹果持续回购，流通股从约 157 亿股降到 152.5 亿股，伯克希尔不动手，持股比例就从 5.6% 升到 6%。
4. 全资子公司的持股上限是 100%，而苹果可以「免费」增持，这是结构上的好处。
5. 苹果的生意比伯克希尔拥有的任何一家都好；BNSF 是很好的生意，但远不及苹果。
6. 消费者宁愿放弃第二辆车也不放弃 iPhone；几年前为税务原因卖出部分苹果股票，被巴菲特称为愚蠢的决定。

## 关键引述

> **“I think he's out of his mind.”**

> 「我看他是脑子坏了。」

> **“it just happens to be a better business than any we own”**
>
> 「它恰好比我们拥有的任何一家生意都好。」

> **“if they had to give up a second car or give up their iPhone, they'd give up their second car”**

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:02:13）：（笑声）好，贝基？

**Becky Quick**（02:02:17）：下一个问题来自埃莉·阿明·特贝特（音），她问：在《Investing the Templeton Way》播客的一期节目里，达莫达兰教授——她敬重他几乎不亚于敬重沃伦和查理——提到，他不习惯某个持仓在组合里占到太大比重，比如占到 25%—35% 的时候。他说苹果现在占伯克希尔组合的 35%，认为这已接近危险区。想知道沃伦和查理能否评论。

**Warren Buffett**（02:02:54）：嗯，我想先说一句，不过查理会给出他的——

**Charlie Munger**（02:03:00）：我看他是脑子坏了。

**Warren Buffett**（02:03:03）：是啊，我就知道会来这一句。（笑声）苹果并不占伯克希尔组合的 35%。伯克希尔的组合包括铁路、能源业务、Garanimals，你说得出来的，喜诗糖果，它们都是企业。而且，你知道，苹果的好处在于我们可以增持。它们回购自己的股票，流通股从大概 157 亿多股，降到 152.5 亿股，我们什么都没做，持股就变成了 6%。所以，我们不可能持有 BNSF 超过 100%，不可能持有 Garanimals 或喜诗糖果超过 100%。那本来挺好，我们很想持有 200%，但做不到。但这些生意是一样的，都是好生意。认为我们对苹果的标准和旗下其他生意不同，那是误解——它恰好比我们拥有的任何一家生意都好。我们在这上面投了不少钱，但没有投在铁路上的钱多。

而苹果是更好的生意。我们的铁路是很好的生意，但远不及苹果的生意。你知道，苹果在消费者那里有一种地位：他们愿意为一部手机付大概 1500 美元，随便多少。而同样是这些人，愿意为第二辆车付 35000 美元。可如果必须在放弃第二辆车和放弃 iPhone 之间选择，他们会放弃第二辆车。我的意思是，这是非凡的产品。我们没有任何一家 100% 持有的生意是这样的。但我们非常、非常、非常高兴拥有 5.6%，或者不管多少，每上升零点一个百分点我们都很高兴。那相当于我们的利润份额增加 1 亿美元。它们用利润去买掉我们的合伙人，我们也很乐意看到那些人卖。指数基金在流通股减少时（笑）不得不卖。你知道，去年我们的持股略升了一点，而几年前我犯过一个错：我卖了一些股票，当时有某些理由，从税务角度那一年确认收益有好处。

但听我说完你就知道，那是个愚蠢的决定。（笑）查理，你的评论已经给过了。但我们并没有 35% 的伯克希尔组合在苹果上。伯克希尔的组合就是我们要运用的资金。我们想拥有好生意，同时也想持有充足的流动性。除此之外，你知道，上限就是我们的错误。谁知道底在哪儿？（笑）查理，你要补充刚才的话吗？

**Charlie Munger**（02:06:25）：嗯，我认为现代大学教育里教的一件蠢事，就是投资普通股时必须极度分散。这想法是疯的。要找出一大堆容易被识别的好机会，没那么容易。如果你只有三个好主意，我宁愿押在我最好的想法上，而不是最差的。当然，有些人分不清自己最好的想法和最差的想法。而一旦认定某笔投资是好生意，他们就会觉得它比实际更好。我认为我们这类错误犯得比别人少，这是我们的福气。我们没那么聪明，但我们大致知道自己聪明的边界在哪儿。这是实践智慧里非常重要的一部分。很多在智商测试里是天才的人，以为自己比实际聪明得多。他们的实际状态是危险。但如果你相当清楚自己能力的边界，你就该忽略我们那些专家关于组合「去分散化」的大部分说法。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:02:13）：(Laughter) OK, Becky?

**Becky Quick**（02:02:17）：This next question comes from Ellie Amin Tebet (PH), who asks, "During an episode of Investing the Templeton Way podcast, Professor Damodaran, who he respects almost as much as Warren and Charlie, mentioned that he is not comfortable with positions becoming a large part of his portfolio. For example, when they reach 25-35%. He mentioned that Apple is now 35% of Berkshire's portfolio and thinks that that is near a danger zone." Wonders if Warren and Charlie can comment.

**Warren Buffett**（02:02:54）：Well, I'd like to make one comment first, but Charlie will come up with —

**Charlie Munger**（02:03:00）：I think he's out of his mind.

**Warren Buffett**（02:03:03）：Yeah, I knew that was coming. (Laughter) Apple is not 35% of Berkshire's portfolio. Berkshire's portfolio includes the railroad, the energy business, Garanimals, you name it, See's Candies, they're all businesses. And, you know, the good thing about Apple is that we can go up. They buy in their stock, and instead of owning 5.6%, they got about 15 billion, 700 and some million shares outstanding. They get down to 15 and a quarter billion without us doing anything. We got 6%. So, we can't own more than 100% of the BNSF. We can't own more than 100% of Garanimals or See's Candies. And it would be nice. We'd love to own 200%, but it just isn't doable. But they're all the same. They're good businesses. And to think that our criteria for Apple is different than the other businesses we own, it just happens to be a better business than any we own. And we put a fair amount of money in it, but we haven't got more money in it than we've got in the railroad. And Apple is a better business. Our railroad is a very good business. But it's not remotely as good as Apple's business. Apple, you know, has a position with consumers where they're paying, you know, maybe the $1,500 bucks or whatever it may be for a phone. And these same people pay $35,000 for having a second car. And if they had to give up a second car or give up their iPhone, they'd give up their second car. I mean, it's an extraordinary product. We don't have anything like that that we own 100% of. But we're very, very, very happy to have 5.6%, or whatever it may be, and we're delighted every tenth of a percent that goes up. That's like adding $100 million to our share of the earnings. And they use the earnings to buy out our partners, which we're glad to see them sell out, too. The index funds have to sell if they (Laugh) bring the number of shares down. And, you know, we went up slightly last year, and I made a mistake a couple years ago when I sold some shares when I had certain reasons why gains were useful to take that year from a tax standpoint. But having heard me say that, it was a dumb decision. (Laugh) And, Charlie, you've already given your comment about it. But we do not have 35% of Berkshire's portfolio. Berkshire's portfolio is the funds we have to work with. And we want to own good businesses. And we also want to have plenty of liquidity. And beyond that, you know, the sky's the limit or our mistakes. Who knows what the bottom is? (Laugh) Charlie, do you want to add anything to your earlier comment?

**Charlie Munger**（02:06:25）：Well, I think one of the inane things that's taught in modern university education is that a vast diversification is absolutely mandatory in investing in common stocks. That is an insane idea. It's not that easy to have a vast plethora of good opportunities that are easily identified. And if you've only got three, I'd rather be in my best ideas instead of my worst. And now, some people can't tell their best ideas from their worst. And the act of deciding that an investment is already good, they get to thinking it's better than it is. I think we make fewer mistakes like that than other people. And that is a blessing to us. We're not so smart, but we kind of know where the edge of our smartness is. That is a very important part of practical intelligence. And a lot of people who are geniuses on IQ tests think they're a lot smarter than they are. And what they are is dangerous. But if you know the edge of your own ability pretty well, you should ignore most of the notions of our experts about what I call "deworsification" of portfolios.
