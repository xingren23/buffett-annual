## 问题

日本 CPI 已超过 3%、接近 2% 目标，日本央行看起来决心加息，而美联储、欧洲央行在考虑降息。日本央行继续加息合理吗？加息会不会让伯克希尔不再追加投资日本股市，甚至兑现现有盈利？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特把判断权交回日本：「我让日本的人自己决定对他们最好的经济路线」，他不评价日本央行的政策。
2. 买入缘由很朴素：约六年前翻一本收录两三千家日本公司的手册，发现五大商社「价格低得离谱」，于是花约一年时间建仓。
3. 明确表态长期持有：**「几十年内都不会卖出一股」**；Abel 补充，设想持有「50 年，或者永远」，并且希望在五大商社基础上做增量的大项目。
4. 他不打算改造被投企业：「我们的主要活动就是鼓掌喝彩」，双方商业习惯不同（日本人喝 Georgia 咖啡，他喝樱桃可乐），但「这是完美的关系」。
5. 日元融资是加分项而非策略：能借日元买日元资产，等于天然对冲，但他强调这不是伯克希尔的固定政策。

**ST Jang**（00:22:44）：Buffett 先生、Abel 先生、Jain 先生，早上好。我是 ST Jang，来自香港。过去五六年，Buffett 先生和 Munger 先生在日本做了一笔非常成功的投资。日本最近的 CPI 目前在 3% 以上，离 2% 的目标不远。日本央行看起来非常决心加息，而美联储、欧洲央行和其他央行正在考虑降息。你认为日本央行继续加息是否合理？它计划中的加息，会不会让你不愿意继续投资日本股市，甚至考虑兑现目前的盈利？非常感谢你们每年筹办这样一场盛会。最后，祝你永远健康，并且继续办这个股东大会。

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:04）：谢谢。我也把同样的善意回赠给日本，就像你刚才对我表达的那样。我让日本的人自己决定对他们最好的经济路线。这是个不可思议的故事，到现在大约六年了。正如你指出的，我当时正在翻一本大概收录了两三千家日本公司的小手册。我唯一的问题是，那本手册我现在看不了了，字太小。其中有五家商社，在日本有一种特殊的名义形式，而它们的售价低得离谱。于是我花了大约一年时间买入，之后我们和这些人熟悉起来，Greg 和我看到的一切，随着时间推移我们越来越喜欢。所以我们接近了当初告诉公司「未经你们同意绝不会超过」的 10% 上限，因此我们最近确实问过他们，这个上限能否放宽，目前正在适度放宽的过程中。我可以说——也替我身后的各位说——在未来 50 年里，我希望那时是他在管事，我们根本不会考虑卖掉这些持股。日本的表现确实非同寻常。我猜 Tim Cook 会告诉你，iPhone 在日本的销量，和美国以外的任何国家相比都不逊色。美国运通会告诉你，他们的产品在日本卖得非常非常好。可口可乐——我们的投资——在日本做得格外好。他们的文明里有不少习惯和我们的运作方式不同。在日本，他们的容器、他们的软饮料，整套分销体系都完全不同。但那五家公司待我们极好。他们主要是和 Greg 谈。我一两年前去过一次。Greg 比我更像个世界公民，所以他其实不怎么说话。你算算，你和这家或那家公司的代表见过多少次面？

**Greg Abel**（00:27:37）：是的。说到这五家，Warren，一年里肯定有那么几次会面。我想我们和这五家公司建立的东西，第一，这是一笔很好的投资；但正如 Warren 提到的，我们真的设想持有这笔投资 50 年，或者永远。同时我认为我们也在建立关系，以便和每一家做增量的事情，我们确实希望在全球范围内和他们一起做大事。他们带来不同的视角和不同的机会，这就是我们要和他们建立长期关系的原因。

**Warren Buffett**（00:28:16）：这是超长期。他们有不同的习俗、不同的经商方式，这在全世界都一样。我们完全没有、也无意去改变他们做过的事，因为他们做得非常成功。我们的主要活动就是鼓掌喝彩。我 94 岁了，还鼓得动。所以，我们会继续持有这些。我们不会卖任何股票——那在几十年内都不会发生。我猜他们会找到机会，因为这五家商社几乎覆盖了全世界。我们偶尔也会找到一些机会，对其中任何一家单个公司来说可能都算大。他们或许会因为我们带来的某些帮助而受益，但这将是一段不断扩大的关系。Berkshire 变得像现在这么大，实在可惜，因为我们很喜欢那个位置，我希望它比现在大得多。但即便是这五家——它们规模很大，在日本是大公司——我们投进去的市值大约在 200 亿美元这个量级，而我宁愿是 1000 亿而不是 200 亿，我就是这么想的。我们还有几笔其他投资，但在 Berkshire，规模是业绩的敌人，我不知道有什么好办法解决这个问题。Charlie 总跟我说，有点问题是好事。我一直没太搞懂，但你要是多听他的话，就会明白这根本不是什么无解的问题。日本这笔投资完全对上了我们的路子。你还有什么要补充的吗？

**Greg Abel**（00:31:13）：没有，我觉得你已经讲到了。就像你说的，这完全对上了我们的路子，我完全同意，Warren。我确实相信长期我们会看到一些非常大的机会，这正是这段关系带来的巨大加分。

**Warren Buffett**（00:31:30）：是的。我想说，他们愿意把机会呈到我们面前，我们也愿意接。我们有钱。我们双方相处得非常融洽，他们的习俗和我们不同。他们喝可口可乐的第一品牌，在日本他们喝一种叫 Georgia Coffee 的东西。所以我没把他们转成樱桃可乐，他们也不会把我转成 Georgia 咖啡。但这是一段完美的关系，我只希望能多有几段。我当年拿起那本小手册时，做梦也没想到会这样——它大概有那么厚，有时一页两家公司，我估计有两三千页。但当你一页一页翻下去，你能发现的东西是很惊人的。我们去年放过一部关于「翻遍每一页」（Turn Every Page）的电影。我要说，翻遍每一页，是进入投资领域的一项重要素质。很少有人这么做；而真这么做的人，也不会告诉你他们发现了什么。所以，你自己得做一点，好吗？Becky？

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**ST Jang**（00:22:44）：Mr. Buffett, Mr. Abel, and Mr. Jain. Good morning. I’m ST Jang. I’m from Hong Kong. Mr. Buffett and Mr. Munger made a very good and successful investment in Japan in the past five or six years. The recent CPI in Japan is currently above 3%. Not far away from its 2% target. The Bank of Japan seems very determined to raise rates while the Fed, ECB, and other central banks are considering cutting them. Do you think the BOJ, Bank of Japan, makes sense to proceed with the rate hike? Will its planned rate hike deter you from further investing Japanese stock market or even considering realising your current profits? Thank you very much for arranging this great event every year. Finally, I wish you healthy always and keep holding this shareholder meeting.

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:04）：Thank you. Well, I’m going to extend the same goodwill to Japan that you just extended to me. I let the people in Japan determine their best course of action in terms of economics. It’s an incredible story, and it’s been about six years now, as you pointed out, I was just going through a little handbook that probably had two or 3000 Japanese companies. The one problem I have is that I can’t read that handbook anymore because the print is too small, and there were these five trading companies that had a special name form in Japan, but they were selling it at ridiculously low prices. So I spent about a year acquiring them, and then we got to know the people better, and everything that Greg and I saw we liked better as we went along. So we got fairly close to the 10% limit that we told the company we would never exceed without their permission, and so we did ask them recently whether that limit could be relaxed, and it’s in the process of being relaxed somewhat. I would say that I’ll speak for the great beyond me that in the next 50 years, and I hope he’s running things in, we won’t give a thought to selling those. I mean, Japan’s record has been extraordinary. My guess is that Tim Cook would tell you that iPhone sales there are about as great as any country outside the United States. American Express would tell you that they sell their product very, very well in Japan. Coca-Cola, our investment, does extraordinarily well in Japan. They have a number of habits in a civilization that operate differently from ours. In Japan, their containers, their soft drinks, they have a whole different sort of distribution system there. But we have been treated extremely well by the five companies. They talked with Greg primarily. I went over there a year or two ago. Greg’s more cosmopolitan than I am, so he isn’t saying much, actually. I don’t know how many times you think you’ve met with representatives of one company or the other?

**Greg Abel**（00:27:37）：Yeah. When you think of the five, there’s definitely a couple of meetings a year, Warren, and I think the thing we’re building with the five companies is one, it’s been a very good investment, but we are really, as Warren touched on, we envision holding the investment for 50 years or forever. But I think we are also building relationships to do incremental things with each of those companies, and we really do hope to do big things with them globally. They bring different perspectives and different opportunities, and we see that’s why we’re building that long-term relationship with them.

**Warren Buffett**（00:28:16）：It’s super long term, and they have different customs, they have different approaches to business, that’s true around the world. We don’t have any intention, in any way, of trying to change what they’ve done because they do it very successfully, and our main activity is just to cheer and clap. I can still do it, 94. So, we will own those. We will not be selling any stock. I mean, that will not happen in decades. My guess is that they will find things because they cover the world pretty much, the five trading companies. We will find things occasionally that may be large for any individual company there. They may, in some way, be assisted by some help we bring to the situation, but that will be an expanding relationship. It’s too bad that Berkshire has gotten as big as it is because we love that position, and I’d like it to be a lot larger than it is. But even with the five companies, being very large, and they’re large companies in Japan, and we’ve got a market in the range of $20 billion invested, but I’d rather have 100 billion than 20 billion, and that’s the way I feel about it. We have several other investments, but size is an enemy of performance at Berkshire, and I don’t know any good way to solve that problem. Charlie always told me that having a few problems was good for me. I never quite understood that, but if you listen to him more, you will understand that it’s not an impossible problem at all. The Japan investment has been right up our alley. Did you want to add anything to that?

**Greg Abel**（00:31:13）：No, I think you’ve touched it. As you said, it’s right up our alley, and I absolutely agree, Warren. I do believe we’ll see some very large opportunities long term, and that’s just been a great plus of that relationship.

**Warren Buffett**（00:31:30）：Yeah, I would say they would like to present us with opportunities. We would like to receive them. We’ve got the money. We both get along very well with each other, and they have different they have different customs than we have. They drink the number one Coca-Cola product. They drink over there, something called Georgia Coffee. So I haven’t converted them to Cherry Coke, and they’re not going to convert me to Georgia coffee. But it’s a perfect relationship. I just wish we could get more like it. I never dreamt of that when I picked up that little handbook, it was about that thick, but sometimes two companies to a page, and a couple of thousand pages, I believe. But it’s amazing what you can find when you just turn the page. We showed a movie last year about Turn Every Page, and I would say that turning every page is one important ingredient to bring to the investment field. That’s what very few people do: turn every page, and the ones that turn every page aren’t going to tell you what they’re finding. So you’ve got to do a little of it yourself, OK? Becky?
