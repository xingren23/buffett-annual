## 问题

你长期相信「美国顺风」和美国的韧性，历史也证明你是对的。但现在美国正在经历可能是革命性的变化，一些投资者开始质疑美国例外论。他们是不是过度悲观？还是这个国家真的进入了需要重新评估的阶段？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特不接受「例外论终结」的框架：美国从建国起就一直在剧烈变化中，「我们永远在变化的过程中，也永远能找到值得批评的地方」。
2. 他用建国文献自证这种「不完美但持续修正」：宪法曾把黑人算作五分之三人口，第二条里男性代词出现 20 次、女性代词 0 次，直到第 19 修正案才补上。
3. 个人层面他反复强调运气：**「我一生最幸运的一天，是我出生的那天」**——1930 年只有约 3% 的世界新生儿出生在美国，而他恰好生于其中。
4. 结论偏向乐观：批评这个国家是常态，但把它当成「正在衰败的赌注」是另一回事。

## 关键引述

> **“We're always in the process of change. We'll always find all kinds of things to criticize in the country.”**
>
> 「我们始终处在变化的过程里，也总能在这个国家找到各种值得批评的地方。」

> **“The luckiest day in my life was the day I was born... I was born in the United States.”**
>
> 「我一生中最幸运的一天，就是我出生的那天……我生在美国。」

**Becky Quick**（00:58:32）：这个问题来自 Jessica Poon，她说：「你长期坚信美国的顺风和美国的韧性，历史也证明你是对的。今天，美国似乎正在经历重大的、可能是革命性的变化。一些投资者开始质疑『美国例外论』这个概念。在你看来，是投资者对美国经济过度悲观了，还是这个国家确实进入了一个需要以新视角重新评估的根本性变化期？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:09）：我得说，Jessica 自己挺相信这一点的。某种意义上，她是我在年报里提到过的某位经理的继孙女，可能不是同一位。但无论如何，美国自建国以来就一直充满着不起眼却革命性的变化。我提到过，我们起步时是个农业社会。我们起步时是一个承诺很高的社会，而兑现得很差。我们说人人生而平等，然后写出一部把黑人算作五分之三个人的宪法。第二条里，男性代词出现了 20 次，女性代词一次都没有。所以一直拖到第 19 修正案通过，才说「哦对，我们 1776 年就答应过女性，现在来处理一下」，然后又很久没有真正处理。我们永远处在变化的过程中，也总能在这个国家找到各种值得批评的地方。我一生中最幸运的一天，就是我出生的那天。我生在美国，当时全世界大约 3% 的新生儿出生在美国。我常说，要是能给我父母发个消息，我大概会说：看在上帝份上，在我出生前搬到美国去。但我只是运气好——我运气好，生得皮肤浅；运气好，生为白人；诸如此类。但如果你认为自我 1930 年出生以来美国没变过，那不对：我们经历了各种事情，经历过大衰退，经历过世界大战，经历过我出生时根本无法想象的原子弹的诞生。所以我不会因为「我们没能解决出现的每一个问题」就灰心。如果我今天出生，你知道，我会在娘胎里一直谈判，直到他们说你可以去美国。所以我们都很幸运。我们这儿还有两位非美国的朋友，就是为了听听另一面。好，4 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（00:58:32）：This question comes from Jessica Poon, who says, “You’ve long been a strong believer in the American tailwind, and the resilience of the United States, and history has proven you correct. Today, the US appears to be undergoing significant, and potentially revolutionary changes. Some investors are now questioning the concept of American exceptionalism. In your view, are investors being overly pessimistic about the US economy, or is the country indeed entering a period of fundamental change that requires a reassessment from a new perspective?”

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:09）：I would say that Jessica quite believes this. In some sense, she is the step-granddaughter of one of our managers that I mentioned in the annual report. It may not be the same one. But in the event, America has been an insignificant, and revolutionary change, ever since it was developed. I mentioned that we started out as an agricultural society. We started out a society with high promises, and we didn’t deliver on it very well. We said all men were created equal, and then we wrote a constitution that counted blacks as three-fifths of a person. In Article 2, you’ll find male pronouns used 20 times, and no female pronouns used. So it took till, I should say until the 19th amendment was passed saying, “Oh yeah, we promised the women back in 1776, and now we’ll do something about it.”, and then we didn’t do something about it for a long time. We’re always in the process of change. We’ll always find all kinds of things to criticize in the country. The luckiest day in my life was the day I was born. You know, I was born in the United. States, and at the time about 3% of all the births in the world are taking place in the United States. I’d like to say that if I had something to do was to send messages out to my parents, for God’s sake, move to the United States before I was born or anything. But I was just lucky, and I was lucky to be born pale, I was lucky to be born white, and all kinds of things. But if you don’t think the United States has changed since I was born in 1930, we’ve gone through all kinds of things, and we’ve gone through great recessions, we’ve gone through world wars, we’ve gone through the development of atomic bomb that we never dreamt of the time I was born. So I would not get discouraged about the fact that that’s not like we solve every problem that comes along. If I were being born today, you know, I would just keep negotiating in the womb until they said you can be in the United States. So we’re all pretty lucky. We’ve got two non-United States guys here just to get the other side. OK, Station 4.
