## 问题

你收购过芝加哥的 Portillo's Hot Dogs，怎么判断它适合伯克希尔的组合？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特的回答是「这事得问 Greg，我完全不知道」——他确实不知道，Abel 现场也表示要「求助场外」。
2. 谜底由子公司负责人 Peter Eastwood 查出：Portillo's 的股东是一家叫 **Berkshire Partners 的私募股权公司**，与伯克希尔·哈撒韦毫无关系，纯属同名误会。
3. 这段插曲顺带成了伯克希尔分权模式的注解：巴菲特每月只看五六十家子公司的报表，而像 Marmon 这样的平台下辖 100 多家公司，「是伯克希尔中的伯克希尔」。
4. 他还讲了 Marmon 与被收购家族（Pritzker）的渊源，说明为什么这种「买平台、保留经理人」的结构能长期跑通。

## 关键引述

> **“We own a lot of companies, but I do like to think I know most of them... I really don't know anything about it.”**
>
> 「我们有很多公司，我自认大部分都知道……但我真的对这家一无所知。」

> **“Portillo's is owned by a private equity firm called Berkshire Partners. So that was the basis of the question. But it's not associated with Berkshire.”**
>
> 「（Abel）Portillo's 的股东是一家叫 Berkshire Partners 的私募股权公司，所以才有这个问题。它和伯克希尔没有关系。」

**Sean Siegel**（00:49:50）：你好，我是来自芝加哥的 Sean Siegel。谢谢你们投入时间，也谢谢执行委员会筹办这场会议，把这么多元背景不同的人聚在一个屋顶下。在 Berkshire 拥有的所有公司里，有一家是你们收购的、总部在芝加哥的 Portillo's Hot Dogs。你当时怎么判断它很适合公司整体组合？

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:26）：这个我得问 Greg，因为我完全不知道。也许是趁我看向别处的时候他买的。

**Greg Abel**（00:50:37）：这个问题我得求助场外了。

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:44）：我们拥有很多公司，我自认大部分都知道，也许是某家公司的子公司下面的子公司。但我真的对这家一无所知，抱歉，但这也许是好事。不过热狗我确实懂一点。

**Greg Abel**（00:51:06）：Warren，我们通过 Marmon 在芝加哥确实有很多公司，那是个很好的机会——我们在那个平台下积累了一批优秀的公司。但正如你说的，我不认为 Portillo's 属于其中。

**Warren Buffett**（00:51:28）：我每个月大概看五六十家公司的财务报表，但拿 Marmon 来说，它本身就拥有 100 多家公司。它是 Jay Pritzker 和他哥哥 Bob 创立的，我们买下它时它已经是一家了不起的公司。当时它已经高度多元化，之后我们又进一步多元化。所以它有点像「伯克希尔中的伯克希尔」，我们发现这种安排运转得很好。有意思的是，Jay Pritzker 是位了不起的管理者，Pritzker 家族有很多分支，往上可以追到 Jay 之前的 A.N. Pritzker。1954 年，美国联邦税法做了非常大的改动。那对我打击不小，因为我当时在哥伦比亚大学，正在读一本 J.K. Lasser 写的关于税法的书，结果他们把那玩意儿整个改掉了。但 1954 年是大年，大变化。这样的年份时不时就来一次，比如 1986 年，说不定哪天你又会见到一次大的。布鲁克林有一家公司叫 Rockwood Chocolates，生产 Rockwood 巧克力豆，我们当年在 Buffett 杂货店卖过，人们拿它做巧克力曲奇。后来发现，可可——最近可可价格大涨——在 1941 年刚允许保险业、为税务目的使用后进先出法（LIFO）时，是每磅 5 美分。Rockwood 巧克力公司采用了 LIFO，所以他们账上持有 3000 万磅可可。1955 年可可价格大涨，我刚搬到纽约。新税法里有一条：如果你在两家或以上的公司里，做了某些安排，且持有满五年，然后从其中一家退出，那么 LIFO 存货的增值可以不缴资本利得税——当时税率大约 48%，也许 52%。所以这里有一笔巨大的利润，因为可可涨价了；但这对他们卖 Rockwood 巧克力豆却是灾难，因为巧克力豆的零售价和批发端的成本对不上。几乎一模一样的事最近又在巧克力行业发生：好时（Hershey）刚发布消息，他们要有一个糟糕的季度，而我们买巧克力要付每磅 4.5 美元，因为西非的局势把可可价格推得极高。总之，Jay Pritzker 买下了 Rockwood 这家巧克力公司的控股权，我那时 24、25 岁，他们召集会议，要用某种方式把其中一项巧克力业务分拆出去，好让这批可可豆的增值被确认、又不用为这笔收益缴大约 50% 的联邦税。于是我去了布鲁克林那场会，会上一个人都没有——这属于「翻遍每一页」的范畴——只有一个人。我 24 岁，他 29 岁，那就是 Jay Pritzker。没人到场，他们那栋楼也挺破，但楼上有一堆公司。Jay 就给我上了一堂课，一堂关于税法的课。我哪怕去读好几年研究生，也学不到他那一小时教给我的东西。后来我们真的买下了那家公司——Rockwood，在他又做了一些别的事之后，它成了 Marmon 的基础。Marmon 的成就之一是造出了赢得首届印第安纳波利斯赛道比赛的车，还发明了后视镜——我不确定这在经济上算多大优势。当年印第安纳波利斯 500 比赛时，车上有两个人，一个负责回头看看别人在干什么，一个负责开车。我们的人病了，于是他们发明了后视镜。所以，如果你想看看 Berkshire 的实验室里在搞什么，我们有人在研究后视镜这类东西。

**Greg Abel**（00:57:40）：嘿，Warren？还好我求助了场外。我们旗下一位干得很出色的经理 Peter Eastwood 查清了：Portillo's 的股东是一家叫 Berkshire Partners 的私募股权公司，所以才有这个问题。但它和 Berkshire 没有关系。这件事我们搞清楚了，谢谢 Peter。

**Warren Buffett**（00:58:19）：这就是我们运作方式的一个样本。好，Becky。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Sean Siegel**（00:49:50）：Hello, I’m Sean Siegel from Chicago, IL. Thank you for investing your time, and to the executive committee for putting on this meeting, and for bringing together a diverse group of people in attendance under one roof Out of all the companies that Berkshire Hathaway owns, there was one that you acquired, the Chicago-based company Portillo’s Hot Dogs. How did you know that this would be a good fit for the overall company’s portfolio?

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:26）：Well, I’ll have to ask Greg about that because I don’t know anything about it. So maybe he bought it when I was looking the other way.

**Greg Abel**（00:50:37）：I think I’ve got to call a friend on this one.

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:44）：We own a lot of companies, but I do like to think I know most of them, but maybe a subsidiary of a subsidiary in some way. But I really don’t know anything about it. I’m sorry, but that may be a good thing. I do know something about hot dogs, though.

**Greg Abel**（00:51:06）：We do have a lot of companies in Chicago, Warren, through Marmon, and that’s been a great opportunity where we’ve accumulated a variety of excellent companies under that portfolio, but as you noted, I don’t believe Portillo’s falls under that.

**Warren Buffett**（00:51:28）：I look at the financial statements of about, perhaps, 50 or 60 of our companies every month but in the case of Marmon, for example, Marmon itself owns over 100 companies. It was created by Jay Pritzker, and his brother Bob, and it was a remarkable company when we bought it. But it was highly diversified already, and then we diversified it further. So it is something of a Berkshire within Berkshire. We found that that’s working very good as an arrangement. It was interesting. Jay Pritzker was a remarkable manager, and there are various branches of the Pritzker family. So it really goes back to A.N. Pritzker before Jay, and so on. But in 1954, they changed the federal tax code very dramatically in the United States. It was quite a blow to me because I’ve been at Columbia, and I’ve been reading a J.K. Lasser book about the tax code, and then they went and changed the whole damn thing. But ‘54 was a big year. Big change. Those years come every now, and then like 1986, and you may see a big one of these days. There was a company called Rockwood Chocolates in Brooklyn, and they made Rockwood Chocolate Bits, which we used to sell at the Buffet grocery store, and people made chocolate chip cookies out of them, and everything, and then it turned out that cocoa, which lately has had a big run to cocoa was $0.05 a pound in 1941 when LIFO was first allowed for insurance, for tax purposes. The Rockwood Chocolate company went into the LIFO. So they owned 30 million pounds of cocoa. Then, cocoa took a run in 1955, and I just moved to New York. There was a provision in the new tax code that if you were in two or more companies, and you did certain things, and you’ve been in them for five years, and you got out of one of them there would be no capital gains tax on LIFO inventory gains, and tax rates were around 48%, maybe 52%, and so there was this huge profit cause the cocoa had gone up in price, but that made it terrible for them in selling Rockwood Chocolate Bits because the price of retail, which of the chocolate bits did not match what was going on at wholesale. Something almost identical has been happening in the chocolate business. Recently, Hershey Chocolate just came out, and they’re going to have a bad quarter, and we’re paying $4.50 a pound for chocolate because things are going on in West Africa that make cocoa prices go up dramatically. In any event, Jay Pritzker bought control of Rockwood, the chocolate company, and as I say, I was 24 or 25 years old, and they called the meeting to split off one of the chocolate businesses in a way that would enable them to recognize the gain on this these cocoa beans without paying roughly 50% federal taxes on the gain. So I went to the meeting, which was in Brooklyn, and nobody was there, this was in the turned every page category, except one guy. I was 24, and he was 29, and it was Jay Pritzker. Nobody had shown up at the meeting, and it was kind of a crummy building they had. But they had a lot of corporates there, and Jay just gave me a lecture or a lesson, I should say, on the tax code, and I could have gone to graduate school for years, and never learned as much as he did, and then later, we actually bought the company, that Rockwood Company, but after he did some other things became the basis for Marmon, and Marmon, among other things, developed the car that won the first Indianapolis Speedway race, and it invented the rearview mirror, which I’m not sure is a great advantage in economics or anything. They used to have, at the Indianapolis 500, two people in the car. One guy was to look back and see what the other people were doing, and the other guy was to drive the car. Our guy got sick, and so they invented a rear-view mirror. So if you want to see what’s going on in the laboratories of Berkshire Hathaway, we’ve got people working on things like the rear-view mirror.

**Greg Abel**（00:57:40）：Hey, Warren? I’m happy a friend called. So that still works. Peter Eastwood, who runs one of our Berkshire subsidiaries, and does a great job of running it, tracked down that Portillo’s is owned by a private equity firm called Berkshire Partners. So that was the basis of the question. But it’s not associated with Berkshire. So, we got to the bottom of that one. Thank you, Peter.

**Warren Buffett**（00:58:19）：That’s just the sample of the way we operate around. OK, Becky.
