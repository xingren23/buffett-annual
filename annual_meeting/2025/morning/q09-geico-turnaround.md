## 问题

几年前 Jain 在这里讲过 GEICO 在 IT 系统现代化上的巨大挑战，也说对手靠 telematics 在定价上领先。如今 GEICO 的扭转已经很明显，具体做了哪些事？这些改变能否形成长期竞争优势？

## 回答要点

1. Jain 归纳两个短板：**「把费率匹配到风险」（matching rate to risk）** 与 **telematics**。五六年前 GEICO 在 telematics 上几乎垫底，如今「和任何人一样好」，已从竞争劣势变成中性甚至优势。
2. 费率匹配风险的能力也补上了，配合运营效率提升，2024 年承保改善「非常壮观」，但「尚未完成」。
3. 巴菲特补了一笔历史账：1976 年以 5000 万美元买下 GEICO 一半股权；如今 100% 持有，仅 2025 年一季度承保利润中属于这 5000 万美元「本金」的部分就有约 10 亿美元。
4. Jain 提醒别只看承保利润：GEICO 还提供 **290 亿美元浮存金**。
5. 巴菲特的落点是「产品没变、定价变精细」：1936 年卖的是同一种车险，差别只在于对风险的判断更精细。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:11:04）：这个问题来自危地马拉的股东 Flavio Montenegro。「几年前在这个会上，Jain 先生讲过 GEICO 在 IT 系统现代化和整合上面临的重大挑战，也提到竞争对手因为使用 telematics 在定价策略上领先。今天，GEICO 的扭转已经很明显，定价和运营都有改善。能否详细说说在 Todd 领导下采取的具体举措，以及这些改变将如何帮助公司在未来几年保持长期竞争优势？

**Ajit Jain**（01:11:40）：好。Todd 在扭转运营这件事上为我们做得非常出色。他接手时，GEICO 有两个明显落后于竞争对手的问题。第一，是 Warren 用的那个说法——我们一直沿用的——「把费率匹配到风险」（matching rate to risk）；第二，是 telematics。五六年前，我们在 telematics 上几乎排在名单最末。此后我们进步很快，telematics 过去是我们的竞争劣势来源，现在已经不是了。我可以说，GEICO 现在的 telematics 和今天任何人一样好。这是一次巨大的追赶。第二，就费率匹配风险而言，我认为我们也追上了竞争对手，在这个领域和别人一样好。再加上成本削减的努力——GEICO 和 Todd 在这上面功不可没。他基本把员工人数减少了 2 万人：从接近 5 万人降到 2 万，我估计这至少相当于 20 亿美元。这一切让 GEICO 变成一个高度聚焦的竞争者。所以过去七个季度，GEICO 的综合成本率（combined ratio）都以「8」开头。我从没想过自己能活着看到有人能把综合成本率做到这么低。所以我认为 GEICO 做得非常好。80 的综合成本率，意味着在个人汽车险的承保端，我们赚的是所有人里最多的。我们取得了很多成绩，Todd 取得了很多成绩，但我不想傲慢到说「任务完成」。我们进步很大，但我仍然认为在技术上我们还要做很多。正如我们谈过的，AI 将是一股巨大的力量，我们在那儿要「追上」，而不是「等它来」。但我们要处于随时待命的状态。所以我认为 GEICO 现在状态很好。Warren，你不想补充点什么吗？

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:08）：这是一个迷人的案例，而这正是商业这场游戏有意思的地方——尤其是我们的生意，每一个都略有不同。它们都有某种挑战，也有许多机会。1976 年我们花 5000 万美元买下 GEICO 的一半。结果那一半就是 GEICO 的一半，5000 万。现在我们持有 100%，而我们一季度赚的 20 亿里，属于那 50% 的部分是 10 亿美元——相对于 5000 万美元的投资，一个季度就是 20 倍。这需要多年积累。但有意思的是，汽车保单这种东西，一百年前根本不存在。我该说 120 年前。它如今是财产意外险业务里最大的单一品类，规模巨大。

**Ajit Jain**（01:15:33）：我只想补充一点：除了承保利润，GEICO 还提供 290 亿美元的浮存金。

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:41）：哦对，还有这个，这可不是不重要。你付了 5000 万美元，换来一门给你 290 亿美元免费资金去运作的生意，外加一个季度 10 亿美元的利润。汽车保险有意思的地方在于：我们是一家 1936 年成立的公司，卖的是 1936 年的同一种产品，只是定价比那时精细得多。当初只是有人做了一个判断——这来自 USAA，那个人判断政府雇员（GEICO 这个名字就是「政府雇员保险公司」）比普通人开车更安全。我不认为他是精算师或别的什么，他只是观察到了这一点，于是离开 USAA（那家公司至今仍非常成功），用几十万美元创办了 GEICO，第一年承保就赚钱，第二年也赚钱。这不是那种公开上市、用十年假账撑着的玩法。他们就是靠定价赚钱，1936 年以来一直如此。你的汽车保单和当年那张长得很像。这个巨大的领域在我们周围生长起来，而且还在增长。当然，没人喜欢买保险，但大家确实喜欢开车。GEICO 是个迷人的故事，这些年来大约有三次，公司因某种原因走偏，然后又回到基本面，它是一门非常、非常好的生意。有一年股东会上我们放过一段 Lorimer Davidson 的录像。1950 年 1 月，他是那栋楼里唯一的人。我某个周六跑过去想拜访，结果华盛顿周六不上班。我一直敲门，最后一位清洁工放我进去，我问清洁工：「除了你，这里还有别人我能聊聊吗？」他没往心里去，说：「六楼还有一个人。」那个人叫 Lorimer Davidson，他为我做了了不起的事。人的一生中会遇到几个这样的人，他们会戏剧性地改变你的人生。如果你有过这么几个，遇到的时候就要珍惜。在 Berkshire 的董事会里我们就有：Tom Murphy、Sandy Gottesman、Walter Scott 和 Bill Scott。我们做对的一件事，是留住人才——把合适的人变成一辈子的资产，他们才能惊人，而且共事起来非常愉快，总是做得比该做的更多。所以，能在某个周六下午和 Lorimer Davidson 聊上，你就要仔细听。这属于「翻遍每一页」的范畴——有些页你想翻得很快——但人在一生中就是会走运，而你要善用你的运气。好，5 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（01:11:04）：This question is from Flavio Montenegro, a shareholder from Guatemala “A couple of years ago, in this meeting, Mr. Jain outlined the significant challenges GEICO faced in modernizing, and integrating its IT systems. It was also mentioned that competitors were ahead in their pricing strategies because of the use of telematics. Today GEICO’s turnaround is evident through strong pricing and operational improvements. Could you provide more details on the specific actions taken under Todd’s leadership, and how those changes will help sustain a long-term competitive advantage in the coming years?

**Ajit Jain**（01:11:40）：Yeah. Todd has done a great job for us in terms of turning around the operations. When he took over, there were two major issues that GEICO was behind its competitors on. Firstly, the term Warren used, and we all have been using is “matching rate to risk.”, and secondly, telematics. We were at the bottom of the list, and so far as telematics are concerned about five or six years ago. Since then, we have made rapid strides, and telematics, which used to be a source of competitive disadvantage to us, is no longer so. I would argue that our telematics at GEICO is about as good as anyone else is today. So that’s been one huge catch-up. Secondly, in terms of matching rate-to-risk there again, I think we have caught up with our competitors, and we are as good as anyone else in the field. All this together with the cost reduction effort that GEICO and Todd get a lot of credit for. He has basically reduced the workforce by 20,000. Starting with something close to 50,000. He’s brought it down to 20,000, and that translates to, I guess, at least $2 billion. All this has allowed GEICO to become a much-focused competitor. So in the last seven quarters, GEICO has shown a combined ratio that has eight in front of it. And I never thought I’d live to see the day when anyone could have a combined ratio as low as it is right now. So I think GEICO has done a great job. Its 80 combined translates to the largest profit anyone is making on the underwriting side in the personal automobile business. We’ve achieved a lot. Todd has achieved a lot, but I do not want to be so arrogant as to say that mission accomplished. We’ve achieved a lot, but I still think we need to do a lot more in technology. AI, as we talked about, is going to be a big force, and we need to play catch-up there, not catch up. But we are to be in a state of readiness. So I think GEICO is in great shape right now. Warren, don’t you want to add anything?

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:08）：It’s a fascinating case study, but that’s what’s so interesting about the whole game of business, but particularly about our businesses, is that each one is a little different. They all have challenges of certain sorts, but they also many certain numbers have many opportunities. We paid $50 million for half of GEICO in 1976. What turned out to be half of GEICO, 50 million. We now own 100%, but 50% of the 2 billion that we earned in the first quarter is a billion dollars, which on a $50 million investment is you know 20 to one in a quarter. So that takes years to develop, but the interesting thing is the auto insurance policy, which didn’t even exist a hundred years ago. Well, I should say 120 years ago. It’s by far the largest item in the property casualty insurance business. It’s huge.

**Ajit Jain**（01:15:33）：The only thing I’d like to add is that in addition to the underwriting profit, GEICO provides $29 billion of float.

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:41）：Oh yeah. In addition, yeah, and that’s not unimportant. You paid $50 million to get the businesses giving you $29 billion to work with for nothing, and on top of that gives you a billion dollars of profit in the quarter. The interesting thing about auto insurance is that we are a company that was started in 1936. We’re selling the same product as in 1936. We’re being more sophisticated about pricing it than we were then. Somebody just made the judgment. That’s all that came from USAA, which made the judgment that government employees, the name GEICO stands for Government Employees Insurance Company, the government employees were better drivers than average. I don’t think he was an actuary or anything else, but he just made an observation, and so he left USAA, which is still a very successful company, and he started GEICO for a few hundred thousand dollars, and he made money the first year from underwriting. He made money the second year. This is not a public offering type of deal, you know, use phony accounting for 10 years, and all that sort of thing. They just price it to make money, and that’s exactly what’s been done since 1936. Your auto insurance policy looks a lot like the one from back then. This huge field has sprung up around us, and it’s still growing. Of course, nobody likes to buy insurance, but they sure like to drive. GEICO is a fascinating story, and about three times over the years, the company has gotten sidetracked one way or another, and then it gets back to its basics, and it’s a wonderful, wonderful business. We showed at this annual meeting one time a message from Lorimer Davidson. In January of 1950, he was the only person in the building. I had gone down on a Saturday to visit but turned out they didn’t work on Saturdays in Washington, I pounded on the door till finally a janitor let me in, and I said to the janitor, “Is there anybody else I can talk to here except you?” He didn’t take it personally, and he said, “Well, there’s one guy on the 6th floor.”, and a fellow named Lorimer Davidson did wonderful things for me. You get a few breaks in life, in terms of people you will meet who will just change your life dramatically. If you’ve had a handful of those, and when you get them, you treasure them. We’ve had them on this board of Berkshire: Tom Murphy, Sandy Gottesman, Walter Scott, and Bill Scott. One thing we’ve done is we’ve held on to human assets, we’ve made lifelong assets out of people that are the right sort, and with incredible talent, but also just lots of fun to work with, and always doing more than their share, and to get a chance to talk to Lorimer Davidson on a Saturday afternoon, you just listen carefully. That comes in the category of “turn every page”, you know, some of them you want to turn pretty fast, But you just get lucky in life, and you want to take advantage of your luck. OK, Station 5.
