## 问题

你总强调耐心，但有没有哪次打破这条原则、快速行动反而让你受益？

## 回答要点

1. 答案是有，而且赚了大钱：「我们之所以赚了很多钱，是因为我们愿意比周围任何人都更快地行动。」他讲了 1966 年通过 Felstiner 接到 Annenberg 家族出售企业的电话、当天与芒格飞纽约的故事。
2. 关键不是「快」本身，而是**「五秒钟内挂电话、五秒钟内说好」的能力**——前提是之前已经读过足够多的东西。
3. Abel 补充：耐心期间并不是闲着，而是在大量阅读和准备，「耐心的一大半，是用来做准备」。
4. 巴菲特说这也是他 90 多岁还在工作的原因：这门生意真正的乐趣是「被人信任」。

## 关键引述

> **“The main thing you have to do is you have to be willing to hang up after five seconds, and you have to be willing to say yes after five seconds.”**
>
> 「最关键的是，你要能在五秒钟内挂掉电话，也要能在五秒钟内说「好」。」

> **“While we're being patient, never underestimate the amount of reading and work that's being done to be prepared to act quickly.”**
>
> 「（Abel）在保持耐心的同时，千万别低估为了能快速出手而做的阅读和功课。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Daniel**（01:02:23）：您好，Buffett 先生。我叫 Daniel，来自新泽西州 Tenafly。首先我想说，能有机会向您提问，我非常感激。说到您的投资原则，你经常强调耐心有多重要。在你投资生涯里，有没有哪一次打破这条原则、快速行动，反而让你受益？谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:46）：这是个好问题。有些时候你必须快速行动。事实上，我们赚了很多钱，正是因为愿意比周围任何人都更快地行动。Jessica Toonkel——我想她是我们那位经理 Ben Rosner 的继孙女。1966 年，我在纽约接到一个叫 Felstiner 的人打来的电话，他说我代表 Annenberg 夫人。其实 Annenberg 家一共有九姐妹，我记得在 Walter Annenberg 这个儿子出生之前。他说我们有一门生意想卖给你。于是我给 Charlie 打电话，问了几个细节，听起来非常有意思。Charlie 和我去了纽约 Will Felstiner 的办公室，他是个极好的人。此后我再没见过他，但当时他替 E.A. Simon 夫人打理事务，她本姓 Annenberg。她的丈夫曾是 Ben Rosner 的合伙人，但已经去世，之后和她共事变得有点紧张，所以他把生意以便宜的价格出让。他要价 600 万美元——200 万现金，加一块位于 Market Street 900 号街区、价值约 200 万的房产。这生意税前一年赚 200 万，售价 600 万。Charlie 和我回到那个地方，Ben Rosner 在场。他正在为要跟合伙人的遗孀共事而心烦。她极其富有。他并不享受这种处境，对出售非常紧张。他对我和 Charlie 说：我会替你们把这门生意做到 12 月 31 日，然后就走了。我拉着 Charlie 走到走廊，我说：如果这家伙年底真的辞职，你可以把所有心理学书都扔了。于是我们买下了这家公司，后来的关系非常好。有那么几次——我就是其中之一——东部的人心里对中西部人有一种刻板印象。Ben 的第一段婚姻娶的是爱荷华州的女性，他就认定中西部来的人都靠谱。而你进入这门生意，能遇到这样一个人，他手上有一门生意要卖你 600 万美元，带着 200 万现金、几百万房产，一年赚 200 万——你知道，那时候你可不想讲耐心。耐心是用在别处的：用来等那个偶尔响起的电话。我的电话有时会响，会是某件让我从床上爬起来的事，我可能正在那儿睡着。你永远不知道它什么时候来，而这正是这件事好玩的地方。所以它是耐心和「愿意当天下午就动手」的组合。对那些说得通的交易，你不该讲耐心；对那些跟你谈永远不会发生之事的人，你倒是要非常耐心。所以耐心不是一种恒定资产，也不是恒定负债。嗯，我本来还想补充。

**Greg Abel**（01:07:55）：我本来想补充一点：你在保持耐心的时候——我恰好知道，我想 Ajit 也是如此，我们所有经理都是如此——看机会时非常有耐心，正如你提到的，我们又想快速行动。但在保持耐心的时候，千万别低估为了能快速出手而做的阅读和功课的量。因为无论是股票，还是我要加上的一批非上市公司，当机会出现时我们得准备好行动；耐心的一大部分，就是用它来做准备。

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:36）：是的，而且它从来不是均匀流入的。这是你能进入的最不均匀的一种活动。你要做的最关键的事，是能在五秒钟内挂掉电话，也能在五秒钟内说「好」。在这门生意里你不能充满自我怀疑。你就把它忘掉，转去做别的事。这门生意里真正的乐趣之一——其实是非常大的乐趣——是有人信任你。你都 90 岁了，钱多到谁都数不清，为什么还要工作？你知道，就算他们今天从零开始、还有机器帮忙，那也不影响你将怎么生活、你的孩子将怎么生活。Charlie 和我只是享受「有人信任我们」这件事，他们 70 年前、60 年前就信任我们，在我们那些合伙事业里。我们从来不找专业投资者来加入合伙。我的所有合伙人里，没有一家机构。我从来就不想要机构，我想要的是人。我不想要那种每三个月听人汇报一次、只听自己想听的话的人。我们得到的正是后者，这也是今天这里有这么多人的原因。所以一切都顺理成章。但在该动手的时候，你不要讲耐心，你要当天就把它做成。好，Becky。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Daniel**（01:02:23）：Hi, Mr. Buffett. My name is Daniel, and I’m from Tenafly, NJ. First of all, I just want to say how grateful I am for getting the opportunity to ask you a question. When it comes to your principles of investing. You often talk about how important it is to be patient. Has there ever been a situation in your investing career where breaking that principle, and acting fast has benefited you? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（01:02:46）：Well, that’s a good question. There are times when you have to act fast. In fact, we made a great deal of money because we’re willing to act faster than anybody around. Jessica Toonskel, I think she’s the step-granddaughter of Ben Rosner, manager of ours. In 1966, I got a call from a man named Felstiner in New York, and he said, I represent Mrs. Annenberg. There were actually nine Annenberg sisters, I believe before Walter Annenberg came along as a son. But he said we have a business we’d like to sell you. So I called Charlie, and I got a few details, and it sounded very interesting. Charlie, and I went back to the office of Will Felstiner in New York, who was a marvellous guy. Never met him since, but he was handling things for Mrs. EAS, E. A Simon, but she was named Annenberg. Her husband had been the partner of Ben Rosner, but he had died and then got kind of tense about working with her. So he offered his business at a bargain price. He offered us a business for $6 million, 2 million in cash, and another 2 million dollar piece of property in the 900 block of Market Street. It was making 2 million a year pretax, and the price was 6 million. Charlie and I went back to this place, and Ben Rosner was there. He was just upset about doing business with his partner’s widow. She was extremely wealthy. He wasn’t enjoying it. He was very nervous about selling it, and he said to me, and Charlie, I’ll run this business for you until December 31st, and then I’m out of here. I got Charlie, we went down the hallway, and I said, If this guy quits at the end of the year, you can throw away every book on psychology. So we bought the company and had a great relationship. I’ve had a couple of times, and I’m one of them where people in the East felt that they had a stereotype, in their mind, of what people from the Midwest were like. Ben had been married. His first marriage was to a woman from Iowa, and he just figured that anybody from the Midwest was okay, and the trick when you get in the business with somebody, they’re getting the room with somebody like that, and they want to sell you something for $6 million, it’s got 2 million in cash, and couple million real estate, and it’s making 2 million a year. You know. You don’t want to be patient then, you want to be patient, then wait to get the occasional call. My phone will ring sometimes, and it’s something that wakes me up. I may be sleeping in there or something. You just never know when that will happen, and that’s what makes it fun. So it’s a combination of patience, and willingness to do something that afternoon if it comes to you. You don’t want to be patient about acting on deals that make sense, and you don’t want to be very patient with people talking to you about things that will never happen. So it’s not a constant asset. It’s not a constant liability to be patient. Well, when I was going to add.

**Greg Abel**（01:07:55）：Well, I was going to add as you’re being patient, I happen to know, and I think that goes for our Ajit also, and all our managers, very patient when we’re looking at opportunities, and as you touched on, we want to act quickly. But while we’re being patient, never underestimate the amount of reading, and work that’s being done to be prepared to act quickly because we do know, be it equities, but I would include a variety of private companies that when the opportunity presents itself, we’re ready to act, and that’s a large part of being patient is using it to be prepared.

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:36）：Yeah, and of course it doesn’t come in anything like an even flow. I mean, the most uneven sort of activity you could get into. The main thing you have to do is you have to be willing to hang up after five seconds, and you have to be willing to say yes after five seconds. You can’t be filled with self-doubt in the business. You just forget it in your work, and go to some other activity. One of the great pleasures, it is a great pleasure actually in this business, is having people trust you. Why work at 90 when you’ve got more money than anybody could count? You know, if they started today, and have machines that are helping them, and everything else. It means nothing in terms of how you’re going to live, how your children are going to live or anything else. Both Charlie, and I just enjoyed the fact that people trusted us, and they trusted us 60 years ago or 70 years ago, and in partnerships we had, and we never sought out professional investors to join our partnerships. Among all my partners, I never had a single institution. I never wanted an institution. I wanted people. I didn’t want people who were sitting around having people present to them every three months and tell them what they wanted to hear, and all that sort of thing. That’s what we got, and that’s why we’ve got this group here today. So it’s all worked out. But you don’t want to be patient when the time comes to act, you want to get it done that day. OK, Becky.
