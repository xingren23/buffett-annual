## 问题

无人驾驶汽车已在美国城市上路。这对以「承保人类驾驶员」为核心的 GEICO 车险业务有多大颠覆风险？今天的车险会不会变成自动驾驶公司/软件公司的产品责任险？

## 回答要点

1. Jain 认为变化是确定的：现有车险定价的核心是「人为失误的频率与严重程度」；自动驾驶更安全、事故更少，这部分保险需求会下降，**转而变成产品责任险**——承保对象从驾驶员变成设计与供应。
2. 同时出现两个反向力量：事故数量大幅下降，但**单次事故的维修与责任成本上升**（传感器、软件、诉讼）。
3. 巴菲特用数据说明「车本身已经变得极安全」：他 1950 年代进 GEICO 时年均保费约 40 美元，如今可以到 2000 美元；同期每亿英里死亡人数从约 6 人降到 1 人出头。
4. 他的态度是拥抱变化：「如果游戏永远不变，那才没意思」；伯克希尔从纺织业转型本身就是先例。

## 关键引述

> **“There's no question that insurance for automobiles is going to change dramatically once self-driving cars become a reality.”**
>
> 「（Jain）一旦自动驾驶成为现实，汽车保险毫无疑问会发生巨大变化。」

> **“If the game didn't change at all, it really wouldn't be very interesting.”**
>
> 「如果这个游戏一点都不变，那它真就没那么有意思了。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（02:07:10）：这个问题来自 Himanshu Bindal，问 Ajit 和 Warren。「无人驾驶汽车已经在没有驾驶员介入的情况下行驶在美国城市的道路上。Warren 和 Ajit 怎么看这些自动驾驶汽车对 GEICO 车险业务的颠覆风险？这项业务本来就是建立在『理解并承保人类驾驶员』之上的。我们今天所谓的车险，会不会干脆变成自动驾驶公司、自动驾驶软件公司的产品责任险？」

**Warren Buffett**（02:07:45）：好，Ajit。

**Ajit Jain**（02:07:47）：是的。毫无疑问，一旦自动驾驶汽车成为现实，汽车保险会发生巨大变化。我们将看到的重大变化，正如你指出的那样。现在卖出去、买进来的大部分保险，核心是驾驶员的失误——失误发生的频率、严重程度，从而决定我们该收多少保费。这些新的自动驾驶汽车越安全、事故越少，这部分保险的需求就越小。取而代之的，正如你提到的，是产品责任险。所以我们在 GEICO 以及其他地方，当然都在努力为这种切换做准备：从为「操作失误」提供保险，转向更有能力为「产品错误」、为这些汽车制造过程中的错误与疏漏提供保障。

**Warren Buffett**（02:08:56）：是的，我们预期我们所有的生意都会发生变化，幸好我们这么做了……是 Charlie 把我推过去的。如果我满足于待在新英格兰的纺织业——尽管此前它七十来年运转得不错——但世界是会变的。如果这个游戏一点都不变，那它真就没那么有意思了。如果你每次挥棒都是全垒打，那比赛就不好玩了；如果你每次打高尔夫都一杆进洞，那也没意思。所以，一路走下去你必须不断思考，你会犯错，诸如此类，而这正是乐趣的一部分。你要是没点问题，脑子反而会变成浆糊。所以车险会变，尽管它变得之少其实很惊人。但它存在的时间也相对短。谁知道一百年后我们在交通上会做什么？再往回看两百年，谁能预测到美国会变成今天这样，人们会这样流动，会这样享乐？这是一个动态的世界。而我们最该担心的，不幸的是：我们已经学会了如何毁灭世界。我们拥有这个美好的世界，而现在我们知道有八个国家——大概还会有第九个——能够毁灭它。而这九个（或其中一些）国家的领导人，我并不认为一定是理想的人选。1905 年爱因斯坦提出 E=mc² 时，他并没有梦想到质量可以转化为能量、并以此改变世界。1930 年我出生时，距爱因斯坦提出那条物理定律已经 25 年，但据我所知，没有人想过它能如何改变未来的战争形态。真的没有人想过。爱因斯坦当时也没想。然后 1939 年，罗斯福收到一封信，那是在德国进军波兰前一个月左右，他在 8 月 1 日前后收到。历史上最著名的那封信来自 Leo Szilard。Szilard 没法让自己的信摆到罗斯福面前，因为「Leo Szilard 是谁啊」？但他让爱因斯坦签了名。罗斯福对物理的理解大概和我差不多，所以他并不懂内容；但他懂「爱因斯坦签了名」。于是他把人叫来——Groves 将军那时可能还不是将军——说我们该为此做点什么。结果我们学会的就是如何毁灭世界，而我们当时必须这么做。德国有海森堡，看起来走在我们前面，我们没法把那个精灵塞回瓶子里。世界确实在变，我们也有了很多美好的东西。但我们面对的是朝鲜那样一位。如果我们批评他的发型，谁知道他会决定做什么？朝鲜要核武器干什么？那对世界能是好事吗？但它不会消失。所以这是个变化的世界，我们正在享受不可思议的变化——它让在座每一个人都过得比两百年前好得多。到目前为止，我们并没有怎么改变人类本身。我们确实改变了大规模杀伤性武器，但人类这个物种没多少进步。我们看看会怎样吧。与此同时，车险当然也会变。而且对我们来说，处理这种变化比当年处理新英格兰纺织业的麻烦要容易。你只能随着世界的发展去应对。就像我说的，在座每一位都生活在最幸运的时期。享受你的运气，同时继续努力弄清接下来会发生什么。保险这一路走来，我们做得还不错。保险业里有几个大问题，我不知道这个行业会怎么适应，尤其是其中某些，但这正是这个游戏有意思的地方。

**Ajit Jain**（02:15:51）：我只想补充一点。我们谈到了向产品责任险的转变——为因为产品设计或供应环节的错误而发生的事故提供保障。除了这个转变，我认为我们还会看到另一个重大变化：需要承保的事故数量会因自动驾驶而大幅下降。但另一方面，每次事故的维修成本——把一切修复到原状的成本——会因为车里装了太多技术而显著上升。这两个变量在「提供保险的总成本」上如何相互作用，我认为仍是一个未解的问题。

**Warren Buffett**（02:16:47）：我给你们两个值得琢磨的数字。1950 年代我走进 GEICO 办公室时，一份保单的平均价格大约是每年 40 美元。这个价格各地差别很大，很容易就到 2000 美元；如果是在城市化程度高的地区，还能更高。而在同一段时间里，车祸死亡人数从大约每亿英里 6 人，降到 1 人出头。所以汽车变得安全得不可思议，而买一份保单的价格涨了大约 50 倍。所以当人们谈论驾驶领域的发展之类的时候，有时候事情比想象的要简单。人们看的是那种「巴克·罗杰斯」式的科幻感，但并不真的去想这门生意的算术到底会怎样。车险行业一直是个巨大的成长型行业。顺便说，内布拉斯加的房主保险价格过去十年翻了一倍——按一般通胀调整之后还是翻倍——因为对流风暴。价格一路狂飙，而在内布拉斯加，即便十年里价格翻倍，承保房主保险仍然不赚钱。所以很难预测这些大变化意味着什么。你只能一直思考下去，但你不要去读那种说「世界要完了」或「因为某个东西世界会很美好」的研究报告。同一时间还有大约 50 项其他发展在发生，你都需要考虑、都需要一路观察。在这门生意里你永远得不到最终答案，你只是到达一个「可以行动」的时点。我们尽量去做概率尽可能高的事，用同样的方式玩这个游戏，但结果会和你以为的不一样。如果你是在经营企业，你每天早上醒来都该想想这一点。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（02:07:10）：This question comes from Himanshu Bindal for Ajit and Warren. “Autonomous vehicles are already driving across roads in American cities with no driver involvement. How do Warren, and Ajit think about any disruption risk from these autonomous vehicles to GEICO’s auto insurance business, which is built around understanding, and underwriting human drivers? Wouldn’t what we call auto insurance today just become a product liability for autonomous vehicles and autonomous software companies?

**Warren Buffett**（02:07:45）：Well, Ajit.

**Ajit Jain**（02:07:47）：Yeah, there’s no question that insurance for automobiles is going to change dramatically once self-driving cars become a reality. The big change that we will see is what you identified. Most of the insurance that is sold, and bought revolves around operator errors, how often they happen, how severe they are, and therefore what premium we ought to charge. To the extent these new self-driving cars are most safe, and are involved in fewer accidents, that insurance will be less required. Instead, it will be substituted by, as you mentioned, product liability. So we at GEICO, and elsewhere certainly try to get ready for that switch where we move from providing insurance for operator errors and be more ready to provide protection for product errors, and errors, and omissions in the construction of these automobiles.

**Warren Buffett**（02:08:56）：Yeah, we expect a change in all our businesses and good thing we did… Charlie pushed me into it, but if I’d settle for being in New England textiles, you know, and even though it worked well for 70 years or so prior, the world changes. If the game didn’t change at all, it really wouldn’t be very interesting. If every time you swung the baseball, you hit a home run in the game, it wouldn’t be interesting. If every time you hit a golf ball, you had a hole-in-one, it wouldn’t be interesting. So the fact that there will be things you have to think about all the time as you go along, and you’ll make mistakes, and all that, and then that’s really part of the fun. I mean, your brain would turn to mush if you didn’t have a few problems now, and then. So, auto insurance will change, although it’s remarkable how little it has changed. But it’s only been around since, you know, for relatively a short time. Who knows what we’ll be doing in transportation a hundred years from now? Going back a couple of hundred years ago, who could have predicted the United States would look like what it does, and people would move like they do, and people would enjoy themselves like they do? I mean, it’s a dynamic world, and the biggest thing we have to worry about, unfortunately, is that we’ve learned how to destroy the world. We’ve got this wonderful world, which now we know that there are eight countries that, and probably a ninth coming, that can destroy it. We don’t have what I would consider necessarily the perfect people leading each one of the nine or some of the nine countries. When Einstein came up with the E=mc² in 1905. He didn’t dream of the fact that mass could be converted into energy in a way that would change the world. When I was born in 1930, knowing about the law of physics that Einstein had come up with 25 years earlier, nobody, to my knowledge, had thought about what can this do to change warfare in the future. Literally, it wasn’t thought. Einstein didn’t think about it at that point, and then in 1939, Roosevelt got a letter around the month before Germany moved into Poland. He then got it around August 1st. The most famous letter in history was from Leo Szilard. Leo Szilard couldn’t get his letter in front of Roosevelt, because whoever heard of Leo Szilard? But he got Einstein to sign it. Roosevelt probably understood about as much about physics as I do, so he didn’t understand it. But he understood that Einstein signed. So he calls in… General Groves may not have been general then, and said we should do something about this, and all we did was learn how to destroy the world, and we needed to do it. Germany had Heisenberg. He looked like he was ahead of us, and we couldn’t put that genie back in the bottle. The world does change, and we’ve got wonderful things. But we have a guy in North Korea. If we criticize his haircut, who knows what he might decide to do? What does North Korea need nuclear weapons for? I mean, can that be a good thing in the world? But they’re not going to go away. So it’s a world of change, and we are enjoying incredible change that’s contributed to everybody in this room living so much better than people were living a couple of hundred years ago. We haven’t changed human beings very much so far. We’ve certainly changed weapons of mass destruction but we haven’t made much progress with the human race. We’ll see what happens with that, but in the meantime, we’ll see changes in auto insurance, too, of course. That will be more, and it is easier for us to deal with it than it was when we had to deal with the problems of turning textiles in New England, and you deal with the world as it develops, and like I say, everybody here is living in the luckiest period. Enjoy your luck, and you still try to figure out the answers to what’s going to happen, and all insurance as we go along, we’ve done pretty well. There are a few big problems in insurance, and I don’t know how the insurance industry adapts to them, particularly, but that makes the game interesting.

**Ajit Jain**（02:15:51）：I’d just like to add we talked about the shift to product liability into protection for accidents that take place because of an error, in terms of how the product was designed or supplied. The only thing I want to add is in addition to that shift, I think what we’ll see is a major shift where the number of accidents that take place and need to be provided for will drop dramatically because of automatic driving. But on the other hand, the cost per repair every time there’s an accident. The cost of repairing, and bringing everything back to where it used to be would go up very significantly because of the amount of technology that’s going into the car. How those two variables interact with each other in terms of the total cost of providing the insurance, I think, is still an open issue.

**Warren Buffett**（02:16:47）：I’ll give you two interesting figures to ponder. When I walked into GEICO’s office in the 1950s. The average price of a policy was around 40 bucks a year. It varies all over the lot depending on location. It’s pretty easy to get up to $2000, and depending on how urban your areas are, and everything, you can get them considerably higher. During that same time, the number of people killed in auto accidents has fallen from roughly six per 100 million miles, rather than a little over one. So cars become incredibly safer, and it costs 50 times as much down or thereabouts to buy an insurance policy. So, when people talk about the developments in car driving and all that sort of thing, it’s a lot easier sometimes. I mean, the Buck Rogers aspect of it, people look at it, but they don’t actually think of what really happens to the math of the business. The auto insurance industry has been a huge growth industry, and for that matter, homeowners’ insurance prices in Nebraska have doubled in the last 10 years. Adjusted for general inflation, and convective storms. They have just gone on a tear, and it is still unprofitable to write home homeowners insurance in Nebraska after doubling the price in the last 10 years. So, it’s very hard to predict what these big changes mean, and you just have to keep thinking all the time, but you don’t want to read some research report that says the world’s coming to an end or the world’s going to be wonderful because of this or that. There are about 50 other developments going on at the same time that you need to think about, and that you need to keep observing as you go along. You never reach an answer in this business. You reach a point of action that you take but we try to get into as high probability things as we think we can do, and play the game in the same way, but it will be different than you think. You should wake up every morning, and think about that, too, if you’re in the business of managing businesses.
