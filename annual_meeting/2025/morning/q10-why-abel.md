## 问题

你说过「只有乔布斯能创造苹果，但只有库克能把它带到今天」。只有你能创造伯克希尔，而 Greg 看起来「太正常了」——他到底凭什么成为你选定的接班人？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特的回答落在「组织能力」而非个人光环：伯克希尔有一套**别人做不到的事的能力**，而这种能力在高度发达的资本主义体系里极难复制，更难的是「培养出周围那批人」。
2. 他强调接班不是找「下一个巴菲特」，而是找能守住并放大这套文化的人；此前他也多次称 Abel 是「outlier 中的 outlier」。
3. Abel 的回应克制：在伯克希尔 25 年多，「当你找到像伯克希尔这样特别的东西，你会爱上它」，谦卑多于荣耀。
4. 巴菲特用婚姻作比：找到对的人需要时间，「有时候等待是值得的」。
5. 本场次稍后（下午场开场）他正式宣布建议董事会在年底把 CEO 交给 Abel。

## 关键引述

> **“We've got an ability to do things that nobody else can do, which is hard to get in a capitalistic system that's been developed as fully as the United States has been.”**
>
> 「我们有能力做别人做不到的事，而这在美国这样充分发展的资本主义体系里极难获得。」

> **“It's almost like finding the right person in marriage.”**
>
> 「这几乎就像在婚姻里找到那个对的人。」

**Benjamin Graham Sanderson**（01:19:58）：我叫 Benjamin Graham Sanderson，来自加州帕萨迪纳。Warren，谢谢你这些年教给我们的一切。刚才你说过：「除了乔布斯，没人能创造苹果；但除了库克，也没人能把它发展到今天这样。」Warren，除了你，也没人能创造 Berkshire，我猜你也把 Greg 看作「outlier 中的 outlier」？但他看起来实在太正常了。抱歉，Greg。

**Warren Buffett**（01:20:26）：这其实是「不正常」的另一种说法，不过谢谢你。

**Benjamin Graham Sanderson**（01:20:33）：所以我希望你能具体讲讲，Greg 身上究竟是什么让你选他做接班人。Greg，我们也期待在未来几十年里更多地了解你。谢谢。

**Greg Abel**（01:20:43）：谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（01:20:51）：你问到了最重要的问题。就生意而言，他们手上有一批极好的企业。我们拥有做别人做不到的事的能力，而这在一个像美国这样充分发展起来的资本主义体系里极难获得。你想想，要创造出在那么、那么大的场地上都极难被复制的东西——我不认为你能培养出周围那批人，更不用说资本地位、历史等等。答案当然是：这需要很长、很长的时间。需要聚拢一小批核心人物，然后慢慢扩散开来，彼此之间有相互信任，人人做得比该做的更多。这些年我在很多企业里待过，我天性对什么都挑剔——我总在找什么不对劲，因为投资的一部分就是「你在漏掉什么」。但我们有这样的人：被要求办这样一场活动，他们会放下本职工作来参与。昨天我在展区走了一个半小时，那些人对我道谢，全情投入地来做大量额外工作，拿不到任何额外报酬。我不知道各家公司跟他们怎么安排，但他们工作努力，而且享受工作。你真的应该去做自己享受的事。我这辈子有过五个老板，每一个我都喜欢，都很有意思。但我还是决定，宁可为自己工作。不过如果你找到一群共事起来很棒的人，那才是该去的地方。我跟我的孩子们说过：你不会像我那么走运——我七八岁就找到了真正让我着迷的东西，但很多人可能要花长得多的时间。有一部电影叫《格伦·米勒传》，格伦·米勒带着一支破乐队熬了 15 年，直到他找到了那个「声音」，做出了第一张金唱片——你知道那首歌，《查塔努加火车》，1941 年。他从一个带着那支乐队的小人物，转身成了别的东西，就因为他找到了自己的声音。我一直跟孩子们说：他们的声音不是我的声音，而且你不一定在第一份工作上就找到它，因为你得吃饭。但如果你像我一样幸运，很小就找到了，那就一直做下去。别太在意起薪，而且要非常小心你为谁工作，因为你会沾染身边人的习惯——有些工作你就是不该接。而你拥有全世界最好的国家、最好的时代。所以我要说，我把这个交给 Greg。关于 Berkshire 这样的地方，你能做的梦都不够大。但你最需要做的一件事，是永远确保自己「明天还能上场」——我指的是在相当规模上的金融活动。有本书说，你只需要富一次。你不该去做任何可能让你下不了场的事。如果周围正在发生极其愚蠢的事，你千万不要参与。如果别人因为借钱、或者因为买了实质是垃圾的证券（指望着后面找到更大的傻瓜）而赚得更多，你就得把那忘掉。它早晚会咬你一口。这个基本的游戏太好了，而你生在这个时代太幸运了。如果我生在 1700 年，我会说：我想回娘胎里去，这算什么事，太难了。但现在我赶上了一个可以整天摆弄自己喜欢的事的行业，这是相当美好的一生。Greg，你有什么要补充或删减的吗？

**Greg Abel**（01:27:30）：没有要删减的。我一直只想说：谦卑多过荣耀——我以前也这么说过——能在这个位置上，能真正成为 Berkshire 的一部分。Warren，现在已经 25 年多了。我有机会成为 Berkshire 的一分子，和你、Ajit、我们的董事会以及公司里很多人共事。正如你提到的，当你找到像 Berkshire 这样特别的东西，你会爱上它，它就变成你每天想做的事，这是不可思议的机会。所以，谢谢你。

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:15）：对提问的这位先生，我还想说：如果你没有立刻找到它，别在这期间饿死，但你终究会找到的，而且会在对的人身上找到。从这个意义上说，这几乎就像在婚姻里找到对的那个人。你们中有些人可能娶的就是第一次约会的那个人——虽然我猜现在都没「约会」这回事了。但你知道，有时候等待也是值得的。好，Becky。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Benjamin Graham Sanderson**（01:19:58）：My name is Benjamin Graham Sanderson from Pasadena, CA Warren, thank you for all you’ve taught us over the years. Earlier, you said, “Nobody but Steve Jobs could have created Apple, but nobody but Tim Cook could have developed it like he has.” Warren, nobody but you could have created Berkshire, and I presume you view Greg as an outlier among outliers? But he seems so normal. Sorry, Greg.

**Warren Buffett**（01:20:26）：That’s a nice way of saying not normal, actually, but I appreciate it.

**Benjamin Graham Sanderson**（01:20:33）：So I was hoping you could share what specifically about Greg makes him your preferred successor, and Greg, we’re excited to get to know you more over the next few decades. Thank you.

**Greg Abel**（01:20:43）：Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（01:20:51）：Well, you’ve hit on the most important question, and now in terms of the business, they’ve got a wonderful group of businesses. We’ve got an ability to do things that nobody else can do, which is hard to get in a capitalistic system that’s been developed as fully as the United States has been. I mean, imagine being able to create something that in a very, very, very big playing field it would be very, very hard to develop anything like that. I don’t think you could develop the people around you with that, let alone the capital position, the history, and everything else. And the answer, of course, is that it does take a long, long time. It takes getting around a small cadre of people, which then spreads out somewhat, where you’ve got mutual trust, where people do more than their share. I’ve been around a lot of businesses over the years, and by nature, I’m somewhat critical of everything. I mean, I’m looking for what’s wrong in things because that’s part of investing is looking. What are you missing? But we’ve got people who, if they’re asked to put on a show like this, instead of doing their regular job, they participate. I went around the groups of people who were exhibiting yesterday for an hour, and a half, and these are people that are thanking me, you know, and totally enthused about coming and doing a lot of work for which they don’t get paid anything extra, and I don’t know anything about the arrangements the individual companies make, but they work hard, and they enjoy their work, and you really want to work in something you enjoy. I’ve had five bosses in life, and I liked every one of them, and they were all interesting. I still decided that I’d rather work for myself than anybody else. But if you find people that are wonderful to work with, you know that’s the place to go. I’ve told my kids that you don’t get lucky like I did when I found that seven or eight years of age, which really interested me but it could have taken a lot longer. There’s a movie called The Glenn Miller Story, and Glenn Miller went on from having a broken-down band for 15 years. He found the sound and created the first gold record, and you know what it was, but it was the Chattanooga Choo Choo in 1941. He turned around from being a nothing with the band that he had till he found the sound, and I have always told my kids ever since that their sound isn’t my sound, and you don’t find it necessarily on the first job you take because you got to eat, you know. But if you get lucky like I did, you find it when you’re very young, and then just keep doing it. Don’t worry too much about starting salaries, and be very careful who you work for because you will take on the habits of the people around you, so there are certain jobs you shouldn’t take. But you’ve got the greatest country in the world, and you got the greatest time in the world. So I would say that I’m handing this over to Greg. You can’t even dream all the dreams that you could have about a place like Berkshire, but the big thing you have to do though, is always to be sure you can play the next day. I mean, in terms of financial activities on a meaningful scale. There was a book about how you only have to get rich once. I mean, you don’t want to do anything but risks. If very stupid things are happening around you, you do not want to participate. If people were making more money because they’re borrowing money or they’re participating in securities that are really pieces of junk but they hope to find a bigger sucker later on, you just have to forget that. That will bite you at some point. The basic game is so good, and you’ve been so lucky to be born now. I mean, if I’ve been born in 1700, I’d say, “I want to go back in the womb. What the hell with this? It’s too hard.” But now I’ve come along to do something where I can just play around all day with things I enjoy doing, and it’s a pretty wonderful life. Anything, Greg, you want to add or subtract from that.

**Greg Abel**（01:27:30）：Nothing to subtract, but I would always just say it couldn’t be more, as I’ve said in the past, more humble than honoured obviously be in this role, but to have actually been part of Berkshire. Warren, it’s now 25-plus years. I had the opportunity to be part of Berkshire and to work with you, Ajit, our board, and many other people in our company, and as you touched on when you find something like that, and you find something like that like Berkshire that’s so special, you fall in love with it, and it becomes just what you want to do every day, and it’s just an incredible opportunity. So, thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:15）：And to the gentleman who asked the question. If you don’t find it immediately, you know, don’t starve to death in the meantime, but you will find it, and you’ll find it in the right individual. In this sense, it’s almost like finding the right person in marriage. I mean, probably some of you may have married the person you met on your first date, although I guess they don’t even have dates anymore. But you know, sometimes it pays to wait too. OK, Becky.
