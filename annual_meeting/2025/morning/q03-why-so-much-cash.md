## 问题

伯克希尔现金及短期投资超过 3000 亿美元，约占总资产 27%（过去 25 年平均 13%），实际持有近 5% 的美国国债市场。这是应对高估值的主动降风险，还是为了给 Abel 留出最大灵活性的交接安排？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特否定了「为接班人铺路」的说法：「我不会高尚到为了给 Greg 好看而自己不投资。他要是因为我留下的东西占了便宜，我反而会不高兴。」
2. 他否认这是刻意的择时降仓：**「不是我们想持有现金，而是好机会不会按部就班地出现」**，投资这门生意的特点就是机会分布极不均匀。
3. 近期曾接近动用 100 亿美元级别的一笔交易，说明「出手的门槛不是没有，而是要看得懂、价格合适、不担心亏钱」。
4. 他引芒格的话说，伯克希尔 50 年的钱主要来自「八到九个想法」：宁可少做，也不要把资金摊薄在平庸的机会上。

## 关键引述

> **“I wouldn't do anything nearly so noble as to withhold investing myself just so that Greg could look good later on.”**
>
> 「我不会高尚到为了日后让 Greg 显得好看，就按住自己不投资。」

> **“The one problem with the investment business is that things don't come along in an orderly fashion, and they never will.”**
>
> 「投资这门生意的问题是，机会从不按部就班地出现，将来也不会。」

> **“Charlie always pointed out that we made most of our money out of about eight or nine ideas over 50 years.”**
>
> 「查理总说，我们 50 年的钱主要是靠八到九个想法赚来的。」

**Becky Quick**（00:33:11）：下一个问题来自纽约的 Advit Prasad。他写道：「今天，Berkshire 持有超过 3000 亿美元的现金及短期投资，约占总资产的 27%，相比过去 25 年 13% 的平均水平，这是历史高位。这也让 Berkshire 实际上持有了整个美国国债市场近 5% 的份额。除了为履行保险义务所需的流动性之外，这个提高现金的决定，主要是针对高估值的主动降风险策略，还是也有意把资产负债表调整到位、让领导层交接更平顺，给 Greg Abel 最大的灵活性和一张干净的画布去做未来的资本配置？」我再加另一位股东 Mike Conway 的一句话，他问：「如果有些『好打的球』正朝你飞过来，你会为此感到鼓舞吗？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:34:15）：我不会高尚到为了日后让 Greg 显得好看，就按住自己不投资。他要是因为我留给他的东西而占了便宜，我反而会不高兴。我们的现金量……比如不久前，我们差一点就花掉 100 亿美元。我们花过 1000 亿。这些决定并不难做：当有人拿出一个对我们说得通、我们看得懂、价格有吸引力、而且不担心亏钱的东西时。投资这门生意唯一的问题是，东西不会按部就班地出现，将来也不会。它不像每天都能有惊人的长期纪录。我们算算，80 年乘以每年 200 个交易日，那是 16000 个交易日。要是每天都有四个机会，而且预期吸引力相当，那当然很好。你知道，如果我是开赌档的，每天都有同样的赔率，我能留下流水的 40%，那唯一的问题就是成交量多大。但我们不是做那种生意。我们做的是极度、极度、极度机会主义的生意。Charlie 总觉得我做的事太多。我认为一辈子做五件事，结果会比做 50 件更好，而我们从来没有足够集中。现在我们手里有 3350 亿的国债，我们宁愿是发展出某种局面，让我们只剩 500 亿之类的，但生意不是那样运作的。我们赚了很多钱，正是因为我们不想永远满仓。其实我们并不认为，被动投资者只做几笔简单的投资、然后一辈子坐着不动有什么不对。但我们选择的是做这个生意。所以我们认为，用一种非常不规则的方式行事，能做得更好一点。假如你告诉我必须投资——比如我们一年进账大约 400 亿，起点是 3350 亿——如果你说我每年必须投 500 亿，直到降到 500 亿，那用这种方式投资会是世界上最蠢的事。东西只是偶尔才会变得极其有吸引力。长期趋势是向上的。没人知道，我当然不知道，Greg 不知道，Ajit 不知道。没人知道市场明天、下周、下个月会怎么走；也没人知道生意明天、下周、下个月会怎样。但大家把所有时间都花在谈论它上面，因为谈这个很容易，可它没有价值。我从没找到一个我愿意在这个话题上听他说的人。另一方面，我发现真正有用的是翻那些我已经看不了的大部头日本公司名册。那是寻宝，每隔一段时间你就会找到点什么，偶尔、极其偶尔——但它会再发生，我不知道什么时候。可能是下周，可能是五年后，但不会是 50 年后。我们会遭到大量「报价」的轰炸，到那时我们会庆幸手上有现金。如果这发生在明天，那会有趣得多，但明天发生的可能性非常、非常小。五年内发生则不低，而且时间越往后，概率越高。大概就是这个道理。你 10 岁的时候，第二天就死的概率很低；你活到 115 岁，那几乎就是板上钉钉，尤其是男性——所有年龄纪录都是女性保持的，我曾想让 Charlie 去做个变性手术来验证这一点。以男性而言，他做得相当不错了，我就这么说吧。好，2 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（00:33:11）：This next question comes from Advit Prasad in New York. He writes, “Today, Berkshire holds over $300 billion in cash and short-term investments, representing about 27% of total assets. A historically high figure compared to the 13% average over the last 25 years. This has also led Berkshire to effectively own nearly 5% of the entire U.S. Treasury market. Beyond the need for liquidity to meet insurance obligations is the decision to raise cash, primarily a de-risking strategy in response to high market valuations, or is it also a deliberate effort to position Berkshire’s balance sheet for a smoother leadership transition, providing Greg Abel with maximum flexibility, and a clean slate for future capital allocation decisions?”, and I will add one line from another shareholder, Mike Conway, who asks, “Are you encouraged you may see some fat pitches coming your way?”

**Warren Buffett**（00:34:15）：I wouldn’t do anything nearly so noble as to withhold investing myself just so that Greg could look good later on. Now, if he gets any edge from what I leave, I’ll resent that. The amount of cash we have… We came pretty close to spending ten billion not that long ago, for example. We spend 100 billion, I mean, and those decisions are not tough to make. When something is offered that makes sense to us, and that we understand, offers good value, and where we don’t worry about losing. The one problem with the investment business is that things don’t come along in an orderly fashion, and they never will. I mean, it isn’t like every day the long-term record is sensational. But that is not a product. See how we had 200 trading days times 80 years on it. I mean, 16,000 trading days. It would be nice if every day you got four opportunities or something like that, and they were expected to be equally attractive. You know, if I were running a numbers racket, you know, every day would have the same expectancy of that I would keep 40% of whatever the handle was, and so the only question would be is how much we transacted, but we’re not running that kind of business. We’re running a business which is very, very, very opportunistic. Charlie always thought I did too many things. I thought if we did about five things in our lifetime, we’d end up doing better than if we did 50, and that we never concentrated enough. If we’ve got 335 billion now in treasuries, we would rather have conditions that have developed where we would have like 50 billion or something like that, but that just isn’t the way the business works. We have made a lot of money by not wanting to be fully invested at all times. We don’t think it’s improper, actually, for people who are passive investors just to make a few simple investments, and sit for their life in. But we’ve made the decision to be in the business. So we think we can do a little better than that by behaving in a very irregular manner. If you told me that I had to invest, well, let’s say that we have roughly 40 billion a year coming in, and we start with 335. If you told me I had to invest 50 billion every year till we got down to 50 billion, that would be the dumbest thing in the world to invest in that manner. Things get extraordinarily attractive very occasionally. The long-term trend is up. Nobody knows, and I certainly don’t know. Greg doesn’t know. Ajit doesn’t know. Nobody knows what the market is going to do tomorrow, next week, or next month, and nobody knows what business is going to do tomorrow, next week or next month. But they spend all their time talking about it because it’s easy to talk about, but it has no value. I’ve never found anybody I wanted to listen to on the subject. On the other hand, I’ve found the leafing through things like those big Japanese books that I can’t read anymore. That’s a treasure hunt, and every now and then, you find something, and occasionally, very occasionally, but it’ll happen again, that I don’t know when. It could be next week, it could be five years off, but it won’t be 50 years off. We will be bombarded with offerings that we’ll be glad we have the cash for, and it’d be a lot more fun if that happened tomorrow, but it’s very unlikely to happen tomorrow. Very, very unlikely to happen tomorrow, but it’s not unlikely to happen in five years, and then the probabilities get higher as you go along. It’s kind of like that. I mean, if you’re 10 years old, the chances that you’re going to die the next day are low. If you get to be 115 or something like that, it’s almost a cinch, particularly if you’re a male. I mean, all the records are held by females in terms of age, and I tried to get Charlie to have a sex change so he could test that. He did pretty well for being a male, I’ll put it that way. OK, station 2.
