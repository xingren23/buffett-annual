## 问题

黑石、阿波罗、KKR 等大型私募近年大举进入保险业，募集永久资本、管理浮存金，试图复制伯克希尔几十年前开创的模式，且往往用更高杠杆、更激进的投资策略。你怎么看它们对伯克希尔保险业务的影响？

## 回答要点

1. Jain 直接给出结论：**「在这个领域我们已经没有竞争力了」**——过去三四年，「一笔交易都没做成」，因为对手愿意出更高价。
2. 要分开看财险与寿险：私募最活跃的是**寿险**端，用负债去配高收益资产；财险端则是另一套逻辑。
3. 巴菲特的批评点在「谁承担后果」：很多人想复制伯克希尔的模式，但不愿复制「CEO 把全部身家永远放在公司里」这一条，「他们算的是另一本账」。
4. 「用别人的钱做蠢事，比用自己的钱容易」；他们做砸了就换个方向，而伯克希尔做砸了，「我要用余生后悔」。
5. 他也不认为财险端可以被复制：起点只是 Omaha 几家小公司，但 1986 年 Ajit 加入后，「别人那时就该放弃了」。

## 关键引述

> **“There's no question that private equity firms have come into this space, and we are no longer competitive in this space.”**
>
> 「（Jain）私募股权公司确实进入了这个领域，而我们在这个领域已经没有竞争力。」

> **“There are people who want to copy Berkshire's model, but usually they don't want to copy it by also copying the model of the CEO having all of this money in the company forever.”**
>
> 「有人想复制伯克希尔的模式，但通常不愿意顺便复制「CEO 把全部身家永远放在公司里」这一条。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Becky Quick**（01:43:53）：这个问题来自新泽西的 Peter Shen，是给 Buffett 先生和 Jain 先生的。近年来，黑石、阿波罗、KKR 这类大型私募股权公司大举进军保险业，募集永久资本、管理浮存金，试图复制 Berkshire 几十年前开创的模式。既然这些公司现在直接用更高的杠杆、更激进的投资策略来争夺保险资产，你怎么看它们对 Berkshire 保险业务和承保纪律的影响？你是否认为私募股权模式会给保单持有人和更广泛的金融体系带来风险？这种竞争是否让 Berkshire 今天更难安全、有利可图地找到并定价保险机会？

**Ajit Jain**（01:44:43）：好。这个问题有一部分很容易回答。私募股权公司确实进入了这个领域，而我们在这个领域已经没有竞争力了。我们过去在这里做过相当多的业务，但过去三四年，我不认为我们做成过一笔。还要把这块业务分成两段看：一段是财产意外险，一段是寿险。你提到的那些私募股权公司，都活跃在寿险这一端，而不是财险这一端。你指出这些公司承担的杠杆风险和信用风险是对的。在经济表现很好、信用利差很低的时候，他们把资产从非常保守的投资——我不说「高辛烷值」——但他们确实把这些资产投到了回报高得多的情形里。正如我说的，只要经济好、信用利差低，他们就会赚钱，而且因为杠杆会赚很多。然而，危险始终存在：某一天监管者可能不高兴了，说你们替保单持有人承担了太多风险，那结局可能就很难看（end in tears）。我们不喜欢这类情形提供的风险回报比，因此我们举了白旗，说：这个细分市场我们现在竞争不了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:46:32）：有人想复制 Berkshire 的模式，但通常不愿意顺便复制「CEO 把全部身家永远放在公司里」这一条。他们算的是另一本账，他们关心的是这个——这就是资本主义。但他们所处的情形完全不同，他们对自己在做的事，受托责任感大概也不太一样。有时行得通，有时行不通。行不通，他们就转去做别的。而在 Berkshire，如果我们做的这些行不通，我要用余生来后悔我创造出来的东西。所以这完全是两套不同的个人方程式。没有一家财险公司能真正复制 Berkshire——一开始也不是这样。一开始我们只有离这儿几英里的 National Indemnity，任何人都可以复制我们当时的样子；但那是在 1986 年 Ajit 加入我们之前。从那一刻起，别人就该放弃了。好，7 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（01:43:53）：This question is from Peter Shen in New Jersey. It’s for Mr. Buffett and Mr. Jain. In recent years, large private equity firms like Blackstone, Apollo, and KKR have aggressively expanded into insurance, raising permanent capital, managing float, and aiming to replicate the model that Berkshire pioneered decades ago, given that these firms are now directly competing for insurance assets, often using higher leverage and more aggressive investment strategies, how do you view their impact on Berkshire’s insurance operations and underwriting discipline? Do you believe that the private equity model poses risks to policyholders in the broader financial system, and has this competition made it more challenging for Berkshire to find, and price insurance opportunities safely, and profitably today?

**Ajit Jain**（01:44:43）：OK. Yeah. Part of the question is very easy. There’s no question that private equity firms have come into this space, and we are no longer competitive in this space. We used to do a fair amount in this space. But in the last three to four years, I don’t think we’ve done a single deal. Now you are to separate this whole segment into two separate segments. One is the property casualty end of the business and the life end of the business. The private equity firms that you mentioned are all very active in the life end of the business, not the property casualty end of the business. You are right in identifying the risks these private equity firms are taking on both in terms of leverage, and in terms of credit risk, and while the economy is doing great, credit spreads are low. These private equity firms who’ve taken the assets from very conservative investments, and I wouldn’t say high octane, but they’ve certainly invested these assets in situations where they get a lot more return on the investment, and as I said, as long as the economy is good, and credit spreads are low, they will make money. They’ll make a lot of money because of leverage. However, there is always the danger that at some point, the regulators might get cranky, and say, you know, you’re taking too much too much risk on behalf of your policyholders, and that could end in tears. We do not like the risk-reward that these situations offer, and therefore, we put up the white flag, and said, you know, we can’t compete in this segment right now.

**Warren Buffett**（01:46:32）：There are people who want to copy Berkshire’s model, but usually they don’t want to copy it by also copying the model of the CEO having all of this money in the company forever. I mean, they’ve got a different equation. They’re interested in, and that’s capitalism. But they have a whole different situation, and they probably have a somewhat different fiduciary feeling about what they’re doing. Sometimes it works, and sometimes it doesn’t work. If it doesn’t work, they go on to other things. If what we do here at Berkshire doesn’t work, I spend the end of my life regretting what I’ve created. So it’s just a whole different personal equation. There is no property casualty company that can basically replicate Berkshire that wasn’t the case at the start. I mean, at the start, we just have National Indemnity a few miles from here, and anybody could have duplicated what we had, but that was before Ajit came with us in 1986, and at that point, the other fellow should have given up. Yeah, Station 7, please.
