## 问题

一季度末现金继续增加，但真正的市场动荡发生在 4 月。近期的波动给伯克希尔带来机会了吗？你在会上提到差点投出 100 亿美元，能多说一点吗？

## 回答要点

1. 关于那笔 100 亿美元的交易：「我可以明确回答——没有（没投成）」，而且「100 亿美元也改变不了什么」。
2. 他对「暴跌」的定性极为冷淡：**「过去 45 天、100 天发生的这些，真的不算什么」**；自 1965 年接手以来，伯克希尔股价曾在短期内三次腰斩，而公司基本面从未出问题。
3. 他拿 1929 年做标尺：道指从 381 点跌到 42 点，「那才叫跌幅」，与当前不是一个量级。
4. 对投资者的要求很直接：如果股价跌 15% 就让你难受，「你需要换一套投资哲学」；「世界不会来适应你，你得去适应世界」。

## 关键引述

> **“What has happened in the last 45 days, 100 days, whatever you want to pick up, it's really nothing.”**
>
> 「过去 45 天、100 天不管你怎么取区间，发生的这些真的不算什么。」

> **“If it makes a difference to you whether your stocks are down 15% or not, you need to get a somewhat different investment philosophy.”**
>
> 「如果股价跌没跌 15% 对你来说有区别，那你就需要换一套投资哲学了。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:57:08）：一季度截至 3 月 31 日，确实显示 Berkshire 的现金比去年底又增加了。但最大的市场动荡发生在 4 月。今天到场的苏格兰股东 Martin Devine 想知道：「近期的市场波动给 Berkshire 带来机会了吗？」Martin 在大约 40 分钟前刚补充了一句，指出你提到 Berkshire 最近差点投出 100 亿美元，想知道你能不能多讲讲。

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:40）：第二个问题我可以给你一个好答案：不能。而且 100 亿美元也改变不了什么，这是另一面。过去 45 天、100 天，随你取哪个区间，发生的这些真的不算什么。自我们收购 Berkshire 以来，公司股价曾在相当短的时期内三次下跌 50%，三次。任何一次都不是公司基本面出了问题，但这次也算不上大动作。1929 年 9 月道琼斯指数到过 381 点，后来跌到 42 点——那是从 100 跌到 11。这次既不是戏剧性的熊市，也不是什么类似的东西。我前面说过，我一年有 250 个交易日，很多年都是如此，我到了能炒股的年纪，我有 17000 或 18000 个交易日，其中有很多时期与现在截然不同。我出生的那天，道琼斯是 240 点，那是 1930 年 8 月 30 日，从那之后到低点，它从 240 跌到 41。所以，如果人们认为这次发生了真正的重大变化，并没有。如果它是上涨 15% 而不是下跌 15%，人们会以惊人的优雅接受它。但如果股价跌没跌 15% 对你来说有区别，那你就需要换一套投资哲学了。世界不会来适应你，你得去适应世界。未来——尤其是在未来 20 年里——你会看到一段时期，市场上有人曾形容它，和以往你见过的任何东西相比，就像「卷发器」。它就是会周期性发生。世界会犯非常、非常大的错误，意外会以戏剧性的方式出现。系统越精密，意外就越可能从意想不到的方向飞来。这就是股市的一部分，也正因如此，它才是值得你投入精力的好地方——前提是你有合适的性情；否则，它是个极糟糕的地方。如果你因为市场下跌而恐慌、因为上涨而兴奋——我不是要特别批评谁，我知道人是有情绪的——但你在投资时必须把情绪留在门外。好，8 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（01:57:08）：The first quarter ended March 31st, and it did show that Berkshire’s cash pile expanded from the end of last year. But the greatest market turmoil came in April. Martin Devine, a shareholder from Scotland who is attending the meeting today, wants to know, “Has the recent market volatility presented Berkshire with opportunities?” Martin just wrote in an addendum in the last 40 minutes or so, pointing out that you mentioned Berkshire almost invested $10 billion recently and wanted to know if you could talk more about that.

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:40）：I can give you a good answer to the second part, which is no. But $10 billion wouldn’t have done that much. That’s the other side and another side of it. What has happened in the last 45 days, 100 days, whatever you want to pick up, whatever this period has been, it’s really nothing. There have been three times since we acquired Berkshire that Berkshire has gone down 50% in a fairly short period of time. Three different times. Nothing was fundamentally wrong with the company at any time, but this was not a huge move. The Dow Jones average hit 381 in September 1929. It got down to 42, so that’s by going from 100 to 11. This has not been a dramatic bear market or anything of the sort. I mean, as I pointed out, I’ve had 250 trading days a year for many years. I’ve been old enough to trade stock. I’ve got 17,000 or 18,000 trading days. There have been plenty of periods that are dramatically different from this. I mean, the day I was born, the Dow Jones was 240, and that was August 30th, 1930, and between that and the low, it went from 240 to 41. So, if people think that it made a really major change, it didn’t. If it had gone up 15% instead of down 15%, people would take that with remarkable grace. But if it makes a difference to you whether your stocks are down 15% or not, you need to get a somewhat different investment philosophy. The world is not going to adapt to you, you’re going to have to adapt to the world. The world is not going to adapt to you, you’re going to have to adapt to the world. You will see a period in the next, certainly in the next 20 years, you’ll see a period that will be in. Somebody in the market described it one time as a “hair curler” compared to anything you’ve seen before. I mean, it just happens periodically. The world makes big, big, big mistakes, and surprises happen in dramatic ways. The more sophisticated the system gets, the more the surprises can be out of the right field. That’s part of the stock market, and that’s what makes it a good place to focus your efforts. If you’ve got the proper temperament for it, and a terrible place to get involved. If you get frightened by markets that decline and get excited when stock markets go up, I don’t mean to sound particularly critical. I mean, I know people have emotions, but you’ve got to check them at the door when you invest. OK. Station 8, please.
