## 问题

在高利率和全球不确定性之下，你还相信「别人恐惧时贪婪」吗？价值投资在今天的环境里是不是遇到了新挑战？为什么你仍然买股票而不是买更多物业？

## 回答要点

1. 房地产的麻烦在于交易过程本身：谈判、时间投入、多方参与、产权关系都复杂得多；「房地产出问题时，你往往发现对手方不止一个股东」。
2. 历史上确有大量地产以极低价易手，但「通常股票更便宜，而且容易得多」，所以股票是默认选项。
3. 芒格更爱做地产交易，晚年还做了不少，「那是他觉得好玩的游戏」；但若让他二选一，答案和巴菲特一样。
4. 言下之意：能力圈与交易成本的比较，比资产类别的优劣更重要。

## 关键引述

> **“With respect to real estate, it's so much harder than stocks in terms of negotiation of deals, time spent, the involvement of multiple parties, and the ownership.”**
>
> 「就房地产而言，它在谈判、时间投入、多方参与和产权上都比股票难得多。」

> **“Usually, when real estate gets in trouble, you'll find out you're dealing with more than the equity holder.”**
>
> 「通常房地产出问题的时候，你会发现要打交道的不止是股东。」

**Jackie Han**（00:40:37）：早上好，Warren、Greg、Ajit。我叫 Jackie Han，来自中国，现在在加拿大多伦多工作。这是我第 8 次参加 Berkshire 股东大会。我和你们相处的时间，可能比大多数人花在 Netflix 上的时间还多。你大概猜得到，中国人家庭对房地产总有偏爱。所以常见的问题不是「你为什么不买房子」，而是「你为什么还在买股票，而不是买更多房产？」我的问题是：在如今的高利率和全球不确定性之下，你还相信「别人恐惧时贪婪」吗？或者说，价值投资在今天的环境里是不是面临新挑战？谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:25）：就房地产而言，它在交易谈判、时间投入、多方参与和产权关系上都比股票难得多。通常房地产出问题的时候，你会发现要打交道的不止是股东。确实有过大额房地产以极低价格易手的时期，但通常股票更便宜，而且容易得多。所以 Charlie 做的房地产更多。Charlie 喜欢房地产交易，他生命最后五年里实际做了不少，那是他觉得好玩的游戏。但我认为，如果你让他在 21 岁做选择——要么一辈子只做股票，要么一辈子做房地产——他一秒钟就会选股票。至少在美国，机会多得多。证券市场呈现的机会，比房地产市场多得多。做地产，你通常面对的是单一业主，或者一个家族，他们持有某处大物业很久了，也许抵押借贷过多，也许人口趋势对他们不利。但对他们来说，这是一个天大的决定。而你走到纽约证券交易所，可以做几十亿美元的交易，完全匿名，五分钟完成；成交就是成交。做地产，当你和一家陷入困境的贷款方谈成一笔大交易，签完约之后才进入下一个阶段，然后人们开始就更多、更多的事情继续谈判。这完全是另一种游戏，某种意义上是另一种性格的人才享受的游戏。2008、2009 年我们做过几笔送上门的房地产交易，但和做一件聪明的、可能更好的证券投资相比，所花的时间根本没法比。在地产交易里，每一句话都和人一样重要。而在股票上，如果有人要卖 2 万股 Berkshire，他们打电话给我们，价格合适，五秒钟就完成，而且交割是确定的。股票这边，部分原因是他们的电话、电邮都有录音记录；但就完成率而言，只要你我在价格上达成一致，基本就是 100%。地产则是在你同意的那一刻才刚开始，然后拖到天荒地老。对 94 岁的人来说，参与一件谈判可能拖上几年的事，并不是最有意思的选择。其实往前追到 1960 年代的 Zeckendorf，他当时要改变世界，加州的 Century City 就是他的手笔，他站在世界之巅，年年建楼。所以这个行业里的人容易出事。银行通常不愿承认问题，而走完银行的流程要很久。他们刚刚才把 Musk 三年前收购（现在的 X）时那笔贷款重组完——三年时间，才理清一笔双方都不准备行动的交易的脉络。我们更喜欢人们随时准备拿起电话，一天之内就能做成几亿美元生意的那种状态。我被惯坏了，但我喜欢被惯坏，所以就保持这样。好，Becky。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Jackie Han**（00:40:37）：Good morning, Warren, Greg, and Ajit. My name is Jackie Han. I’m from China and now work in Toronto, Canada. This is my 8th Berkshire Hathaway meeting at this point. I’ve probably spent more time with you than most people spend on Netflix. As you might guess, coming from a Chinese family, we always have a soft spot for real estate. So the usual question isn’t Why don’t you own a house? Is it why you are still buying stocks instead of more property? So here is my question. With today’s high interest rates and global uncertainty, do you still believe in being greedy when others are fearful, or the value investing facing new challenges in today’s environment? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:25）：With respect to real estate, it’s so much harder than stocks in terms of negotiation of deals, time spent, the involvement of multiple parties, and the ownership. Usually, when real estate gets in trouble, you’ll find out you’re dealing with more than the equity holder. But there have been times when large amounts of real estate have changed hands at bargain prices, but usually, stocks were cheaper, and they were a lot easier to do. So, Charlie did more real estate. Charlie enjoyed real estate transactions, and he actually did a fair number of them in the last five years of his life. He was playing a game that was interesting to him. But I think if you’d asked him to make a choice when he was 21. He had to be in stock exclusively. The rest of his life was in real estate. For the rest of his life, he would have chosen the stocks in the second. There’s just so much more opportunity, at least, in the United States. There’s so much more opportunity that presents itself in the security market than it does in real estate. In real estate, you’re usually dealing with a single owner or a family that owns maybe a large property they’ve had a long time. Maybe they’ve borrowed too much money against it. Maybe the population trends are against them. But to them, it’s an enormous decision. When you walk down to the New York Stock Exchange, you can do billions of dollars worth of business, totally anonymous, and you can do it in five minutes, and the trades are complete when they’re complete. In real estate, when you make a deal, a big deal with a distressed lender, and you know, when you sign the deal, then you go into another phase, then people start negotiating more things, and more things. It’s a whole different game, and a different type of person, to some extent, enjoys the game. We did a few real estate deals that came our way in 2008 and 9, but the amount of time that they would take us compared to doing something intelligent, and probably better, and securities, there was just no comparison. I mean, in a real estate deal, every sentence is as important as a person. In stocks, if somebody needs to sell 20,000 shares of Berkshire or something, they call us, and the price is right, it’s done in five seconds, and it closes all the time. In stocks, partly that’s because they’re probably having their wires or phones or whatever it is recorded as to what they’ve said, and everything but the completion rate for working on anything. In stocks, assuming you got a meeting of the minds on prices, essentially 100%. In real estate, it just begins when you agree, and it takes forever. For a guy, 94, it’s not the most interesting thing to get involved in something where the negotiations could take years. In fact, if you go all the way back to Zeckendorf in the 1960s, he was going to change the world, and Century City, out in California, is a product of this. He was sitting on top of the world with the years building. So people tend to get in trouble in that business. The banks usually don’t want to recognize it, but that takes a long time to go through the bank processes. They just got through redoing the Musk loan that he made when he was buying, three years ago, the company that’s now X, and you know, three years to work out a transaction where you’ve got parties on both sides that aren’t ready to act. We find it much better when people are just ready to pick up the phone, and you can do hundreds of millions of dollars worth of business in a day. I’ve been spoiled, but I like being spoiled, so we’ll keep it that way. OK, Becky.
