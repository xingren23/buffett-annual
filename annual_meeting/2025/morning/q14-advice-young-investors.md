## 问题

作为一个对投资感兴趣的年轻人，你职业生涯早期有哪些关键教训？对想建立自己投资哲学的年轻人有什么建议？

## 回答要点

1. 第一位的不是估值方法，而是**你与谁为伍**：「你会朝着你交往的人的方向走」，这条比其他都重要。
2. 他点名了几位已故的老友——Tom Murphy、Sandy Gottesman、Walter Scott——「和这样的人在一起，你会过上更好的人生」。
3. 选工作的标准：「找一件不需要钱也愿意做的事」，而不是看起点薪水。
4. 不要因为某人赚了很多钱就去模仿他；要找能让你变得更好的人。
5. 他承认这些道理要到年纪大才真正体会得到，所以年轻时「听进去」本身就是优势。

## 关键引述

> **“Who you associate with — it's just enormously important.”**
>
> 「你和谁交往——这一点重要得不得了。」

> **“There are people who will make you want to be better than you are.”**
>
> 「有些人会让你想成为一个更好的自己。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Marie**（01:48:17）：你好，我叫 Marie，来自马萨诸塞州 Melrose。谢谢你们今天的时间。作为一个对投资感兴趣的年轻人，我很想听听你的洞见，Buffett 先生。您职业生涯早期学到过哪些关键教训？对想建立自己投资哲学的年轻投资者，您有什么建议？谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（01:48:45）：这些问题很好，我希望自己年轻时也能想到。你和谁交往，这一点重要得不得了，而且别指望每个决定都做对。你的人生会朝着你共事的人、你敬佩的人、成为你朋友的人的方向发展。我提到过几位近几年去世的老友。那些人，哪怕他们一起做的是只有 Berkshire 万分之一规模的生意，他们也是你会选的那类人。有些人会让你想成为一个更好的自己；你要和比你更好的人待在一起，和那些你感觉比你更好的人待在一起，因为你会朝着你交往的人的方向走，这是你会慢慢体会到的。当然，这些道理要到年纪大才真正领会得到。有些因素有多重要，不到很老很难真正体会。但当你身边有 Tom Murphy、Sandy Gottesman、Walter Scott 这样的人，你就会过上更好的人生。而不是出去看到某人赚了很多钱，就决定去模仿他之类。所以我也会尽量和聪明的人交往，能向他们学到很多。我会去找那种「即使不需要钱我也愿意做」的事。人生真正要找的，是一份「不需要钱你也愿意干」的工作——我已经这样做了非常、非常久。我点到的那些人都有这个特点。我提到的每一个人，总是做得比该做的更多，而且从不争抢超过自己那份的功劳。他们的行事方式，就是你会希望任何共事者有的样子。当你找到他们，要珍惜；找不到的时候，你还是得继续做那些让你有饭吃的事，但不要放弃四处看看——你终究会遇到有人为你做了不起的事。我前面提到过，我去 GEICO 敲门，门是锁着的。谁知道那扇门后面是什么？我进去了，十分钟之内就遇到了那个后来对我帮助极大的人。当然，如果有人要帮你，你也要想办法帮到他，这样善意和好行为就会复利——不幸的是，人生里也会有反过来的复利。我很幸运，有一个适合过这种生活的环境；而别人可能有完全不同的环境，他们必须去克服。但如果你运气好，不必为此内疚。你住在美国——世界上有 80 亿人，美国有 3.3 亿——你很大程度上已经赢了这场游戏，接下来就尽量用好它。但你不要和那些要求你、或者告诉你去做什么不该做的事的人或企业为伍，这是个问题。不同职业会筛选出不同类型的人。我觉得有意思的是，在投资这行，很多人赚到一大笔钱之后就离开了。如果你真想找到一件无论是否需要钱都会一直做下去的事……Greg 不需要钱，Ajit 也完全不需要，但他们享受自己做的事，而且干得实在太好了。我有幸长期观察这套东西是怎么运作的。我认识的最好的管理者——谁最合适有很多争议——其实是老 Tom Murphy，他快 98 岁了。我从没见过任何人比 Murph 更能激发别人的潜力。如果你想成为一个更好的人，你就该去为 Tom Murphy 工作。有很多成功人士并不具备这一点。我不是说那是成功的唯一路径，但我肯定那是最令人愉快的路径。Berkshire 的经历相当戏剧性：从 1963 年和 Sandy Gottesman 共事，直到他几年前去世；和 Walter Scott 共事 30 年。你几乎不可能不受教益，你会一直在学。你不仅会学到如何在商业上成功，还会学到如何在人生中成功。所以这就是我的建议。而且不知为何，这样的人好像也活得更久。这挺神奇的——我谈到的这些人，包括我自己，我愿意把这归功于此，再加上其他几点。但我认为，一个快乐的人比一个在做自己并不那么敬佩之事的人活得更长。好，我想接下来是 Becky。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Marie**（01:48:17）：Hi, my name is Marie. I’m from Melrose, MA Thank you for the time today. As a young person interested in investing like myself. I would love to hear your insights, Mr. Buffett. What were some pivotal lessons you learned early in your career, and what advice do you have for young investors who are looking to develop their investment philosophy? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（01:48:45）：Those are good questions. I wish I thought of them myself earlier in my life. Who you associate with it’s just enormously important, and don’t expect you’ll make every decision right on that. I mean, you’re going to have your life progress in the general direction of the people that you work with, that you admire, that become your friends. I mentioned a few fellows who have died in the last couple of years. Well, all of those people were people, if they were working together on something ten-thousandth the size of Berkshire, I mean, they’d be the kind of people you choose. There are people who will make you want to be better than you are, and you want to hang out with people who are better than you are, and that you feel are better than you are, because you’re going to go in the direction of the people that you associate with, and that’s something you’ll learn. Of course, you’ve learned late in life. It’s hard to really appreciate how important some of those factors are until you get much older. But when you’ve got people around you, like Tom Murphy, Sandy Gottesman, and Walter Scott, then you’re just going to live a better life. If you just go out and look, and somebody is just making a lot of money, and decide you’re going to try, and copy them or something of the sort. So I would try to be associated with smart people, too, where I could learn a lot from them. I would try to look for something that I would do if I didn’t need the money. I mean, what you’re really looking for in life is something where you’ve got a job that you’d hold if you didn’t need the money, and I’ve had that Charlie out for a very, very long time. All the fellows that I named had it. Every one of those I named, always did more than their share, and they thought like they never sought more than their share of the credit. They just behave the way you’d like anybody you work with, and when you find them, you treasure them, and when you don’t find them, you still keep doing whatever causes you to eat or enables you to eat but you don’t give up on looking around, and you will find people do wonderful things for you. So, I mentioned earlier that you know, going down to GEICO, and knocking on the door when the door was locked. I mean, who knows what was behind that doorway? I went in, and in 10 minutes, I found the man who was going to be just wonderfully helpful to me. Of course, if somebody’s going to be helpful to you, you want to try to figure out ways to be helpful to them so you get a compounding of good intentions and good behaviour, and unfortunately, you can get the reverse of that in life, too. I was lucky to have a good environment for living that kind of life, and other people, you know, have a whole different environmental situation, they have to overcome it. But don’t feel guilty about your good luck if you’ve got it. If you live in the United States, there are 8 billion people in the world, and there are 330 million in the United States, you’ve already won the game to a great degree, and then just keep making the most of it. But you don’t want to associate with people or enterprises that ask you to do something or tell you to do something that you shouldn’t be doing. That’s one of the problems. I mean, different professions select for different types of people. It’s interesting to me that in the investment business, many people get out of it after they’ve made a pile of money. If you really want something that you’ll stick around for, whether you need the money… Greg doesn’t need the money, Ajit doesn’t need the money, remotely, but they enjoy what they do, and they’re so damn good at it. I’ve had the advantage of seeing how that works over time. The best manager I ever knew, and there’s a lot of contention for who that would be, but actually was Tom Murphy Sr, who lives in his almost 98, and I’ve never seen anybody who could get the potential out of other people more than Murph. If you want to become a better person, you want to work for Tom Murphy, and there are all kinds of successful people who really don’t bring that to the party. I’m not saying that’s the only way to succeed. I think it’s the most pleasant way to succeed for sure. The Berkshire experience is pretty dramatic. I mean, to operate with Sandy Gottesman from 1963 until he died a couple of years ago, Walter Scott for 30 years. You really can’t miss it. You’ll learn all the time. You’ll not only learn how to be successful in business, you’ll learn how to be successful in life. So, that’s my recommendation, and for some reason, apparently, you live longer too. It’s pretty amazing, I mean, these people I’m talking about, including myself, I mean, I’d like to attribute it to this, and a few other things, but I think a happy person lives longer than somebody that’s doing some things that they don’t really admire that much in life. OK. Let’s move on to, I guess, it’s Becky next.
