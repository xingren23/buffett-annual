## 问题

2025 年美元对主要货币快速贬值，伯克希尔是否采取措施降低汇率风险及其对季度、年度利润的影响？另外，公司目前借日元来对冲日本股票投资的汇率风险，未来会不会做更多外币负债？

## 回答要点

1. **不做系统性对冲**：「我们基本从不因为季度或年度利润受汇率影响而做什么」，会计上的汇兑损益不是决策依据。
2. 日本是特例：因为打算长期持有、且日元融资极便宜，才做了资产负债的币种匹配；「这是我们第一次这么做」，不构成公司政策。
3. Abel 补充：真正让人放心的是那五家日本公司本身，以及最终以日元实现的现金流；能借日元只是「额外的礼物」。
4. 历史上唯一一次大额汇率押注是 2000 年代初做多一篮子货币（等于做空美元），持有两三年赚了「几十亿美元」，在当时是很大的数目。

## 关键引述

> **“We don't do anything based on its impact on quarterly and annual earnings.”**
>
> 「我们不会因为对季度和年度利润的影响而做任何操作。」

> **“That's the first time we've done that, and we've owned lots of securities in foreign currency.”**
>
> 「这是我们第一次这么做，而我们持有过大量外币计价的证券。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:29:00）：这是南达科他州 Rapid City 的 Mark Bonki 和 Helen Fredrickson 提的问题。「2025 年美元对其他外币快速贬值，Berkshire 是否在采取措施降低这一汇率风险，以及它对季度和年度利润的影响？如果有，请说明。」我再加一位股东 Mary Chang 的问题：「Berkshire 目前借入日元来对冲其汇率风险和日本股票投资。未来你们会不会持有未对冲的外币计价资产？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:29:36）：会。其实我们一直差不多是这样。日本的情况不同，因为我们打算长期持有那个头寸，而且融资成本实在太便宜。我在某种程度上确实尝试用日元负债去匹配买入，但那不是我们的政策。事实上，那是我们第一次这么做，而我们持有过大量外币计价的证券。所以，就你问的「对季度和年度利润的影响」而言，我们什么都不做。我们不会因为对季报、年报的影响而做任何操作。从来没有什么董事会讨论过这个。我记得和 Charlie 的一次对话，我说：如果我们这么做，我们的年度利润会是多少多少，因此我们应该……无论是会计还是别的什么，那个数字最后是多少就是多少。真正重要的是五年、十年、二十年后我们在哪里。一旦你开始盯着「我要做出一个什么数字」，你很快就会受到诱惑——至少这是我看过 20 家公司之后的经验。人总会以某种方式摆弄数字，有时是认真地摆弄。我见过一些人，在其他各方面我都信任他们，但他们认为摆弄数字完全没问题。我们不这么想，我们根本不去想这件事。其实上个季度日元的关系造成了一些 GAAP 口径的费用，但那毫无影响，下个月或明年它就变了。当然，我们不会想拥有任何我们认为处于一种真要完蛋的货币里的东西。这才是大事——我们担心美元。政府有一种让货币随时间贬值的倾向，没有任何制度能胜过它。你可以选独裁者，可以选代议制，什么都可以试；但人们总会有推动货币走弱的压力。我在年报里很简短地提到过：让我害怕的是美国的财政政策，因为它的制定方式、以及所有的动机，都在做很多会给货币带来麻烦的事。但这不限于美国，全世界都是如此，有些地方还经常失控。你知道，他们贬值货币的幅度令人瞠目，而且还在继续。人们可以研究经济学，可以设计各种安排；但归根结底，如果有一批人控制着货币，他们就能印钞，或者像几百年前那样「剪币」，这样的人永远会有。这就是他们那份工作的性质。我不是说他们特别邪恶，但政府的自然路径就是让货币越来越不值钱，而这有重要后果。要在制度里建立制衡来阻止这件事，非常困难。我们这儿最近挺热闹的——头 100 天也好、最近 100 天也好，随你怎么叫。人们试图确保自己不承担财政风险、确保游戏没有结束——而它永远不会结束。最终，如果你去查二战后那些大通胀，那是一张列不完的清单，同样的名字一再出现。所以货币的价值是件可怕的事，而我们没有什么好办法战胜它。在这个日本头寸上我们确实有办法，因为我们预期持有 50 年、100 年甚至更久，而我们将拥有的是最终以日元计价、且容易预测的东西——只要利差还在，我们就继续做日元负债。但那不是因为我们在意季度或年度利润。Greg，你有什么要说的吗？

**Greg Abel**（01:36:11）：我只想说，就这个问题而言，我们对投资那五家日本公司毫无疑问是非常安心的，我们也清楚自己是在投日元资产。能借日元，几乎只是个不错的增量机会；但我们对那些日本公司、以及最终会实现的货币（也就是日元）都很安心。

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:41）：我们只做过一次大的汇率操作，就是我前面提到的那次，和我给《财富》写那篇文章有点关系。我们做多了一篮子大约 12 种其他货币——我记得只有四五种是真正的大货币。所谓「做多」，意味着我们做空美元。我们持有那个头寸两三年，赚了几十亿美元，在当时对我们是很可观的数目。Charlie 一直觉得，如果要在股票之外挑一个领域投资——他对债券懂很多，对房地产懂很多，对很多事情都懂——他说他认为做外汇能赚很多钱。我们做过一次。并非不可想象我们会再做一次，但可能性不大。美国有可能发生一些事，让我们想持有大量其他货币。我想如果我们做了某笔非常大的投资，比如在欧洲某国，可能会出现需要在对方货币里做大量融资的情况。但那次只是看起来显而易见该做的事。日本的情况是：我们能以极低的持有成本借入日元，而我们对这些证券带来的收入感觉很好。如果现在的条件能持续几十年——它不会，从来不会——我们大概会继续这么做，但世界是会变的，所以别把这当成预测。好，6 区。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（01:29:00）：This is a question from Mark Bonki and Helen Fredrickson in Rapid City South Dakota. “As the US dollar quickly loses value in relation to other foreign currencies in 2025, is Berkshire Hathaway taking steps to minimize this currency risk, and its impact on quarterly, and annual earnings? If so, please explain. I’ll just add Mary Chang, another shareholder. “Berkshire currently borrows in Japanese yen to offset its currency risk and its Japanese stock investments. In the future, will you invest in foreign currency-denominated assets unhedged?”

**Warren Buffett**（01:29:36）：Yeah. Well, we always have, pretty much. The Japanese situation is different because we intend to stay long in that position, and the funding situation is so cheap. I’ve attempted to some degree to match purchases against yen-denominated funding but that’s not a policy of ours. In fact, that’s the first time we’ve done that, and we’ve owned lots of securities in foreign currency. So, we do nothing in terms of the question about its impact on quarterly, and annual earnings. We don’t do anything based on its impact on quarterly, and annual earnings. I mean, there’s never been a board meeting. I can remember the conversation I had with Charlie when I said, “If we do this, our annual earnings will be this, you know, and therefore we ought to… Whether it’s accounting or with anything, the number will turn out to be what it will be. What counts is where we are five 10 or 20 years from now. And if you start focusing on what number you’re going to produce, you will quickly get tempted, at least based on the experience I’ve seen from viewing 20 companies. One way or another, I’ll play around with the numbers, and sometimes seriously play around with the numbers, and I’ve seen people that you know, I trust them in all kinds of other ways, but they regard playing around with numbers as perfectly okay, and that’s just not something. We just don’t think about that. Actually, the relationship with the yen in the last quarter resulted in certain GAAP charges, but it doesn’t make any difference. It will change next month or next year, and obviously, we wouldn’t want to own anything that we thought was in a currency that was really going to hell. That’s the big thing, we worry about with the United States currency. I mean, it’s the tendency of a government to want to debase its currency over time. There’s no system that beats that. You can pick dictators. You can pick representatives. You can do anything. But the people, there will be a push toward weaker currencies. And of course, I mentioned very briefly in the annual report that fiscal policy is what scares me in the United States because it’s made the way it is, and all the motivations are doing a lot of things that will cause trouble with money, but that’s not limited to the United States. It’s all over the world, and in some places, it gets out of control regularly. As you know, they devalue it. Rates that are breathtaking and that’s continued. I mean, people can study economics, and you can have all kinds of arrangements. But in the end, if you’ve got people that control the currency, you can issue pay for money, and you will or you can engage in clipping currencies like they used to centuries ago, and there will always be people. It’s the nature of their job. I’m not singling them out as particularly evil or anything like that, but the natural course of government is to make the currency worth less over time, and there are important consequences. It’s very hard to build checks and balances into the system to keep that from happening, and we’ve had a lot of fun here, either the first 100 days or the last 100 days, whatever you want to call it. What happens when people try to make sure that they aren’t running fiscal risks, and that the game isn’t over, and never will be over. You know, in finality, if you look up, and you search the great inflations of post-World War 2, it’s just a list that goes on forever, and the same names keep popping up, and everything. So value of currency is a scary thing, and we don’t have any great system for beating that. We do in this particular Japanese position because we expect to hold it for 50 or 100 years or more, and we will be owning something that’s denominated in the end, and easily predictable, and we’ll just as long as the carry-on is right. We’ll attempt the issue of Japanese-denominated liabilities, but that’s not because of anything we care about in terms of quarterly or annual earnings. Greg, do you have anything to say?

**Greg Abel**（01:36:11）：I was just going to say that relative to the question, there’s no question we were fundamentally very comfortable with investing in the five Japanese companies, and recognizing we’re investing in yen. The fact we could then borrow in yen was almost just like a nice incremental opportunity, but we are very comfortable both with the Japanese companies and with the currency we would ultimately realize, IE, in the yen.

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:41）：We only made, as I referred to earlier one big currency play which was connected a little bit with when I wrote that article for Fortune. We got along 12 other currencies, as I remember, only four or five of them are really big currencies. But when I say we got long, it means we’re short the dollar. So we held that position for a couple of years, and we made several billion dollars on it which was significant to us then. Charlie always felt that if you had to pick an area outside of stocks in which to invest… And he knew a lot about bonds. He knew a lot about real estate. He knew a lot about a lot of things. But he said he thought he could make a lot of money out of being in foreign currency. We’ve done it once. It’s not inconceivable that we would do it again, but it’s unlikely, there could be things happen in the United States that would make us want to own a lot of other currencies, and I suppose if we made some very large investment, European country or some, there might be a situation where we would do a lot of financing in their currency, but it was something that just was sort of obvious to do. In the Japanese situation where we had the ability to borrow yen at a very, very low carrying cost, and we felt very good about the income we’d been receiving from these securities. If the present condition, which it won’t, I mean, it never does, prevail for decades and decades, we would probably keep doing the same sort of thing, but things change in the world too, so don’t take that as a prediction. OK. Section 6.
