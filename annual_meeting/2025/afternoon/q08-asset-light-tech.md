## 问题

2017 年股东会上你说大科技公司「几乎不需要投入资本就能运转」，是理想生意。如今苹果、Alphabet、微软、亚马逊都在为 AI 大举增加资本开支，你怎么看「轻资产」这个判断？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特先把原则说清：**「不投钱就赚很多钱，永远好过投很多钱才赚很多钱」**。
2. 他用可口可乐作对比：卖浓缩液/糖浆几乎不占资本，而装瓶厂是重资产生意——**同一个品牌，两端的经济特征完全不同**。
3. 对 AI 资本开支他没有直接下判断，但把话题引回制度层面：美国资本主义的原创版本是洛克菲勒、卡内基「自己掏钱建钢厂、炼厂、管道」，现在的诀窍变成了「用别人的钱」。
4. 他的提醒不是道德批判，而是风险识别：「你不能怪人表现得像人，但你得知道他们的动机是什么」。

## 关键引述

> **“It's always better to make a lot of money without putting up anything than to make a lot of money by putting up a lot of money.”**
>
> 「不投入什么就赚很多钱，永远好过投入很多钱才赚很多钱。」

> **“Now the trick is to use other people's money, basically.”**
>
> 「现在的诀窍，基本上就是用别人的钱。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（00:57:13）：这个问题来自 Achit Patel，是关于大型科技股的。2017 年股东会上，Warren 你说这些公司「几乎不需要投入资本就能运转」，把它们称为理想生意——指的是苹果、Alphabet、微软、亚马逊这些科技巨头。现在这些公司都在宣布为 AI 宏图投入巨额资本开支，你有没有重新考虑过「轻资产」这个说法，以及因此对它们的看法？

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:47）：不投入什么就赚很多钱，永远好过投入很多钱才赚很多钱。所以，一门几乎不占资本的生意：可口可乐。成品——经过装瓶厂等等环节——非常占资本；但卖糖浆、卖浓缩液那一段，不占多少资本。所以一边是好得不得了的生意，另一边是——要看它在哪儿、情况如何。可口可乐在每个地方都受欢迎。但有些地方，如果你做的是装瓶生意，那是要真金白银的：你有真卡车在路上跑，有各种机器，有不断到来的资本开支。我们有占资本极少、资本回报极高的生意；而政客们谈论的那些「高回报」，实际上按资本算并不高。财产意外险是一门有点罕见的生意：你需要资本作为「保证基金」，让人相信你会守约，但你可以拿这笔资本去买别的低资本消耗的资产——比如买苹果，就能支撑这门生意。所以那可以是一门相当好的生意，这也是我们长期表现不错的原因之一。至于「七大科技公司」（magnificent 7）的资本密集度会走到什么程度，会很有意思；它们现在比几年前更资本密集了。基本上，苹果这些年并不真的需要资本，而且它回购股票、大幅减少了股本。那个世界将来是否还是这样，是个问题。好莱坞的答案一向是从别人那里拿钱来出资本。这个国家有很多人变得非常富有，本质上就是想出了如何让别人出资本。资金管理这行就是这样：他们非常非常富，因为他们对别人的资本收取分成（override）。顺便说，如果你们去年为 Berkshire 支付 1% 的投资管理费，那你们要付 80 亿美元的管理费，而你们其实并不需要付。但投资管理是门很好的生意：别人出资本，你向他们收费，无论他们赚不赚钱；如果他们赚了，你还能收更多。对做这行的人来说，这是个设计精良的生意，谁又能怪他们呢——这就是资本主义。我在所罗门工作时见过这套运作，但我其实不需要看，我早知道它存在。人生的诀窍就是：拿到别人的资本，在上面拿分成。Charlie 和我过了一段时间觉得这生意不够体面，但我们不是在批评它的有效性，只是后来它不再吸引我们。不过我做了十二年左右。Charlie 和我与别人不同的地方只有一点：我们把自己的钱全投进去，所以真的用自己的资本分担亏损，同时我们对别人的资本拿分成。而现在有些人已经进化到：对别人的资本拿分成，自己几乎不出什么资本。那是门非常好的生意，但可能导致大量滥用。Greg，你观察过美国的资本管理，你会说加拿大在这方面落后吗，或者别的什么？

**Greg Abel**（01:03:02）：我认为就资本制度而言，Warren，非常可比。毫无疑问——我们都知道——就资本主义制度而言，美国很难被任何国家比下去。想到加拿大，只是政府想承担某些责任或义务，而不打算留给公共部门；那只是社会、或者加拿大人民做出的一个决定。你再看另一个国家，澳大利亚或者其他地方，情况就不同。但想到资本主义，那种驱动力、以及恰当配置资本的意愿，是非常相似的。

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:56）：如果你想想，它在美国创造出了奇迹。但最初，洛克菲勒、肯尼迪、卡内基那些人，他们是真的掏钱去建钢厂、建炼厂、铺管道等等，他们自己出钱做。现在的诀窍基本上变成了用别人的钱。你不能怪人表现得像人，但你得知道他们的动机是什么。美国的资本主义取得了你从未见过的成功。它是这样组合起来的：一座宏伟的大教堂，产出了世界从未见过的经济体；然后在旁边附设了一个巨大的赌场。于是你有了大教堂和赌场，赌场里人人都很开心，大量资金在换手。你必须确保大教堂也能得到喂养，因为有那种诱惑。而现在转向赌场的诱惑非常高——那里的人说：我们有魔法盒子，有各种能为你创造奇迹的东西。那是人们最快乐的地方，是别人向你许诺最多的地方，也是推动这些的人赚钱最多的地方。资本、赌场与大教堂之间的平衡，对美国未来一百年非常重要：必须确保大教堂不被赌场吞掉，因为人们真的很喜欢去赌场，那确实好玩得多。他们摇铃，你赢了就给你端酒，一切都设计好了，就是把钱从一个口袋挪到另一个口袋。而在大教堂里，人们设计的是能为三亿多人生产商品和服务的东西，历史上从未有过。我们发展出的这套制度很有意思，但它管用。它分配回报的方式看起来极其任性——「人得到他应得的」这个说法很难站得住脚。但如果你据此去反驳，其他任何制度看起来都更好；答案是我们还没找到。所以我们就留给下一代，让他们用通灵板把答案发给我吧。好，2 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（00:57:13）：This question comes from Achit Patel, and it’s about the big-cap technology stocks. In the 2017 annual meeting, you said Warren, you really don’t need any money to run these companies and referred to them as ideal businesses, referring to the big tech companies, Apple, Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon. With all of those companies now announcing massive capital investment endeavours and around AI ambitions, have you rethought the above comment just in terms of them being asset-light and what you think of them as a result?

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:47）：Well, it’s always better to make a lot of money without putting up anything than to make a lot of money by putting up a lot of money. So, a business that takes no capital to speak of: Coca-Cola. The finished product, which has gone through bottling companies and everything, takes a lot of capital. But in terms of selling the syrup or the concentrate that goes into it, it doesn’t take a lot of capital. So one is a fabulous business, and one is a, yeah, it depends where it is and everything like that. Coca-Cola is popular in every place. But some places, and I mean, if you’re in the bottling business, it costs real money. You have real trucks out there, and you have all kinds of machinery, and you have capital expenditures coming up. We’ve got businesses that take very little capital that make really high returns on capital, and the ones the politicians talk about are making high returns, actually aren’t making high returns, in terms of capital. Property casualty insurance is kind of a rare business because you need capital as a guarantee fund that you will keep your promises, but you can use it to buy other low-intensive capital. I mean, you can buy Apple, and that would support that business. So that can be a pretty good business, and that’s one of the reasons we’ve done well over time. It’ll be interesting to see how much capital intensity. There’s more capital intensity going on with the magnificent 7 than there was a few years ago. I mean, basically, Apple has not really needed any capital over the years and its repurchased shares that dramatic amount of reduction. Whether that world is the same in the future or not is something. Hollywood’s answer was always to get their money from other people to put up the capital. A lot of people have gotten very rich in the country by essentially figuring out how to get others to put up the capital, and that’s what people do in the money management business. They get very, very rich because they get an override on other people’s capital. Incidentally, if all of you were paying 1% for investment management fees at Berkshire last year, you would have paid $8 billion for managing, and you really wouldn’t have had to do it, but investment management is a very good game because other people put up the capital and you charge them for the capital, whether they do well or not. And then you can charge them a lot more if they do well. I mean it’s a well-designed business for the people who practice it, and who can blame them, I mean, that is capitalism. I saw that in operation when I was working at Solomon, but I didn’t need to see it. I knew it existed anyway. The trick in life is to get somebody else’s capital, get an override on it. Charlie and I decided it wasn’t too elegant the business after a while, but we were not criticizing the efficacy of it. It just didn’t appeal to us after a while. I did it for 12 years, though, or something. The one difference that Charlie and I did from other people is that we put all our own money in and did it so we really did share the losses with our own capital, but we got an override on other people’s capital. And that’s been people have made advances where they get the override on other people’s capital without putting up any of their own capital to speak of. And that’s a very good business, but it can lead to a lot of abuse. Greg, you’ve watched capital management in the United States, and would you say that Canada’s behind or any in that respect?

**Greg Abel**（01:03:02）：Well, I think when it comes to their capital system, Warren, it’s very comparable. There’s no question other than as we know, I mean I think the US as far as having a capitalist system, it would be tough to be touched by any country, and when I think of Canada, there’s just certain responsibilities or obligations that the government wants to take on and aren’t going to leave with the public sector. And that’s just a decision that’s made by society or by the Canadian people, or if you look at another country, Australia or wherever it may be, it’s different. But when I think of capitalism, that drive is there and the desire to, you know, allocate capital properly. It’s very similar.

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:56）：It’s produced wonders in the United States if you think about it. But originally, with the Rockefellers and Kennedys and Carnegies and all that, they actually put up money to build a steel mill, whatever it may have been, with refineries and pipelines and all that sort of thing. They put up money to do it. Now the trick is to use other people’s money, basically. And you know you can’t blame human beings for behaving like humans, but you should be aware of what their motivations are. Capitalism in the United States has succeeded like nothing you’ve ever seen. It’s a combination of this magnificent cathedral, which has produced an economy like nothing the world’s ever seen. And then it’s got this massive casino attached. So you got the cathedral and the casino, and then the casino, everybody’s having a good time, and there’s lots of money changing hands and everything. You’ve got to make sure the cathedral gets fed, too, because of the temptation. And the temptation is very high now to go over to the casino where people say, you know, we’ve got magic boxes and all kinds of things that will do wonderful things for you. That’s where people are happiest. That’s where you get the most promised to you. That’s where the most money is for the people who are pushing things. The balance between the capital and the casino and the cathedral is very important that the United States in the next 100 years make sure that the cathedral is not overtaken by the casino, because people really like to go to casinos. And it’s just so much more fun. They bring bells, and when you win, they bring you drinks and everything else, and it’s designed to move money from one pocket to another. In the cathedrals, they are basically designing things that will be producing goods and services for 300 and some million people, like it’s never been done before in history. That’s an interesting system we developed, but it works. It dispenses rewards in what seems like a terribly capricious manner. I mean, the idea that people get what they deserve in life, and it’s hard to make that argument. But if you argue with that, any other system always seems to work better; the answer is we haven’t found one. So we’ll leave it to the next generation to send me the answer by Ouija board or whatever. OK. Station 2.
