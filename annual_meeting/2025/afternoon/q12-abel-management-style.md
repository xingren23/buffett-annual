## 问题

巴菲特对运营子公司是「放手不管」的风格（hands-off）。你会怎么描述自己的管理方式？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特抢答一个字：**「更好」（Better）**。
2. Abel 讲了过程：2018 年接手这个角色后，他必须逐家深入，让经理人讲清商业模式、风险与机会所在；这套「被迫学习」成了他的管理底座。
3. 巴菲特的自我批评是本段最有价值的部分：**「Greg 做得比我好，因为我根本不想像他那么努力」**——他之所以能「偷懒」，是因为生意本身足够好，加上股权结构让他不会被炒。
4. 关键判断：「做得好，不等于不能做得更好」，而「很多人其实希望被管理、需要有人帮他们被管理」。
5. 这是全篇对「总部该多干预还是少干预」最直接的表态，也预示了后巴菲特时代的风格差异。

## 关键引述

> **“It's working way better with Greg than with me because I just didn't want to work as hard as he worked.”**
>
> 「在 Greg 手里这套运转得比我好得多，因为我不想像他那么努力。」

> **“The fact that you can do pretty well doesn't mean you couldn't do better.”**
>
> 「你能做得不错，并不意味着你不能做得更好。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:28:53）：Greg，这个问题是问你的，来自股东 Jay Milroy。他写道：「Buffett 先生对运营子公司是『放手不管』的风格。你会怎么描述自己的方式？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:29:07）：更好。

**Greg Abel**（01:29:13）：我们有那些经理人在一线。我要说，回到 2018 年，能担任这个角色非常幸运，因为我必须大量学习各项业务。毫无疑问，Warren 当年买下这些企业时，他有那种总体性的了解；而我绝对必须和每一家去接触。他们在分享商业模式、方法、以及对风险与机会所在的看法上都非常棒。在这个过程中，我确实有很多问题、也想和他们深入交流。Warren 说好奇心很重要——一路走下去都要好奇。那也会是我的风格：对他们的业务、他们的框架提出问题、给出评论。同时，他们拥有很好的企业，而且运作得非常自主，这一点保持不变。但如果我看到机会——也许是在另一家企业看到的东西，或者我在他们行业里看到的机会——我们会讨论，看是否值得推进，或者我们是否恰当地应对了风险。我发现我们所有的经理人都非常乐于参与、愿意进行这样的对话。我想这就是我方式的一个反映。我还要说，想到我们的经理人，他们非常自主，他们经营自己的企业，比我更懂它。但如果我看到一个机会，值得他们花时间去和另一位经理聊聊——比如 GEICO 经历过技术转型，并不是只有他们需要考虑这件事——我们要确保对的人在对话，弄清如何从前人的经验中获益。所以我会说：更主动，但希望是以非常正面的方式。我们有一批卓越的人，所以在我经历的这个阶段里，这套方式运转得格外好。

**Warren Buffett**（01:31:27）：在 Greg 手里这套运转得比我好得多，因为我不想像他那么努力，而我当年可以蒙混过关——因为我们有一门基本上很好的生意，非常好的生意；而且因为有股权结构、加上我们业绩不错，我并不担心你们会把我炒掉。但「你能做得不错」并不意味着「你不能做得更好」。而 Greg 在很多事情上能做得更好。很多人希望被管理、需要有人在管理上帮他们；有些人不。有些人你只要别管他。我们有过一些经理人，你要是开始给他们下指令，那才是疯了，因为他们会直接辞职。我不会怪他们，因为我自己就是那种类型。但很多人确实欢迎方向和帮助，尤其是当他们是从 Greg 这样的人那里得到——他自己就是这么活的，而不是高高在上地说「你该这么做」，然后自己去做别的事。一个 manager 自己的行为和他要求下属的行为不一致，时间一长就不管用。人们想要一个自己敬佩的管理者，而如果那些人宣称一套、做得又是另一套，他们就不会敬佩他。一个组织的品质往下走比往上走容易得多。如果老板行为不端，所有人都会行为不端，那真的会传染。在上层管理中传染性没那么强，但如果管理者为了给自己行方便做一点小动作，很快——假设你经营一家零售店——很快所有员工都会告诉朋友他们有折扣；朋友想要什么，就记在他账上再拿折扣。一旦你开始向下偏离，那真的极具传染性，而且很难重建。所以你确实需要顶端有一个行为端正、不为自己谋私的人。我们有很多经理人极力避免做那种事，也有少数人反着来。公司多了，你会看到各种行为形态。Greg 会对此采取行动，而我一般比较宽松、不太去处理，但他在这一点上做得比我好得多。好，4 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（01:28:53）：Greg, this question is for you. It comes from a shareholder named Jay Milroy, who writes, “Mr. Buffett has a hands-off approach to managing the operating subsidiaries. How would you describe your approach?”

**Warren Buffett**（01:29:07）：Better.

**Greg Abel**（01:29:13）：Well, we’ve got our managers over there and I would say going back to 2018, it’s been very fortunate being this role because one I had to learn a lot of the businesses and there’s no question as Warren bought the businesses, he had that general knowledge. I absolutely had to engage with each of them, and they’ve been great in sharing their business models, their approach, and their thoughts around where the risks and opportunities are. And I think as we went through that, there’s no question I had questions and wanted to engage with them. And Warren talks about the curiosity being important as you go through things. That would be my style, but have questions and comments around their business, their frameworks. At the same time, they have great businesses, and they run them very autonomously and that remains in place. But if there’s opportunities to see where maybe seen something in another business or an opportunity, I may see in their industry, we’re going to discuss it and see if that’s something we should pursue or are we’re properly addressing the risk. I found all our managers to be absolutely engaging on that and want to have those dialogues. And I’d say that’s a reflection of my approach. I’d also say that when you think of our managers, again, very autonomous, they run their businesses, they know it better than I ever will. But if I see an opportunity that it’s well worth their time to talk to another one of our managers. If it’s GEICO, and they’ve gone through a technology transformation. They’re not by themselves that need to be thinking that way. We want to make sure the right folks are talking and figuring out how we can benefit from the prior experiences. So I would say, more active, but hopefully in a very positive way, and we got an exceptional group. So it’s worked out exceptionally well as I’ve gone through that period of time.

**Warren Buffett**（01:31:27）：It’s working way better with Greg than with me because I just didn’t want to work as hard as he worked and I could get away with it because we’ve got a basically good business, very good business and I wasn’t in danger of you firing me by virtue of both ownership and the fact that we would do pretty well. But the fact that you can do pretty well doesn’t mean you couldn’t do better. And Greg can do better at many things. Many people want to be managed, need help in being managed. Some don’t. Some you just leave alone. We’ve had managers it would have been crazy to start giving instructions to because they just quit. And I wouldn’t blame them because I’d be the same type myself. But a lot of people really do welcome direction and help, particularly when they’re getting it from somebody like Greg that really lives the life himself and doesn’t just come down from on high and say, “Here’s what you do”, while I do something else. A manager who behaves differently than what he’s asking the people beneath them to behave, it just doesn’t work over time. People want a manager that they admire, and they’re not going to admire them if those people profess to behave in one manner and behave in another manner. It’s easier for an organization to see its quality move downward than it upward. I mean, if the boss behaves badly, it causes everybody to behave badly. I mean, that is really catching. It’s not as catching on the way in upper management, but if the manager is doing a little things to grease his own situation, pretty soon… Let’s say you’re running a retail establishment, pretty soon, all the employees are telling their friends that they get a discount. If their friend wants something, they’ll put it on their account and then get the discount. Once you start deviating downward, it is really contagious and it’s hard to rebuild, so you really need someone who behaves well on top and is not playing games for their own benefit. And we get a lot of managers that bend over backwards not to do that sort of thing, and then we get a few that bend over forwards. If you get enough companies, you’re going to get a lot of forms of behaviour, and Greg does something about it, and I’ve generally been lax in doing something about it, but he’s done a way better job with that than I have. OK. Station 4.
