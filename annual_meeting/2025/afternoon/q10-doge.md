## 问题

你认为 DOGE（政府效率部）对美国长期的健康是净正面还是净负面？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特先调侃「给我出个难一点的」，然后把问题抽象成**官僚制本身**：官僚主义「无处不在且具有传染性」，大公司里也普遍如此，「多数都可以管得更好，伯克希尔在很多方面也一样」。
2. 政府是「终极形态」，因为**它几乎没有制衡**。
3. 他把这条线索接到货币上：正因为缺乏约束，政府可以印钞，这才是「让你对未来货币感到不安」的根源。
4. 他没有直接评价 DOGE 这个机构本身——这是他一贯的处理方式：谈机制，不谈具体政治人物。

## 关键引述

> **“I think that bureaucracy is something that is amazingly prevalent and contagious, even in our capitalist system and the big corporations.”**
>
> 「我认为官僚主义极其普遍且具有传染性，即便在我们的资本主义体系和大公司里也是如此。」

> **“The government is the ultimate. So it really doesn't have any checks on it.”**
>
> 「政府是终极形态，它实际上没有任何制衡。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:14:17）：这个问题来自俄勒冈州波特兰的 Scott Williams。他说：「你认为 DOGE（政府效率部）的净效果，对美国长期的健康是正面的还是负面的？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:29）：你干嘛不给我出个难一点的？我认为官僚主义极其普遍且具有传染性，即便在我们的资本主义体系和大公司里也是如此。绝大多数公司看起来都还能管得更好，我确信 Berkshire 在很多方面也一样。而政府是终极形态，它实际上没有任何制衡。这就是为什么它让你在一定程度上对未来货币感到不安：他们能印钞。如果你有一批靠向人们许诺而当选的人——这不是说他们在各种议题上不真诚——但没有哪个政客会对人说：「我真觉得你口气不太好，不介意的话请站远一点。」这种事不会发生。我认为「如何控制政府收入与支出」这个问题从来没有被完全解决过，而且确实严重伤害过许多文明。我不认为我们对此免疫，我们已经很接近了。但如果你告诉我，在一个民主国家里你要怎么进去真正改变事情——你知道，我们现在的财政赤字是不可持续的。在很长很长的时间里不可持续；我们不知道那是两年还是二十年，因为从来没有过像美国这样的国家。但那句话是：「如果一件事不能永远持续下去，它就会结束。」这是著名经济学家 Herbert Stein 说的。我们在做一件不可持续的事，而它有这样一个特点：到某个点就变得无法控制，基本上你就放弃了。Paul Volcker 在美国阻止了那件事发生，但我们多次接近。美国还是有过非常严重的通胀，只是还没有失控过；而这不是你想去试验的东西，因为它会自我喂养。所以我不想要那份工作——去纠正美国收支的问题，现在缺口大约 7%，而大概 3% 才是可持续的；你离那个水平越远，就越接近「无法控制」的起点。我认为这活儿我不想干，但我认为应该有人干，而国会似乎并不擅长。作为一个国家，我们一直有很多问题，但这一项是我们自己招来的。我们有世界上从未见过的收入流、资本生成流、大脑生产机器。而如果你要找一种把它搞砸的方式，那就跟货币有关。很多地方都发生过。理论上，你应该让把事情搞砸的人承担重大的下行后果。有下行，也有上行。这是「世界史上最成功的公司」才会有的问题。眼下我们还有一点空间去解决。就说到这儿，我闭嘴，去 3 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（01:14:17）：This question comes from Scott Williams in Portland, OR. He said, “Do you think the net benefit of DOGE will be positive or negative for the long-term health of the United States?”

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:29）：Well, why don’t you give me a hard one? I think that bureaucracy is something that is amazingly prevalent and contagious, even in our capitalist system and the big corporations. Overwhelmingly, most of them look like they could be run better. I’m sure Berkshire does in many respects. And the government is the ultimate. So it really doesn’t have any checks on it. That’s why it scares you to some extent about what the future of the currency will be, because they can print currency and if you have people that get elected by promising people things and that doesn’t mean that they aren’t sincere about all kinds of items, but there’s no politician that says to anybody that, “I really think you have bad breath and if you don’t mind would just step over that away from me.” It just doesn’t happen. I think the problem of how you control revenue and expenses in government is the one that is never fully solved and has really hurt dramatically many civilizations. I don’t think we’re immune to it, and we’ve come close to it. But if you tell me how in the democracy you go in and really change things, you know, we’re operating at a fiscal deficit now that is unsustainable. Over a very long period of time, we don’t know whether that means two years or 20 years because there’s never been a country like the United States, but you know that, “If something can’t go on forever, it will end.” The quote is from Herbert Stein, a famous economist. We are doing something that is unsustainable, and it has the aspect that it becomes uncontrollable to a certain point. I mean, essentially, you just give up on it. And Paul Volcker, you know, kept that from happening in the United States. But we came close multiple times. We still had very substantial inflation in the United States, but it’s never run away yet, and that’s not something you want to try and experiment with because it feeds on itself. So I wouldn’t want the job of trying to correct what’s going on in revenue and the expenditures of the United States, with roughly a 7% gap when probably a 3% gap is sustainable and then the further away you get from that, the more you get to where the uncontrollable begins and I think that it’s a job I don’t want, but it’s a job I think should be done and Congress does not seem good at doing it. We’ve got a lot of problems always as a country, but this is one we bring on ourselves. We have a revenue stream, a capital-producing stream, a brains-producing machine like the world has never seen. And if you picked a way to screw it up, it would involve the currency. That’s happened a lot of places. In theory, you would make it so there was a substantial downside for anybody that screw things up. There is a downside, there’s upside. It’s the problem of the most successful company in the history of the world. At this point, we have a little room to go and solve. With that, I’ll shut up and go on to Station 3.
