## 问题

未来十年 Greg 要配置的资本将超过伯克希尔历史上任何一个时期。他对资本配置的看法如何？会延续「能力圈」那一套吗？

## 回答要点

1. Abel 的出发点是文化与价值观先行：伯克希尔的配置逻辑建立在「经理人自治 + 总部极少干预」的组织基础上，先守住这个前提，再谈机会。
2. 他把最大机会指向**电力与能源基础设施**：满足长期电力需求所需的资本「极其庞大」，伯克希尔「处于有利位置」。
3. 巴菲特接着把话题抬到国家层面：美国需要一次「电力系统的重新思考与再定向」，问题性质类似当年的州际公路系统——**50 个州各自为政做不成，必须借助联邦政府的力量**。
4. 他也泼冷水：任何这类工程「在每个州都会有反对者」，如果当年州际公路要公投，早就被拖死了。
5. 隐含结论：伯克希尔能出钱、能出力，但绕不开政治决策，而政治决策不在股东资金该承担的风险范围内。

## 关键引述

> **“The capital required to meet the long-term needs of what's currently projected as demand is enormous and we as Berkshire we'll be in a good position to help address those needs.”**
>
> 「（Abel）要满足目前预测的长期电力需求，所需资本极其庞大，伯克希尔处于有利位置去帮助解决。」

> **“The muscle of the federal government will be needed.”**
>
> 「需要联邦政府的力量。」

> **“If they took a vote on the Interstate Highway system, it would have been slowed down to an incredible degree.”**
>
> 「如果当年州际公路系统要投票，它会被拖慢到难以想象的程度。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（00:10:25）：这个问题来自亚利桑那州 Scottsdale 的股东 David Rubin，是问 Greg 的。「几十年来我们听惯了、也熟悉 Warren 和 Charlie 的投资逻辑以及他们的能力圈。在接任 CEO 后的头十年里，Greg 要配置的资本将超过 Berkshire 历史上任何一个时期配置过的量。鉴于此，我想听听 Greg 对资本配置的看法，尤其是投向新业务。」

**Greg Abel**（00:10:59）：好的。想到 Berkshire，我们是很幸运的——这一点我们谈过——先说我们的起点。显然要谈到投资和相关的资本配置，但我们的起点很好：公司内部有很好的文化。我们有价值观，这些价值观是我们管理团队、实际上是由 Warren 以及 Charlie 和所有与公司相关的人共同定义的，这些价值观为 Berkshire 的未来打下了很好的基础。显然，随着我们投放资本、配置资本，这对 Berkshire 的前行至关重要，同样重要的是围绕风险的管理。说到我们的价值观，有两条绝对关键。第一是维护 Berkshire 和我们公司的声誉。我认为无论投资还是各家企业的经营方式，这永远是优先事项，是必须放在最前面的东西。同样，回到 Warren 关于资产负债表的说法，我们会有一个堡垒般的资产负债表。我认为我们的首席董事 Sue Decker 昨天讲得很好：我们现在有一大笔现金，而拥有它是巨大的资产，这会继续是我们的哲学。当我们可以投放时，我们会投得很好；但同样，我们把它视为一项战略资产，它让我们能挺过困难时期，同时不必依赖任何人。所以，这仍将是我们的投资哲学。我们会保持是 Berkshire，我们从不会依赖某家银行或其他方来让 Berkshire 成功。接下来我要谈，资本配置绝对关键，但与它同行的是风险管理和对风险的理解，这落在我们所有经理身上——保险的、非保险的——我们会把它贯穿整个 Berkshire。还有一条价值观，和我要讲的方向有关：归根到底，我们有一批很好的经营公司，它们能产生可观的现金流——无论是保险公司创造浮存金，还是各类非保险公司每年产生大量现金流。我们打算继续确保这是 Berkshire 的实力所在，这对我们的长期成功绝对关键。有了这些现金流、浮存金，加上前面提到的我们资产负债表上已有的可观资源，我们会真正延续一套非常相似——或者说完全相同——的哲学，和我们现在以及过去 60 年一直用的那套。我们会先看自己业务内部的机会，我指的是保险和非保险业务内部：它们是否资本充足、是否有空间去经营自己的业务、这种空间是否会持续存在？它们会自主运作，但最终 Berkshire 仍然管理着投入这些业务、以及可能从这些业务中流出的资本。其次的机会，是整体收购、100% 收购企业。有时候时机很好，我们能做到。Warren 提到过，上个季度就有一笔很有意思的 100 亿美元收购，但相对风险而言，价值必须对。如果对了，我们就想拥有它；如果不是时候，将来还会有拥有这类资产的时机。再往后，是通过股权拥有公司的一部分。但正如 Warren 一直强调的，这也是我们思考这类公司的方式：虽然我们只拥有一家公司的一部分，但我们拥有的是那部分现金流、是那部分资产负债表，不只是一张股票凭证。而在我们动手时，我们真的会带着「长期拥有这家公司」的想法去做。同样要理解的是，无论是 100% 的公司还是 1% 的公司：我们是否彻底理解，是否对其经济前景有强烈的判断？Warren 前面说过，五年后、十年后、二十年后——如果我们对那没有判断，我们就不会投，无论是 100% 收购还是通过股票买 2%。我们必须彻底理解那些前景长什么样；而与理解前景相伴的，是理解这些业务的底层风险。这其实就是过去 60 年 Warren 和团队配置资本的哲学。它不会变，这也是我们往前的做法。

**Warren Buffett**（00:17:47）：这件事我不想讲太久，但它很重要，因为很明显，这个国家需要一次巨大的改进、重新思考，某种程度上甚至需要重新定向。我们已经超出了美国本该有的那个模型。某种意义上，这个问题类似于当年的州际公路系统：你真的需要政府的力量才能把事情做成，因为当有 48 个或 50 个司法辖区、各自有各自的想法时，事情就不好办。当然，二战时我们用一美元一小时的薪酬把人请来，我们知道必须疯狂地造船，我们知道必须在几天之内——不是几周、不是几个月——把福特汽车从汽车制造商转成飞机制造商。有一些真正重大的投资情形，而我们在私人体系里拥有别人没有的资本。我们在发电和输电这个领域有特殊的技术能力，这个国家会需要它。但我们必须想出办法，从政府的角度、公众的角度、以及 Berkshire 的角度都说得通——而我们还没想出来。我不知道 Greg 是否愿意解释几个主要难题，但这是一个明确的、当下就存在的、需要数千亿美元投入的用途。你知道，有些人设基金，说他们只是「组装东西」就能拿钱，但那不是处理方式。处理方式是政府与私营产业之间某种类似战时的合作。我想当年修州际公路时，政府并没有自己拉一支队伍去浇水泥，但你需要合作。我认为在能源上我们正处在那个时点，但我不认为我们取得了什么实质性进展。再多讲讲。

**Greg Abel**（00:21:02）：好。我认为这些并不特殊，它某种程度上反映了我们所处的位置。各个行业都会有非常重大的投资机会。正如 Warren 提到的电力行业、能源领域，我们凭借现有业务对此很熟悉。满足目前预测的长期需求所需的资本极其庞大，而 Berkshire 处于有利位置去帮助解决这些需求。但围绕它的模式、以及部署这类资本需要解决的风险，会和今天不同。

**Warren Buffett**（00:21:53）：是的，需要联邦政府的力量。但考验在于：能不能让 48 个或 50 个（视情况而定）司法辖区合作去做一件有反对意见的事——我们在每一个州都会遇到反对。如果二战或者州际公路系统要投票，它会被拖慢到难以想象的程度。所以问题是，如何运用这个国家的优势，真正把它变成它本应具备的样子，让一个由 48 个相连州加两个不相连州组成的共和国能够自我保全。会发生什么，值得观察。但我们确实有资本，也确实拥有极少数地方才有的知识。我们知道这盘游戏是什么，但要把能源、知识和资本这些东西拼在一起，并不容易。这应该是这个国家有能力做到的事，但这个国家不是那样设计的：它不是为「有 48 个不同辖区、任何一个都能把你正在尝试的事搞砸」而设计的。战时达成一致是一回事，和平时期是另一回事。这是下一代人的题目，但它很重要。好，10 号站。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Becky Quick**（00:10:25）：This question comes from David Rubin, a shareholder from Scottsdale, AZ. It’s a question for Greg. “We’ve heard over the decades and are familiar with Warren and Charlie’s investment thesis and their circle of competence. During the first ten years after taking over as CEO, Greg will be tasked with allocating more capital during that time than Berkshire has had to allocate in its history. Given this, I’d like to hear from Greg about his views on capital allocation, particularly into new businesses.”

**Greg Abel**（00:10:59）：Sure. It’s very fortunate when you think of Berkshire again, and we’ve talked about this where we start from and clearly touch on the investment in the related investment allocation, but we start from where great place where we’ve got a great culture within the business. We have values that we as a management team and really as defined by Warren and those of Charlie and everybody associated with the business, got great values that really set Berkshire up well for the future, and obviously as we deploy capital and allocate capital, it’s critical to Berkshire as we go forward and equally it’s around managing risk. But when I think of our values, there are a couple that are absolutely critical. One will maintain the reputation of Berkshire and that of our company. I view that in investing or how we operate things across each of our businesses, that will always be a priority and something that will ensure it is in the forefront of our minds. I think equally, as we then look at our, back to Warren’s balance sheet comment, we will have a fortress of a balance sheet. I thought Sue Decker, our lead director, said it well yesterday. We’ve got a significant set of cash right now, but it’s an enormous asset to have that, and that will continue to be a philosophy. When we can deploy it, we’ll deploy it well. But equally, we do recognize it as a strategic asset and it allows us to weather the difficult times and at the same and not be dependent on anybody. So, again, that will be an investment philosophy. We will remain Berkshire, and we will never be dependent on a bank or some other party for Berkshire to be successful. I would then touch on allocation of capital being absolutely critical, but with that comes management of risk and understanding risk, and that falls upon all our managers, insurance, non-insurance, but we’ll bring that across Berkshire. The other value I would touch on, but that really relates to where I’m going, is that, ultimately, we have a great set of operating companies that do produce significant cash flows. Be it in the insurance companies creating float or various non-insurance companies producing significant cash flows on an annual basis. We intend to continue to ensure that’s the strength of Berkshire as we go forward. It’s absolutely critical to our long-term success. Now, with those cash flows and with the float, and then equally as touched on, we have significant resources already in our balance sheet. We’ll really continue to move forward with a very similar philosophy, or it’s an identical philosophy to what we’ve had currently and for the past 60 years. We’ll start by looking at those opportunities within our business. And by that, I mean, within our insurance, non-insurance businesses. Are they properly capitalized and have the opportunity to manage their business, and will that continue to exist? They’ll operate in an autonomous way. But in the end, Berkshire still manages the capital that will go into those businesses or what potentially will come out of those businesses. Equally, the next opportunity is to acquire businesses in their totality or 100%. There are great times when we can do that. Warren touched on. We had one that was interesting in the last quarter, the $10 billion acquisition, but again, the value relative to the risk has to be right. And if it’s right, we want to own it. If it’s not the time, there’ll be another time to own assets like that. Then there’s the opportunity to own pieces of companies through equity. But as Warrens always highlighted, and again, this will be our approach to how we think around those companies. Though we own a piece of the company. We own a piece of that cash flow. We own a piece of their balance sheet. It’s not just a share certificate. And as we approach that, really, we’ll approach it with the thought that we’re going to own this company for the long term. It’s again understanding what, be it the 100% company or the 1% company. Do we thoroughly understand, and do we have a strong view of what the economic prospects of those companies will look like? And Warren said it earlier, five years from now, 10 years from now, 20 years from now, if we don’t have a view of that, we won’t be investing, be it 100% or 2% of a company through equities. We have to thoroughly understand what those prospects look like, and associated with understanding those prospects, we need to understand the underlying risk of the businesses and it’s really the investment philosophy and how Warren and the team have allocated capital for the past 60 years. It will not change, and it’s the approach we will take as we go forward.

**Warren Buffett**（00:17:47）：I don’t want to go on too long on this, but this is important because it’s very obvious that the country, for example, needs an incredible improvement, rethinking, redirection, even to some extent. We’ve outgrown what would be the model that America should have. And in a sense, it’s a problem something akin to the interstate highway system, where you needed the power of the government really to get things done because it doesn’t work so well when you get 48 or 50 jurisdictions that each have their own way of thinking about things. Of course, in World War Two, we called in people at a dollar an hour, and we knew we had to turn out ships like crazy, and we knew that we had to convert Ford Motor from being a car manufacturer into an aircraft manufacturer in a matter of days, not weeks, not months. There are certain really major investment situations where we have capital like nobody else has in the private system. We have particular knowhow in the whole generation and transmission arena. The country is going to need it. But we have to figure out a way that makes sense from the standpoint of the government, from the standpoint of the public, and from the standpoint of Berkshire, and we haven’t figured that out yet. I don’t know whether Greg wants to even explain a few of the major problems, but that is a clear and present use of hundreds of billions of dollars. You know, you have people that set up funds and say, they’re getting paid for just assembling stuff. But that’s not the way to handle it. The way to handle it is to have some kind of government-private industry cooperation similar to what you do in a war. I don’t think when they were doing the highway system, that the government set up his own guys that were going to pour a whole cement or anything like that, but they you needed a cooperation and we’re at that point, I think in terms of energy, but I don’t think we’ve made any progress particularly. Tell us more about that.

**Greg Abel**（00:21:02）：Yeah. And I think these are not unique in that it’s sort of reflective of where we’re at. There will be very significant investment opportunities across a variety of industries. As Warren touched on in the electric industry or the energy space, we obviously know that well with our existing business. The capital required to meet the long-term needs of what’s currently projected as demand is enormous and we as Berkshire we’ll be in a good position to help address those needs, but the model around it and the risks that need to be addressed to deploy that type of capital will be different than they are today.

**Warren Buffett**（00:21:53）：Yeah, the muscle of the federal government will be needed. But the test of whether you can have 48 or 50, depending on the nature of things, jurisdictions that are cooperating to do something that has opposition, we’ll have opposition in every single state. And if they took in the vote during World War 2 or they took a vote on the Interstate Highway system, it would have been slowed down to an incredible degree. So the question is how to use the strengths of this country to actually turn it into what it should be capable of or self-preserving in a republic with 48 connected and a couple of unconnected States. It will be interesting to see what happens, but we do have capital, and we actually have some knowledge that very few places have. I mean, that we know what the games are about, but putting together that energy with knowledge and with capital and everything is it’s just not easy. It should be something that we’re capable of in the country, but the country was not designed in a certain way; it was not designed for having 48 different jurisdictions that could mess up anything that we’re attempting to do. During wartime, it’s one thing to get agreement, but during peacetime, it’s a different problem. That’s going to be one for the next generation, but it’s important. OK, station 10.
