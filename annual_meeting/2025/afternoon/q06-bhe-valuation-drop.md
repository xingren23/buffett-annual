## 问题

2022 年末从 Greg Abel 手中买 1% BHE 花了 8.7 亿美元，隐含估值 870 亿美元；2024 年买剩余 8% 时，隐含估值已明显更低，两年间下跌约 44%。这是野火诉讼的结果，还是监管环境变化？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特承认**无法量化**最终在 PacifiCorp 上会损失多少，「这取决于后续判决与政治决策」。
2. 真正的变化是「态度」：太平洋西北地区这一案例所展现的司法与监管态度，让伯克希尔重新评估**各州的可运营性**。
3. 他的措辞罕见地重：「有些州现在对我们是**砒霜**（rat poison）」——即便多数州仍然友好。
4. 这与上午「该退出就退出」的表态互相印证：公用事业的估值折价，本质是「政治风险定价」。

## 关键引述

> **“We don't know how much we'll lose out on PacifiCorp and decisions that are made.”**
>
> 「我们不知道在 PacifiCorp 上最终会损失多少，也不知道会做出什么决定。」

> **“There are some, now, they're rat poison, as Charlie would say, to operate in.”**
>
> 「有些州——用查理的话说——现在对我们是砒霜，不适合在那里经营。」

**Becky Quick**（00:47:04）：这个问题你在很多方面都提到过，也接着上一个回答，但我从好几个人那里都收到了同一个问题，所以我想问一下。长期股东、巴拿马的 Ricardo Briz 说，他非常高兴看到 Berkshire 收购 BHE 的 100%。这分两步完成：第一步是 2022 年底，从 Greg Abel 手中买 1%，花了 8.7 亿美元，隐含 BHE 估值 870 亿美元；第二步是 2024 年，从 Walter Scott Jr. 家族手中买下剩余 8%，花 39 亿美元，隐含企业估值 488 亿美元。第二笔更大的交易意味着，仅仅两年，估值就下降了 44%。Ricardo 写道，PacifiCorp 的责任似乎不足以解释这一点。那么，是什么因素造成了 BHE 在这两个时点上的价值差异？（图注：Berkshire 为少数股权支付的价格所隐含的 BHE 整体估值——2022 年：1% 花了 8.7 亿，对应 870 亿；2024 年：8% 花了 39 亿，对应 488 亿。）

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:06）：我们不知道在 PacifiCorp 上最终会损失多少、也不知道会做出什么决定。但我们也知道，那个具体案例所展现出的某些态度，在整个公用事业体系里我都能找到对应，有很多州到目前为止对经营非常友好、相当体面。而有些州——用查理的话说——现在对我们是砒霜。这种认知被强化了。当我们看到太平洋西北地区发生的事，再被我们在其他情形下看到的「公用事业如何被对待」所强化。而且，这不仅仅是 PacifiCorp 牵涉什么的直接问题，它是对一种社会趋势的外推。第二，我们在房地产业务上还有一个完全没想到的决定。这类事情会改变价值，法院也会改变价值。当你只持有可流通证券时，做这些判断要容易得多；当你拥有实体企业时就不是。70 年里我看着各个行业和公司发生的事，做过很多这类判断。但 Greg 做了退出的决定，我们觉得没问题。他对房地产领域、公用事业领域接下来会发生什么并不掌握内情，而我们也没有心情去卖任何一门生意。但基于社会因素，Berkshire Hathaway Energy 的价值确实比两年前低了不少，这种情况在我们一些业务里会发生。纺织业务就肯定发生过。公用事业这门生意，已经不如几年前那么好；如果谁不信，就去看夏威夷电力（Hawaiian Electric），再看加州眼下野火局势里的爱迪生（Edison）以及他们的社会趋势。哦，我监视器上刚收到一条提示：「书已售完」。那你们就把钱花在别的东西上吧。这是软糖，我正在吃。但解释就是这样：价值会变，而且不总是向上变。当年和 Greg 做交易时，我们本会乐意以那个价格买断 Scott 家族；而当我们要和 Scott 家族打交道时，我们不会乐意付 Greg 拿到的那个价格。这就像 Berkshire 的股票：我们在某个价位买回，条件变了就在更低的价位买回。多年来我们付得越来越多，因为价值在积累，但它不是直线上升的。我要说，我们今天收购公用事业公司的热情，和两年前不同了。别的行业也会这样，但在公用事业上相当戏剧性。之所以在公用事业上尤其戏剧性，是因为它们将需要大量资金。所以，如果你需要大量资金，你大概就该以某种方式行事，鼓励人们给你大量资金，然后我们再看会走到哪里。我们希望公用事业做得好，但我们要对 Berkshire Hathaway 的股东负责。好，又是 1 号站。

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:06）：We don’t know how much we’ll lose out on PacifiCorp and decisions that are made, but we also know that certain of the attitudes demonstrated by that particular example, I’ve got analogues throughout the utility system and there’s a lot of States that so far have been very good, decent to operate in. And there are some, now, they’re rat poison. As Charlie would say, to operate in that knowledge was accentuated. When we saw what happened in the Pacific Northwest and it’s accentuated by what we’ve seen as to how utilities have been treated in certain other situations. And then on top of that, it wasn’t just a direct question of what was involved at PacifiCorp. It’s an extrapolation of a societal trend. Secondly, we also had a decision we didn’t expect at all in the real estate business. Those kinds of things can change values, and courts can change values. It’s a lot easier to make those decisions when you just own marketable securities than when you own businesses. I’ve made plenty of those decisions as I’ve watched what has happened in various industries and companies over 70 years. But Greg made the decision, which was fine with us, to get out. He had no knowledge of what was going to be happening in either the real estate field or the utility field, and we’re not in the mood to sell any business of it. But Berkshire Hathaway Energy is worth considerably less money than it was two years ago, based on societal factors, and that happens in some of our businesses. It certainly happened to our textile business. The public utility business is not as good a business as it was a couple of years ago, and if anybody doesn’t believe that thing, look at Hawaiian Electric and they look and look at Edison in the current wildfires situation in California and their societal trends. Oh, I just got a note here on my monitor that says, “The books are now sold out.” So you spend your money on other things. Here’s fudge. This is what I’m eating. But that’s the explanation, values change, and they don’t always change upwards. When we made the deal with Greg, we would have been happy to buy out the Scott family at that price, and when we needed to deal with the Scott Company, we wouldn’t have been happy to pay Greg the price he received. But that’s like Berkshire shares we bought in stock at X, and we buy in stock at less than X if conditions change. Over the years, we pay more and more because it builds in value, but it doesn’t do it in a straight line. I would say that our enthusiasm for buying public utility companies is different now than it would have been a couple of years ago. That happens in other industries, too, but it’s pretty dramatic in public utilities. And it’s particularly dramatic in public utilities because they are going to need lots of money. So, if you’re going to need lots of money, you probably ought to behave in a way that encourages people to give you lots of money, and then we will see where we go. We’d like to see Public Utilities do well, but our responsibilities to the shareholders of Berkshire Hathaway. OK, station 1, again.
