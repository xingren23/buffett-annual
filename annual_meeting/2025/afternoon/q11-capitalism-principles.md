## 问题

想象现在是 1776 年，你和本杰明·富兰克林坐在一起，为新国家的制度奠基。你会主张哪些核心经济原则，来建立一个公平、有韧性、机会驱动的资本主义社会？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特先让贤：「我会对富兰克林说，你继续想就好，别问我——你会想出比我更好的主意。」他称富兰克林可能是「最后一个通晓这个国家各个方面的人」。
2. 他借富兰克林的遗嘱讲**复利**：那份遗嘱把一笔钱留给波士顿和费城，要求按复利滚存多年，是他常讲的「复利奇迹」案例。
3. 真正的原则落回他信里写过的那句：**储蓄 + 明智地把储蓄配置出去**，才是让社会产出持续增长的前提；「如果美国人把生产出来的全消费掉，这个国家只会原地打转」。
4. 他也承认资本主义「有缺陷、有滥用，某些方面比以往更严重」，但仍是「其他经济体系无法匹敌的奇迹制造机」。

## 关键引述

> **“I would probably say to Ben Franklin, you just keep thinking and don't talk to me because you'll come up with some better ideas than I will.”**
>
> 「我大概会对本·富兰克林说：你继续想就好，别问我，因为你会想出比我更好的主意。」

> **“It has its faults and abuses — in certain respects more egregious now than ever — but it also can work wonders unmatched by other economic systems.”**
>
> 「它有缺陷也有滥用——某些方面比以往更严重——但它也能创造出其他经济体系无法匹敌的奇迹。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Sasha**（01:20:19）：您好，Buffett 先生。我叫 Sasha，来自德国。首先我要感谢你，因为你对我的人生、以及我所爱之人的人生影响巨大，这是无价的。Buffett 先生，想象现在是 1776 年，你坐在本杰明·富兰克林身边，帮助塑造一个新国家的基础。为了建设一个公平、有韧性、机会驱动的资本主义社会，一个能支撑子孙后代长期繁荣的社会，你会主张哪些核心经济原则？

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:12）：这是个好问题。但我大概会对本·富兰克林说：你继续想就好，别问我，因为你会想出比我更好的主意。他是个极其了不起的人，可能几乎是最后一个通晓这个国家各个方面的人。他发明了各种东西。顺便说，我们刚在谈复利的力量——他留下一份遗嘱，给费城留了一笔钱、给波士顿留了另一笔，要作为范例滚存几百年。你知道复利的力量，诸如此类，他远远超越了他的时代。所以如果我和他坐在那棵树下，我能做的最好的事，就是让开、让他继续思考。他看到了成功可能给社会带来的问题，也看到了其他问题——各领域更紧迫的问题——但还有：如何对待 80 亿人的问题。因为我们没法把自己和世界其他地方分开。我们可以做世界的榜样，我认为既然我们在这个国家享有如此多的好运、而且我们确实有一套相当不错的制度，我们就该如此。我不认为对着世界讲道理能走多远——「你应该告诉他们该怎么过我们的生活」。我想，几百年前还是另一批国家在主导世界，现在你开始给他们建议，这会招来一定程度的怨气。对着你刚刚赢了这场游戏的一群人说教，在沟通和说服上是真正的错误。我会建议本：想清楚怎么赢下这场游戏，同时保持相当的谦逊。我还会告诉他，尽量设计一个不要发明太多能毁灭地球的东西的制度。你知道，那些东西一旦放出去就变得无法控制。我们当年造原子弹别无选择，但拥有这种能力的国家从一个扩展到八个、九个——大概很快还有伊朗——这就是错误。这个社会承受不起这种错误：把问题从「一个变量」变成「九个变量」去解。这在当时完全可以理解。原子弹首次使用时我父亲在国会，Sam Rayburn 让整个众议院毫不知情，这很惊人——他们本该批所有拨款，435 位议员，却完全不知道自己是在为洛斯阿拉莫斯拨款、不知道芝加哥在干什么、田纳西在干什么。但无论如何，我们确实拥有一个远超本·富兰克林想象的社会。它在朝正确的方向走。在解决某些问题上——就像我们做出「人人生而平等」这类宽泛宣言，然后我们又做了一些我们做过的事——但总体而言，我们朝着正确方向前进，同时也面临问题。我不知道本·富兰克林会怎么处理「大规模杀伤性武器落到许多人手里」这个问题，当你看到一个本质上存在赢家和输家的世界，而赢家羞辱输家、干各种事情。我把这些留给这里年轻得多的人去得出答案。但这仍然是最美好的。美国发生过的事，你做梦都想不出来。所以这是最好的地方、最好的时代，远远领先。你想想几百年前：有人拔掉你几颗牙，往你嘴里灌威士忌。那就是勉强糊口。尤其在中西部，想象每年都要等密苏里河封冻，才能知道你的马车能不能过去，车上可能还坐着孕妇。我这一生里发生的正面变化，实在惊人。那么问题就是：怎么保持它？怎么改进它？我确实认为，这一切的根本，是拥有一种不被贬值的货币。当所有信任政府的人被坑、所有想出办法从中牟利的人变富或更富时，这对一个社会的稳定意味着什么，你可以自己想。我不认为你会想要一个那样运转的社会。总之，那是第 3 区。接下来是 Becky，对吗？

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Sasha**（01:20:19）：Hello, Mr. Buffett. My name is Sasha from Germany. And first of all, I want to thank you because you made such a great impact on my life and the lives of the people I love, and that’s priceless. Mr. Buffett, imagine it’s 1776. And you’re sitting alongside Benjamin Franklin, helping to shape the foundation of a new nation. What core economic principles would you advocate for building a fair, resilient and opportunity-driven capitalism society, one that supports long-term prosperity for future generations?

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:12）：Well, that’s a good question, but I would probably say to Ben Franklin, you just keep thinking and don’t talk to me because you’ll come up with some better ideas than I will. He was an incredibly remarkable person. I mean, he’s almost probably the last person to have a grasp of every aspect of activity in the country. He invented all kinds of things, and he incidentally… We were talking about the power of compound and that sort of thing, he left a will that left some money to Philadelphia and another sum to Boston that would serve as an example for a couple of hundred years. You know the power of compounding and all kinds of things that he was so far ahead of his time that the best thing I could do if I were under that tree with him was to get out of his way and let him keep thinking. He saw the problems that success could bring to a society as well as other problems, I mean, more immediate problems in all kinds of fields, but the problems of how to take 8 billion people, because there’s no way we can separate ourselves from the rest of the world. We can be an example for the rest of the world, and I think it behooves us since we have had all this good fortune in this country, and we do have a pretty good system. I don’t think you get very far by lecturing the world on how. You were the one who should tell them what they should do with our lives. I think you get a certain amount of resentment when just a few hundred years ago, a whole different group of countries were running the world and now you start giving them advice, and I think it’s a real mistake in communication and persuasion to lecture a bunch of people where you just won the game. I would advise Ben to figure out how to win the game and keep a certain amount of humility at the same time. And I would tell him to try and design a system that doesn’t invent too many things that can destroy the planet. You know, they become uncontrollable once you get them out there. There was no alternative to us developing the atom bomb, but the expansion of the number of people who have the ability from one to eight, and nine, probably pretty soon, with Iran. I mean, that’s a mistake. This society just could not afford to make, I mean, solve the problem in nine variables instead of simply one. Now it’s totally understandable. My dad was in Congress when the atom bomb was first used, it’s amazing how Sam Rayburn kept the House of Representatives uninformed because they are supposed to appropriate all the money and 435 congressmen there and they had no idea they were appropriating money for Los Alamos or what was going on in Chicago or what was going on in Tennessee. But anyway, we do have a society that is far beyond anything that Ben Franklin dreamed of. It’s moving in the right direction. Towards solving some problems the way we made the kind of broad declarations about all men being created equal, etc. Then we did some of the things we did, but generally speaking, we moved in the right direction, but we faced problems. I don’t know how Ben Franklin would attack the problem of what you do once you get weapons of mass destruction in many hands, and when you essentially look at the world and something where there are winners and losers. And that the winners humiliate the losers and do all kinds of things. I’ll let the people are a lot younger here. I’ll let them figure out the answers on that, but it’s still the most wonderful. There’s never been anything you could dream of like what happened in the United States. So it’s the best place and the best time to be alive by miles in the street. Just think of a couple of hundred years ago, and somebody yanking out a few of your teeth and pouring whiskey down you. I mean, it’s subsistence and particularly in this Midwest, just imagine waiting till the Missouri froze over every year just to see whether you get your wagon across, and maybe they have a pregnant woman in the back. It’s just amazing what has happened of a positive nature during my lifetime. And then the question, how do you keep it in? How do you improve it? I do think that the fundamentals of it all, though, are having a currency that does not get debased. What that does to the stability of a society where all the people that trust their government get screwed and all the people that figure out ways to profit from it become rich or richer. I don’t think you want a society that operates in that manner. So anyway. Let’s see, that was Section 3. So we’re going to Becky, right?
