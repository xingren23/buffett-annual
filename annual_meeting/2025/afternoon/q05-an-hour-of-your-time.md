## 问题

74 年前的 1951 年 1 月，你坐 8 小时火车从纽约到华盛顿，只为去 GEICO  office 学保险，到了发现门是锁的，你还是坚持敲门。今天 Wid Ahmed 就在这里——在「时间老人」赢之前，你愿意给他一小时吗？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特的回应不是答应，也不是拒绝，而是**把这个请求普遍化**：「要给这里 4 万人都给一小时的话，我余生会很有意思。」
2. 他接着给出了当年「能见到 CEO」的真实原因：那时公司没有投资者关系部门，「几乎所有 CEO 都会见我，因为他们觉得不会再见到我」。
3. 他的方法至今可用：**只问两个问题**——「你是怎么走到今天的？」以及「你怎么看竞争和你所处的行业？」他当场解释了为什么这两个问题足够。
4. 落点是可复制性：不是靠人脉，而是靠「准备好问题 + 敢于直接上门」。

## 关键引述

> **“To grant an hour to everybody of the 40,000 here — we'll have an interesting time the rest of my life.”**
>
> 「要给这里 4 万人每人都给一小时——我余生会很有意思。」

> **“Almost every CEO would see me because they figured they'd never see me again.”**
>
> 「几乎所有 CEO 都会见我，因为他们觉得以后再也不会见到我了。」

**Alitia Burek**（00:40:37）：你好，我叫 Alitia Burek，来自波兰，目前住在芝加哥。这个问题是替我认识的一位很励志的先生 Wid Ahmed 问的，他今天就在现场。Buffett 先生，将近 74 年前，1951 年 1 月一个寒冷的周六，你坐了 8 小时火车从纽约到华盛顿。你此行的全部依据只是一个希望：也许有人能再多教你一些保险业的事。到了 GEICO 办公室，门是锁着的。你一直坚持，直到一位清洁工把你带进去。你把与 Lorimer Davidson 的那次会面视为保险浮存金的起点——那是 Berkshire 成功的火箭燃料。2011 年，我 15 岁，带着同样的决心写信给你，请求见面。你很友善地回了信，说你只剩 3000 天，还有更紧迫的优先事项。那么，Buffett 先生，距你回信已经过去 5000 天了。所以，受你 1951 年的坚持和 B 夫人的韧性的启发，我谦卑地再次提出请求：只要 David 当年给你的时间的四分之一——在你办公室一小时。你也许会问，为什么必须是您。你常讲一位从奥斯维辛幸存下来的波兰犹太人的故事，他曾对你说：「Warren，我交朋友很慢，因为我看一个人时会问自己：他会把我藏起来吗？」你说过，会把你藏起来的人数，是衡量一生是否过得好的最好检验。那么我相信，在这场会上你并没有 4 万名股东，你有的是 4 万名会把你藏起来的人。你是一生过得极其精彩的明证。但即便是你，也还没有完全见证这种转变性的力量……

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:28）：我会让你来写我的传记，我想那正是你这个问题的用意。

**Alitia Burek**（00:42:35）：所以，我恭敬地再问一次，Buffett 先生：在「时间老人」赢之前，你愿意给 Wid Ahmed 一小时，或者你能挤出的任何时间吗？非常感谢你的时间。

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:49）：谢谢。我要说的是：如果要给这里 4 万人都给一小时，我余生会很有意思。但我给你一个诀窍。我很年轻时就发现，那些公司当年都比较偏门，那时还没有投资者关系部门。几乎所有 CEO 都会见我，因为他们觉得以后再也不会见到我了，而且他们平时也接不到这种电话。我会问两个问题——顺便说，我得先向他们解释，这其实不是个坏主意：如果你要走进某人办公室，说想占用他 10 分钟，就带个沙漏，放在对方桌上翻过来，让它走 10 分钟，然后说你会准时离开——除非他们请你留下。这就是条件。但一旦你得到这个机会：如果他们做煤炭生意，就说这行——恰好是我 70 年前感兴趣的行业之一——你只问一个问题：如果要被困在一座荒岛上，十年里只能持有他们竞争对手中的一只股票，会选哪一只，为什么？等他回答完，你再问同一个问题的反面：如果你要带着全部身家上那座岛、做空其中一只，你会选哪只，为什么？因为每个管理者都喜欢谈竞争对手。他们一谈起竞争对手，就像一群小学生。我大概就是靠确保他们觉得我不会待太久、同时让他们占着发言权去谈竞争对手，学到了不少行业知识。那些日子里我一直闭着嘴。现在他们把投资者关系做成了部门，说实话，所有有规模的公司都是。所以你有 3000 家公司之类的，每家几乎都有投资者关系部。他们的工作就是说：你今天能做的最好的事就是买我们的股票。这整套概念纯属愚蠢，但这是个大生意，而且越来越大，投资者关系部也越来越大，这就是我们现在的状况。所以，自己做一点功课，用你自己的方式。Berkshire Hathaway 有大量公开材料给你读，等你全部读完，你会比 Berkshire 大多数员工懂得还多。所以你不需要一次当面会谈。就算按一人一小时，光是这里 4 万人，加上 Becky 众多的听众和观众，也根本不成立。所以我佩服你的努力，但你只能接受这个结果，就当作你在这里得到的敬意。好，Becky。

**Alitia Burek**（00:40:37）：Hello, my name is Alitia Burek, and I’m from Poland, but I currently live in Chicago. This question is on behalf of an inspiring man that I know, Wid Ahmed, who’s here with us today. Mr. Buffett, nearly 74 years ago, on a cold Saturday in January 1951. You travelled 8 hours by train from New York to Washington. You went all the way on nothing but hope that someone might teach you more about the insurance industry. Arriving at GEICO’s office to find the doors locked. You persisted until a janitor led you inside. You credit that meeting with Lorimer Davidson for the insurance float. That was the rocket fuel behind Berkshire’s success. In 2011, when I was 15, I wrote to you with similar determination, asking to meet. You kindly wrote back, saying you had only 3000 days left and more pressing priorities. Well, Mr. Buffett, it’s now been 5000 days since you replied. And so, inspired by your persistence in 1951 and the tenacity of Mrs. B, I humbly renew my request for just a quarter of the time Davey gave you, a single hour in your office. You may wonder why it must be you have often shared an anecdote about a Polish Jew who survived Auschwitz, who said to you once, “Warren, I’m very slow to make friends because when I look at someone, I ask myself, would they hide me?” You said that the number of people who would hide you was the best test of a life well-lived. Well, I believe that at this meeting you don’t have 40,000 shareholders, but you have 40,000 people who would hide you. You are a testament to a life extraordinarily well lived. Yet even you have not fully witnessed this transformative power of a client…

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:28）：I’ll let you write my biography, but I think that’s the point of your question.

**Alitia Burek**（00:42:35）：So respectfully, I ask again, Mr. Buffett, before Father Time wins, will you please grant Wid Ahmed an hour of your time or any time you can dedicate? Thank you so much for your time.

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:49）：Thank you, and I will say this to grant an hour to everybody of the 40,000 here. We’ll have an interesting time the rest of my life. But I will give you one tip. I found that when I was very young, and these companies were offbeat, they didn’t have investor relations departments then. Almost every CEO would see me because they figured they’d never see me again, and they weren’t getting calls like that. I would ask two questions, as I would have to explain to them, it’s not a bad idea, incidentally. If you’re going to walk into somebody’s office and you say you want 10 minutes of their time, take an hourglass and stick it on the desk of the person you’re talking to and turn it up. So it’s going to go for 10 minutes and just say you’re going to get, you’re going to leave in 10 minutes. Unless they ask you to stay, and that’s the terms. But then once you have that… If they’re in the coal business, we’ll say, which happened to be one that I was interested in 70 years ago or so, they just ask one question that if they were to be stuck on a desert island and they had to own only one of their competitors’ stock during the 10 years, which one would it be and why? And then after they give you the answer, you ask the same thing if you would be short your net worth while you were on that island, which would it be, and why? Because every manager likes to talk about their competitors. They’re like a bunch of school kids when they get into talking about their competitors. I probably learned more about various industries by just making sure that they didn’t think I’d stay too long, and that in the meantime, they would have the floor and talk about their competitors. I kept my own mouth shut in those days. They’ve departmentalised investor relations. All companies of size, frankly, now. So you’ve got, you know, 3000 companies or whatever they have, and they all have departments, and each one of them has an Investor Relations department practically. And their job is to say, This is the best thing you can do today is buy our stock. We’ll let the whole concept is totally idiocy. But it’s a big business, and it gets bigger, and the Investor Relations department gets bigger, and it’s what we have now. But do a little of your own work, your own way. But Berkshire Hathaway has got plenty of material out there for you to read, and when you get through reading it all, you’ll know way more than most of the people who work at Berkshire. So you don’t need a personal interview. If we take an hour times even the 40,000 people we may have here, plus Becky’s many listeners and viewers, it just doesn’t work. So I admire your effort, but you’ll just have to settle for that, as the admiration that you get in this. OK. Becky.
