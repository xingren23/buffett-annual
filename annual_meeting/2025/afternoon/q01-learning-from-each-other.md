## 问题

三年前你回答「希望被记住是什么」时说「他是个老师」。那么 Greg，你从 Warren 身上学到的最深刻的一件事是什么？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特先开玩笑：「我现在最希望被人记住的是——活得够老。」
2. Abel 的回答与投资无关：希望被记住为「一个好父亲，同样也是一个教练」——他把这套角色用在家人、朋友和自己带的冰球、棒球队孩子身上。
3. 巴菲特真正的「教学内核」随后才出现：**他看资产负债表的时间比看利润表多**，而且先看 8 到 10 年的资产负债表，再看利润表。
4. 原因很直接：有些东西在资产负债表上更难隐藏或做手脚，「华尔街不太关注资产负债表」。
5. 这是本场最具体的一条方法论输出，也呼应了他此前多次讲过的「先看家底，再看盈利」。

## 关键引述

> **“I spend more time looking at balance sheets than I do income statements and Wall Street really doesn't pay much attention to balance sheets.”**
>
> 「我看资产负债表的时间比看利润表多，而华尔街其实不太关注资产负债表。」

> **“There are certain things that are harder to hide or play games with on the balance sheet than you can with the income statement.”**
>
> 「有些东西在资产负债表上比在利润表上更难隐藏、更难做手脚。」

**Robert**（00:02:33）：嗨，Warren。我叫 Robert，是来自加拿大多伦多的股东。Warren，三年前 Charlie Rose 问过你一个问题，大意是「你希望人们记住你什么」，你的回答是「他是个老师」。顺着这个思路，Greg，我很好奇你能不能讲一个你从 Warren 那里学到非常深刻的东西的故事。我相信你学到了很多，但如果有哪个故事会浮现在你脑海里，我们很想听。反过来，如果时间允许，Warren，你从 Greg 身上学到了什么？谢谢。

**Warren Buffett**（00:03:14）：好，我马上把话筒交给 Greg。不过现在我最希望被人记住的是——活得够老。

**Greg Abel**（00:03:25）：Warren 显然是一位了不起的老师，我每天都从中受益；正如我多年来谈过的，我很幸运。如果此刻要被记住为什么，我当然希望被记住为「一个好父亲」，同样也是「一个教练」——这既是针对家人朋友，也是针对我在冰球、棒球等队伍里带的那些孩子。我想我们有机会回到他那个年纪，所以显然那是我希望被记住的方式，希望那是很多很多年以后的事。但我确实喜欢把 Warren 真正看作一位老师，每一天都有机会继续学习。Warren 和我每周都有很强的对话，我们总是在谈 Berkshire 的机会、或者全球和美国正在发生的事，每一次都是真正的学习时刻。我或许可以回到我和 Warren 的第一次会面，因为那至今印在我脑子里。显然那是一次不可思议的机会：Warren 当时正在收购、或者说在考虑收购 Mid-America Energy Holdings。我有机会和我的合伙人一起，在一个周六上午过去，我们讨论了这门生意，Warren 面前摊着财务报表。我和任何人一样，本以为他会问几个关于业务表现之类的问题，但 Warren 立刻锁定在资产负债表上，以及我们有一些衍生品合约——「大规模杀伤性武器」——这件事上。在公用事业业务里，我们确实有，因为要用它们去匹配某些头寸，而它们从来不会完美匹配，但在受监管的业务里你必须有。我记得 Warren 立刻就抓住了这一点，问它的构成、底层风险是什么，想彻底弄清楚。那个头寸并不大，但那确实是他收购 Mid-American 时担心的风险之一，而且考虑到安然（Enron）和之前发生的一切，这是个非常切题的问题。后续是这样的：后来美国出现了一场电力和天然气的能源危机，很多人赚了大钱——那大概是 18 个月之后。而 Warren 几年后问我的跟进问题——我知道那更多是检查、是考察——是：「那么在这场能源危机里我们赚了多少钱？我们赚了很多吗？我们有投机性的头寸吗？我们在赚钱吗？」答案是：我们今天赚的并不比六个月前更多，因为那些衍生品真正是为了支持我们的业务，而不是投机。所以，就是那种对「我在买什么、围绕它的风险是什么」的专注，至今让我印象深刻。Warren？

**Warren Buffett**（00:07:25）：是的，这件事我们在这儿其实从没谈过。我看资产负债表的时间比看利润表多，而华尔街其实不太关注资产负债表。我喜欢先看 8 到 10 年的资产负债表，然后才看利润表。有些东西在资产负债表上比在利润表上更难隐藏、更难做手脚。当然，两者都不能给你任何问题的完整答案，但你仍然要理解这些数字在说什么、没说什么、不能说什么，以及管理层希望它们说什么、而审计师不希望它们说什么。这里面可学的东西很多，而在我看来，从资产负债表里学到的东西比大多数人以为的要多。我不担心因为这一点被人记住——人们不会记得。比如他们对 Katharine Graham 记得就远远不够，而她以多种方式塑造了美国，影响方方面面。历史实在太迷人了。而 Charlie 大概是你能想象的最善于学习的人。Charlie 从不满足于任何主题的表面，他真的想弄懂它，他总跟我说：在你能够比对手更好地陈述反对你的论点之前，不要对任何事采取立场——你应该能把他们的理由讲得比他们自己还好。他是位了不起的老师，我提到的其他几位也是。当然，我父亲也是一位不可思议的老师。所以，你要善用那些会让你成为更好的人，其余的，大体上就忘掉吧。好，我们进入第 12 个。Becky，下一个。

## 英文原文（实录摘录）

**Robert**（00:02:33）：Hi, Warren. My name is Robert, and I’m a shareholder from Toronto, Canada. So, Warren, three years ago, Charlie Rose asked your question to the effect of what you wanted to be remembered for. And your reply was, “He was a teacher.” So in that spirit, Greg, I’m curious to hear maybe a story of where you learned something very, you know, profound from Warren. I’m sure you learned many things, but if there is a story that kind of comes up in your mind, we’d love to hear it, and vice versa, if we have the time. Warren, what’s something that you may be learned from Greg? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett**（00:03:14）：Yeah, I will turn this over to Greg in just one second, but now the main thing I’d like to be known for is old age.

**Greg Abel**（00:03:25）：Well, Warren is obviously a remarkable teacher and I benefit from that every day and as I’ve already touched on for many years, I’m fortunate that if I had to be remembered as something right now, obviously, I want to be remembered as a great father, but equally, a coach and that goes to family, friends and just being involved with the kids I coach in hockey or baseball or whatever it may be. I think we’ve got a great opportunity to get back to him at a very young age, so obviously, those would be how I’d want to be remembered, and hopefully that will be many, many years from now, but I love thinking of Warren truly as a teacher and he and every day get the opportunity to continue to learn. Warren and I this dialogue is strong every week and we’re always talking about opportunities in Berkshire or things that are going on globally or in the US and each one is truly a learning moment. I’ll maybe go back to the very first meeting with Warren because it still stands out in my mind, obviously, it was an incredible opportunity. Warren was buying or acquiring Mid-America Energy Holdings company at that time, or thinking about it, I had the opportunity with my partners to go over there on a Saturday morning, and we discussed the business, and Warren had the financial statements in front of them. Like anybody, I was sort of expecting a few questions on how the business was performing or a variety of things, but Warren locked in immediately to what was on the balance sheet and the fact that we had some derivative contracts, the weapons of mass destruction. And associated with the utility business, we do have them because they use them to match certain positions, and they never match perfectly. But you do have them, and we’re required in the regulated business. I just remember Warren going to it immediately and asking about the composition of it and what the underlying risk was, and wanting to thoroughly understand. It wasn’t that big of a position, but it was absolutely one of the risks he was concerned about as he was acquiring Mid-American and obviously, in light of Enron and everything that had gone on, it was a very pertinent question. The follow-up to that was then there was an energy crisis in the US around electricity and natural gas, and a variety of folks who were making significant sums of money. This is a year, 18 months later. And Warren’s follow-up question, a couple of years later, to me was, and I knew it was more just checking or testing, “So how much money are we making during this energy crisis? Are we making a lot? Do we have the speculative positions in place and are we making?” And the answer was we’re really not making any more today than we would have been six months ago, because all those derivatives were truly to support our business and weren’t speculative. So, just that focus on understanding the business of what he was acquiring, understanding the risks around it, still stands out in my mind. Warren?

**Warren Buffett**（00:07:25）：Yeah, that’s one thing we’ve really never talked about here, but I spend more time looking at balance sheets than I do income statements and Wall Street really doesn’t pay much attention to balance sheets, but I like to look at balance sheets over an 8 or 10 year period before I even look at the income account. There are certain things that are harder to hide or play games with on the balance sheet than you can with the income statement. I mean, neither one gives you the total answer on anything, but you still want to understand what the figures are saying and what they don’t say and what they can’t say and what the management will like them to say, that the auditors wouldn’t like them to say. And I mean, there’s just a lot to be learned, and you do learn more from balance sheets in my view than most people give them credit for. I’m not worried about being remembered for that, people don’t remember. Well, they don’t remember enough about Katharine Graham, for example, in terms of this story that shaped America in many ways. Certainly, had just all kinds of impact, and I think I mean history is so fascinating, and Charlie was probably the best person you could imagine in what he learned. Charlie was never satisfied with just superficial things about any subject. He really wanted to understand it, and he always would tell me not to take a position on anything until you can describe the arguments against it better than the person who is arguing with you. That you should be able to argue their case better than they can. He was a remarkable teacher and but so and so with those other fellows I mentioned. And, of course, my dad was an incredible teacher. So you make the most of the people you meet who are going to make you a better person, and probably forget about the rest to quite an extent. OK, let’s move on to number 12. Becky, here. Next.
