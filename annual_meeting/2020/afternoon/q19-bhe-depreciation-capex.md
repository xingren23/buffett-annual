## 问题

股东注意到伯克希尔 2019 年把 46% 的资本支出投向伯克希尔·哈撒韦能源。他请格雷格用大概数字讲讲资本支出、经济折旧与 GAAP 折旧的差异，并说明作为股东应在多长的时间框架内确认这些大额投资的股本回报。

## 回答要点

1. 2019 年伯克希尔 46% 的资本支出投向伯克希尔·哈撒韦能源；阿贝尔将资本计划分为维护现有资产（10—20—30 年）与新增风电、输电、太阳能的增量增长资本两类。
2. 受监管业务占比超过 85%（83%），投入资本仍能赚取回报；折旧绰绰有余，超过折旧的部分才视为增长性资本支出。
3. 目前约 400 亿美元项目在筹备，将在未来九到十年推进，约一半维护资产、略多于一半为增量，另有约 300 亿美元接近成为真实机会。
4. 2020 年完成的输电项目始于 2008 年收购 PacifiCorp，耗时 12 年投运，期间先就投入资本赚钱、投产后就全额赚钱。
5. 巴菲特称能源不是暴富而是「保持富有」之道，回报像样但非超高，需政府授权并保护消费者与出资人，已运转 20 年且跑道很长。

**Becky Quick**（01:10:43）：格雷格，我问你一个资本配置的问题。这条来自 Matt Libel：「伯克希尔在 2019 年把 46% 的资本支出投给了伯克希尔·哈撒韦能源。能否用大概的数字讲讲，你怎么看资本开支、经济折旧和 GAAP 折旧之间的区别？还要说明，我们作为股东，该用多长的时间框架来确认这些大额投资带来的契约性股本回报。」

**Greg Abel**（01:11:16）：看伯克希尔·哈撒韦能源的资本计划，我们其实分两类来看。第一，未来 10 年、20 年、30 年，维持现有资产到底需要多少钱？这不是增量，就是维护资产，对应的是折旧。我们一直想在所有业务里搞清楚：维持资产所需的钱，是高于折旧、等于折旧，还是低于折旧？很高兴地说，就我们现有资产和维护方式而言，折旧一般足够充裕，只要把它再投回资产维护就行。

有意思的是，我们能源业务的大头是受监管的，占比超过 85%，准确说是 83%。投回去的这笔钱，我们照样能赚到回报。这跟传统模式不太一样：你投钱进去，是为了维持现有的盈利流。所以差别没有那么大，但这笔资本我们确实能赚。

我们真正花大量时间的，是沃伦和我反复掂量的那些大把机会，那才是新机会真正需要的增量资本。比如新建风电、新建输电线路，服务风电或其他太阳能。这些对业务都是增量的，带来业务增长。它需要资本，但也确实推动能源业务长大。所以基本就是两个桶。我们会用一个略低于折旧的数，确信业务能在那个水平上维持；超过这个数的，我们才真正看作增量资本开支，也就是增长性开支。

**Warren Buffett**（01:13:26）：对。我们手上大概有，有多少来着，400 亿左右的项目在做？

**Greg Abel**（01:13:36）：对。就像沃伦说的，我们有大约 400 亿的项目在做，摊在未来大概九年、十年的周期里。其中约一半视为维护资产，略多于一半是真正的增量，而且都是已知项目，我们会推进。我还高兴地告诉大家，另外还有约 300 亿，离变成真实机会已经不远了。

但正如沃伦所说，这要花大量时间，也是大量工作。举个例子，我们 2020 年要完工的输电项目，早在 2008 年收购 PacifiCorp 时就启动了。我记得当时做那个输电规划，心想六到八年后就能投运，结果过了十二年。这段时间里，我们投进去的资本一直在赚回报，等它投运，就按全额赚。我们对这些机会很满意，不过这么说吧，我们撒了很多种子。

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:54）：对。这些也不是什么超高回报的项目，但长期看回报不错。配置这些资本，我们几乎处在独一无二的位置。政府实体当然也能做，但就民营企业而言，它们要花很长时间，赚的是不错的回报。我一直这么说能源业务：它不是让你暴富的路子，却是让你守住财富的路子。

我们会投很多钱，拿到不错的回报，但不是超级回报。这种事本来就不该赚超级回报，你拿到的是政府授权你做某些事的权利。这种授权既要保护消费者，也该保护出钱的人。这套玩法已经运转了 20 年，前面还有很长一段跑道。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（01:10:43）：Greg, let me ask you one of these capital allocation questions. This one comes from Matt Libel, and he says, "Berkshire directed 46 percent of capital expenditure in 2019 to Berkshire Hathaway Energy. Can you walk us through, with round numbers, how you think differences in capex spending versus economic depreciation versus GAAP depreciation, and help explain the timeframe over which we should recognize the contract — the contractive return on equity from these large investments, as we are shareholders in — as we, as shareholders, are making in Berkshire Hathaway Energy."

**Greg Abel**（01:11:16）：So, when we look at Berkshire Hathaway Energy and their capital programs, we try to really look at — look at it as it was highlighted, really, in a couple different packages. One, what does it actually require to maintain the existing assets for the next 10-20-30 years? I.e., it's not incremental, it's effectively maintaining the asset, the reflection of depreciation. And our goal is always to clearly understand, across our businesses, do we have businesses that require more than our depreciation, or equal, or less. And, happy to say, with the assets we have in place and how we've maintained the energy assets, we generally look at our depreciation as being more than adequate, if we deploy it back into capital to maintain the asset. Now, the unique thing in the lion's share of our energy businesses that are regulated — and that exceeds 85 percent of them — 83 percent of them — we still earn on that capital we deploy back into that business. So, it's not a traditional model where you're putting it in, but you're effectively putting it in to maintain your existing earnings stream. So, it's not drastically different, but we do earn on that capital. But what we do spend a lot of time, and that's what, when Warren and I think about the substantial amounts of opportunities, that's incremental capital that is truly needed within new opportunities. So, it's built — it's to build incremental wind, incremental transmission, that services the wind or other types of renewable solar. That's all incremental to the business and drives incremental growth in the business. It does require capital, but it does drive growth within the energy business. So, there's really the two buckets. I think we would use a number a little bit lower than the depreciation we're comfortable the business can be maintained at that level. And as we deploy amounts above that, we really do view that as, quote, incremental or growth capex.

**Warren Buffett**（01:13:26）：Yeah, we have what — what — 40 billion or something of — what do we have in, sort of, kind of, in the works?

**Greg Abel**（01:13:36）：Oh, yeah. So, we have basically, as Warren's highlighting, 40 billion in the works of capital, that's over the next, effectively, nine years, 10-year period. A little — approximately half of that, we would view as maintaining our assets. More — a little more than half of it is truly incremental, but — and that are known — those are known projects we're going to move forward with. And I would be happy to report, we probably have another 30 million — 30 billion — that aren't far off of becoming real opportunities in that business. Now, as Warren said, that takes a lot of time. It's a lot of work. The transmission projects, for example, we're finishing in 2020 were initiated in 2008 when we bought PacifiCorp. I remember working on that transmission plan, putting it together, thinking six to eight years from now, we'll have them in operation. Twelve years later, and over that period of time, we earned on that capital we have invested, and then when it comes into service, we earn on the whole amount. So, we're very pleased with the opportunity, but it — we plant a lot of seeds, put it that way.

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:54）：Yeah. And these are not — it's not like they're super-high return thing. But they're decent returns over time. And we're almost uniquely situated to deploy the capital, as opposed — I mean, you could have government entities do it too —but in terms of a private enterprise — and they take a long time. They earn decent returns. I've always said about the energy business, it's not a way to get real rich, but it's a way to stay real rich, and — We will deploy a lot — a lot of money at decent returns — not super returns, you shouldn't earn super returns on that sort of thing, I mean — it does — you are getting rights to do certain things that governmental authorities are authorizing. And they should protect consumers and — but they also should protect people that put up the capital. And — You know, it's worked now for 20 years, and it's got a long runway ahead.
