## 问题

马里兰大学金融学临床教授 David Kass 提问：伯克希尔投资了许多有股票回购计划的公司，近期出现了针对回购的反对浪潮，巴菲特对此有何看法？

## 回答要点

1. 回购只是向股东分配现金的方式，与分红的区别在于只给想要钱的人
2. 巴菲特自 2006 年起赠股，慈善机构需及时用钱，而他仍保留更多美元投入但权益比例下降
3. 公司应保留明智增长所需资金，并在股价低于内在价值时回购，这是对继续持股股东有利的做法
4. 回购应价格敏感、需求敏感；说要花 50 亿或 100 亿回购却不知换来什么，是愚蠢的
5. 摩根大通 CEO 杰米·戴蒙和伯克希尔都说过：当对持续持股的股东有利时才回购
6. 阿贝尔称有些公司财务运作过于取巧，用尽资产负债表回购、不留缓冲，导致反对浪潮；回购会政治化，但伯克希尔按对股东合理的原则行事

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:44:37）：重点是诀窍。这个问题来自 David Kass，马里兰大学金融学临床教授。他问：「伯克希尔投资过很多有股票回购计划的公司。最近出现了反对回购的浪潮。你对此怎么看？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:44:55）：嗯，现在反对回购在政治上很正确，你知道，他们还要把它写进贷款计划里。关于回购，有很多疯狂的说法。回购非常简单。我是说，它就是向股东分配现金的一种方式。假设你我还有格雷格，我们三个，决定买一家汽车经销商，或者一个麦当劳加盟店什么的。我们各出 100 万美元，或者说不管多少。我们相处融洽，生意也做大了，诸如此类。其中一个真想把属于自己那份盈利花掉，另外两个想把钱留在生意里继续增长。如果我们三个这么做，而且我们是唯一的股东，我们不会给所有人定一个 100% 的分红率。我们也不会冻结那个想退出的人。合理的做法是买下他的一部分，他每年想从盈利里拿走的那部分，买他一部分股份，另外两个人在公司的权益就上升了。第三个人因为留下的钱，权益也稍微多一点，但他也能从生意里拿出一些钱。无论哪种方式，你都是在把钱从生意里拿出来。一种叫分红，不管你要不要都发给大家。回购呢，是把钱给想要的人。

我从 2006 年起就在执行送股的政策。我会送出很多股票。但那些收到股票的，慈善机构，必须很快把钱花掉，基本上是按当期、大致如此。所以他们拿到价值 30 亿的股票，或别的数额。实际上，我在减少自己在伯克希尔的权益。但我仍然，伯克希尔留存资本仍多过我送出的，所以我投进去的美元更多，但我的占比下降了；而那些需要现金去做慈善的人，把股票变现。我不强迫我姐姐，或任何人，去拿一大笔她不想要的钱。她想把钱全部，再投回生意。而不想这么做的人，可以卖掉一部分股票。公司最终处在同样的位置。我们分配掉了一些增长不需要的资本。

那么公司该不该回购，取决于两件事。一是它应该留下智能增长所需资金，这没问题。二是，这一点从没人提，它应该以低于自身认为的价值回购。现在，这当中他们会犯错，但你在很多生意里都会犯错。不过总体上，这应该是指导原则。据我所知，摩根大通的杰米·戴蒙说过一次，我们也说过好几次，你知道，我们留存，我们会在回购对继续持股的股东有利时回购。但你读到那些回购问题，说我们要花 50 亿或 100 亿回购。那好比说，我今年要花 50 亿去买一家公司，却不知道这笔钱能买到什么。显然应该对价格敏感。显然应该对需求敏感。但当条件合适时，回购也应该是理所当然的，不该有丝毫污点，就跟分红一样。现在那些开始大声疾呼、说公司回购股票有多可怕的人，我要说，他们以前分红也同样可怕，（笑），那样他们现在会有更多钱，但他们在当时做的是明智的事，我希望他们今后继续做明智的事。格雷格？

**Greg Abel**（01:49:13）：不，我知道你过去唯一评论过的，沃伦，是，我想我们看到的一点是，显然我们支持回购，但有些公司过去，大概他们的财务操作有点，

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:28）：极端。

**Greg Abel**（01:49:29）：——极端、过于花哨，实际上是把资产负债表的每一分钱都拿来回购，在那种时候，他们真的一点缓冲都没给自己的业务留下，以应对任何类型的事件或颠簸。我——你知道，我们会看到那种情况，我认为那是它们非常不幸的结局，因此才招来一些反弹。但仍然有很多公司，如你所说，很多做得很对。

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:53）：对。不，如果他们回购是因为时髦，因为他们真的喜欢这样一个想法，去做一件提升剩余股份价值的事，这本身没错，但如果他们做得非常，顺便说，我见过一些回购计划，确实是愚蠢的。但我不认为它不道德。我只是觉得它蠢，（笑），基本上。而且，另一方面，我们青睐这样的公司：满足增长所需的全部要求，并且如格雷格所说，保持稳健的资产负债表等等，为意想不到的事留出安全边际；如果它们发现自己的股价低于，这门生意的内在价值，我认为它们若不回购自己的股票，就是犯了大错，而且，这会成为一个政治皮球，就像我说的，当在这个国家做某件事变得政治上正确时，如果你是政客，最好的做法就是上车。但伯克希尔会做它认为对股东合理的事。我们也喜欢投资那些这样想的公司。显然，不是所有公司都这样。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（01:44:37）：Emphasis on trick. This question comes from David Kass. He is a clinical professor of finance at the University of Maryland. And he says, "Berkshire has invested in many companies with stock buyback programs. Recently there's been a backlash against buybacks. What are your views on this subject?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:44:55）：Well, it's very politically correct to be against buybacks now, you know, and they're going to incorporate it in the loan program. The — there's a lot of crazy things said on buybacks. Buybacks are so simple. I mean, it's a way of distributing cash to shareholders. And let's just say that you and I and Greg — the three of us — decide to buy an auto dealership, or a McDonald's franchise, or something. We each put a million dollars in, you know, or whatever the number may be. And we get along with each other and the business grows and all of that. And one of us really wants to spend our share of the earnings. And the other two want to leave the money in the business to grow. Now if the three of us did that, and we’re the only shareholders, we would not establish a 100 percent dividend payout for everybody. And we wouldn't freeze the one that wanted to get out, either. The logical thing to do is to buy a portion — whatever that person wants to spend annually from the earnings — buy a portion of their stock, and the other two find their interest in the company goes up. And the third person still has a little more of an interest by what they leave in, but they also can take some money out of the business. You're taking money out of the business in either case. And one you call dividends and you send it to everybody whether they want it or not. And with buybacks, you give it to the ones who want the money. And I have been following a policy of giving away stock, now, since 2006. And I'll give away a lot of stock. But the people — the philanthropies — that that receive it — the gifts — have to spend the money very promptly, within, you know — on a current basis, more or less. So, they are getting $3 billion worth of stock, or whatever it may be. And I'm, in effect, reducing my interest in Berkshire. But I'm still — Berkshire is still retaining more capital than I'm giving away, so I have more dollars invested, but my interest goes down, and the people that need the cash to carry out the philanthropic efforts, they cash out the stock. And I don't force — I don't force my sister, or whoever it may be, to take a bunch of money she doesn't want. She wants it reinvested — all of it — reinvested in the business. And people that don't want to, can sell some of their stock. And the company ends up in the same position. We've distributed some of the capital that we don't need for growth. Now, whether the company should buy it depends on a couple of things. One is they ought to retain the money they need for intelligent growth prospects, that's fine. And secondly — and this is a point that's never mentioned — they should be buying it back below what they think it's worth. Now, they'll make mistakes in that, but you make mistakes in a lot of businesses. But over — that should be the guiding principle. And to my knowledge — JPMorgan — (CEO) Jamie Dimon said it once, and we've said it various times, you know — we retained — we will repurchase shares when it's to the advantage of the continuing shareholder to have us do so. But you read about all these buyback problems that we're going to spend 5 billion buying it back, or 10 billion. Well, that's like saying, I'm going to go out and buy some business this year for 5 billion without knowing what you're going to get for the money. It should be price sensitive, obviously. It should be needs sensitive, obviously. But when the conditions are right, it should also be obvious to repurchase shares, and there shouldn't be the slightest taint to it any more than there is to dividends. And people that have now, sort of, taken up the cries about how terrible it was that companies bought back stock — I will say it was terrible for them to pay dividends, too — (laughs) — and they'd have more money now, but they were doing what was intelligent at the time, and I hope they continue to do what's intelligent as they go forth. Greg?

**Greg Abel**（01:49:13）：No, I — the only thing I know you've commented on in the past, Warren, is that — I think the one thing we are seeing, and obviously, we're supportive of buybacks — but there are companies that used — probably their financial engineering was just a little —

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:28）：Extreme.

**Greg Abel**（01:49:29）：— extreme and too cute, that effectively you're using every ounce of your balance sheet to buy back stock at a time where you're really creating no cushion for your business for any type of event or bump in the road, and I — you know, we're going to see that, and I think that's a very unfortunate outcome of them, and hence you get some of the backlash. But there's still companies, as you highlighted, many that do it right.

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:53）：Yeah. No, if they're buying it back because it's fashionable, because they really do like the idea that —there's nothing wrong with doing — taking an action that increases the value of the remaining shares, but if they're doing it very — and incidentally, I've been witness to some programs, where it really is stupid. But I don't think it's immoral. I just think it's stupid — (laughs) — you know, basically. And — on the other hand, I — we favor companies that take care of all their requirements for growth and, as Greg says, maintain sound balance sheets and all of that, leave a margin of error for things that you can get surprised with, and if they find their stock selling below the — what the business is intrinsically worth, I think that they're making a big mistake if they don't buy in their stock, and — It's going to be a political football, and like I say, that when it becomes politically correct to do something in this country — if you're a politician, the best thing to do is get on board. But Berkshire is going to do what it thinks makes sense for shareholders. And we — and we like investing in companies that think that way, too. And not all companies, obviously, do.
