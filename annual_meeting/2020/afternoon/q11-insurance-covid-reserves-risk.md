## 问题

来自佐治亚州亚特兰大的股东斯蒂芬·泰德问：其他保险公司已因预计新冠赔付而提高准备金并报告损失，但伯克希尔今早发布的 10-Q 似乎没有体现多少这类未来预计损失。这是为什么？伯克希尔承保哪些风险使其受疫情影响较小，又有哪些承保业务可能受影响？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特称疫情已引发并将继续引发大量诉讼，仅辩护成本就是巨大开支；车险作为保费规模第一大业务相对更可界定。
2. 标准商业综合险条款通常只在财产发生物质损坏时才赔付营业中断，许多保单明确不涵盖疫情导致的营业中断。
3. 伯克希尔在商业综合险上规模不大，但会有索赔与诉讼成本；比例上不像大量承保营业中断险的公司那样严重。
4. 巴菲特举例：伯克希尔曾在法国因邻处火灾蔓延投保营业中断险，但客户罢工导致供货中断通常不在保障内。
5. 某些保险公司将因签发的保单付出相对其规模而言巨额赔付；伯克希尔历史上准备金提取偏保守、至少充足，且不向经理下达预期数字。

**Becky Quick**（00:41:07）：下一个问题来自佐治亚州亚特兰大的股东 Stephen Tedder：「能否帮我们理解新冠疫情对我们保险业务的影响？其他保险公司报告说，因为预计未来会因冠状病毒支付索赔而增加了准备金，出现了亏损。「然而在今早发布的伯克希尔 10-Q 里，我们似乎没有报告多少这类未来预期损失。这是为什么？伯克希尔承保的是哪类风险，让我们不受疫情影响？或者反过来，我们承保的哪些业务可能会受影响？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:48）：已经发生的事引发的诉讼量会很大，更别提还没发生的事。光是应诉成本就是一笔巨额开支，取决于量有多大。

在车险领域，按保费规模，这是我们的第一大领域，领先幅度不小，情况更可界定。但谁知道会引出什么诉讼？

在所谓的「商业综合险」（commercial multiple peril）里，涉及财产损失，有些人选择购买营业中断险。许多保单的合同条款写得很清楚：即使你加保了这一项，商业综合险下也不会赔营业中断。但另一些保单会。我知道，我想我知道有一家公司，细节不清楚，承保了相当数量，覆盖，或者至少可以有力地主张覆盖疫情引起的营业中断。那么它们的位置就和标准条款非常不同：标准条款说，只有当财产受到物理损坏时才赔营业中断。

你可以买到各种不同保单。我们在商业综合险上业务量不大，所以这不像我们的车险之类的东西。但我们一定会有索赔，也一定会有诉讼成本。只是比例上，跟那些大量承保营业中断作为商业综合险一部分的公司不一样。

不过，买了营业中断险并不是自动就能赔。比如，我觉得要是通用汽车罢工，他们确实罢过工，那你得有覆盖罢工的营业中断险才算。事实上，我们大概写过美国唯一一份（笑）讲营业中断险的年报：我们在法国有一处产业，邻近一处小得多的房产发生了火灾，火蔓延到我们的工厂，造成大量物理损坏，我们买了与之挂钩的营业中断险。可如果我们把汽车零部件卖给的一家公司罢工了，我们的业务被打断，那份保险并不覆盖，除非你专门买了。

所以，有些索赔因当前局势而完全站得住脚；还有大量索赔会进入诉讼，但站不住脚。毫无疑问有些保险公司，我特别知道一家，相对其规模，会因为所签保单赔很多钱。

我认为我们的准备金，我们的历史也表明，我们通常，提得偏保守，至少充足，这当然是我们的本意。我们不对任何保险业务的经理说我们希望他们报出什么数字，不做这种事。他们自己评估损失，其中为「社会通胀」留了一块，为各种事情留了一块。总体而言，伯克希尔的准备金提得一直相当准，我没有理由认为现在不是这样。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（00:41:07）：This next question comes from Stephen Tedder. He's a shareholder in Atlanta, Georgia, and he says, "Would you please help us understand the effects of COVID-19 on our insurance businesses. Other insurance companies have reported losses from boosting reserves for future insurance claims that they expect to be paying as a result of coronavirus. "Yet in Berkshire's 10-Q released this morning, we do not appear to have reported much of these future expected losses. Can you tell us why this is the case? What kind of risks Berkshire is underwriting that allows us not to be affected by the pandemic? Or conversely, what we are writing that might be?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:48）：Well, the amount of litigation that is going to be generated out of what's already happened, let alone what may happen, is going to be huge. You know, just the cost of defending litigation is a huge, enormous expense, depending on how much there. Now, in the auto insurance field, which is our number one field in terms of premium volume, by some margin, that's more definable. But who knows what comes out of it in terms of litigation? But in what they call "commercial multiple peril," which involves property losses, and where some people elect to buy business interruption coverage, many policies, quite clearly in the contract language, would not have a claim for business interruption under a commercial multiple peril policy, where you've elected that. But other policies do. I know of — I think I know of one company — I don't know the details — that's written a fair amount where they cover — or they — certainly there's a good argument perhaps that they cover — business interruption that might arise from a pandemic. Well, they're in a very different position than the standard language, which says that you recover for business interruption only if there's — involves physical damage to the property. And you can buy all kinds of different policies. We are not big in the commercial multiple peril business. So, I mean, this is not like our auto business or anything of the sort. But we will have — we will have claims. We'll have litigation costs. But proportionally, it's not the same with us as with some other companies, which have been much heavier in writing business interruption as part of a commercial multiple peril. But you don't automatically get coverage if you have business interruption. I mean, for example, I think it would be unusual if, say, General Motors had a strike, which they did, and that they have business interruption that covers a strike. Now, we actually wrote about — probably the only annual report in the United States — we wrote about business interruption insurance — (laughs) — because we had it over in France, when one of our properties was adjacent to a property that's — much smaller property — that had a fire, and then it spread to our plant, and it caused a lot of physical damage. And we had bought — we have business interruption that ties in with that. But, if we had some company we were selling auto parts to, and they had a strike, our business would be interrupted, but it's not covered by that. I mean, that is not part of the coverage, unless you specifically really buy it. So, there's some claims that are going to be very valid related to this, the present situation. There'll be an awful lot that there'll be litigation on that won't be valid. There's no question that some insurance companies — I know one in particular — that will pay a lot of money, relative to their size, in terms of policies that they've written. And I think we have reserved — and our history shows we generally have reserved — on the conservative side, adequately, at least. And that's certainly our intent. And we tell no managers of any of our insurance operations what numbers we expect from them, or do any of that. They evaluate their losses. And they build in something for social inflation. They build in things for — all kinds of things. And generally speaking, Berkshire has been pretty accurate in its reserving, and I have no reason to think that we're otherwise than that currently.
