## 问题

罗伯特·托马斯提问：为什么你一方面建议听众现在买入股票，另一方面自己却因持有巨额现金而不愿现在买入？这是否自相矛盾？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特称现金仓位从最坏情形看并不算大，伯克希尔为会自行催生连锁反应的多重问题做准备，而非单一问题。
2. 他以2008—2009年危机为例：房地美、房利美9月初被接管，随后货币市场基金跌破面值，问题接连触发。
3. 巴菲特并未推荐今天、明天或下周买入，强调是否买入取决于个人财务状况与心理承受能力。
4. 买入应像持有一座农场那样做好长期持有、从不看报价的准备，并接受股价下跌50%甚至更多。
5. 他回顾伯克希尔历史上股价曾三次下跌50%，若用借款持有可能被清仓，而当时公司本身并无问题。
6. 巴菲特称不知道今天是否是买入的好日子，但相信20—30年长周期会奏效，两年内则无法预料。

## 关键引述

> **“you shouldn't buy stocks unless you expect, in my view, you expect to hold them for a very extended period”**
>
> 「依我看，除非你打算持有它们非常长的时间，否则你不该买股票。」

> **“You've got to be prepared, when you buy a stock, to have it go down 50 percent — or more — and be comfortable with it”**
>
> 「买股票时必须做好准备，接受它下跌50%甚至更多，并对此感到坦然。」

> **“I don't know whether today is a great day to buy stocks.”**
>
> 「我不知道今天是不是买入股票的好日子。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（00:05:05）：下一个问题来自加拿大多伦多的 Robert Tomas。他说：「沃伦，你为什么一边建议听众现在买入，一边自己又不愿买——你手里庞大的现金头寸就是证明？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:05:19）：第一，正如我说过的，从我考虑最坏情形的角度看，这笔现金并不算大。有些事我认为几乎不可能，概率很低，我希望它们不要发生，但不等于它们不会发生。比如保险业务，我们可能遇上全国有史以来最大的飓风，但这并不排除一个月后再来最大的地震。所以我们不是只为某一个问题做准备，而是为那些有时会自己催生后续问题的一连串麻烦做准备。

2008、2009 年，第一天你看不到所有问题，真正引爆它的是 9 月初房地美、房利美这类政府支持机构被接管，接着货币基金跌破面值。一件事绊倒另一件事。我们脑子里装的最坏情形，大概比大多数人想的可怕得多。所以我不觉得这笔现金庞大。

我也不是在建议大家今天、明天、下周或下个月买股票。这完全取决于你自己的情况。但依我看，除非你预期持有很长时间，除非你在财务上和心理上都准备好了像持有一座农场那样持有它，从不看报价，也根本不必关注，否则不该买股票。

而且你抓不到底部，没人能替你抓到底部。买股票时你要做好准备：它可能跌 50% 甚至更多，而只要你对这份持有本身有把握，就能坦然接受。

我大概在一两年前，就是最近上一份年报里，指出过：伯克希尔历史上股价三次跌过 50%，三次不同的时期。如果你用借来的钱持有，早就被清空了。那三次，伯克希尔本身没有任何问题。

但如果你盯着股价，一看到它这样那样就觉得自己必须行动，或者听别人一句话就动，「别的都在涨，你怎么还拿着这个？」，那真的不行。你必须处在正确的心理状态。

坦白说，有些人没那么沉得住气，有些人比别人更容易害怕。这就像病毒，它对一些人的凶狠程度远超另一些人。恐惧这东西，在钱的问题上我从来没真正感受过，我想查理也没有。有些人心理上扛得住。如果你扛不住，那真的不该持有股票，因为你总会在错误的时点买卖。

也别指望别人告诉你怎么做。你该做你自己懂的事。自己不懂，就会被下一个跟你说话的人影响。所以你得处在一个能持有的位置上。

我不知道今天是不是买股票的好日子。我知道 20 年、30 年它会奏效。我不知道两年内会不会奏效。你周一早上买的一只股票，或者整个市场，是赚是赔，我毫无头绪。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（00:05:05）：The next question comes from Robert Tomas, from Toronto, Canada, and he says, "Warren, why are you recommending listeners to buy now, yet you're not comfortable buying now, as evidenced by your huge cash position?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:05:19）：Well, A, as I've explained, the position isn't that huge when I look at worst-case possibilities. I would say that there are things that I think are quite impossible — improbable — and I hope they don't happen, but that doesn't mean they won't happen. I mean, for example, in our insurance business, we can have the world's — or the country's — number one hurricane that it's ever had, but that doesn't preclude the fact we could have the biggest earthquake a month later. So, we — we are not — we don't prepare ourselves for a single problem, we prepare ourselves for problems that sometimes create their own momentum. I mean, in 2008 and '09, you didn't see all the problems the first day when really — what really kicked it off was when the Freddie and Fannie, the GSEs, went into conservatorship in early September. And then when money market funds broke the buck, I mean — there are things that trip other things. And we take a very much — a worst-case scenario into mind that probably is a considerably worse case than most people do. So, I don't look at it as huge. And I'm not — I'm not recommending that people buy stocks today or tomorrow or next week or next month. I think it all depends on your circumstances. But you shouldn't buy stocks unless you expect, in my view, you expect to hold them for a very extended period and you are prepared financially and psychologically to hold them the same way you would hold a farm, and never look at a quote, and never pay — you don't need to pay attention to them. I mean, the main thing to do — and you're not going to pick the bottom and you're not going — nobody else can pick it for you, or anything of the sort. You've got to be prepared, when you buy a stock, to have it go down 50 percent — or more — and be comfortable with it, as long as you're comfortable with the holding. And I pointed out — I think a year, maybe two years ago in the annual report — almost just the one before this most recent one — I pointed out that there have been three times in Berkshire's history when the price of Berkshire stock went down 50 percent — three different times. Now, if you hold it on borrowed money, you know, you could have been cleaned out. There wasn't anything wrong with Berkshire when those three times occurred. But if you're going to — if you're going to look at the price of the stock, and think that you have to act because it's doing this or that, or somebody else tells you a line, I mean, "How can you stay with that," when something else is going up or anything. Really, you've got to be in the right psychological position. And frankly, some people are not really careful. Some people are more subject to fear than others. It's like the virus — it strikes some people with much greater ferocity than others. And fear is — fear is something I've really never felt financially, but — I don't think Charlie's felt it, either. Some people can handle it psychologically. If you can't handle it psychologically, then you really shouldn't own stocks, because you're going to buy and sell them at the wrong time. And you should not count on somebody else telling you this. You should you should do something you understand yourself. If you don't understand it yourself, you're going to be affected by the next person you talk to. And so, you should — you should be in a position to hold. And I don't know whether today is a great day to buy stocks. I know it will work out over 20 or 30 years. I don't know whether it will work out over two years at all. I have no idea whether you'll be ahead or behind on a stock you buy on Monday morning — or the market.
