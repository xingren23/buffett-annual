## 问题

作为伯克希尔 B 股的长期股东，股东想了解沃伦对家乡奥马哈的小型持仓——东方贸易公司与内布拉斯加家具城——的看法。这些小企业可能受到新冠居家隔离令的不利影响。它们是否申请了 PPP 薪资保护计划贷款或参与了相关计划？如果不符合资格或未参与，伯克希尔将如何支持它们以继续雇用员工？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特表示，据他所知，包括内布拉斯加家具城和东方贸易在内的旗下企业都没有申请政府资金，并认为这两家前景良好。
2. 伯克希尔过去六个月在报纸业务上的投入可能比全国任何人都多，接手了一笔本会出问题的贷款，并让报纸交由李氏企业（Lee）运营，以提升存活机会。
3. 巴菲特指出，有些企业注定消失，如纺织业、巴尔的摩百货商店和礼品券业务，「这就是资本主义」；未来 10 年、50 年伯克希尔也会有企业消失，但总体会成长。
4. 他因此再次建议普通人买指数基金，除伯克希尔外不愿把所有钱押在单一公司；阿贝尔称未发现任何企业申请 PPP 贷款，各规模企业进入危机时状况都很稳健，正准备重新出发。
5. 巴菲特称没人知道疫情会持续多久，多数人认为夏季会减弱但可能卷土重来，「病毒将决定我们的行为」，健康的未知数造成经济的未知数，未来六个月或一年不可过于自信。

**Becky Quick**（00:52:16）：这是一个相近的问题，来自 Seth Freiden：「作为伯克希尔 B 股的长期股东，我想知道沃伦对小持股的看法，特别是东方贸易公司（Oriental Trading）和内布拉斯加家具城——它们都在你的家乡奥马哈。」他猜这些较小的业务单元受到了 COVID 居家令的不利影响，所以想知道：东方贸易或其他小业务是否申请了 PPP（小企业管理局薪资保护计划）贷款，或参与了那些法案？如果不合格或没参与，伯克希尔将如何支持这些小企业、确保它们能继续雇用员工？

**Warren Buffett**（00:52:55）：据我所知，它们谁都没有去拿政府的钱。提到的这两家，我不喜欢谈具体公司，但我可以向你保证，在我看来，内布拉斯加家具城和东方贸易都有很好的未来。但我不想，显然不想把清单一家家念下去，因为有些我并不知道答案。

其实我们早就认定：报纸如果由李氏企业来经营，比我们自己经营更有可能活下去。正如我刚才说的，我们实际上投入了相当多的钱，过去六个月我们投给报业的钱，可能比全国几乎任何人都多，因为我们接手了一笔原本会在一年、大概一年半后出问题的贷款，让他们只需面对一家贷款方，而不是一群。他们经营报纸比我们做得更好。而这一直是我们的偏好：如果一门生意在我们手里看起来无法持续，而我们能找到认为会做得更好的人，我们就乐意让他们来经营。所以如果我们有问题的生意，我们更愿意找一个认为能经营得比我们更好、而且很可能确实更好的人。

但有些生意就是会消失。我们起步就是纺织生意；我们还创办过一家叫 Diversified Retailing 的公司，后来并入了伯克希尔，成为伯克希尔的一部分，它起步于巴尔的摩的一家百货商店。1966 年百货商店看起来还不错，但世界已经不再眷顾它们。我们曾经还有赠券生意，撑得比谁都久，但世界把赠券抛在了身后。某些生意就是会这样，这就是资本主义。未来十年、五十年，这也会发生在一些伯克希尔的业务上。

我们相信能找到更多会成长的业务，整体上伯克希尔会增长。但我们不认为，你拥有很多生意，每家都注定成功。这就是为什么我建议大家买指数基金。除了伯克希尔，我不愿意把我所有的钱押在任何一家公司上，虽然有几家我不介意接近这个程度。但这个世界上你会遇到意外：有些我们以为很好的生意结果没那么好，也有生意比我们想的好。让世界来判断我们长期的击球率吧。格雷格？

**Greg Abel**（00:56:25）：我只补充一句、再强调一遍：关于 PPP 贷款，我们不知道有哪家子公司拿了。如我所说，我们鼓励过它们，如果它们觉得有可能要对话——我们不知道有哪家在申请。

另外，回看进入危机时我们的业务，反应非常好。我们的大业务、中型业务，乃至更小的，在疫情中都处于非常稳健的状态，现在真的准备好重新出发了。它们在评估、在倾听，会面对不同的客户，会有不同的消费者行为——我们的员工怎么工作，很多人现在在家办公，这合理吗？我们经营的社区也都变了。但我们真的已经从「好吧，我们挺过危机了」走到了现在真的在规划重新出发。我认为我们的业务处在极其稳健的位置。

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:31）：我们不知道什么时候——

**Becky Quick**（00:57:33）：下一个问题——

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:42）：我只是想说，我们不知道这段时间会持续多久，没人知道。我们也不知道，大多数人认为，他们比我知道得多，病毒在夏季月份传播会在某种程度上减弱。我要说，但很多人认为它会在晚些时候卷土重来。如果公众因为某种重新开放、因为夏季的减弱而燃起希望，然后对病毒的第二波攻击作何反应，就像福奇博士说的，病毒将决定我们的行为。

我们做了很多聪明的事，也有很多非常聪明的人，但仍有未知数。健康方面的未知数造成了经济上的未知数。我们必须一路评估。我当然疯狂地希望，一旦被压制住，它就不会卷土重来，我们能重新适应。但事情并不总是完美运转。这并不意味着有更好的行动方案，我完全不会去批评人们做过的事。我只是觉得你在应对一个巨大的未知数。过去几个月它对世界的扰乱、对习惯的改变、对企业造成的危险，说明你最好不要太确信未来六个月或一年它会怎样。

**Becky Quick**（00:52:16）：This is a similarly related question. It comes From Seth Freiden, who says, "As a long-term shareholder of Berkshire B shares, I'd like to know Warren's viewpoint around smaller holdings, specifically Oriental Trading Company and Nebraska Furniture Mart, that are based in your hometown of Omaha." He imagines that those smaller business units have been adversely impacted by COVID shelter-in-place mandates. So he would like to know if Oriental Trading, or other small business units, applied for PPP (Small Business Administration Paycheck Protection Program) loans or participated in those acts? And if they didn't qualify for a loan or didn't participate, then how will Berkshire support those smaller businesses to make sure that they can continue to employ their employees?

**Warren Buffett**（00:52:55）：Well, to my knowledge, none of them have gone in for government money and — The two that are mentioned — I don't like to get into specific companies — but I can assure you that the Nebraska Furniture Mart and Oriental Trading, in my view, have a fine future. But I — I don't want to talk about — go down the list, obviously, of every single company, because some of them I don't know the answer to. You know, we actually decided some time ago that our newspapers would have a much better chance of surviving if they were run as part of Lee, than if we ran them independently. And, as I said earlier, we actually put considerably more money — we probably put more money in the newspaper business than virtually anybody in the country in the last six months, because we took over a loan that would have been a problem in the year — whatever it might have been, maybe, a year and a half — and we enable them to just deal with one lender rather than a group. And they are doing a better job with the newspapers then we would do. And that's always our preference. If we've got a business that's — that does not have — looks like it is not going to sustain itself over time in our hands, if we can find somebody else that we think will do a better job, we'd love to have them run it. So, if we have a problem business, we would prefer to find somebody that thinks they can do a better job, and probably can do a better job, of running it than we can. But some businesses just disappear. We — we started with a textile business. We started a company called Diversified Retailing, which merged into Berkshire — became part of Berkshire — and it started with a department store in Baltimore. And department stores looked good in 1966, but the world has gone against them. And we had a trading stamp business at one time, and we stayed longer than anybody else, but the world left trading stamps behind. And that's going to happen with some businesses. That's capitalism. And it will happen to some Berkshire businesses over the next 10 years, in the next 50 years. We think we'll find more of them that will grow and, net, that Berkshire will grow, but we do not think if you own a great many businesses, that everyone is destined for success. That's why I suggest to people they buy an index fund. I do not — with the exception of Berkshire — I would not want to put all my money in any one company, although there's a few I wouldn't mind being very close to that. But — I don't think — you know — you get surprises in this world. And there will be businesses that we think are very good that turn out not to be so good. And there will be other businesses that turn out better than we think. And It's up to the world to judge our batting average over time. Greg?

**Greg Abel**（00:56:25）：Well, I would just add, and echo again, that when it comes to the PPP loans, we're not aware of any of our businesses taking them. And, you know, as I said, we encouraged them, if they were ever thinking that there is going to be a dialog, and we're not aware of any businesses pursuing them. I would also just add that when you look at our businesses as we went into the crisis, they responded very well. So, as we look at our businesses — and Warren touched on this — our large businesses, our mid-sized businesses, and even as you go down from there, they're in very sound shape as we go through the pandemic, and are really preparing to emerge now. So, they're evaluating, they listen. They're going to have a different customer. There's going to be different consumer behaviors — how our employees work, i.e., a lot of them work at home now — does that make sense? And the communities we're operating in have all changed. But we're literally moving from the point of — OK, we're making it through the crisis, and really planning to reemerge now. And I would say our businesses are in an extremely sound place.

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:31）：We don't know when the —

**Becky Quick**（00:57:33）：This next question —

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:42）：Well, I just — what we don't know — we don't — we don't know how long this period lasts. And nobody knows. You know, we don't know whether the — most people think — and they know more about it than I do — that the virus will, you know, to some extent, decline in its spread during summer months. And I would say — but many think that it will come back at some later date. And how the American public reacts, if they get their hopes up through some reopening — some through — through some summer diminution — and how they would react to a second attack, in effect, by the virus — it's like Dr. Fauci — you know, the virus is going to determine our behavior. You know, we — and we're doing a lot of smart things, and we've got a lot of very smart people, but there are unknowns. And the unknowns that apply to the health aspect create unknowns in the economy. And we will — we'll have to keep evaluating things as we go along. I hope, like crazy, obviously, that — that once suppressed, it doesn't come back and that we readjust. But things don't always work perfectly. That doesn't mean there was a better course of action that would not — I would not go around criticizing people at all for what they've done, or anything of the sort. I just think you're dealing with a huge unknown. And I think that the degree to which it's disturbed the world, and changed habits, and endangered businesses, in the last couple of months, indicates that you'd better be — not be too sure of yourself about what will — what it'll do in the next six months or year or whatever.
