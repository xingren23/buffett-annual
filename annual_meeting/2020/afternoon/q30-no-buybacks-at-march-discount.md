## 问题

股东 Andrew Wenke 提问：为什么巴菲特没有在 3 月份回购伯克希尔股票，当时股价已跌到比他 1 月和 2 月回购时低 30% 的水平？

## 回答要点

1. 股价低 30% 的时间非常短暂，巴菲特并不认为相对现值而言折扣有显著不同
2. 引用凯恩斯「如果事实变了，我就改变想法」，称一直在考虑回购，但态度并未改变
3. 现在买入并不比三个月、六个月或九个月前更有吸引力，回购始终是一种可能
4. 坦承航空股头寸是错误，伯克希尔因此价值降低，某些东西的价值确实下降了
5. 价格从未达到比其他选择（包括金钱的期权价值）明显更好、值得大举回购的水平
6. 阿贝尔认同回购方针正确：只在认为对股东有利时才回购，并非一直回购

## 关键引述

> **“If the facts change, I change my mind. What do you do, sir?”**
>
> 「如果事实变了，我就改变想法。那你呢，先生？」

> **“Our airline position was a mistake.”**
>
> 「我们的航空股头寸是个错误。」

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:06）：贝基？

**Becky Quick**（01:59:07）：好。这个问题——我一直在找一个这样的，因为我收到好几个类似的——我刚才就在找一个。这条来自 Andrew Wenke。他问：「能否问问沃伦，为什么 3 月伯克希尔股价跌到比他 1 月、2 月回购时低 30% 的价格时，他没有回购？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:28）：对。那个低 30% 的时期非常、非常、非常短暂，但，我们，我认为，相对于现值，伯克希尔股票现在的折价，跟我们付更高价格时相比，并没有显著不同。你知道，就像（经济学家约翰·梅纳德）凯恩斯说的，或者不管谁说的：「事实变了，我就改变想法。你呢，先生？」所以，我们一直在考虑这件事。但我不觉得现在买伯克希尔，比三个月、六个月或九个月前感觉更，远为更有吸引力。它一直是个可能选项。我们走着瞧。格雷格，你也考虑回购。（笑）

**Greg Abel**（02:00:35）：不，我——我认为我们的做法，沃伦，是对的。我是说，你总是——我实在没什么可补充的，除了这个做法是对的。当我们看到回购对股东是正确的事时，我们就去做。这并不意味着我们一直在回购，或者这个看法会变。

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:55）：可能存在某个价格，相对于当时的价值，不是相对于一年前的价值，我是说。有些东西的价值下降了。我们的航空股头寸是个错误。因为我把那个头寸，伯克希尔今天的价值比我没买时要低。还有其他类似的决定。而且它们不是，你知道，现在买这些股票并不比我们当时买更有吸引力。也没更没吸引力。我是说，扯平了。但我们没做任何，我们，它没有变得，股价没有到一个水平，或者说不曾处在一个水平，让我们觉得它明显比其他东西好得多，包括现金的期权价值，从而大规模出手。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:06）：Becky?

**Becky Quick**（01:59:07）：All right. So, this question — I was looking for one of these, because I got several questions that came in similar to this — I was looking for one of these a moment ago. This one's from Andrew Wenke. He says, "Can you ask Warren why he didn't purchase — repurchase — Berkshire shares in March when they dropped to a price that was 30 percent lower than the price that he had repurchased shares for in January and February?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:28）：Yeah. It was very, very, very short period where they were 30 percent less, but — We — I don't think Berkshire shares, relative to present value, are at a significantly different discount than they were when we were paying somewhat higher prices. You know, it's like (economist John Maynard) Keynes said, or whoever it was, "If the facts change, I change my mind. What do you do, sir?" So — it's — we always think about it. But I don't feel that it's more — far more compelling to buy Berkshire shares now than I would have felt three months, or six months, or nine months ago. It's always — it's always a possibility. And we'll see what happens. Greg, you've — you think about repurchasing shares. (Laughs)

**Greg Abel**（02:00:35）：No, I — I think our approach, Warren, is the right approach. I mean, you're always — I can't really add anything other than the approach is the right approach. We approach it when we see it's the right thing for our shareholders to be repurchasing. And that doesn't mean we're repurchasing all the time, or the view doesn't change.

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:55）：There could be a price, relative to value at the time, not relative to what it was worth a year ago, I mean. The value of certain things have decreased. Our airline position was a mistake. Berkshire is worth less today because I took that position than if I hadn't. And there are other decisions like that. And they're not, you know — it is not more compelling to buy the shares now than it was when we were buying them. It's not less compelling. I mean, it's a wash. But we didn't do any — we — It's not gotten — the price has not gotten to a level — or not been at a level — where it really feels way better to us than other things, including the option value of money, to step up in a big, big way.
