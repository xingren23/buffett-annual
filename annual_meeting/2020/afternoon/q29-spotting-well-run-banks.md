## 问题

股东 Thomas Lin 援引巴菲特的观点：只要资产端不做蠢事，银行业就是好生意；但疫情可能给贷款带来很大压力，过去几年的蠢事恐将集中爆发。他询问：通过阅读年报、10-Q 和其他公开信息，应寻找哪些线索来判断一家银行是否由真正的银行家经营、从而避免做蠢事？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特认为银行体系并非此次停摆的问题所在，与 2008—09 年银行深陷危机、状况迥异
2. 美联储主席鲍威尔这次比当年的伯南克轻松：银行状况远好于从前，不再是需要救助的问题源
3. FDIC 为存款背书，并以加快的评估费和差别对待大银行的方式积累了 1000 亿美元储备
4. 银行用大量他人的钱经营，经济问题严重时强健银行也会承压，所幸有美联储体系在背后支撑
5. 能源贷款和消费信贷会出现额外问题，银行一季度已在增提拨备、资本充足，但并非主要担忧
6. 阿贝尔指出疫情持续多久、是否有第二波仍属未知风险，银行须像其他企业一样设法应对

## 关键引述

> **“I don't see special problems in the banking industry now.”**
>
> 「我认为现在银行业没有特别的问题。」

> **“the banking system is not the problem in this particular shutdown”**
>
> 「银行体系并不是这次停摆中的问题所在。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（01:53:58）：这是中国台湾的 Thomas Lin 提的问题。他说：「沃伦曾说，只要不在资产端做蠢事，银行业就是门好生意。鉴于疫情可能给贷款带来很大压力，过去几年做的蠢事很可能集中爆发。通过阅读年报、10-Q 和其他公开信息，你在寻找哪些线索，来判断一家银行是否由真正的银行家经营、能避免做蠢事？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:23）：这是个很好的问题。但我要说，这一次让（美联储）主席（杰罗姆）鲍尔的工作比 2008、09 年稍微轻松一点的，是银行的状态好得多，所以，在思考什么对经济有利时，他不必同时纠结拿银行 A 或银行 B 怎么办，是把它们跟别人合并，还是给系统增加，额外的压力什么的。银行在 2008、09 年深深卷入了问题。它们当中有些做了不该做的事。它们的财务状况跟现在完全不同，所以，在这次停摆中，银行体系不是问题所在。是我们作为一个民族，决定大规模关停一部分经济。这事发生，不是任何人的错。世上确实会发生这种事。地震会发生，你知道。巨大飓风会发生。这次是不一样的事，但，银行需要监管。我是说，你知道，它们从联邦存款保险公司（FDIC）受益，但政府为你的存款背书，代价之一就是你得行为良好，我认为银行表现得很好，状态也很健康。我是说，这就是我说 FDIC 已经积累了 1000 亿美元的原因。近年来，他们在某些时期加快了对银行的评估收费，甚至对大银行区别对待。所以他们在那里攒下了巨额准备金。他们也重建了自己的资产负债表，目前，他们不是鲍尔主席的麻烦，而在 2008、09 年，他们曾是伯南克主席的大麻烦。

至于怎么识别那些正在做蠢事的人，并不容易，因为，嗯，有时候容易。但，（笑），我没看到多少让我不安的东西。但说到底，银行是用别人的大量资金运作的机构，如果经济里的问题严重到一定程度，即使是强健的银行也可能承受很大压力，我们会非常高兴有联邦储备体系站在它们背后。我不认为银行业现在有特殊问题。我能想到一些可能性。杰米·戴蒙在摩根大通的报告里略微提到过。你可以设想出给银行带来巨大压力的情形，它们并非完全不可能。这就是为什么我们有一个（听不清）。我认为总体上，银行体系不会成为问题。但我不会百分百肯定地这么说，因为世上确实存在银行可能出问题的某些可能。它们的能源贷款会出问题。它们的某些，你知道，消费者信贷会有额外问题。它们会，你知道，它们身处，但它们自己知道。它们准备金充足。好吧，它们资本充足。它们在第一季度还在增提准备金，也许还需要提更多。但它们根本不是我主要担心的事。我们持有不少银行。我们持有不少银行股。格雷格，你有什么想法吗？

**Greg Abel**（01:58:45）：没有，我真的——你刚才也大致提到了，就是我们不知道这场疫情会持续多久，不知道会不会有第二轮事件，这些都是此刻真正未知的风险，银行必须继续设法应对，就像所有企业一样。但显然你已经强调过这一点了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:05）：对。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（01:53:58）：This is one that comes from Thomas Lin in Taiwan. He says, "Warren once said that banking is a good business if you don't do dumb things on the asset side. Given that the pandemic might put a lot of pressure on the loans, dumb things that got done in the past few years are likely to explode. "Through reading annual reports, 10-Qs, and other public information, what clues are you looking for to decide whether a bank is run by a true banker who avoids doing dumb things?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:23）：That's a very good question. But I would say that the one thing that made (Federal Reserve) Chairman (Jerome) Powell's job a little easier this time than it was in 2008 and '09, is that the banks are in far better shape, so — In terms of thinking about what was good for the economy, he wasn't at the same time worrying about what he was going to do with bank A or bank B, to merge them with somebody else, or put strain — added strains on the system or anything. The banks — the banks were very involved with the problem in 2008 and '09. They had — they had done some things they shouldn't have done in —some of them. And they were certainly in far different financial condition than now, so that — the banking system is not the problem in this particular shutdown. The government — we decided, as a people, to shut down part of the economy in a big way. And it was not the fault of anyone that it happened. Things do happen in this world. Earthquakes happen, you know. Huge hurricanes happen. This was something different, but — The banks — banks need regulation. I mean, you know, they benefit from the FDIC, but part of having the government standing behind your deposits is to behave well, and I think that the banks have behaved very well, and I think they're in very good shape. I mean, that's why the FDIC has built up a hundred billion dollars that I've talked about. I mean, they've assessed the banks, in recent years, at accelerated amounts in certain periods, and they even differentiated against the big banks. So, they built up great reserves there. And they built their own balance sheets, and they are not, presently, part of Chairman Powell's problem, whereas they were very much a part of Chairman Bernanke's problem back in 2008 and '09. How you spot the people that are doing the dumb things is not easy because — well, sometimes it's easy. But — (laughs) — I don't see a lot that bothers me. But banks are, in the end, institutions that operate with significant amounts of other people's money, and if problems become severe enough in an economy, even strong banks can be under a lot of stress, and we'll be very glad we've got the Federal Reserve System standing behind them. I don't see special problems in the banking industry now. I could think of possibilities. And (CEO) Jamie Dimon referred to this a little bit in the Morgan — JPMorgan report. You can dream of scenarios that puts a lot of strain on banks, and they're not totally impossible. And that's why we have a (inaudible). And I think that — I think overall, the banking system is not going to be the problem. But I'm not — I wouldn't say that with a hundred percent certainty because there are certain possibilities that exist in this world where banks can have problems. They're going to have problems with energy loans. They're going to have problems some — you know, they're going to have extra problems with consumer credit. They're going to have — you know, they're in — but they know it. And they're well reserved. Well, they're well capitalized for it. They were reserve building in the first quarter, and they may need to build more reserves. But they are not a primary worry of mine at all. We own a lot of banks. We own a lot of bank stocks. Greg, do you have any thoughts on it?

**Greg Abel**（01:58:45）：No, I really — you touched on it earlier, too, just in general, which is we don't know how long this pandemic will go, we don't know if there's going to be a second event, which are just risks that are really unknown at this time, and the banks will have to continue to manage through that, as businesses do. But you've already highlighted that, obviously.

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:05）：Yeah.
