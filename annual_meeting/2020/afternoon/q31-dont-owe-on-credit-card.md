## 问题

来自以色列的三位投资者询问信用卡行业：如何解释近年信用卡平均利率相对联邦基金利率的上升？哪些力量可能让利率维持在当前水平附近，哪些力量又可能在未来压低它？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特称对利率问题贡献有限：信用卡利率受竞争和损失可能性影响，后者近几个月显著上升
2. 其在信用卡业务中有利益，影响美国运通及旗下银行，旗下部分家具公司也有自有信用卡
3. 建议人们不要把信用卡当作可以随意攫取的存钱罐
4. 举例：若欠 18% 的卡债，手里有钱第一件事就是还清，胜过任何投资点子
5. 调侃若女儿有钱，不如「让她借给你」，因为你付 18% 她找不到更好的交易
6. 阿贝尔认同：三个孩子谨慎用卡，数字时代用卡增多，但目标是必须还款，否则有增量风险

## 关键引述

> **“I think people should avoid using credit cards as a piggy bank to be raided.”**
>
> 「我认为人们应该避免把信用卡当作一个可以随意攫取的存钱罐。」

> **“I don't know how to make 18 percent.”**
>
> 「我不知道怎么才能赚到 18%。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Becky Quick**（02:01:54）：这个问题来自以色列的三位投资者，Lidor Zloof、Yossi Zloof 和 Dan Gorfung。他们想了解信用卡行业。问题是：「近年信用卡平均利率相对联邦基金利率上升，你如何解释？你认为哪些力量可能把它维持在当前水平或附近，哪些力量可能在未来压低它？」

**Warren Buffett**（02:02:21）：嗯，这不是我，你知道，某种程度上它影响美国运通，也影响我们持有的银行。但信用卡利率显然会对竞争做出反应，显然也会对损失可能性做出反应，而后者在过去几个月明显上升，尽管它可能从过去某些你可以挑出来的时期降了下来，但在这个问题上，我实在没什么可贡献的。我们不在，嗯，这话不对。我们的家具公司，有几家有自己信用卡，尽管它们很多生意也是用别人的信用卡做的。我对人的一般建议是，我是说，你知道，我们虽然在信用卡里有利益，但人们，我认为人们应该避免把信用卡当成可以随便掏的存钱罐。不久前有个女士来找我，她（听不清）一些钱，不太多，但对她很多，她是我朋友，她问：「我该怎么处理这笔钱？」我说：「嗯，你信用卡欠多少？」她说：「嗯，我欠 X。

」我说：「那你该做的」，我不知道她付多少利率，但我想，你知道，也许，我记得我问过她，她知道，大概 18% 之类的。我说：「我不知道怎么赚到 18%。」你知道，我是说，如果我欠着 18% 的钱，我拿到任何钱，第一件事就是把它还掉。这会比我任何投资点子都强得多。而这不是你想听的话。后来谈话中，她提到她女儿，她女儿有一两千美元之类的。她说：「那我该怎么处理……」，她报出女儿名字，「……的钱？」我说：「让她借给你。」（笑）「我是说，如果你愿意付 18%，（笑声），或别的什么，我是说，她找不到比这更好的交易了。我借钱给你。」这根本讲不通。你不可能一辈子用那种利率借钱还能过得更好（听不清）。所以我建议大家，这与我自己，或伯克希尔在某些情形下的利益相悖，但全世界都爱上了信用卡，可我要建议任何人，人生第一件事就是别欠，我不认为，你以后可以再做别的，但别付哪怕 12% 给别人。

我是说，就是，先还掉，然后，如果他们信用真的很好而不愿这么做，亲自来找我，我借钱给你，（笑），按那个利率。格雷格，你怎么跟你孩子说？（笑）

**Greg Abel**（02:05:42）：同样的建议，极好的建议。（笑）不，我有三个孩子，都谨慎用卡，我想更多是为了，不是，但显然，人们进入数字世界和电商世界后会用得多得多，但目标必须是还清。这并不意味着你，因为不得不用卡做那些交易，就让余额累积起来，但如今这里有额外的风险。

**Warren Buffett**（02:06:12）：对有些人来说，这是图个方便。

**Greg Abel**（02:06:19）：对，对。

**Warren Buffett**（02:06:20）：但——我会很难——如果我要为钱付 12%，或别的什么，那不是好事。（笑）你不会看到伯克希尔付这个钱。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Becky Quick**（02:01:54）：This question comes from three investors in Israel, Lidor Zloof, Yossi Zloof, and Dan Gorfung. They want to know about the credit card industry. It says, "How do you explain the rise in the average credit card interest rate in recent years compared to the federal funds rate? What are the forces that you think might keep it at or around current levels, and what are the forces that might drive it lower in the future?"

**Warren Buffett**（02:02:21）：Well, that is not a subject that I'm — you know — obviously, it affects American Express to some degree, it affects the banks we own. But interest rates on credit cards respond to competition, obviously, to loss potential, which, obviously, has gone up significantly in the last few months — although it's gone down, perhaps from some other periods you can pick in the past — but I don't really have much I can bring to the party on that question. We are not in the — well, that isn't true. Our furniture companies, a couple of them, have their own credit card, although they do a lot of business on other people's credit cards. My general advice to people — I mean, you know — we have an interest in credit cards, but people — I don't — I think people should avoid using credit cards as a piggy bank to be raided. I had a woman come to see me here not long ago, and she'd (inaudible) some money — and not very much, but it was a lot to her — she's a friend of mine — and she said, "What should I do with it?" And I said, "Well, what do you owe on your credit card?" And she's, "Well, I owe X." And I said, "Well, what you should do" — I don't know what interest rate she was paying, but I think, you know — maybe — I think I asked her, and she knew, and something like 18 percent or something. I said, "I don't know how to make 18 percent." You know, I mean — if I owed any money at 18 percent, the first thing I'd do with any money I had, would be to pay it off. It's going to be way better than any investment idea I've got. And that wasn't what you wanted to hear. And then later on in the conversation, she talked about her daughter, and her daughter had a thousand dollars or two thousand dollars, or something. And she said, "Well, what should I do with …" — and she named the girls — "… money?" And I said, "Have her lend it to you." (Laughs) "I mean, if you're willing to pay 18 percent —(laughter) — or whatever, I mean, she's not going to find a better deal. I'll lend you money." It just doesn't — it doesn't make sense. You can't go through life, you know, borrowing money at those rates and be better off (inaudible). So, I encourage everybody — it's contrary to my own — or Berkshire's interests in certain cases — but the world is in love with credit cards — but I would suggest to anybody that the first thing they do in life is not owe — I don't think — you can get to something else later on — but don't be paying — don't be paying, even 12 percent, to anybody. I mean it's — Pay that off and then — and if they're really a good credit and they don't want to do it, come and see me personally, I'll lend you the money — (laughs) — at that rate. Greg, what do you tell your children? (Laughs)

**Greg Abel**（02:05:42）：The same advice — excellent advice. (Laughs) No, I have three that carefully use their credit card, and more I would say for — not the — but, obviously, people use it a lot more as they go into the digital world and e-commerce world, but then the goal has to be to repay it. It doesn't mean you — because you have to use it for those type of transactions, you run up the balance, but there's an incremental risk there now.

**Warren Buffett**（02:06:12）：It's a matter of convenience for some people.

**Greg Abel**（02:06:19）：Yeah, yeah.

**Warren Buffett**（02:06:20）：But — I would have trouble — if I were paying 12 percent for money, or whatever it might be, it would not be a good thing. (Laughs) You won't see Berkshire paying that. (Laughter)
