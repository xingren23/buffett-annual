## 问题

富国银行丑闻、美国运通丢掉 Costco、联合航空服务问题、可口可乐汽水消费放缓——伯克希尔花多少时间审视这些核心持仓？继续持有是否意味着投资逻辑依然成立？

## 回答要点

1. 美国运通、可口可乐、富国银行合计已是几百亿美元量级的持仓，都是「非常喜欢」的生意，各有不同特征
2. 伯克希尔是四大航空公司（含联合航空）的最大股东，那是行业层面的判断而非个股判断
3. 买入时从未假设这些公司不会有问题、不会有竞争；买的是「手里的牌非常硬」，以及财务政策与行业位置
4. 「经济城堡」比喻：资本主义下必有人来抢城堡，需要护城河与能打的骑士，但劫掠者永远存在
5. 可口可乐创立于 1886 年、美国运通 1850 年代起家（也是韦尔斯与法戈创办），都经历过多轮挑战
6. 巴菲特与芒格每天都在审视持仓；芒格当日无补充，巴菲特笑称「不参与就降薪」

## 关键引述

> **“you basically have an economic castle”**
>
> 「本质上你就拥有一座经济城堡。」

> **“But there are going to be marauders. And they'll never go away.”**
>
> 「但劫掠者一定会有，永远都会有。」

> **“I would say that I do it every day.”**
>
> 「我可以说，我每天都在做。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:05）：好，贝基？贝基·奎克。

**Becky Quick**（00:36:09）：这个问题来自俄克拉何马州塔尔萨的股东马克·布莱克利。他说：「伯克希尔几只核心持股最近的消息比平常多。富国银行陷在激励与新账户丑闻里；美国运通丢了 Costco 的合作，又在高端卡市场追赶；联合航空有客户服务问题；可口可乐的汽水消费在放缓。伯克希尔花多少时间审视这些股票持仓？如果公司继续持有，是否可以说投资逻辑依然成立？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:41）：嗯，我们花很多时间去想。这些都是非常大的持仓。把美国运通、可口可乐、富国银行加起来，已经是几百亿美元的量级。这些生意我们非常喜欢，它们的特点各不相同。你提到联合航空，实际上我们是四大航空公司里最大的股东，四家都持有，我们是最大的。那更多是对整个行业的判断。但任何生意都有问题，其中一些又有非常大的优势。你提到美国运通。如果你读它的一季报，看白金卡那部分，白金卡做得非常好：全球都在增长，我记得英国刷卡额增长 17% 左右，日本、墨西哥按本币算增长 15%，美国也非常好。这些生意都有竞争。我们买美国运通、富国银行、联合航空、可口可乐的时候，从来没有假设它们永远不会有问题、永远不会有竞争。

我们买它们，是因为我们认为它们手里的牌非常硬，也因为其中不少公司的财务政策我们喜欢，位置我们喜欢。我们买过很多生意，确实会去看它们是否有可持续的竞争优势。我们也清楚，一门很好的生意，一定会有大批竞争者想把它抢走；然后你要判断这家公司、这个产品、这个管理层有没有能力挡住竞争者。竞争者不会消失。我不点名，但这些公司总体上位置都很好。我打过这样的比方：如果你有一门出色的生意，哪怕小如喜诗糖果，本质上你就拥有一座经济城堡。而在资本主义里，人们一定会来抢这座城堡。所以你需要在城堡周围挖一条护城河，用各种办法保护它；城堡里还要有一位相当能打的骑士，来击退劫掠者。

但劫掠者一定会有，永远都会有。你看，可口可乐是 1886 年；美国运通是 18——我不记得，51 还是 52 年，从快递业务起家；富国银行的创立年份我说不上来。顺便说一句，美国运通也是韦尔斯和法戈创办的。这些公司经历过无数挑战，未来还会有更多。我们旗下的公司也有挑战，保险业务也有挑战。我们 1968 年以 800 万美元买下国民赔偿公司起步。运气好，有 GEICO 的托尼·奈斯利，有阿吉特·贾因——他为你们创造的财富比我多得多，还有一些你们可能都没听说过的小公司，为我们做得非常出色。

所以保险业永远有竞争，但一个真正聪明的管理层、配上像样的分销体系，总有很多事可以做，去挡住劫掠者。至于那个具体问题：花多少时间审视持仓？我可以说，我每天都在做。我相信查理也每天都在做。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:40:50）：嗯，我也不觉得自己有什么可补充的。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:40:53）：他要是不参与，我们就降他薪水。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:05）：OK, Becky? Becky Quick.

**Becky Quick**（00:36:09）：This question comes from a shareholder named Mark Blakley in Tulsa, Oklahoma, who says, "There has been more news than usual in some of Berkshire's core stock holdings. "Wells Fargo in the incentive and new account scandal, American Express losing the Costco relationship and playing catch-up in the premium card space, United Airlines and customer service issues, Coca-Cola and slowing soda consumption. "How much time is spent reviewing Berkshire's stock holdings? And is it safe to assume, if Berkshire continues to hold these stocks, that the thesis remains intact?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:41）：Well, we spend a lot of time think — those are very large holdings. If you add up American Express, Coca-Cola, and Wells Fargo, I mean, you're getting up, you know, well into the high tens of billions of dollars. And those are businesses we like very much. There're different characteristics. In the case of — you mentioned United Airlines, we actually are the largest holder of all four of the — we're the largest holder of the four largest airlines. And that is much more of an industry thought. But all businesses have problems. And some of them have some very big plusses. I personally — you mentioned American Express. If you read American Express's first quarter report and talk about their Platinum Card, the Platinum Card is doing very well. The gains around the world. You know, I think there were 17 percent or something like that in billings in the U.K. and 15 percent is original currency — or the local currency — Japan, Mexico, and very good in the United States. There's competition in all these businesses. If we thought — we did not buy American Express or Wells Fargo or United Airlines, Coca-Cola, with the idea that they would never have problems or never have competition. What we did buy — why we did buy them — is we thought they had very, very strong hands. And we liked the financial policies in the cases of many of them. We liked their position. We've bought a lot of businesses. And we do look to see where we think they have durable competitive advantage. And we recognize that if you've got a very good business, you're going to have plenty of competitors that are going to try and take it away from you. And then you make a judgment as to the ability of your particular company and product and management to ward off competitors. They won't go away, but the — we think — I'm not going to get into specific names on it — but those companies generally are very well-positioned. I've likened essentially — if you've got a wonderful business, even if it was a small one like See's Candy, you basically have an economic castle. And in capitalism, people are going to try and take away that castle from you. So, you want a moat around it, protecting it in various ways that can protect it. And then you want a knight in the castle that's pretty darn good at warding off marauders. But there are going to be marauders. And they'll never go away. And if you look at — I think Coca-Cola was 1886. American Express was 18 — I don't know — '51 or '52 — starting out with an express business. Wells Fargo was — I don't know what year they were started. Incidentally, I — American Express was started by [Henry] Wells and [William] Fargo as well. So these companies had lots of challenges. And they'll have more challenges. And the companies we own have had challenges. Our insurance business has had challenges. But, you know, we started with National Indemnity's $8 million purchase in 1968. And fortunately, we've had people like Tony Nicely at GEICO. And we've had Ajit Jain, who's added tens of billions of value. And we've got some smaller companies that you probably don't even know about, but really have done a terrific job for us. So there'll always be competition in insurance, but there'll always be things to do that a really intelligent management with a decent distribution system, various things going for him, can do to ward off the marauders. So I — there was a specific question, "How much time is spent reviewing the holdings?" I would say that I do it every day. I'm sure Charlie does it every day. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:40:50）：Well, I don't think I had anything to add to that, either. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:40:53）：We'll cut his salary if he doesn't participate here. (Laughter)
