## 问题

两位靠彼此交流想法，才大幅减少了资本配置失误。这种情况在伯克希尔未来的很长时间里还能延续吗？总部与子公司两个层面都算。

## 回答要点

1. 芒格直言「不可能延续很久」；巴菲特回一句「别这么早就认输」
2. 未来十年，下一任管理者大概要配置 4000 亿美元，超过此前全部历史投入的总和
3. 因此资本配置能力必须是继任者的核心才能，而不只是众多技能之一
4. 比喻：在卡内基音乐厅拉小提琴拉到那儿，上台后人家递给你一架钢琴
5. 巴菲特称之为「钱商」：智商 120 与 140 的人，智力测验分数相近，但头脑擅长的事不同
6. 芒格补充：回购股票本身就是一个选项，所以这不是无解的难题

## 关键引述

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:39:59）：好，第九站。

**Audience Member**（02:40:02）：我是伊利诺伊州格尼的尚卡尔·阿南特。谢谢你们为我们所做的一切。我有个问题：你们两位主要通过彼此交流想法，才在很大程度上避开了资本配置的失误。这种情况在伯克希尔未来的很长时间里还能延续吗？我关心的是总部和子公司两个层面。

**Charlie Munger**（02:40:27）：不可能延续很久。

**Warren Buffett**（02:40:35）：我——（笑）别这么早就认输，查理。（笑）伯克希尔任命的任何继任者，资本配置能力（而且是被证明过的能力）一定会是董事会最看重的，眼下则是我和查理的推荐中最看重的。资本配置在伯克希尔极其重要。我们现在有 2800 亿还是 2900 亿美元的股东权益。单看未来十年（没人能对此做准确预测），光是折旧每年就有 70 亿美元左右。未来十年，下一任管理者大概要配置 4000 亿美元甚至更多，比此前所有年份投入的还多。也就是说，十年之后，伯克希尔这组企业里，这十年投进去的钱会超过之前全部历史的总和。

所以 CEO 这个位置上必须有一位非常明智的资本配置者。我们会有的。如果让一个资本配置能力不行的人坐在这个位置上，那是巨大的错误。资本配置大概应该接近他的主要才能。伯克希尔有个优势：我们知道这件事有多重要，而且始终聚焦于此。在很多公司，人们是靠某种能力爬到顶端的（有时是销售，有时来自法务），然后资本配置就落到他们手里。他们也许会设战略思考部门，也许会听投行的话，但他们最好自己能做。如果出身不同、没做过，那就像我在某封信里写的：好比你在卡内基音乐厅拉小提琴拉到了那儿，然后走上台，人家递给你一架钢琴。

如果上来的人在其他领域很有本事、却没有资本配置能力，伯克希尔不会好。我把这种能力叫作「钱商」。有的人智商 120、140，智力测验分数差不多，但头脑擅长的事情各不相同。我认识一些非常聪明的人没有钱商，会做出非常不智的财务决定；他们能做常人做不了的各种事，只是脑子不是那样接线的。我也认识另一些人，做 SAT 之类并不出色，但一辈子没做过一个愚蠢的金钱决定。查理肯定也见过同样的事。所以我们要的是这个人（希望他还有很多别的才能），但绝对不要一个缺少钱商的人。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:44:48）：嗯，还有回购股票这个选项，所以这不算什么无解的难题。总会有明智的办法。

**Warren Buffett**（02:45:00）：而有「钱商」的人会看得出什么时候回购合理、什么时候不合理。事实上，对某些管理层来说，怎么看待回购是个很好的测试，因为只要你思路直，这道题并不复杂。可有人这样想，有人不这样想——他们在别的方面大概强得多，但一碰到「回购是否合理」这种看起来很清楚的题，就会说出很傻的话。查理，还有吗？

**Charlie Munger**（02:45:39）：没有。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:39:59）：OK. Station 9.

**Audience Member**（02:40:02）：I'm from — Shankar Anant from Gurnee, Illinois. Thank you for doing everything you do for us. I have a question. The two of you have largely avoided capital allocation mistakes by bouncing ideas off of one another. Will this continue long into Berkshire's future? And I'd like to — I'm interested in both at headquarters and at subsidiaries.

**Charlie Munger**（02:40:27）：It can't continue very long.

**Warren Buffett**（02:40:35）：I — (Laughter) Don't get defeatist, Charlie. (Laughter) Any successor that's put in at Berkshire, capital allocation abilities, and proven capital allocation abilities, are certain to be uppermost in board's minds or in, in the current case — in terms of my recommendation, Charlie's recommendation, for what happens after we're not around. Capital allocation is incredibly important at Berkshire. Right now we have 280 or -90 billion, whatever it may be, of shareholders' equity. If you take the next decade alone, you know, nobody can make accurate predictions on this. But in the next 10 years, if you just take — and depreciation right now is another seven billion a year, something on that order. The next manager in the decade is going to have to allocate, maybe, 400 billion or something like that, maybe more. And it's more than already has been put in. So 10 years from now, Berkshire will be an aggregation of businesses where more money has been put in in that decade than everything that took place ahead of time. So you need a very sensible capital allocator in the job of being CEO of Berkshire. And we will have one. It would be a terrible mistake to have someone in this job where, really, capital allocation might be — might even be their main talent. It probably should be very close to their main talent. And of course, we have an advantage at Berkshire, in that we do know how important that is and there is that focus on it. And in a great many companies, people get to the top through ability, and sales sometimes, if they come from the legal side, something like that — all different sides — and they then have the capital allocation, sort of, in their hands. Now, they may not establish strategic thinking divisions. And they may listen to investment bankers and everything, but they better be able to do it themselves. And if they've come from a different background or haven't done it, it's a little bit, as I put in one of my letters, I think — it's like getting to Carnegie Hall playing the violin, and then you walk out on the stage and they hand you a piano. I mean, it is something that — Berkshire would not do well if somebody was put in who had a lot of skills in other areas but really did not have an ability to capital allocation. I've talked about it as being something I call a "money mind." I mean, people can have 120 IQs or 140 IQs or whatever it may be, very similar scoring abilities in terms of intelligence tests. And some of them have minds that are good at one kind of thing and some of them another. I've known very bright people that do not have money minds, and they can make very unintelligent decisions. They can do all kinds of other things that most mortals can't do. But it just doesn't, it isn't the way their wiring works. And I've known other people that really would not do that brilliantly. They do fine, but on an SAT test or something like that. But they've never made a dumb money decision in their life. And Charlie, I'm sure, has seen the same thing. So we do want somebody — and hopefully they've got a lot of talents — but we certainly do not want somebody that — if they lack a money mind. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:44:48）：Well, there's also the option of buying in stock, which — so, it isn't like it's some hopeless problem. One way or another, something intelligent will be done.

**Warren Buffett**（02:45:00）：And a money mind will recognize when it makes sense to buy in stock and doesn't. You know, and — In fact, it's a pretty good test for some people, in terms of managements, how they think about something like buying in stock, because it's not a very complicated equation if you sort of think straight about that sort of a subject. But some people think that way and some don't, and they're probably miles better at something else. But they say some very silly things when you get to something that seems so clear as whether, say, buying in stock makes sense. Anything further, Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:45:39）：No.
