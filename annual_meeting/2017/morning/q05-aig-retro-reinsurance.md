## 问题

伯克希尔与 AIG 达成史上最大的追溯再保险交易：以 102 亿美元保费，承接 AIG 在 2015 年底之前承保业务最多 200 亿美元的赔款。考虑到 AIG 的准备金缺口历史与浮存金的投资机会，这笔合约最终赚钱的把握有多大？

## 回答要点

1. 结构：AIG 自留前 250 亿美元，其后的 250 亿美元中伯克希尔承担 80%，上限 200 亿；对价 102 亿美元，适用于 2015 年 12 月 31 日前承保或赚取的损失
2. 巴菲特称这类交易 99% 的判断由阿吉特·贾因完成，自己只占 1%；预测一定会有偏差，所以刻意保守
3. 这是同类交易中规模最大的一笔；第二大来自多年前伦敦劳合社体系剥离的主体；有一笔十几亿美元保费的交易明确判断失误
4. 隐患在于资金成本：手握 900 多亿美元现金，新增的 102 亿美元眼下只能赚「花生米」，不符合让交易有吸引力的算式
5. 巴菲特认为这对 AIG 也是好交易——用 102 亿美元卸掉 200 亿潜在损失，并平息了准备金不足的质疑
6. 芒格：本质上是危险的活动，「而这正是它的吸引力之一」；并调侃潜在对手「屈指可数」其实意思是「一家」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:00）：好，杰伊·格尔布。

**Jay Gelb**（00:41:07）：这个问题关于伯克希尔与 AIG 的追溯再保险交易，是同类交易中规模最大的一笔。考虑到 AIG 过往的准备金缺口，以及伯克希尔可以投资这笔浮存金的机会，这份合约以 100 亿美元保费覆盖 AIG 最多 200 亿美元准备金。你对它最终能为伯克希尔赚钱有多大信心？

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:33）：嗯，做每一笔交易的时候，我都觉得它聪明。然后有时候（笑），事情进展下去我才发现不是。这笔交易杰伊清楚，但很多人可能不熟悉：AIG 把 250 亿美元（超额部分的），250 亿美元负债中的 80% 转给了我们。换句话说，前 250 亿美元由他们自己承担；接下来的 250 亿美元里，他们赔多少，我们付其中的 80%，上限 200 亿美元。为此他们付给我们 102 亿美元。这适用于他们 2015 年 12 月 31 日之前承保或赚取的、许多业务类别的损失。阿吉特·贾因为伯克希尔、为你们赚的钱比我多得多，这类交易由他评估。我们自己也会讨论不少，我只是觉得有意思，尤其是他们要给我们的这 102 亿美元很有意思。我们的结论是：今天拿到 102 亿美元，而这笔大业务最多赔 200 亿美元（从现在到审判日之间），我们认为这笔会做得不错。

AIG 做这件事有充分理由，因为他们的准备金一直受到批评。这笔交易基本上（大概也应该），让「那部分业务准备金不足」的质疑平息了。我们拿到 102 亿美元，问题是我们多快赔出去、赔多少。这件事 99% 的思考是阿吉特做的，我只占 1%。我们会做赔付预测，也知道无论怎么预测都会错，所以尽量保守。这类交易我们做过不少，这是最大的一笔；第二大的，是多年前从伦敦劳合社体系里剥离出来的一家公司。有一笔保费十几亿美元的交易我们判断错了，明显错了。还有几笔能否赚钱，取决于你假设我们在这笔资金上赚了多少。它们还可以，但大概没有我们想的那么好。总体上说，我们做这类生意还不错。但这在我们手上躺着 900 多亿美元现金的时候，就没那么好了——一季度收进来的这 102 亿美元，眼下只赚点花生米。而花生米并不符合让这笔交易有吸引力的算式。所以我们必须假设这些钱会找到用处，只是这钱会跟我们待上相当长一段时间。我认为我们的计算偏保守，而且和 AIG 的算法并不相同：赔付等等，我们自己估。我其实认为，从 AIG 的角度看这也是笔相当好的交易。他们用 102 亿美元卸掉了 200 亿美元的潜在损失，而且我认为他们让投资界相信：2015 年之前那段期间，不太可能出现这笔交易没有覆盖到的、不利的准备金发展。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:50）：嗯，我认为这本质上是一种危险的活动。而这正是它的吸引力之一。我不认为世界上还有哪两个人比阿吉特和沃伦更擅长这类交易，也没有别人有我们这样的经验。多给我一些这样的生意，我愿意多担一点心。

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:16）：还有一点要提：实际上，我们是全世界唯一一家会承保这类合约、而且结果能让对方满意的保险机构。有人把 102 亿美元交给你，说「我指望你还 200 亿，哪怕那是 50 年后，最后一块钱也得还」——愿意把 102 亿美元交出去的对象，屈指可数。所以对面的人很有限。能有这个量级交易的，也没几家。不过——

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:53）：「屈指可数」是个好说法。他的意思是「一家」。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:56）：是。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:00）：OK, Jay Gelb.

**Jay Gelb**（00:41:07）：This question is on Berkshire's retroactive reinsurance deal with AIG, which was the largest ever of its kind. Based on AIG's track record of reserve deficiencies and the opportunity for Berkshire to invest the float, what is your level of confidence that this contract covering up to $20 billion of AIG's reserves in return for $10 billion of premiums will ultimately be profitable for Berkshire?

**Warren Buffett**（00:41:33）：Well, at the time we do every deal, I think it's smart. And then sometimes — (laughs) — I find out otherwise as we go along. The deal, that Jay knows, but might be unfamiliar to many people, is that AIG transferred to us the liability for 80 percent of 25 billion — excess — of 25 billion. In other words, they had to pay the first 25 billion. And then on the next 25 billion, we had to pay 80 percent of what they paid up to a limit of 20 billion, 80 percent of 25. And we got paid $10.2 billion for that. And we had — and this applies to their losses in many classes of business written — or earned — before December 31st, 2015. So Ajit Jain, who has made a lot more money for Berkshire than I — for you — than I have, but he evaluates that sort of transaction. We talk about it a fair amount ourselves. I just find it interesting. I particularly find the 10.2 billion that they're going to give us interesting. And the — we come to the conclusion that we think we'll do well by getting 10.2 billion today with a maximum payout of 20 billion over some — I mean, between now and judgment day — on this large piece of business. AIG had very good reasons for doing this, because their reserves had been under criticism. And this essentially — probably — and should have, I think — put to bed the question of whether they were underreserved on that business. And we get the 10.2 billion. And the question is how fast we pay out the money and how much money we pay out. And Ajit does 99 percent of the thinking on that. And I do one percent. And we project out what we think will happen. And we know whatever our projection is, that it will be wrong, but we try to be conservative. And we've done a fair amount of these deals. This is the largest. The second largest was a creature that was formed out of Lloyd's of London some years ago. And we've been wrong on one transaction that involved something over a billion of premium. I mean clearly wrong. And there are a couple of others that may or may not work out depending on what you assume we have earned on the funds. But they're OK. But they probably didn't come out as well as we thought they would, though. But overall, we've done OK on this. It's less OK when we're sitting around with 90-plus billion of cash. So the incremental 10.2 billion we took in in the first quarter is earning us peanuts at the moment. And peanuts is not what fits into the formula for making this an attractive deal. So we have — we do have to assume we'll find uses of the money, but the money will be with us quite a while. And I think our calculations are on the conservative side. They are not the identical calculations that AIG makes. I mean, we come up with our own estimate of payouts and all of that. And I think it — actually, I think it was quite a good transaction from AIG's standpoint. Because they did take 20 billion of potential losses off for 10.2 billion. And I think they satisfied the investing community that they were quite unlikely to have adverse development in the period prior to 2015 that was not accounted for by this transaction. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:50）：Well, I think it's intrinsically a dangerous kind of activity. And — but that's one of its attractions. I don't think there are any two people in the world that are better at this kind of transaction than Ajit and Warren. And nobody else has had the experience we've had. Just get me in a lot more of those businesses and I'll accept a little extra worry.

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:16）：There's one thing I should mention, too, that we actually were the only insurance operation in the world that would write that sort of a contract and that — where it would be satisfactory to the other party. I mean, when somebody hands you $10.2 billion and says, "I'm counting on you to pay 20 billion back, even if it's 50 years from now, on the last dollar," there are very few people that they'd want to hand 10.2 billion to. And there — So it's a — there's limited people on the other side. I mean, there's not that many people remotely that have that kind of size deal. But —

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:53）："Very few" is a good expression. He means "one." (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:56）：Yeah.
