## 问题

伯克希尔过去买轻资本、高现金流的生意，如今买的是低增长、重资本的生意。若继续投轻资本公司，股东是否会更划算？

## 回答要点

1. 买高资产回报、几乎不占用资本又能增长的生意，远胜于买需要大量资本才能增长的公司
2. 美国市值前五的公司合计逾 2.5 万亿美元，约占全美股市市值 10%，而它们本质上可以在零股权资本下运营
3. 这与卡内基建钢厂、洛克菲勒建炼厂的年代完全不同——那时增长必须靠大量再投资与股权资本
4. 「那个世界真的变了，而我认为人们没有充分意识到这个差别」；这一趋势远未结束
5. 伯克希尔也拥有几家资本回报极高但不增长的公司，照样喜欢；只是买不到能看懂又会增长的同类
6. 芒格以美国化工业为例：陶氏、杜邦当年二十几倍市盈率、看起来拥有世界，如今大宗商品化、已成苦生意

## 关键引述

> **“you could run them with no equity capital at all”**
>
> 「它们本质上可以在完全没有股权资本的情况下运营。」

> **“That world has really changed, and I don't think people quite appreciate the difference.”**

> **“A few are going to win big in it.”**
>
> 「只有少数人能大赢。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:02）：好，第六站。

**Audience Member**（01:50:06）：早上好。我是悉尼的马库斯·伯恩斯。巴菲特先生，我的问题是：你过去买的是轻资本、高现金流的生意，现在买的却是低增长、重资本的生意。我理解伯克希尔能产生大量现金流，但如果你继续投资轻资本公司，股东是不是会更划算？

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:29）：嗯，我们当然愿意找那样的公司。毫无疑问，买一家高资产回报、几乎不占用资本又能增长的生意，远胜于买一家需要大量资本才能增长的公司。这每天都不一样，但我相信（而且我认为这一点被低估了），美国市值最大的五家公司，某几天我们在其中、某几天不在。假设某天我们不在，它们市值合计仍超过 2.5 万亿美元。这是个巨大的数字，大概接近整个美国股市总市值的 10%。而这五家公司，本质上可以在完全没有股权资本的情况下运营。一分钱都不需要。这跟安德鲁·卡内基建钢厂、用利润再建一座钢厂、并在此过程中变得极其富有的世界完全不同；跟洛克菲勒建炼厂、买油罐车也是一回事。总的来说，在资本主义的漫长历史里，要增长、要赚大钱，就需要大量再投资、需要大量股权资本，铁路就是好例子。

那个世界真的变了，而我认为人们没有充分意识到这个差别。你确实不需要任何钱，就能运营这五家合计市值超过 2.5 万亿美元的公司；而它们已经把三四十年前《财富》500 强榜上那些熟悉的名字（埃克森、通用汽车，随便你说），远远甩在后面。所以我们当然愿意——毫无疑问，一门不占用资本、还能增长、所需股权资本回报近乎无限的生意，是理想生意。我们拥有几家资本回报极高的公司，但它们不增长。我们照样喜欢它们，但如果它们处在能增长的领域，相信我，它们会是我们名单上的第一号。只是我们看不到能买、又能看懂的这类标的。

但你说得完全对：这样部署资金，比我们买重资本生意要好得多、好得多、好得多。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:53:36）：对。美国化工公司一度是极好的投资。陶氏和杜邦的市盈率二十几倍，它们不断建越来越复杂的工厂、雇越来越多的化学博士，看起来像是拥有了世界。现在，大多数化工产品某种程度上大宗商品化了，做大化工生产商是苦生意。而像苹果、谷歌这些新玩家冒出来，正站在世界之巅。我认为提问者说得基本对：世界变了很多，而那些做出正确决定、进入这些与旧生意如此不同的新领域的人，做得非常好。

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:26）：对。安德鲁·梅隆要是看到今天的高市值公司，会完全摸不着头脑。凭空创造出几千亿美元的价值，而几乎没有资产——没有有形资产——

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:47）：而且很快。

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:48）：而且很快，对。但世界就是这样。谷歌卖给你一次点击，GEICO 就要付 11 美元之类——这跟花好几年找矿址、开发铁矿供应钢厂、修铁路把铁矿石运到产钢地、再建分销点，完全不是一回事。我们最初审视的那个世界，美国的资本主义体系基本建立在有形资产、再投资之上，伴随着大量创新与发明。但如果你恰好擅长，能不靠资本就建起几千亿美元市值，这要好太多。这是与过去不同的世界，而且我认为这个世界很可能会延续下去。趋势远没有结束。

**Charlie Munger**（01:56:01）：现在风险投资领域里拼命追这类机会的人，很多正在亏大钱。这是个美妙的领域，但不是每个人都能大赢。只有少数人能大赢。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:02）：OK. Station 6.

**Audience Member**（01:50:06）：Good morning. It's Marcus Burns from Sydney, Australia. My question, Mr. Buffett, is, you used to buy capital-light, cash-generative businesses, but now buy lower-growth, capital-consumptive businesses. I realize Berkshire generates a lot of cash flow, but would shareholders have been better off if you had continued to invest in capital-light companies?

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:29）：Well, we'd love to find them. I mean, there's no question that buying a high-return-on-assets, very light-capital-intensive business that's going to grow beats the hell out of buying something that requires a lot of capital to grow. And this varies from day to day, but I believe — and I don't think it's sufficiently appreciated. I believe that probably the five largest American companies by market cap — and some days we're in that group and some days we aren't — let's assume we're not in that group on a given day — they have a market value of over $2 1/2 trillion, and that 2 1/2 trillion is a big number. I don't know whether the aggregate market cap of the U.S. market is, but that's probably getting up close to 10 percent of the whole market cap of the United States. And if you take those five companies, essentially, you could run them with no equity capital at all. None. That is a very different world than when Andrew Carnegie was building a steel mill and then using the earnings to build another steel mill and getting very rich in the process, or Rockefeller was building refineries and buying tank cars and everything. Generally speaking, over — for a very long time in our capitalism, growing and earning large amounts of money required considerable reinvestment of capital and large amounts of equity capital, the railroads being a good example. That world has really changed, and I don't think people quite appreciate the difference. You literally don't need any money to run the five companies that are worth collectively more than $2 1/2 trillion, and who have outpaced any number of those names that were familiar, if you looked at the Fortune 500 list 30 or 40 years ago, you know, whether it was Exxon or General Motors or you name it. So we would love — I mean, there's no question that a business that doesn't take any capital and grows and has, you know, almost infinite returns on required equity capital, is the ideal business. And we own a couple of businesses — a few businesses — that earn extraordinary returns on capital, but they don't grow. We still love them, but if they had — if they were in fields that would grow, believe me, we wouldn't — you know, they would be number one on our list. We aren't seeing those that we can buy and that we understand well. But you are absolutely right that that's a far, far, far better way of laying out money than what we're able to do when buying capital-intensive businesses. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:53:36）：Yeah. The chemical companies of America, at one time, were wonderful investments. Dow and DuPont sold at 20-some times earnings, and they kept building more and more complicated plants and hiring more Ph.D. chemists, and it looked like they owned the world. Now, most chemical products are sort of commoditized and it's a tough business being a big chemical producer. And in comes all these other people like Apple and Google and they're just on top of the world. I think the questioner's basically right that the world has changed a lot, and that the people who have made the right decisions in getting into these new businesses that are so different from the old ones have done very well.

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:26）：Yeah, Andrew Mellon would be absolutely baffled by looking at the high-cap companies now. I mean, the idea that you could create hundreds of billions of value essentially without assets — without tangible assets —

**Charlie Munger**（01:54:47）：Fast.

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:48）：Fast, yeah. But that is the world. I mean, there is — When Google can sell you something that — where GEICO was paying 11 bucks or something every time somebody clicked something — that is a lot different than spending years finding the right site and developing, you know, iron mines to supply the steel plants and, you know, railroads to haul the iron to where the steel is produced and distribution points, and all that sort of thing. Our world was built — you know, when we first looked at it, our U.S. — our capitalist system, basically, was built on tangible assets, and reinvestment, and all that sort of thing, and a lot of innovation and invention to go with it. But this is so much better, if you happen to be good at it, to essentially be able to build hundreds of billions of market value without really needing any capital. That is a different world than existed in the past. And I think, listen, I think it's a world that is likely to continue. I mean, the trend is, I don't think the trend in that direction is over by a long shot.

**Charlie Munger**（01:56:01）：A lot of the people who are chasing that sort of thing very hard now in the venture capital field are losing a lot of money. It's a wonderful field, but not everybody's going to win big in it. A few are going to win big in it.
