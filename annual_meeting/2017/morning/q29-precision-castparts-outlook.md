## 问题

精密铸件是伯克希尔史上第二大收购，但 2016 年报信息披露有限。它目前经营如何？前景中最让人兴奋与最让人担心的是什么？除无形资产摊销外，2016 年是否还有影响利润的购买价格调整？

## 回答要点

1. 无形资产摊销每年 4 亿多美元且不可税前扣除，是唯一的大额购买价格调整；巴菲特认为经济商誉并未按此速度减损
2. 已经做了两笔补强收购，以后还会有更多——「我们有一位极出色的经理人，在航空领域的位置也很好」
3. 长期风险主要是 3D 打印能否抢走部分市场；飞机制造本身不必担心，但交付节奏相对订单储备可能大幅波动
4. 核心优势是交付可靠性：发动机要服役 20—25 年、载几百人，整机厂绝不愿因一个零件拖住 99% 完工的飞机
5. 巴菲特：「我不认为有谁的名声比马克·多尼根和这家公司更好」——指交付
6. 芒格：这是好生意、价格公允，但「不是旧日子那种便宜到尖叫的价格」；优质企业现在要付的溢价高得多
7. 承接下一章：2018 年起股票须按市值计价，GAAP 利润将更加失真——「那真的很蠢」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:54:03）：乔纳森。

**Jonathan Brandt**（02:54:07）：精密铸件是伯克希尔历史上第二大的收购。2016 年报里关于它的定性和定量信息都不多。能不能在这里更新一下它目前的情况：前景中让你兴奋的是什么，最让你担心的是什么？我还想知道，除了无形资产摊销之外，2016 年有没有像 2015 年 Van Tuyl 那样、对精密铸件利润造成负面影响的其他重大购买价格调整？最后，眼下有没有补强收购的机会？

**Warren Buffett**（02:54:44）：嗯，我们其实已经做过几笔收购，以后还会做更多合适的，因为我们有一位极出色的经理人，在航空领域的位置也很好，所以会有合理的收购机会。目前已经做了两笔，长期还会更多。无形资产摊销是唯一的大额购买价格调整，每年 4 亿多美元，而且不能税前扣除。在我心里，那就是 4 亿多美元的盈利。我不认为精密铸件的经济商誉每年会以这个速度减损。这一点我在一定程度上解释过。作为一门极长期的生意，你可以担心 3D 打印。我不认为需要担心飞机制造本身。但飞机的交付节奏，相对于任何给定的订单储备，在多数情况下都可能大幅波动。

所以交付量可能相当不稳定，但长期需求我不担心。真正的问题是：有没有人能做得更好或更便宜；或者像我说的，3D 打印会不会在某些方面抢走一部分市场。总体而言，我要说我对精密铸件感觉非常好。这是门极长期的生意，我们有些合同期限非常长。新机型的启动可能被推迟之类，这都正常。但你看隔壁展厅里那台发动机——如果由你来组装那台发动机，要服役 20 年、25 年，载着几百人飞，你会非常在意你的供应商。你在意的不仅是做工质量（那当然绝对在意），如果你再往上游是发动机厂商或飞机制造商，你还会非常在意交付的可靠性。因为你不想要一架飞机、一台发动机，完工 99%，却卡在某个零件有缺陷之类的问题上，迟迟交不出去。可靠性极其重要。

而在交付这件事上，我不认为有谁的名声比马克·多尼根和这家公司更好。所以我非常喜欢买下精密铸件这件事。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:58:10）：嗯，还有一点很有意思：这是一门很好的生意，以公允的价格买下——但这已经不是旧日子那种便宜到尖叫的价格了。

**Warren Buffett**（02:58:23）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（02:58:23）：对优质企业，现在要付的溢价比起从前高得多。

**Warren Buffett**（02:58:29）：对，完全属实。你拿不到便宜价。顺便说，那 4 亿多美元还会持续相当长时间。我们会在年报里解释，就像我们会解释铁路的折旧计提其实不够一样。会计就是这么运作的。

**Warren Buffett**（02:58:45）：还有，从明年 1 月 1 日起（这件事我都不太想讲），会计处理对伯克希尔和其他公司来说要变成一场噩梦，因为他们要求我们把持有的股票按市值计价，好像我们是华尔街的交易公司一样。可口可乐、美国运通等等的价值变动，每个季度都要走利润表。按这套理论，其实是每天都在走，所以真的会让人看不明白。我们的职责是把事情解释清楚，让你们在看到 GAAP 利润时不至于迷惑。但按这套规则报出来的 GAAP 利润，如果只看最下面那一行，会比现在更加毫无意义。而且——

**Charlie Munger**（02:59:38）：那未必是个好主意。

**Warren Buffett**（02:59:42）：不，我认为这是个糟糕的主意，但我们得应付。我的职责是解释 GAAP 会计在多大程度上有助于你们评估伯克希尔，以及它在什么时候会扭曲事实。会计本来就不该用来描述价值。反过来说，如果你在分析企业，只要真正读懂它，它是估算价值时极其有用的工具。所以你不能怪审计行业去做他们认为该做的事——他们的工作不是呈现价值。尽管用了这些市场价值——

**Charlie Munger**（03:00:24）：但你可以怪审计——

**Warren Buffett**（03:00:25）：什么？

**Charlie Munger**（03:00:26）：这件事你可以怪审计行业。

**Warren Buffett**（03:00:28）：好吧。

**Charlie Munger**（03:00:28）：那真的很蠢。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（03:00:33）：嗯，这一点我同意。（笑）但我们会尽最大努力——我们永远会给你们审计过的数字，然后解释它们在两个方向上的缺陷，以及看这些数字、据此判断你持有资产的价值时，哪些该用、哪些大概该忽略。我会像我给姐妹或任何人解释那样给你们解释——我们想让你们明白自己拥有什么。凡是这方面真正重要的细节，我们都会覆盖。对于一个市值 4000 亿美元的公司，你能谈一百万件只算次要的事；但如果是查理和我在讨论这家公司，那些数字和解读才是我们估算企业价值时认为重要的。不过这也难免——你不能怪媒体，他们每个季度只有几段话要写伯克希尔的利润。

但如果他们只看最下面那一行，今年还算可笑的东西，明年会因为 2018 年生效的新规变得彻底荒诞。

**Jonathan Brandt**（02:54:07）：Precision Castparts represents the second largest acquisition Berkshire has ever made. There wasn't much qualitative or quantitative information about it in the 2016 annual. Would you be willing to update us here with how it is doing currently, what excites you about its prospects, and what worries you most about it? I'm also curious if there were any meaningful purchase price adjustments beyond intangible amortization that negatively impacted Precision's earnings in 2016, as was the case with Van Tuyl in 2015? And finally, are there any opportunities in sight for bolt-on acquisitions?

**Warren Buffett**（02:54:44）：Yeah, we've actually made acquisitions, and we will make more that fit there, because we've got an extraordinary manager. And we've got a terrific position in the aircraft field. So there will be sensible — there will be the chance for sensible acquisitions. And we've already made two, anyway. And we will make more over time. The — it's — The amortization of intangibles is the only big purchase price adjustment. That's something over $400 million a year, nondeductible. In my mind, that's 400-and-some million of earnings. I do not regard the economic goodwill of Precision Castparts being diminished at that rate annually. That is a — and, you know, I've explained that in some degree. The — As a very long-term business, you can worry about 3-D printing. I don't think you have to worry about aircrafts being manufactured. But aircraft deliveries can be substantially altered in relation to any given backlog in most cases. So the deliveries can be fairly volatile, but I don't think the long-term demand is anything I worry about. And the question is, whether anybody can do it better or cheaper, or like I say, whether 3-D printing at least takes away part of the field in some respects. But overall, I would tell you I feel very good about Precision Castparts. It is a very long-term business. I mean, we have contracts that run for a very long time, and like I say, the initiation of a new plane may be delayed or something of the sort. But if you take a look at the engine that's in the other adjoining room here and in our exhibition hall, you would, if you were putting that engine together for the 20 or 25-year life or whatever it may have, carrying hundreds of people, you would care very much about your supplier. And you'd care not only in the quality, you know — which would be, absolutely you'd care — of the work being done. But you also, if you were an engine manufacturer or an aircraft manufacturer further down the line, you would care very much about the reliability of delivery on something. Because you do not want a plane that — or an engine — that's 99 percent complete while somebody's dealing with a problem of faulty parts or anything else that would delay delivery. So I love the fact we bought Precision Castparts. Charlie?

**Warren Buffett**（02:58:23）：No.

**Warren Buffett**（02:58:29）：Yeah, that's absolutely true, and we — you don't get a bargain price. The 400-plus million incidentally, you know, goes on for quite a while, too. **Warren Buffett**（02:58:45）：And starting — I don't even want to tell you about this one — but starting the first of next year, accounting is going to become sort of a nightmare in terms of Berkshire and other companies because they're going to have us mark our equities to market just like we were a Wall Street trading firm or something. And those changes in the value of Coca-Cola, or American Express, or everything, are going to run through the income account every quarter. In fact, they run through it every day in this theory, so that it really will get confusing. Now, it's our job to explain things so that you aren't confused when we report GAAP earnings, but GAAP earnings, as reported, will become even more meaningless, if looking only at the bottom line, than they are now, and —

**Charlie Munger**（03:00:24）：But you can blame the audit —

**Charlie Munger**（03:00:26）：You can blame the audit profession for that one.

**Charlie Munger**（03:00:28）：That was really stupid. (Laughter)

And I'll explain it to you the same way I would explain it to my sisters or anybody else that — you know, we want you to understand what you own.
