## 问题

多年回避科技股之后，巴菲特先买 IBM、后买苹果，上周五又说 IBM 未达预期并卖掉三分之一持仓。他看 IBM 与苹果的方式有何不同？在投资科技公司上又学到了什么？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特承认六年前开始买 IBM 时，预期它这六年会表现更好，判断错了
2. 苹果更像消费品公司，分析护城河要看消费者行为；IBM 要看企业客户，是两类不同的决策
3. 芒格：回避科技股是因为「不跑到别人更强的地方去打球」；回头看最大的错误是看懂了谷歌却没买
4. GEICO 早期每次点击付给谷歌 10 到 11 美元，LASIK 类关键词约 60 到 70 美元且无增量成本，巴菲特近距离看到却没下手
5. 芒格补充「我们也搞砸了沃尔玛——十拿九稳，看懂了却没做」；巴菲特：「看懂了，执行才算数」
6. 巴菲特称贝索斯同时在零售与云计算两个行业从零建起两台经济机器，是罕见的商业成就；伯克希尔「一股亚马逊都没拥有过」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:35）：安德鲁·罗斯·索金。

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（00:50:38）：早上好，沃伦。这个问题来自一位老股东，我得说一下，他昨晚在希尔顿酒店大堂拦住我，问了这个。「沃伦，多年来你一直远离科技公司，说它们太难预测、没有护城河。后来你投了 IBM，似乎改变了对科技的看法；最近又投了苹果。可上周五你说 IBM 没达到你的预期，卖掉了我持仓的三分之一。你看 IBM 和苹果的方式不同吗？在投资科技公司这件事上，你学到了什么？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:51:13）：嗯，我确实把它们看得很不一样。但很明显，六年前我开始买 IBM 的时候，我以为它在这六年里会表现得比实际更好。而苹果，我认为它处在完全不同的生意里。从分析护城河、分析消费者行为这些角度看，苹果更像一家消费品公司。它当然是个内含各种技术的产品，但如果要预测它未来的客户会做什么——这与 IBM 的客户不同——是另一类分析。这不等于我判断正确，时间会给出答案。但这是两类不同的决策。第一笔我错了，第二笔对不对，我们以后会知道。我不认为它们是可以直接类比的，也不完全是风马牛不相及，大概介于两者之间。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:52:30）：嗯，我们避开科技股，是因为我们觉得在那里没有优势，而别人有。我认为不跑到别人更强的地方去打球，是个好主意。但你要是问我，回头看我们在科技领域最大的错误是什么，我认为我们当年聪明到足以看懂谷歌。早期那些广告的效果比别的方式好太多。所以我要说，那件事上我们辜负了你们：我们看得懂，却没有做。这种事我们也常干。

**Warren Buffett**（00:53:04）：对。比如我们很早就通过 GEICO 成了他们的客户。我看到——这些数字早就过时了，但我记得我们当时每次点击付给他们 10 到 11 美元。每次有人点一下那个小东西，你就能收 10 块钱、11 块钱，而你自己几乎没有成本——这是门好生意，除非有人把它抢走。所以我们是近距离看着它的威力。顺便说，如果你们今晚在酒店房间里没事干，就一直点 Progressive 什么的——（笑）别真这么干。（笑）这念头只是刚好闪过我脑子。（笑）你几乎从没见过这样的生意——做 LASIK 近视手术这类关键词，我记得每次点击是 60 到 70 美元，没有任何增量成本。

而且我认识那些人：招股书是他们自己设计的，他们来找过我，是在他们上市后不久，比伯克希尔上市还晚一点。所以我有大把机会提问、让自己搞明白。但我把它搞砸了（笑）。

**Charlie Munger**（00:54:28）：我们也搞砸了沃尔玛。那本来是十拿九稳的事，我们聪明到看懂了，却没有做。

**Warren Buffett**（00:54:36）：对。看懂了——执行才算数。所以（笑）……

而且我在 IBM 上可能犯了两个错误。在我看来，预测各类业务里谁会赢、云计算这类领域会进入多激烈的价格竞争，都更难。前几天我说过一句话，确实很了不起，我还问查理能不能想到类似的例子：一个人几乎同时地在两个相当不同的行业里，建起了两台非凡的经济机器——

**Charlie Munger**（00:55:33）：从零起步。

**Warren Buffett**（00:55:34）：从零起步，而对手带着大量资本和其他一切。在零售业做到，又在云计算做到，像杰夫·贝索斯这样——梅隆家族那类人投资过很多不同行业，但他是两家从零起步的公司的实际 CEO。安迪·格鲁夫在英特尔常说：「设想你有一颗银弹，可以射掉一个竞争对手，你会射谁？」我想，无论在云计算还是零售，都有很多人会把那颗银弹瞄准杰夫。他做的事是另一种玩法，但在《华盛顿邮报》，他这把牌打得大概没人能更好。所以这是了不起的商业成就：他亲自参与执行，不只是出钱，做成了两家大概最让对手忌惮的公司。查理，你还有补充吗？

**Charlie Munger**（00:56:53）：嗯，我们有点像梅隆家族，是老派的、干得还行的人。而杰夫·贝索斯是另一个物种。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:05）：顺便说，我们完全错过了。我们从来没拥有过一股亚马逊。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:50:35）：Andrew Ross Sorkin.

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（00:50:38）：Good morning, Warren. This question comes from a long-time shareholder who I should tell you accosted me last night in the lobby of the Hilton Hotel with this question. "Warren, for years, you stayed away from technology companies, saying they were too hard to predict and didn't have moats. Then you seemed to change your view about technology when you invested in IBM, and again when you recently invested in Apple. "But then on Friday you said IBM had not met your expectations and sold a third of our stake. "Do you view IBM and Apple differently? And what have you learned about investing in technology companies?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:51:13）：Well, I do view them differently. But, you know, obviously, when I bought the IBM — started buying it six years ago — I thought it would do better in the six years that have elapsed than it has. And Apple — I regard them as being in quite different businesses. I think Apple is much more of a consumer products business, in terms of the — in terms of sort of analyzing moats around it, and consumer behavior, and all that sort of thing. It's obviously a product with all kinds of tech built into it. But in terms of laying out what their prospective customers will do in the future, as opposed to, say, IBM's customers, it's a different sort of analysis. That doesn't mean it's correct. And we'll find out over time. But they are two different types of decisions. And I was wrong on the first one, and we'll find out whether I'm right or wrong on the second. But I do not regard them as apples and apples, and I don't quite regard them as apples and oranges, but they're — it's somewhat in between on that. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:52:30）：Well, we avoided the tech stocks, because we felt we had no advantage there and other people did. And I think that's a good idea not to play where the other people are better. But, you know, if you ask me, in retrospect, what was our worst mistake in the tech field, I think we were smart enough to figure out Google. Those ads worked so much better in the early days than anything else. So I would say that we failed you there. And we were smart enough to do it and didn't do it. We do that all the time, too.

**Warren Buffett**（00:53:04）：Yeah. We were their customer very early on with GEICO, for example. And we saw — I don't — these figures are way out of date, but I — as I remember, you know, we were paying them 10 or 11 dollars a click or something like that. And any time you're paying somebody 10 or 11 bucks every time somebody just punches a little thing where you've got no cost at all, you know, that's a good business, unless somebody's going to take it away from you. And so we were close up, seeing the impact of that. And incidentally, if any of you don't have anything to do in your hotel rooms tonight, just keep punching Progressive or something. And — (Laughter) Don't really do that. (Laughter) The thought just happened to cross my mind. The — (Laughter) But, you know, that is — and you've never seen a business — almost never seen a business — like it, where — And I think for LASIK surgery and things like that, I think the figures were, you know, 60 or 70 bucks a click with no incremental — no cost. So — and I knew the guys. I mean, they actually designed their prospectus. They came to see me. And they — a little bit after the original one, when they went public, a little bit after Berkshire even. And so I had plenty of ways to ask questions or anything of the sort, educate myself. But I blew it — (laughs) — and —

**Charlie Munger**（00:54:28）：We blew Walmart, too. When it was a total cinch, we were smart enough to figure that out and we didn't.

**Warren Buffett**（00:54:36）：Yeah, figuring out — execution is what counts. So — (Laughs) Anyway, we'll — and I could be making two mistakes on IBM. I mean, the — you know, they're — they — It's harder to predict, in my view, the winners in various items, or how much price competition will enter in to something like cloud services and all of that. I will — I made a statement the other day, which it's really remarkable, and I was — I asked Charlie whether he could think of a situation like it — where one person has built an extraordinary economic machine in two really pretty different industries, you know, almost simultaneously, as has happened —

**Charlie Munger**（00:55:33）：From a standing start at zero.

**Warren Buffett**（00:55:34）：From a standing start at zero, with other — with competitors with lots of capital and everything else. To do it in retailing and to do it with the cloud, like Jeff Bezos has done, I mean, I — People like the Mellons invested in a lot of different industries and all of that. But he has been, in effect, the CEO, simultaneously, of two businesses starting from scratch that if — you know, Andy Grove used to use — at Intel — used to say, you know, "Think about if you had a silver bullet and you could shoot it at — and get rid of one of your competitors, who would it be?" Well, I think that both in the cloud and in retail, there are a lot of people that would aim that silver bullet at Jeff. And he's done — it's a different sort of game — but he's, you know, at The Washington Post, he's played that hand as well as anybody I think possibly could. So it's a remarkable business achievement, where he's been involved, actually, in the execution, not just bankrolling it, of two businesses that are probably as feared by their competitors, almost, as any you can find. It's — Charlie, you got further thoughts?

**Charlie Munger**（00:56:53）：Well, we're sort of like the Mellons, old-fashioned people who done all right. And Jeff Bezos is a different species. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:05）：And we missed it entirely, incidentally. We never owned a share of Amazon. (Laughs)
