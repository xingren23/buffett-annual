## 问题

为什么建议妻子在巴菲特去世后投资指数基金而不是伯克希尔？芒格给后代的建议据说是「别蠢到去卖」。

## 回答要点

1. 妻子不会卖出任何伯克希尔去换指数基金——巴菲特名下每一股伯克希尔都将捐给慈善，已分配约 40%
2. 选择标准不是收益最大化，而是「让钱不成为问题」：不再有人上门劝她卖掉、改做别的
3. 持有标普指数基金几乎没有可能不够用；另留一笔现金应对股市暴跌或交易所停摆
4. 奥马哈的凯蒂姑妈因持有伯克希尔，97 岁时身家数亿，却每四五个月写信问「我会不会把钱花光」
5. 巴菲特：这不是收益问题，是让一个非专业投资者避免被各种建议包围的问题
6. 芒格立场不同：「芒格家不一样，我要他们继续持有伯克希尔」，但承认标普那套算法很难战胜

## 关键引述

> **“The important thing is that she never worries about money the rest of her life.”**
>
> 「重要的是，她余生不必为钱操心。」

> **“I want them to hold the Berkshire.”**
>
> 「我要他们继续持有伯克希尔。」

> **“absent something happening with weapons of mass destruction”**

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:53）：贝基？

**Becky Quick**（01:25:57）：我想问一个可以接着卡罗尔那个问题往下问的问题。查理在回答里说，他认为伯克希尔整体上会比标普 500 表现更好。亚拉巴马州伯明翰的股东克拉克·卡梅伦（音）持有 281 股伯克希尔 B 股，他来信问：「为什么你建议妻子在你去世后投资指数基金，而不是伯克希尔·哈撒韦？我相信芒格给后代的建议是『别蠢到去卖』。」

**Warren Buffett**（01:26:26）：对。（笑）她不会卖掉任何伯克希尔去换指数基金。我所有的伯克希尔股票，每一股，都会捐给慈善。所以我根本不把自己看作伯克希尔的所有者（掌声），它已经承诺出去了，到目前为止大约 40% 已经分配。所以问题是：一个不是投资专业人士的人（我希望到遗产清算时她已经相当高龄），最好的投资是什么？所谓最好，是指任何形式的担忧都最少，最少有人跑来跟她说「你为什么不卖掉这个、去做点别的」之类。她的钱会超过她所需。那么最重要的，就是让钱不成为问题。如果她持有标普指数基金，几乎没有任何可能（除非发生大规模杀伤性武器之类的事）让她不够用。她还会留一点现金，这样万一某天股市暴跌、交易所关门一段时间之类，她仍然觉得自己钱很宽裕。

目标不是收益最大化：她拿到的钱翻一倍还是翻两倍，没有任何区别。重要的是她余生不必为钱操心。奥马哈有位凯蒂姑妈，查理很熟，她丈夫跟我都在她那儿工作过。她一辈子非常辛苦，一辈子住在 45 街和希科里街那栋房子，我记得当初只花了 8000 美元。因为持有伯克希尔，她活到 97 岁，最后有了几亿美元。（笑）她每四五个月给我写一封信，说：「亲爱的沃伦，我不愿打扰你，但我会不会把钱花光？」（笑）我回信说：「亲爱的凯蒂，这问题问得好，因为如果你活 986 年，你确实会花光。」（笑）然后四五个月后，她又写来同样一封信。我见过太多次：如果你有很多钱，它绝不该成为你生活里的负数。

而且一旦你有钱，就有人带着各种建议来找你，有时是好意，有时不是。所以如果你手上是这样一笔必然能交付的东西，而且假如全在伯克希尔，他们会说「要是沃伦今天还在，他会让你这么干」。我不想让任何人经历这些。至于标普，我认为我建议的，正是很高比例的人都该做的事。我也不认为持有单只股票的人能有同样的安心；他们身边有邻居、朋友、亲戚想去做点别的，像我说的，有时是好意，有时不是。所以我认为这是个能取得好结果、而且大概率能坚持住的政策。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:10）：嗯，正如贝基说的，芒格家不一样。我要他们继续持有伯克希尔。

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:16）：我也想继续持有伯克希尔。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:23）：不，我是说我不喜欢那个——我承认这个逻辑：标普那套算法很难战胜，一篮子大公司的分散组合，对大多数人来说几乎不可能超越。但我拿着伯克希尔就是更自在。

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:44）：嗯，这是家族生意。

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:48）：对。

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:49）：对。但我见过太多人，尤其是上了年纪之后，容易受影响，只能听任别人上门来游说。

**Charlie Munger**（01:31:00）：嗯，如果你要替继承人挡住别人的愚蠢，也许你需要一套好制度。不过我对这个话题没什么兴趣。

**Warren Buffett**（01:31:08）：好。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:53）：Becky?

**Becky Quick**（01:25:57）：I'd like to ask a question that can serve as a follow-up to the question that Carol had asked. And Charlie, in that response, said that he thinks that Berkshire's businesses on the whole will do better than the S&P 500. Clark Cameron (PH) from Birmingham, Alabama, who owns 281 shares of Berkshire B, writes in and asks, "Why have you advised your wife to invest in index funds after your death rather than Berkshire Hathaway? I believe Munger has counseled his offspring to quote, 'Not be so dumb as to sell.'"

**Warren Buffett**（01:26:26）：Yeah. (Laughter) She won't be selling any Berkshire to buy the index funds. All of my Berkshire, every single share, will go to philanthropy. So the — I don't even regard myself as owning Berkshire, you know, basically — (applause) — it's committed. And so far, about 40 percent has already been distributed. So the question is, somebody who is not an investment professional will be, I hope, reasonably elderly by the time that the estate gets settled. And what is the best investment, meaning one that there would be less worry of any kind connected with and less people coming around and saying, "Why don't you sell this and do something else?" and all those things. She's going to have more money than she needs. And the big thing, then, you want is money not to be a problem. And there will be no way that if she holds the S&P — or virtually no way, absent something happening with weapons of mass destruction — but virtually no way that she won't — she'll have all the money that she possibly can use. She'll have a little liquid money so that if stocks are down tremendously at some point, there's — they close the stock exchange for a while, anything like that — she'll still feel that she's got plenty of money. And the object is not to maximize. It doesn't make any difference whether the amount she gets doubles or triples or anything of the sort. The important thing is that she never worries about money the rest of her life. And I had an Aunt Katie here in Omaha, who Charlie knew well, and worked for her husband, as did I. And she worked very hard all her life. And had lived in a house she'd paid, I think, I don't know, $8,000 for at 45th and Hickory all her life. And because she was in Berkshire, she ended up — she lived to 97 — she ended up with, you know, a few hundred million. (Laughter) And she would write me a letter every four or five months. And she said, "Dear Warren, you know, I hate to bother you. But am I going to run out of money?" And — (laughter) — I would write her back. And I'd say, "Dear Katie, it's a good question because, if you live 986 years, you're going to run out of money." And — (laughter) — then about four or five months later, she'd write me the same letter again. And I have seen there's no way in the world, if you've got plenty of money, that it should become a minus in your life. And there will be people, if you've got a lot of money, that come around with various suggestions for you, sometimes well-meaning, sometimes not so well-meaning. So if you've got something as certain to deliver — you know, it was all in Berkshire, they'd say, "Well, if Warren was alive today, you know, he would be telling you to do this." I just don't want anybody to go through that. And the S&P will be a — I think actually what I'm suggesting is what — a very high percentage of people should do something like that. And I don't think they will have as — I think there's a chance they won't have as much peace of mind if they own one stock. And they've got neighbors and friends and relatives that are trying to do some — like I say, sometimes well-intentioned, sometimes otherwise, to do something else. And so I think it's a policy that'll get a good result and is likely to stick. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:10）：Well, as Becky said, the Mungers are different. I want them to hold the Berkshire.

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:16）：Well, I want to hold the Berkshire, too. (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:23）：No, but I mean I don't like the — I recognize the logic of the fact that that S&P algorithm is very hard to beat. You know, diversified portfolio of big companies. It's all but impossible for most people. But, you know, it's — I'm just more comfortable with the Berkshire.

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:44）：Well, it's the family business.

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:48）：Yeah.

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:49）：Yeah. But it — I've just — I've seen too many people as they get older, particularly, being susceptible and just having to listen to the arguments of people coming along.

**Charlie Munger**（01:31:00）：Well, if you're going to protect your heirs from the stupidity of others, you may have some good system. But I'm not much interested in that subject.

**Warren Buffett**（01:31:08）：OK. (Laughter)
