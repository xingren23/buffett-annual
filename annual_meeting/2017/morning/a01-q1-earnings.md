## 背景

年会开场后，巴菲特用五分钟讲了一季度业绩，重点不在数字本身，而在三件事：已实现投资损益为什么毫无意义、GEICO 逆势抢新保单的算盘、以及浮存金又多了 140 亿美元。这一段是理解后文「净利润将变得毫无用处」与「900 多亿现金」两处讨论的起点。

## 要点

1. 3 月 31 日账上未实现收益超过 900 亿美元，「想报任何数字都能报出来」，所以已实现损益本身说明不了什么
2. 买卖时点只与标的内在价值有关，伯克希尔从不做盈利预测
3. 今年对「宁可实现亏损、不实现收益」的偏好略微加强：收益按 35% 征税，亏损也按 35% 抵税；若税率将下调，今年的亏损抵税价值更高
4. 一季度承保是业绩的摆动项；具体细节要看 10-Q，季度摘要「并没有触及估值的主要要点」
5. GEICO 前四个月净增 70 万张保单，是巴菲特记忆中最高的数字，去年约 30 万；两家主要竞争对手公开表示收缩新业务，因为首年新业务必然亏损
6. 首年业务的赔付率比续保业务高近 10 个百分点，加上获客成本，「写大量新业务当年一定亏钱」——而这正合伯克希尔之意
7. 浮存金同比增加 160 亿美元，其中 140 亿来自一季度；现金及国债已超过 900 亿美元

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应。

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:54）：我们的业绩报告昨天发布了。正如我们一再解释的，任何一期的已实现投资损益其实都毫无意义。如果我们想，可以兑现大量收益；也可以兑现大量亏损。但我们根本不考虑操作的时点，只考虑我们所买或所卖资产的内在价值。我们不做盈利预测。3 月 31 日，我们账上有 900 多亿美元未实现净收益。所以，如果我们想把资本利得算进利润，几乎任何你能想到的数字我们都报得出来。

因此在一季度——我得说，如果其他条件相同，我们今年有一个非常非常非常轻微的偏好，任何年份都如此，只是今年更明显一点——对于估值相同的两只证券，我们宁愿实现亏损而不是收益，因为税的效果不同。今年这一点还多强调了一分，因为我们的收益按 35% 征税，那么亏损也按 35% 抵税。而税率有可能下调，意味着今年之后亏损对我们的抵税价值会低于今年。这不是什么大事，但会形成极轻微的偏好。随着年底临近，如果——我不做任何预测——如果真有一项降低税率的法案落地，那么推迟收益、提前实现亏损可能会成为更重要的因素。

一季度的摆动项是保险承保。关于这一点，10-Q 里写得更详细，你们可以在网上查到。如果你真的想认真评估我们的盈利或我们的业务，就该去读 10-Q，因为正如我们每季度都指出的，摘要报告并没有触及估值上的若干要点。

关于保险，我只提两个我很喜欢的因素。在前四个月——不是前三个月，是前四个月——GEICO 净增了 70 万张保单，这是我记忆中最高的数字。过去也许有过更大的数字，我没有逐一回溯。去年我记得大约是 30 万。这段时间对 GEICO 非常好，因为我们的几家主要竞争对手决定——而且公开说了，其中一家前几天还重申过，尽管他们后来改了政策——有意收缩新业务，因为新业务第一年必然带来显著亏损。

有获客成本。而且说来奇怪，首年业务的赔付率往往比续保业务高近 10 个百分点。所以不仅有获客成本，赔付率本身也更高。你写大量新业务，当年在这部分上一定亏钱。我们写了大量新业务，而至少两家竞争对手宣布要暂时收着点，因为他们不想承担首年亏损。这简直是给我们量身定做的，于是我们就把油门踩到底，尽量多写业务——好业务。当然，这也是有代价的。

第二个因素——它不进损益表——但一季度有一件重要的事：我们的浮存金增加了。那张幻灯片上显示，同比增加了 160 亿美元，其中 140 亿来自今年一季度。所以浮存金增加了 140 亿美元。这些年来我一直在说，浮存金要再增长会很难，我现在还是这么说。但能有 140 亿甚至更多，是件好事——这也是为什么你去看 10-Q 会发现，我们的现金及现金等价物（含短期国债）已经远超 900 亿美元。

所以我对一季度感觉很好，尽管经营利润略有下降。一个季度说明不了什么。长期看，真正重要的是我们是否在提升旗下企业的价值。我总是关心当期数字，但我总在想着未来的数字。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:54）：And our earnings report was put out yesterday. The — as we regularly explain, the realized investment gains or losses in any period really mean nothing. I mean, they — We could take a lot of gains if we wanted to. We could take a lot of losses if we wanted to. But we don't really think about the timing of what we do at all, except in relation to the intrinsic value of what we're buying or selling. We are not — We do not make earnings forecasts. And we have — on March 31st, we have over $90 billion of net unrealized gains. So if we wanted to report almost any number you can think of and count capital gains as part of the earnings, we could do it. So in the first quarter — and I would say that we have a very, very, very slight preference this year, if everything else were equal — well, it's true in any year, but it's a little more so this year — we would rather take losses than gains, because of the tax effect if two securities were equally valued. And there's probably just one touch more of emphasis on that this year, because we are taxed on gains at 35 percent, which means we also get the benefit — the tax benefit — at 35 percent of any losses we take. And I would say that there's some chance of that rate being lower, meaning that losses would have less tax value to us after this year than they would have this — after this year than this year. That is not a big deal, but it would be a very slight preference. And it may get to be more of a factor in deferring any gains, and perhaps accelerating any losses, as the year gets closer to December 31st, assuming — and I'm making no predictions about it — but assuming that there were to be a tax act that had the effect of reducing the earnings. So in the first quarter, insurance underwriting was the swing factor. And the — there's a lot more about this in our 10-Q, which you can look up on the internet. And you really, if you're seriously interested in evaluating our earnings or our businesses, you should go to the 10-Q, because the summary report, as we point out every quarter, does not really get to a number of the main points of valuation. I would just mention two factors in connection with the insurance situation, which I love. In the first four months — not the first three months — but the first four months, GEICO's had a net gain of 700,000 policy holders, and that's the highest number I can remember. There may have been a figure larger than that somewhere in the past. I did not go back and look at them all. But last year I believe that figure was like 300,000. And this has been a wonderful period for us at GEICO, because several of our major competitors have decided — and they publicly stated this — in fact one of them just reiterated it the other day — although they've now changed their policy — but they intentionally cut back on new business because new business carries with it a significant loss in the first year. There's just costs of acquiring new business. Plus the loss ratio, strangely enough, on first-year business tends to run almost 10 points higher than on renewal business. And so not only do you have acquisition costs, but you actually have a higher loss ratio. So when you write a lot of new business, you're going to lose money on that portion of the business that year. And we wrote a lot of new business, and at least two of our competitors announced that they were lightening up for a while on new business, because they did not want to pay the penalty of the first-year loss. And, of course, that's made to order for us, so we just put our foot to the floor and tried to write as much business — good business — as we can. And there are costs to that. A second factor — well, it was not a factor in the P&L — but an important event in the first quarter is that we increased our float. And on the slide, I believe it shows that year-over-year, 16 billion. Fourteen billion of that came in the first quarter of this year, so we had a $14 billion increase in float. And for some years I've been telling you it's going to be hard to increase the float at all, and I still will tell you the same thing. But it's nice to have $14 billion or more, which is one reason, if you look at our 10-Q, you will see that our cash and cash equivalents, including Treasury bills, now has come to well over 90 billion. So I think I feel very good about the first quarter, even though our operating earnings were down a little bit. One quarter means nothing. I mean, over time, what really counts is whether we're building the value of the businesses that we own. And I'm always interested in the current figures, but I'm always dreaming about the future figures.
