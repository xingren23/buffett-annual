## 问题

芒格说巴菲特最大的才华是「一台学习机器」，从不停止更新观点。过去几年他学到的最有意思的东西是什么？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特自评是「专精型」学习机器，查理吸收面宽得多，且在自己的专业领域做得一样好
2. 「当你发现自己某件事上错了，那会带来很大乐趣」——那才是真正学到东西的时刻
3. 芒格：买苹果是好迹象，「他确实在奥马哈到处跑，问能不能把孙子孙女的平板拿走」
4. 「更重要的是，我们不会把旧招数忘掉」——以波多黎各破产、欧洲只持德国国债为例
5. 巴菲特归功于菲利普·费雪《怎样选择成长股》里的「闲聊法」：跟十家煤炭公司老板聊竞争对手，比他们自己更懂行业
6. 芒格：「如果 ISCAR 早十年出现，我们绝不会买；如果精密铸件早十年出现，我们绝不会买。天哪，我们还在学习」

## 关键引述

> **“you're a learning machine”**
>
> 「一台学习机器。」

> **“the problem is not in getting the new ideas but shedding the old ones”**
>
> 「问题不在于接受新观念，而在于丢掉旧观念。」

> **“We are learning. And, my God, we're still learning.”**
>
> 「我们在学习。天哪，我们还在学习。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:23）：第六站？

**Audience Member**（01:21:28）：下午好。我叫萨莉·伯恩斯，来自澳大利亚，目前住在得克萨斯州奥斯汀。巴菲特先生，我听说芒格先生说，你最大的才华是「一台学习机器」，从不停止更新观点。过去几年你学到的最有意思的东西是什么？

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:57）：嗯，学习很有趣。我得说，查理比我更像一台学习机器。我是专精型的，而他在我的专业领域做得跟我一样好，同时对世界正在发生什么的吸收面比我宽得多。这个世界越来越迷人。当你发现自己某件事上错了，那会带来很大乐趣——那时你才真正学到，旧的想法其实不那么对，你必须适应新的。而这当然很难。我说不准会挑出哪一件。不过我认为，美国正在发生的事极其极其有意思，政治上、各种方面都是。世界展开的方式在加速。我确实享受去琢磨不仅将来会发生什么、还有现在正在发生什么。但我不认为我有什么对你特别有用的洞见。也许查理有。

**Charlie Munger**（01:23:06）：嗯，我认为买苹果的股票是沃伦的一个好迹象。（笑）他确实在奥马哈到处跑，问能不能把他孙子孙女的平板拿走。（巴菲特笑）他做了市场调研。我确实认为我们一直在学习。更重要的是，我们不会把旧招数忘掉，这非常重要。你看看那些靠印钱、撒谎之类来解决问题的。看看波多黎各。谁会想到美国的一个属地会破产？我就能预测到，因为他们行事像白痴。（笑）所以——

**Warren Buffett**（01:23:59）：而我们没有买任何波多黎各债券。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:24:03）：没有。你去欧洲看看，应该看看我们在欧洲被要求持有的政府债券组合。不仅没有希腊债券，全是德国债券。在伯克希尔，你到处都能看到有人在按常识办事。这是很大的乐趣。再把这一点跟极度机会主义结合起来——恐慌来临时能出手——就像一场需要两只手的游戏，你用两只手在打。手里有一套像样的曲目是有帮助的。沃伦，我们学到的东西真是太多了。过去十年我们做的很多事，二十年前我们不会做。

**Warren Buffett**（01:24:59）：对，确实。不过你想想——这挺有意思，我以前提过。最好的一本投资书之一，我想是 1958 年写的，我大概 1960 年读到，菲利普·费雪的《怎样选择成长股》。他讲——

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:19）：他选的那些公司最后都完蛋了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:24:48）：但书里讲了「闲聊法」的重要性和用处。那不是我从本杰明·格雷厄姆那里学到的，但时不时非常有用。当然它不能解决所有问题，还有很多更——

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:39）：我看到你在美国运通色拉油丑闻里就用过。

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:42）：对，对。

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:43）：几十年后你在苹果上还在用。

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:45）：对。在某些情况下，你只要多问问题，就能学到很多。这一点我归功于菲利普·费雪，那本书很老了。但正如查理说的，他挑选的那些「永久赢家」有些后来也衰落了。可基本思路（有时只要去问，就能学到很多东西），是成立的。我年轻时精力更充沛，如果对煤炭行业产生兴趣，我会去跟十家煤炭公司的老板谈。聊到后面，等他们放松下来、愿意说话了，我就问：「如果你必须去荒岛十年，把全家所有的钱投到你竞争对手中的一家，你会选谁，为什么？」然后再问：「如果你必须做空一家竞争对手十年、押上全家所有的钱，你选谁，为什么？」人人都爱谈自己的竞争对手。你跟十家公司这样聊完，对煤炭行业经济性的把握，可能比他们任何一个人都更准。总有办法把事弄明白。有时有用，有时没用，但有时非常有用。

至于「永远多学一点」这个态度——我在这一点上比查理专精得多。他想学一切，我只想学对伯克希尔有用的东西。（笑）但这是个对世界非常有用的态度。有人说过，问题不在于接受新观念，而在于丢掉旧观念。这话很有道理。

**Charlie Munger**（01:27:47）：如果 ISCAR 早十年出现，我们绝不会买。如果精密铸件早十年出现，我们绝不会买。我们在学习。天哪，我们还在学习。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:23）：Station 6?

**Audience Member**（01:21:28）：Good afternoon. My name Sally Burns. I'm from Australia. But I currently reside in Austin, Texas. My question, Mr. Buffett, I have heard that Mr. Munger says your greatest talent is that you're a learning machine, that you never stop updating your views. What are the most interesting things you've learned over the last few years?

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:57）：Well, it is fun to learn. I would say Charlie is much more of a learning machine than I am. I'm a specialized one, and he's a much — he does as well as I do in my specialty. And, then, he's got a much more general absorption rate than I have about what's going on in the world. But, you know, it's a world that gets more fascinating all the time. And a lot of fun can occur when you learn you were wrong on something. It — you know, that's when you really learn that the old ideas really weren't so correct. And you have to adapt to new ones. And that, of course, is difficult. I don't know that I would pick out — well, I think, actually, what's going on, you know, in America is terribly, terribly interesting, you know, and politically, all kinds of things. But just the way the world's unfolding, it's moving fast. I do enjoy trying, you know, to figure out not only what's going to happen, but what's even happening now. But I don't think I've got any special insights that would be useful to you. But maybe Charlie does.

**Charlie Munger**（01:23:06）：Well, I think buying the Apple stock is a good sign in Warren. (Laughter) And he did run around Omaha and ask if he could take his grandchildren's tablets away. (Buffett laughs) And he did market research. And I do think we keep learning. And more important, we keep — we don't unlearn the old tricks. And that is really important. You look at the people who try and solve their problems by printing money and lying and so forth. Take Puerto Rico. Who would've guessed that a territory of the United States would be in bankruptcy? Well, I would've predicted it because they behave like idiots. (Laughter) And so —

**Warren Buffett**（01:23:59）：And we did not buy any Puerto Rico bonds. (Laughs)

**Charlie Munger**（01:24:03）：No. And if you go to Europe — you go to Europe, you should look at the government bond portfolios we're required to hold in Europe. There's not only no Greek bonds, they're the bonds of nobody but Germany. Everywhere you look in Berkshire, somebody is being sensible. And that is a great pleasure. And if you combine that with being very opportunistic so that when something comes along like a panic, why, it's a nice — it's like playing with two hands instead of one on a game that requires two hands. It helps to have a fair-sized repertoire. And, Warren, we've learned so damn much. There are all kinds of things we've done over the last 10 years we would not have done 20 years ago.

**Warren Buffett**（01:24:59）：Yeah. That's true, although if you take — it's interesting. I've mentioned this before. But one of the best books on investment was written, I think, in 1958. I think I read it around 1960, by Phil Fisher, called Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits. And he told —

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:19）：All the countries went — companies went to hell eventually.

**Warren Buffett**（01:24:48）：But it talked about the importance, I mean, or the usefulness of, what do you call, the "scuttlebutt method." And, you know, that was something I didn't learn from [Benjamin] Graham. But every now and then, it's turned out to be very useful. Now, it doesn't solve everything. And, I mean, there's a whole lot of more —

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:39）：I saw you do it with American Express in the Salad Oil scandal.

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:42）：Yeah, yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:43）：You're still doing at Apple, you know, decades later.

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:45）：Yeah. It — in certain cases, you actually can learn a lot just by asking a lot of questions. And I give Phil Fisher credit. That book goes back a lot of years. But as Charlie said, some of the companies he picked as winners forever did sort of peter out on him. But the basic idea, that you can learn a lot of things just by asking in some cases — I mean, I used to — I mean, if I got interested in the coal industry — just say to pick one out of the air — you know, when I was much younger, more energetic, if I went and talked to the heads of 10 coal companies and I asked each one of them — way later into the conversation, after they got feeling very — they felt like talking. And I would just, you know, I'd just say, "If you had to go away for 10 years on a desert island and you had to put all of your family's money into one of your competitors, which one would it be and why?" And then, you know, and then I'd ask them if they had to sell short one of their competitors for 10 years, all their family money, why? And they — everybody loves talking about their competitors. And if you do that with 10 different companies, you'll probably have a better fix on the economics of the coal industry than any one of those individuals has. I mean, the — it — there's ways of getting at things. And sometimes they're useful. Sometimes, they're not. But sometimes, they can be very useful. And, you know, the idea of just learning more all the time about — I'm more specialized in that by far than Charlie. I mean, he wants to learn about everything. And I just want to learn about something that'll help Berkshire. But — (laughs) — it's a very, you know, it's a very useful attitude toward — have toward — the world. And, of course, I don't know who said it. But somebody said the problem is not in getting the new ideas but shedding the old ones. And there's a lot of truth to that.

**Charlie Munger**（01:27:47）：We would never have bought ISCAR if it had come along 10 years earlier. We would never have bought Precision Castparts if it had come along 10 years earlier. We are learning. And, my God, we're still learning.
