## 问题

两个问题：一是为什么 EBITDA 不是给企业估值的好参数；二是两位在人生、家庭或商业上有没有遗憾，若有一件事可以重来会是什么？

## 回答要点

1. 遗憾只答商业部分：「我希望能更早认识查理」——从巴菲特 29 岁、芒格 35 岁开始合作，原本还有更早的机会
2. 两人曾在同一家杂货店工作过，只是不在同一时间
3. 折旧是最糟的费用：先花钱、后记费用，是「反向浮存金」，把它从倍数里剔除会误导
4. 同等条件下，买一家几乎不需固定资产投入就能赚到 X 的公司，远好于买一家要大量折旧才能赚到 X 的公司
5. 「聚焦 EBITDA 极其符合华尔街的利益」——更高的借债能力、更高的估值；巴菲特称明年可能多写一点这个话题
6. 芒格：把折旧当作非费用「不是体面的行为」；更糟的是商学院也在教，「那才是恐怖加倍」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:24）：好，我们去第十站，看能不能有所改善。（笑）

**Audience Member**（01:54:29）：你好。我叫林立军，来自上海的老虎环球基金。这是我第六年从上海来这里。我得说，沃伦、查理，你们被全球千千万万、甚至几十亿人深深尊敬和爱戴。今天我有两个问题。第一，你们在致股东信里说，EBITDA 不是给企业估值的好参数。为什么不是？能展开讲讲吗？第二，你们两位都过着非常成功、非常幸福、备受尊敬的生活。我想分别问你们：回头看，从个人角度，你们人生中有遗憾吗？如果有一件事可以重来——家庭、个人或商业上的——那是什么？非常感谢。

**Warren Buffett**（01:55:54）：嗯，个人方面的问题，我不认为你该指望我们回答。但商业上，我要说：我希望能更早认识查理。（笑）从我 29 岁、他 35 岁起，我们一直很有乐趣。但如果早很多年开始，会更有意思。我们本来有机会——我们在同一家杂货店工作过，只是不在同一时间。

**Warren Buffett**（01:56:19）：关于 EBITDA，折旧就是费用，而且是最糟糕的一种费用。我们喜欢谈浮存金——浮存金是我们先拿到钱、后付费用。折旧是你先花钱、后记费用，那是「反向浮存金」，不是好事。把它放进倍数里——在其他条件相同的情况下，买一家基本没有固定资产投入、没有折旧就能赚到 X 的公司，远好于买一家为了赚到 X 要有大量折旧的公司。我明年可能会多写一点这个话题，因为这实在是一场大众幻觉。当然，聚焦 EBITDA 极其符合华尔街的利益，因为它能带来更高的借债能力、更高的估值之类。所以过去 20 年它变得非常流行。但它是个极具误导性的统计口径，可以被用来干非常有害的事。查理，这两件事你有补充吗？

**Charlie Munger**（01:57:39）：我认为你低估了这个话题的可怕程度，也低估了把这个词引入企业估值的那批人有多令人作呕。这就好比一个地产租赁经纪人，手上有一套一千平方英尺的新写字间要出租，却说它有 2000 英尺。那不是体面的行为。而这个词就是这样进入日常用语的。任何头脑正常的人都不会认为折旧不是费用。

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:11）：对。但这非常符合华尔街的利益。

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:18）：是的。所以——

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:20）：你——

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:20）：——他们才这么干。

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:20）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:20）：它让倍数看起来更低。

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:22）：而令人惊讶的是它竟然被接受了。不管怎样，这恰好说明人是如何使用语言、如何推销对自己有用的概念的。「2 和 20」也是同一类事。付完 2 和 20 之后真正跑赢的钱，相对所产生的费用，我向你保证，足以对这个安排构成可怕的控诉。但只要它能卖出去，它就会被卖。

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:05）：而现在商学院也在用它。那才是恐怖加倍。一批贼开始用一个词已经够糟了，当它普遍到商学院也跟着照抄，那就不是什么好结果。

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:25）：好。（掌声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:54:24）：OK. We'll move to station 10 and see if we can improve on it. (Laughter)

**Audience Member**（01:54:29）：Hi. My name is Andy Lijun Lin from Loyal Valley Innovation Capital from Shanghai. This is my sixth year from Shanghai to here. I have say — I have to say to you two, Warren and Charlie, you are highly respected and deeply loved by millions and millions, or even billions, globally. I have two questions today. First question, in your letters to shareholders you said you believe EBITDA is not a good parameter to value a business. Why it's not? Can you elaborate on that? Second question, you both have very successful and happy lives with great respect. My question is to each of you. In retrospect, from a personal standpoint, do you have regrets in life? If there is one thing you could have done differently in your life, family, personal, or business, what is it? Thank you very much.

**Warren Buffett**（01:55:54）：Yeah. I don't think you should expect us to answer that on personal. But in business, I would say I wish I'd met Charlie earlier. (Laughs) We've had a lot of fun ever since I was 29 and he was 35. But it would've been even more fun if we'd started many, many years earlier. We had a chance to. We worked in the same grocery store but not at the same time.

**Warren Buffett**（01:56:19）：In respect to EBITDA, depreciation is an expense. And it's the worst kind of an expense. You know, we love to talk about float. And float is where we get the money first and we have the expense later. Depreciation is where you spend the money first, you know, and, then, record the expense later. And it's reverse float. And it's not a good thing. And to have that enter into a multiple — it's much better to buy a business that has, everything else being equal — has no depreciation because it has, essentially, no investment and fixed assets that makes X, than it is to buy a company where there's a lot of depreciation in getting to X. And I — actually, I may write a little bit more on that next year, just because it's such a mass delusion. And, of course, it's in the interests of Wall Street, enormously, to focus on something called EBITDA because it results in higher borrowing power, higher valuations, and all of that sort of thing. So it's become very popular in the last 20 years, but I — it's a very misleading statistic that can be used in very pernicious ways. Charlie, on either one of those subjects?

**Charlie Munger**（01:57:39）：I think you've understated the horrors of the subject and the disgusting nature of the people that brought that term into the valuation of business. It was just — It would be like a leasing broker of real estate who's got a thousand square-foot new suite to be leased, and he says it's got 2,000 feet in it. That's not honorable behavior. And that's the way that term got into common usage. Nobody in his right mind would think that depreciation is not an expense.

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:11）：Yeah. It — but it's very much in the interest of Wall Street.

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:18）：Yes. That's why —

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:20）：You —

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:20）：— they did it.

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:20）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（01:58:20）：It made the multiple seem lower.

**Warren Buffett**（01:58:22）：And what's amazing is the way it's accepted, actually. But anyway, it just illustrates how people use language, you know, and sell concepts that work to their own use. And "2 and 20" has the same sort of thing. I mean, the number of people — the amount of money that's overperformed after paying 2 and 20, compared to the expenses that have been incurred, I will assure you, makes for a terrible indictment of that particular arrangement. But as long as it can get sold, it will get sold. And —

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:05）：And, now, they use it in the business schools. Now, that is horror squared. I mean — (laughter) — it's bad enough that a bunch of thieves start using a term. But when it gets so common that the business schools copy it, that is not a — that's not a good result.

**Warren Buffett**（01:59:25）：OK. (Applause)
