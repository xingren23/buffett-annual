## 问题

未来三年伯克希尔能源的风电投资预计是太阳能的七倍以上，其中多少来自 PacifiCorp 的 35 亿美元扩建计划？风电经济性是否明显优于太阳能，还是差异来自真实装机需求？

## 回答要点

1. 不存在「太阳能装机超过所需」的判断，关键是有什么项目送上门；对两类项目都有巨大胃口
2. 若那段时间只碰到太阳能项目而没有风电，照样会投，反之亦然
3. 结构性优势在于伯克希尔缴很多税，而风光项目都涉及税收因素；多数电力公司付完股息后所剩无几、税负也不显著
4. 艾奥瓦州的电价远低于州内主要竞争对手，也低于所有相邻州，并承诺多年不涨价
5. 项目来源既有自主开发，也有外部提供；「明天有人拿来 10 亿或 30 亿美元的太阳能项目，我们随时准备做」
6. 地域决定优劣：艾奥瓦风好、加州光好，但对伯克希尔而言哪里都能做

## 关键引述

> **“we've got a big appetite for wind or solar”**
>
> 「我们对风电和太阳能都有很大的胃口。」

> **“There's no specific preference between the two.”**
>
> 「两者之间没有特定偏好。」

> **“the more, the better”**
>
> 「越多越好。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:35）：好，格雷格。

**Gregg Warren**（00:56:38）：沃伦，过去五年伯克希尔能源在太阳能和风电上的投资大体相当，各投入约 47 亿美元的资本项目。按公司对 2017 到 2019 年的年末资本开支预测，未来三年风电投资预计是太阳能的七倍以上，超过 45 亿美元投向风电。想请教：这部分未来开支里有多少与 PacifiCorp 最近宣布的 35 亿美元扩建计划有关——该计划重点改造和扩充这家子公司现有的风电资产？考虑到中美能源在风电上也投入巨大，风电的经济性是否明显优于太阳能？还是说这种差异更多来自真实的装机需求，那是否意味着我们的太阳能装机远超所需？

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:34）：嗯，我们并不从「太阳能装机超过所需」这个角度看问题。真正的问题是有什么项目送上门。这些项目有的是我们自己开发的，有的是别人提供给我们的，而我们对风电和太阳能都有很大的胃口。从这些数字看，我们最近见到的风电项目更多，但我们对两者没有偏好。如果那段时间我们看到 50 亿美元有吸引力的太阳能项目、却没碰到风电，我们照样会做那 50 亿，反之亦然。所以我们对两个领域都有巨大的胃口。我们的位置特别好，因为我过去解释过：我们缴很多税，而风电和太阳能项目都涉及税收因素，我们比很多——尤其是电力公司——更能处理这些。多数电力公司付完股息后剩不下多少钱，而且往往税负并不显著。伯克希尔缴很多税，也有很多钱，所以只是项目来了算不算得过账的问题。

我们在艾奥瓦非常幸运，找到很多合理的项目，结果我们的电价远低于该州的主要竞争对手，也低于任何与我们相邻的州。我们向艾奥瓦人承诺很多很多年不涨价，这是有保证的。这件事做得极好。但如果明天有人拿来一个太阳能项目，10 亿也好、30 亿也好，我们随时准备做。没有特定偏好，越多越好。当然，这取决于地理位置：艾奥瓦的风很好，加州的光很好。但对我们来说，哪里都能做，而且哪里都会做。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:35）：OK. Gregg.

**Gregg Warren**（00:56:38）：Warren, during the past five years, Berkshire Energy's investments in solar and wind generation have been about equal, with around 4.7 billion dedicated to capital projects in each segment. Based on the company's end-of-year capital spending forecast for 2017 through 2019, investments in wind generation were expected to be more than seven times greater than investments in solar generation the next three years, with just over $4.5 billion going into wind generation. Just wondering how much of that future spending is tied to PacifiCorp's recently announced $3.5 billion expansion plan, which is heavily weighted towards improving and expanding the subsidiary's existing wind fleet, and whether the economics for wind are that much better than solar given that MidAmerican has also been spending heavily on wind investments? Or is this disparity between the two segments being driven more by genuine capacity needs, which would imply that you have much more solar capacity than you need?

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:34）：Yeah. It is — we don't look at it as having more solar capacity than we need or anything like — It's really a question of what comes along. I mean, and these — the projects, they're internally generated, they're externally offered to us, and we've got a big appetite for wind or solar. We have seen — you know — just based on those figures, we've seen more wind lately. But we have no bias toward either one. I mean, if we saw five billion of attractive solar projects we could do and didn't happen to see any wind during that period, it wouldn't slow us down from doing the five billion or vice versa. So we are — we have an appetite, a huge appetite, for projects in either area. We're particularly well situated, as I think I've explained or talked about in the past, because we pay lots of taxes. And therefore, solar and wind projects all involve a tax aspect to them. And we can handle those much better than many other — certainly, electric utilities. Most electric utilities really, A, don't have that much money left over after dividends and these — frequently, the taxes aren't that significant. At Berkshire, we pay lots of taxes, and we've got lots of money. So it's really just a question of doing the math on the deals as they come along. We've been very fortunate in Iowa, in finding lots of projects that made sense. And as a result, we've had a — we've got a much lower price for electricity than our main competitor in the state. We've got a lower price than in any states that touch us. We've told the people of Iowa we won't — they won't have a price increase for many, many, many years — guaranteed that. So this worked out extremely well. But if somebody walks in with a solar project tomorrow and it takes a billion dollars or it takes three billion dollars, we're ready to do it. There's no specific — And the more, the better. There's no specific preference between the two. Obviously, it depends where you are in the country. I mean, Iowa's terrific for wind. And, obviously, California's terrific for sun. And there are geographical advantages to one or the other. But from our standpoint, we can do them anyplace. And we will do them anyplace.
