## 问题

现金与国债持仓接近 1000 亿美元。一年前巴菲特曾说，若出现这种情况可能把回购门槛提高到 1.2 倍账面价值以上。关于上调回购门槛，最新想法是什么？

## 回答要点

1. 到某个时点必须重新审视：无法有效部署的多余现金该回购还是分红
2. 分红与回购给市场的预期不同（例如分红不宜削减），这些因素都要计入
3. 当前仍对部署资本足够乐观，不愿把回购价推到距内在价值只差一点点的水平
4. 但「到某个点，举证责任就确实落到我们身上」——最不愿长期持有一百倍市盈率却不增长的东西
5. 芒格补充：按税后利润算，这笔「生意」的倍数还更高
6. 巴菲特：三年后没法再站在这里说手握 1500 亿现金还干得很漂亮；确信未来十年会出现能大规模行动的市场，只是不知是下周还是九年后

## 关键引述

> **“the burden of proof is definitely on us”**
>
> 「举证责任就确实落到我们身上。」

> **“the last thing we like to do is own something at a hundred times earnings where the earnings can't grow”**
>
> 「我们最不愿意做的事，就是持有一样市盈率一百倍、盈利却不增长的东西。」

> **“The answer is maybe.”**
>
> 「答案是「也许」。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:24）：杰伊？

**Jay Gelb**（00:04:26）：伯克希尔的现金和国债持仓正在逼近 1000 亿美元。沃伦，一年前你说过，如果出现这种情况，伯克希尔可能会把回购的最低估值门槛提高到 1.2 倍账面价值以上。关于上调回购门槛，你最新的想法是什么？

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:58）：嗯，等到那个时候（而且可能来得相当快，甚至我还在的时候）。届时如果我们真觉得，在合理时间内没法把这笔钱投到我们喜欢的东西上，那我们就必须重新审视一件事：这些无法有效部署的资金该怎么办。届时我们会做决定，可能两者都有，但可能是回购，也可能是分红。分红政策和回购政策给人带来的预期不一样（比如分红不能随便削减之类），这些都得算进去。但如果我们真觉得手上这笔现金在合理时间内不太可能用掉（属于多余现金），同时又认为以对留任股东仍有吸引力的价格大规模回购是可行的，那可能很合理。目前我们对部署资本还是足够乐观，所以不愿意把回购价推到离内在价值只差一点点的地方。

但到某个点，举证责任就确实落到我们身上。我们最不愿意做的事，就是持有一样市盈率一百倍、盈利却不增长的东西。正如你指出的，我们有近 1000 亿——900 多亿美元投在一门「生意」上，而我们为此付出近一百倍市盈率。那其实是门很烂的生意。

**Charlie Munger**（00:06:59）：按税后利润算，倍数还更高。

**Warren Buffett**（00:07:02）：对。所以我们不喜欢这样，也不该长期这样用你们的钱。接下来的问题是：我们能不能把它投出去？我要说，历史站在我们这边，但如果电话能响起来，而不是只能靠翻历史书，那会更有意思。我确信未来十年里——可能是下周，也可能是九年后——会出现一些市场，让我们能大规模地做明智的事。但如果要等九年，那就没什么意思了。我不认为会等那么久，这只是基于人怎么行为、政府怎么行为、世界怎么运转的判断。但就像我说的，到某个点，举证责任会真正转到我们身上。三年后我没法再站在这里告诉你们，我们手里攥着 1500 亿美元现金，还说我们干得很漂亮。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:08:12）：嗯，我同意。答案是「也许」。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:08:17）：他确实有展开阐述的倾向。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:24）：Jay?

**Jay Gelb**（00:04:26）：Berkshire's cash and Treasury bill holdings are approaching $100 billion. Warren, a year ago, you said Berkshire might increase its minimum valuation for share buybacks above 1.2 times book value if this occurred. What are your latest thoughts on raising the share repurchase threshold?

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:58）：Yeah, the — when the time comes — and it could come reasonably soon, even while I'm around — but [if] we really don't think we can get the money out in a reasonable period of time into things we like, we have to reexamine then what we do with those funds that we don't think can be deployed well. And at that time, we'd make a decision. And it might include both, but it could be repurchases. It could be dividends. There are different inferences that people draw from a dividend policy than from a repurchase policy that, in terms of expectations that you won't cut a dividend and that sort of thing. So you have to factor that all in. But if we really — if we felt that we had cash that was unlikely to be used — excess cash — in a reasonable period of time, and we thought repurchases at a price that was still attractive to continuing shareholders was feasible in a substantial sum, that could make a lot of sense. At the moment, we're still optimistic enough about deploying the capital that we wouldn't be inclined to move to a price much closer where there's only a narrow spread between an intrinsic value and the repurchase price. But at a point, the burden of proof is definitely on us. I mean, that — I — the last thing we like to do is own something at a hundred times earnings where the earnings can't grow. I mean, we're — as you point out, we've got almost a hundred billion — it's $90-plus billion invested in a business, we'll call it a business, where we're paying almost a hundred times earnings. And it's kind of a lousy business.

**Charlie Munger**（00:06:59）：It's more after after-tax earnings.

**Warren Buffett**（00:07:02）：Yeah. So, it — you know, we don't like that. And we shouldn't use your money that way for a long period of time. And, then, the question is, you know, are we going to be able to deploy it? And I would say that history is on our side, but it'd be more fun if the phone would ring instead of just relying on history books. And, you know, I am sure that sometime in the next 10 years — and it could be next week or it could be nine years from now — there will be markets in which we can do intelligent things on a big scale. But it would be no fun if that happens to be nine years off. And I don't think it will be, but just based on how humans behave and how governments behave and how the world behaves. But like I say, at a point, the burden of proof really shifts to us, big-time. And there's no way I can come back here three years from now and tell you that we hold 150 billion or so in cash or more, and we think we're doing something brilliant by doing it. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:08:12）：Well, I agree with you. The answer is maybe. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:08:17）：He does have a tendency to elaborate. (Laughter)
