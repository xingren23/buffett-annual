## 问题

公司价值主要由经营业务驱动，且 GEICO、中美能源、伯灵顿北方等收购标的从未按经济价值重估。每股账面价值还是衡量伯克希尔价值的合适指标吗？

## 回答要点

1. 仍有一定相关性，但远不如从前；随着时间推移，市值更有意义，账面价值只是起点
2. 证券不会比账面记录更值钱；但一些小企业的价值约为账面记录的十倍，也有差劲的资产
3. 最好的方法仍是直接计算内在企业价值，但无法精确；120% 的门槛极大概率低估了内在价值
4. 若是一家由十人持有的私营公司，可以每年逐项估值；业务太多时这种办法过于主观
5. 芒格：保险子公司里对冲股东权益的股票资产不值完整市值，因为被锁在高税率体系里
6. 芒格：喜欢「可交易证券下跌、自有业务上涨」；三十年来已用大量不可交易证券替换可交易证券

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:02）：杰伊。

**Jay Gelb**（00:43:04）：这个问题关于伯克希尔的内在价值。公司价值的很大一部分来自经营业务，而不是证券组合的表现。而且，此前收购的企业并没有按经济价值重估，包括 GEICO、中美能源和伯灵顿北方。基于这些因素，每股账面价值还是衡量伯克希尔价值的合适指标吗？

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:35）：嗯，它还有一定相关性，但比过去差得多了。所以我不想完全放弃每股账面价值这个指标，但随着时间推移，市值往往更有意义。它是一个起点。显然，我们的证券不会比账面记录的价值更高；另一方面，我们拥有你提到的那类企业，还有一些小企业，价值大概是账面记录的十倍；当然也有一些差劲的。我认为最好的方法当然是直接计算内在企业价值，但它不可能精确。我们认为，120% 这个门槛极大概率低估了内在价值——当然，如果全部资产都是证券，120% 就太高了。但随着业务演变、随着我们在经营业务里积累起会计上不予确认的价值，我认为它作为基础数字仍有用处。

如果这是一家私营公司，在座十个人共同拥有，我们每年坐下来把各项业务逐个估值，以此作为基础价值就行。但当我们拥有这么多业务时，这就太主观了。所以最简单的办法还是沿用现在的标准，同时认清它的局限。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:19）：对。我认为保险公司里那些对冲股东权益的股权资产，其实不值完整的市值，因为它们被锁在高税率体系里。所以基本上，我喜欢可交易证券下跌、而我们自己的业务上涨。

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:50）：对，我们正朝这个方向努力，已经努力了三十年左右。

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:56）：我们做得还不错。

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:16）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:03）：我们已经用大量不可交易的证券，替换掉了大量可交易的证券，前者价值高得多。

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:09）：对。而且除此之外，这样经营也更有乐趣，不过——

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:14）：对。我们因此认识了很多本来不会认识的人——

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:15）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:16）：——好人。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:02）：Jay.

**Jay Gelb**（00:43:04）：This question is on Berkshire's intrinsic value. A substantial portion of the company's value is driven by operating businesses rather than the performance of the securities portfolio. Also, the values of previously acquired businesses are not marked up to their economic value, including GEICO, MidAmerican, and Burlington Northern. Based on these factors, is book value per share still a relevant metric for valuing Berkshire?

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:35）：Well, it's got some relevance, but it's got a whole lot less relevance than it used to. And that's why — I don't want to drop the book value per share factor, but the market value tends to have more significance as the decades roll along. It's a starting point. And clearly, our securities aren't worth more than we're carrying for — carrying them for — at that time. And, on the other hand, we've got the kind of businesses you've mentioned. But we've got some small businesses that are worth 10 times or so, you know, what would — could carry it for. We've also got some clunkers, too. But I think the best method, of course, is just to calculate intrinsic business value. But it can't be precise. We know — we think the probability's exceptionally high that 120 percent understates it. Although, if it was all in securities, you know, 120 percent would be too high. But as the businesses have evolved, as we built in unrecognized value at the operating businesses — unrecognized for accounting purposes — I think it still has some use as being kind of the base figure we use. If it were a private company and 10 of us here owned it, instead we'd just sit down annually and calculate the businesses one by one and use that as a base value. But that gets pretty subjective when you've got as many as we do. And so, I think the easiest thing is to use the standards we're using now, recognizing the limitations in them. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:19）：Yeah. I think the equities in the insurance company offsetting shareholders equity in the company are really not worth the full market value because they're locked away in a high-tax system. And so I, basically, like it when our marketable securities go down and our own businesses go up.

**Warren Buffett**（00:45:50）：Yeah, we're working to that end. We've been working that way for 30 years now or something like that.

**Charlie Munger**（00:45:56）：We've done a pretty good job, too.

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:16）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:03）：We have a lot of — we've replaced a lot of marketable securities with unmarketable securities that are worth a lot more.

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:09）：Yeah. And it's actually a more enjoyable way to operate, too, beyond that, but —

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:14）：Yeah. We know a lot of people we wouldn't otherwise —

**Warren Buffett**（00:46:15）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:16）：— be with. Good people.
