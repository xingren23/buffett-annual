## 问题

分销渠道向线上转移、实体零售陷入困境，Fruit of the Loom 是否受影响？这些困难是短期的吗？

## 回答要点

1. 目前基本没有受到影响；但「任何人如果不认为线上正在大幅改变零售，那是错的」
2. 家具业务今年股东周末期间一周做了 4500 万美元，同店增长很好；内布拉斯加家具城线上已接近成交量的 10%
3. 线上增长中有相当比例仍到店取货——省掉的只是进店与排队时间
4. 百货公司的演化史：百年前靠品类与有轨电车，之后是购物中心与汽车，如今是互联网
5. 「百货公司基本上已经搬到线上了，只是品类大大扩展、更便利、价格更低」
6. 巴菲特：正因为当年百货生意烂到被认清，才退出来；每股 6 美元的东西如今约值 10 万美元，要感谢董事桑迪·戈特斯曼

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:58）：好，贝基？

**Becky Quick**（00:37:00）：这个问题来自佐治亚州亚特兰大的德鲁·埃斯特斯。他问：「Fruit of the Loom 是否因分销渠道向线上转移、以及实体零售的困境而遇到困难？如果是，你认为这些困难是短期的吗？」然后德鲁又加了一句：「我希望千禧一代没有把内裤的潮流也扭过去。」（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:28）：嗯，这个他可能比我懂。（笑）答案基本上是：到目前为止没有。但任何人如果不认为线上正在大幅改变零售，或者以为自己完全不受影响，那是错的——世界正在剧变。就像我说的，Fruit of the Loom 我确实没看到变化。我们的家具业务，今年股东周末期间又创了纪录，我在年报里提过，一周做了 4500 万美元。我们的家具业务，除了我们自己的线上业务之外，很难看出线上带来的影响；同店增长非常好。不管是内布拉斯加家具城，还是 RC Willey（在萨克拉门托、里诺、博伊西、盐湖城），还是波士顿的 Jordan's，同店表现都很好。

所以我们确实没看到，但十年前也有很多我们没看到、后来却成真的事。有件事你可能会觉得有意思：奥马哈的家具城是一家极出色的企业，它的线上业务增长非常可观。我也许记错，但我觉得已经接近成交量的 10% 了——说这话之前我得先跟布卢姆金家核实。不过其中很大比例的人仍然会到店里来取货。看来他们省掉的只是进店或排队的时间。这个比例让我有点意外。唯一确定的是，我们一直在看这些数字，试图弄清它们在说什么。到目前为止，我不会说线上显著影响了 Fruit of the Loom，也不会说显著影响了家具业务。

但我毫不幻想十年后的样子会跟今天一样。你想想，一百年前那些大百货公司提供什么？是惊人的选择。奥马哈有家大百货公司，就有一千件婚纱；你要是住在周边小镇，当地商家可能只有两件。所以百货公司是那种令人兴奋的体验：品类多、价格公道、交通方便，因为人们坐有轨电车去。后来出现了购物中心，把原来的垂直布局摊平成水平布局，改成多个业主，但仍然保留惊人的品类、组合、一站式的便利，以及可达性——因为现在人们开车了。再往后是折扣店等等。而现在有了互联网：品类做到了极致，价格很低，物流完全被解决。百货公司基本上已经搬到线上了，只是品类大大扩展、更便利、价格更低。世界在演化，还会继续演化，但速度大大加快了。品牌会以各种方式受到考验。

它们必须决定：是自己做线上，还是通过亚马逊，还是在拥抱新渠道的同时抓住旧的分销方式。零售和品牌领域有很多非常值得观察的问题。我可以向你们保证，未来十年会有一些意外。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:42:05）：如果我们还在百货公司这个行业里，那肯定不舒服。沃伦，想想我们躲过了什么。

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:12）：对，我们其实非常幸运，因为我们当年就在百货公司行业里，而且生意烂到我们认清了它。要是稍微好一点，我们就会抱着不放。我们要深深感谢桑迪·戈特斯曼——我们的董事，就坐在第一排——是他把我们从这个行业里带出来的，当时查理、桑迪和我是合伙人。我们每股付了 6 美元，那笔东西现在大概值每股 10 万美元，正因为我们退出了这个行业。如果当年生意稍微好一点，现在可能只值每股 10 美元、12 美元。所以有时候是运气。我们也并不怀念它，对吧查理？霍奇柴尔德-科恩。

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:00）：不怀念。一点也不。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:36:58）：OK. Becky?

**Becky Quick**（00:37:00）：This question comes from Drew Estes in Atlanta, Georgia. And he asks, "Is Fruit of the Loom experiencing difficulties related to the distribution channel shift towards online and the troubles in the brick and mortar retail world? If so, do you believe the difficulties are short term in nature?" And, then, Drew goes on to add, "I'm hoping millennials haven't bucked the underwear trend, too." (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:37:28）：Yeah. Well, he may know more about that than I do. (Laughter) The answer is essentially no, so far. But anybody that doesn't think that online isn't changing retail in a big way, and that anybody who thinks they're totally insulated from it is correct — I mean, the world is changing big time. And like I say, at Fruit of the Loom, I don't — it really hasn't changed. And at our furniture operation, which is setting a record so far again this year for the shareholder's weekend. You know, I mentioned it in the report, but I think we did $45 million in one week. And our furniture operations — it's hard to see any effect from online, outside of our own online operations. It had really good same-store gains. You can take, you know, whether it's the Nebraska Furniture Mart, but RC Willey, whether it's in Sacramento, or Reno, or Boise, or Salt Lake City, or Jordan's, which, in Boston, has done very well on a same-store basis. So, we don't really see it, but there were a lot of things we didn't see 10 years ago that then materialized. One thing you may find interesting is that the Furniture Mart here in Omaha, which is an extraordinary operation — the online has grown very substantially. And I may be wrong on this, but I think it's getting up to — I'd like to check this with the Blumkins before I say it, but I think it's getting pretty close to 10 percent or so of volume. But it's a very significant percentage of those people still go and pick the product up at the Furniture Mart. So apparently, they — it's the time spent entering the store or maybe at check-out lines or whatever it may be. I'm surprised that it gets to be that percentage. But the one thing about it is we keep looking at the figures and trying to figure out what they're telling us. So far, I would not say that it's affected Fruit of the Loom in a significant manner. I would not say it's affected the furniture operation in a significant manner. But I have no illusions that 10 years is going to look — from now — is going to look anything like today. If you think about it, you know, if you go back a hundred years to the great department stores, what did they offer? They offered incredible selection. You know, if you had a big department store in Omaha, you had the thousand bridal dresses. And if you lived in a small town around, the local guy had two or something of the sort. So the department store was the big, exciting experience of variety and decent prices and convenient transportation, because people took the street cars to get there. And, then, along came the shopping center. And they took what was vertical before. And they made it horizontal. And they changed it into multiple ownerships. But they still kept incredible variety, and assortments, and convenience of going to one place, and accessible transportation because, now, the car was the method. And now you go to — and, you know, and then, we went for the discount stores and all of that. But now you've got the internet. And you've got the ultimate, in terms of assortments. And you've got people that are coming in at low prices. And the transportation is taken care of entirely, so the evolution that has taken place — The department store is online now, basically, except much expanded in assortment, much more convenient, and lower prices. So the world has evolved, and it's going to keep evolving. But the speed has increased dramatically. And what'll happen with — the brands are going to be tested in a variety of ways that — and they have to make decisions as to whether they try to do an online themselves, or work through an Amazon, or whether they try to hang onto the old methods of distribution while embracing new ones. There's a lot of questions in retail and in branding that are very interesting to watch. And you'll get some surprises in the next 10 years, I can promise you that. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:42:05）：It's — it would be certainly — be unpleasant if we were in the department store business. Just think of what we avoided, Warren.

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:12）：Yeah, we got very lucky, actually, because we were in the department store business, and our business was so lousy that we recognized it. If it had been a little bit better, we would've hung on. And we owe a tremendous gratitude to Sandy Gottesman, our director who's here in the front row, because he got us out of the business when Charlie and I, and Sandy, were partners in that. And something we paid six dollars a share for, I think it's worth about $100,000 a share now, because we got out of the business. And if it had been a somewhat better business, you know, it might be worth 10 or $12 a share now. So, sometimes you get lucky. We don't miss it either, do we, Charlie? Hochschild Kohn.

**Charlie Munger**（00:43:00）：No. We don't miss it.
