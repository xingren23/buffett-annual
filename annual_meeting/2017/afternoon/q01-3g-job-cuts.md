## 问题

3G 的核心打法是收购后大幅削减成本，往往裁掉数千岗位。在特朗普政府强调保住美国就业的背景下，若收购美国公司并进一步裁员，伯克希尔还会与 3G 合作吗？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特把 3G 的逻辑归到生产率：若生产率毫无变化，今天仍会过 1776 年的日子
2. 3G 的特别之处是「做得非常快」，且遣散条件相当厚道，但不愿让两个人干一个人的活
3. 伯克希尔更愿意买本来就高效的公司，「我们一点也不享受变得更高效这个过程」
4. 两人个人会放弃直接收购那种「主要好处靠减少人手」的公司，但认为提高生产率有益于社会
5. 例证：伯克希尔自己的纺织厂几十年间裁掉约 2000 人，最终仍未救活；百货公司则被沃尔玛、后来的亚马逊取代
6. 芒格：提高生产率没错，但会招来大量适得其反的舆论，「你对，并不意味着你就该一直这么做」

## 关键引述

> **“If there had been no change in productivity, we would be living the same life as people lived in 1776.”**
>
> 「如果生产率毫无变化，我们现在过的还是 1776 年的日子。」

> **“we don't enjoy the process at all of getting more productive”**
>
> 「我们一点也不享受「变得更高效」这个过程。」

> **“Just because you're right doesn't mean you should always do it.”**
>
> 「你对，并不意味着你就该一直这么做。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:00:00）：台上都齐了吧？好，我们重新开始。直接从贝基问起。

**Becky Quick**（00:00:17）：好的。这个问题来自安妮·纽曼（音），她说自己是 B 股股东。她的问题是：「3G 资本的主要投资策略是收购后大幅削减成本，通常会裁掉成千上万个岗位。在当前美国总统强调保住美国就业的背景下，如果这类投资会导致收购美国公司、裁掉更多美国岗位，伯克希尔是否还会考虑与 3G 合作？」

**Warren Buffett**（00:00:58）：嗯，基本上，3G 的管理层（我在卡夫亨氏近距离观察过他们），他们相信公司应当尽可能高效。当然，这个房间里的人、奥马哈的人、全美国的人，生活改善都来自生产率的提升。如果生产率毫无变化，我们现在过的还是 1776 年的日子。

3G 的人做事非常快，很擅长用比原来更少的人让一门生意更高效。但这件事，钢铁、汽车，随便你说哪个行业，我们一直在做，这正是我们能过得这么好的原因。在伯克希尔，我们更愿意（这件事我一年前写过），我们更愿意买本来就高效运营的公司，因为坦率地说，我们一点也不享受「变得更高效」这个过程，那不愉快。但正是这个过程让国家进步。至今没有人找到办法，能在不提高人均生产率的前提下让人均消费翻倍。问题在于，如果你认为政治上会因此受损，整体上是否明智——政治后果确实会落到企业身上。所以我没法给你一个斩钉截铁的回答。但我可以告诉你：他们不仅专注生产率、而且做得很聪明，同时也极度重视产品改进、创新，以及其他你希望管理层重视的事。我希望你们吃午饭时尝了卡夫亨氏那款芝士蛋糕，会同意我：产品改进和创新跟生产率一样，都是 3G 打法的一部分。就我个人而言，我们经历过买下一家纺织厂、雇了几千人、最后慢慢倒闭的过程，也经历过百货公司那种走向消亡的生意。待在一门裁员的生意里，远不如待在一门增员的生意里愉快。所以查理和我个人大概会放弃让伯克希尔直接去买那种「主要好处靠减少人手提高生产率」的公司。但我认为，从提高生产率的角度思考是有益于社会的，而 3G 的人在这方面做得很好。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:04:04）：嗯，我同意。提高生产率没什么错。反过来说，做这件事会招来大量适得其反的舆论。你对，并不意味着你就该一直这么做。

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:19）：对，我同意。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:00:00）：Panel all here? OK. We're back for action. And we'll go right to Becky.

**Becky Quick**（00:00:17）：All right. This question comes from Anne Newman (PH). She says that she's a shareholder of the Class B stock. And her question is, "The primary investment strategy of 3G Capital is extreme cost-cutting after the purchase of a company. This typically includes the elimination of thousands of jobs. "With the current U.S. president focusing on retention of U.S. jobs, will Berkshire Hathaway still consider future investments with 3G Capital if those investments result in the purchase of U.S. companies and the elimination of more U.S. jobs?"

**Warren Buffett**（00:00:58）：Now, the, essentially, 3G management — and I've watched them up very close at Kraft Heinz — is — basically, they don't — they believe in having a company as productive as possible. And, of course, the gains in this world, for the people in this room, and people in Omaha, and people throughout America, have come through gains in productivity. If there had been no change in productivity, we would be living the same life as people lived in 1776. Now, the people — the 3G people — do it very fast. And they're very good at making a business productive with fewer people than operated before. But that — they've been, you know, we've been doing that in every industry, whether it's steel, or cars, or you name it. And that's why we live as well as we do. We prefer at Berkshire — I wrote about this a year ago — we prefer to buy companies that are already run efficiently because, frankly, we don't enjoy the process at all of getting more productive. I mean, it's not pleasant. But it is what has enabled the country to progress. And nobody has figured out a way to double people's consumption per capita without, in some way, improving productivity per capita. It's a good question in the — whether it's smart overall if you think you're going to suffer politically because political consequences do hit businesses. So I don't know that I can answer the question categorically. But I can tell you that they not only focus on productivity and do it in a very intelligent way, but they also focus, to a terrific degree, on product improvement, innovation, and all of the other things that you want a management to focus on. And I hope that, at the lunchtime, if you had the Kraft Heinz cheesecake, you'll agree with me that product improvement and innovation there is a — is just as much a part of the 3G playbook as productivity. I don't — Personally, we have been through the process of buying into a textile business that employed a couple thousand people and went out of business over a period of time, or a department store, a business that was headed for oblivion. And it is just not as much fun to be in a business that cuts jobs rather than one that adds jobs. So, Charlie and I would probably forego, personally, having Berkshire directly buy businesses where the main benefits were come — would come from increasing productivity by actually having fewer workers. But I think it's pro-social to think in terms of improving productivity. And I think that people at 3G do a very good job at that. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:04:04）：Well, I agree. I don't see anything wrong with increasing productivity. On the other hand, there's a lot of counterproductive publicity to doing it. Just because you're right doesn't mean you should always do it.

**Warren Buffett**（00:04:19）：Yeah. I'd agree with that.
