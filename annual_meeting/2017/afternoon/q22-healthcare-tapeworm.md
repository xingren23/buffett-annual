## 问题

2012 年巴菲特称医疗是美国与美国企业的头号问题。现政府废除并替代奥巴马医改的方案，最终会有利于经济和伯克希尔吗？

## 回答要点

1. 企业税占 GDP 从 1960 年前后的约 4% 降到 2%，医疗支出却从 5% 升到 17%——「医疗成本才是美国经济竞争力的绦虫」
2. 早年有六七个国家医疗支出与美国的 5% 相当，如今美国 17%、它们 10%—11%，美国已落后五到六个百分点
3. 对巴菲特个人而言，新法案若生效，他去年的联邦所得税会下降 17%——「对像我这样的人是一笔巨大减税」
4. 减税必然意味着赤字上升或从别处征税；参议院会有不同版本，最终结果不可知
5. 芒格：问题是用得太多——对近乎没救的人做过多化疗，「其中很大一部分极不道德」
6. 芒格讲雷丁市「寡妇制造者」心脏手术的故事：手术率是正常水平的 20 倍，Tenet 还要求其他医院学习

## 关键引述

> **“it is a huge tax cut for guys like me”**
>
> 「对像我这样的人来说，这是一笔巨大的减税。」

> **“we're getting too much medicine”**
>
> 「我们用了太多的医疗。」

> **“A lot of it is deeply immoral.”**
>
> 「其中很大一部分极不道德。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:27:58）：好，安德鲁？

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（01:28:00）：嗨，沃伦，这是我最后一个问题。2012 年你被引述说：「我认为医疗问题——是美国的头号问题，也是美国企业的头号问题。我们还没有解决它。」你认为现政府废除并替代奥巴马医改的方案，最终会有利于经济和伯克希尔吗？

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:24）：嗯，我给你两个答案。第一，回到 1960 年前后，企业税约占 GDP 的 4%（中间有波动），现在是 2% 左右。而那时医疗支出占 GDP 的 5%，现在是 17% 左右。所以当美国企业说税收扼杀了我们的竞争力之类，他们谈的这件事占 GDP 的比重已经从 4% 降到 2%；而医疗成本（很大部分由企业承担），却从 5% 升到 17%。所以如果真要说，医疗成本才是美国经济竞争力的绦虫。企业知道这一点，只是觉得自己无力改变。税收体系并没有削弱伯克希尔在全球的竞争力。我们的医疗成本涨得惊人，还会继续大涨。你看世界其他地方，回到早年，有六七个国家的医疗支出跟我们的 5% 差不多；我们到了 17%，他们在 10% 或 11%。

所以他们（包括那些医疗成本相当高的国家），已经取得了五到六个百分点的优势。

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:02）：而且那还是社会化医疗。

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:04）：对。所以这是个巨大的问题。我当年说的那些现在依然成立，而且更严重了。这不是哪个政党执政的问题，几乎超越了政治。至于几天前通过的新法案，与奥巴马政府的法案相比，非常有意思。我只能告诉你，这项法案对一个人的净效应是：如果提案生效，我去年的联邦所得税会下降 17%。所以，对像我这样的人来说，这是一笔巨大的减税。法案其余部分的影响，你们自己去判断。但有一点我可以肯定：年调整后总收入 25 万美元、又有大量投资收入的人，会得到巨额减税。而减税之后，要么赤字上升，要么从别人身上收。所以按目前文本，这就是唯一可预测的效应。参议院会做不同的版本，然后开联席会议，谁知道最后是什么。

但那确实写在几天前通过的法案里。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:31:54）：嗯，医疗这部分我当然同意你。我不喜欢的是——我们用了太多的医疗。对已经近乎没救的人做太多化疗，联邦医疗保险和其他环节里各种荒唐的事都在发生。既得利益太多，很难改。但我认为，任何理性的人从外部客观地看美国医疗体系（我们都喜欢那些新的救命技术、新的化疗、新药），天哪，这个体系是疯的，成本失控。它确实让我们的制造商相对于那些由政府支付医疗账单的国家处于很大劣势。所以我完全同意沃伦。

**Warren Buffett**（01:32:56）：如果非要下注，十年后我们占 GDP 的比重会比 17% 更高还是更低？

**Charlie Munger**（01:33:00）：嗯，如果目前趋势延续，会越来越高。这个体系维持现状有巨大的既得利益，他们声音很大、行动积极，而我们其余人漠不关心。结果自然是糟糕的。我还要说，在这个议题上，两党彼此憎恨到都没法理性思考，这也没帮忙。

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:27）：（掌声）有意思的是，联邦政府支出（或者说收入），大约 3.5 万亿美元。大家对那个数字的担忧程度，其实一直稳定在 GDP 的 18% 上下浮动几个点。但其中有 3 万多亿美元花在医疗上，人人都想要最好的，这完全能理解。可相对于整个联邦预算，这是个非常大的数字。谈到美国工业的世界竞争力，这是我们与世界其他地方越来越脱节的单一最大变量。而政党很难去动它。从根本上说，这是个政治议题。

**Charlie Munger**（01:34:32）：其中很大一部分极不道德。一群医院的人和医生像豺狼一样围着一具垂死者的尸体分食，那不是好看的景象。

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:48）：跟他们讲讲加州那伙人——（掌声）——

**Charlie Munger**（01:34:51）：哦对。

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:52）：完美的——这是——

**Charlie Munger**（01:34:53）：这是雷丁市。这是我最喜欢的故事之一。雷丁有一批非常有野心的心脏科医生和心脏外科医生，他们形成了一个念头：心脏就是个「寡妇制造者」。于是每个病人进来，他们都说：「你胸腔里有个寡妇制造者，我们知道怎么修。」于是给所有人都推荐心脏手术。当然，手术量变得巨大。他们的结果非常好，因为没有人比根本不需要心脏手术的人恢复得更好。（笑）他们赚了太多钱，以至于所在的医院连锁 Tenet 让旗下所有其他医院都来学：你们为什么不能像雷丁那样？这是真事。这事持续了很久很久。最后碰到一位受人爱戴的天主教神父，他们对他说：「你胸腔里有个寡妇制造者。」他不信，然后他把这事捅了出去。

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:51）：他是神父，你能理解他为什么不信。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:35:57）：总之——你一旦形成套路，就一直用下去：心脏是寡妇制造者，是寡妇制造者。后来我遇到一位把这些医生赶出医疗行业的医生，我问他：「到最后，他们觉得自己做错了吗？」他说：「没有，查理。他们认为自己在做对人有好处的事。」这就是为什么这类事这么难修。人们在靠干坏事赚钱、越来越成功的过程中产生的自我欺骗，绝不能被低估。这种事很多。（掌声）那么严重的荒唐事，你回头看小小的富国银行简直像无辜——它不过在激励制度上出了点小问题，而这里的心脏手术率是正常水平的 20 倍。你若是经营医院，总该注意到吧。可他们确实注意到了——他们希望其他医院也这么干。

**Warren Buffett**（01:37:10）：他们的成功率确实很高。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:27:58）：OK. Andrew?

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（01:28:00）：Hi. Warren, this is my final question. In 2012, you were quoted as saying, "I think the health care problem in — is the number one problem of America and of American business. We have not dealt with that yet." Do you believe that the current administration's plan to repeal and replace ACA will ultimately benefit the economy and Berkshire or not?

**Warren Buffett**（01:28:24）：Yeah. Well, I'll answer — I'll give you two answers here, the first one being that if you go back to 1960 or thereabouts, corporate taxes were about four percent of GDP. I mean, they bounced around some. And, now, they're about two percent of GDP. And at that time, health care was five percent of GDP. And now, it's about 17 percent of GDP. So when American business talks about taxes strangling our competitiveness or that sort of thing, they're talking about something that, as a percentage of GDP, has gone down from four to two while medical costs, which are borne to a great extent by business, have gone from five to 17 percent. So medical costs are the tapeworm of economic — American economic competitiveness, I mean, if you're really talking about it. And that — and business knows that. They don't feel they can do much about it, but it is not — The tax system is not crippling Berkshire competitiveness around the world or anything of the sort. Our health costs have gone up incredibly and will go up a lot more. And if you look at the rest of the world, there were a half a dozen countries that were around our five percent if you go back to the early years. And while we're at 17, now, they're at 10 or 11. So they have gained a five or six point advantage — the world — even in these countries with fairly high medical costs.

**Charlie Munger**（01:30:02）：And that's with socialized medicine.

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:04）：Yeah. So it's a huge — whatever I said then goes and is accentuated now. And that isn't a problem — I mean, that is a problem this society is having trouble with and is going to have more trouble with, and — regardless of which party's in power or anything of the sort. It almost transcends that. In terms of the new act that was passed a couple days ago versus the Obama administration act, it's a very interesting thing. All I can tell you is the net effect of that act on one person is that my taxes — my federal income taxes — would've gone down 17 percent last year, if the act — if what was proposed went into effect. So, it is a huge tax cut for guys like me. And you'll have to figure out the effects of the rest of the act. But the one thing I can tell you is if it goes through the White House — put in, I mean, it — anybody with $250,000 a year of adjusted gross income and a lot of investment income is going to have a huge tax cut. And when there's a tax cut, either the deficit goes up or they get the taxes from somebody else. So, as it stands now, it is — that is the one predictable effect, if it should pass, as it — and it — the Senate will do something different and hold a conference. And who knows what happens? But that is in the law that was passed a couple days ago. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:31:54）：Well, I certainly agree with you about the medical care. What I don't like about the medical care is that a lot of — we're getting too much medicine. There's too much chemotherapy on people that are all but dead, and all kinds of crazy things go on in Medicare and in other parts of the health system. And every — there are so many vested interests that it's very hard to change. But I don't think any rational person looking objectively from the outside of the American system of medical care — we all love all the new life-saving stuff, and the new chemotherapies, and the new drugs, and all that. But, my God, the system is crazy. And the cost is just going wild. And it does put our manufacturers at a big disadvantage with other people where the government is paying the medical bills. And so, I agree with Warren totally.

**Warren Buffett**（01:32:56）：If you had to bet, 10 years from now, we'll be higher or lower than 17 percent of GDP?

**Charlie Munger**（01:33:00）：Well, if present trends continue, it'll get more and more. There are huge vested interests in having this thing continue the way it is. And they're very vocal and active. And the rest of us are indifferent. So, naturally, we get a terrible result. And I would say that on this issue, both parties hate each other so much that neither one of them can think rationally. And I don't think that helps, either.

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:27）：It's — (Applause) It is kind of interesting that, you know, with — the federal government spends — or raises, we'll say — 3 1/2 trillion or something like that — I mean, the degree of concern everybody has about that — although that's stayed fairly steady in the 18 percent or so of GDP plus or minus a couple points — but three trillion-plus is spent on health care. And everybody wants the best. And it's perfectly understandable. But it's a very, very — it's a big number compared to the whole federal budget. I mean, there's some overlap and all of that. But it's — If you talk about world competitiveness of American industry, it's the biggest single variable where we keep getting more and more out of whack with the rest of the world. And it's very tough for political parties to attack it. Yet, it's, you know, it — basically, it's a political subject.

**Charlie Munger**（01:34:32）：A lot of it is deeply immoral. If you have a group of hospital people and doctors that are feasting like a bunch of jackals on the carcass of some dying person, it's not a pretty sight.

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:48）：Tell them about that group out — (applause) — in California that —

**Charlie Munger**（01:34:51）：Oh yes.

**Warren Buffett**（01:34:52）：Perfect — this is —

**Charlie Munger**（01:34:53）：This is Redding. This is one of my favorite stories. There are a bunch of very ambitious cardiologist and heart surgeons in Redding. And they got the thought that, really, what a heart was was a "widowmaker." So everybody — every patient that came in, they said, "You've got a widowmaker in your chest. And we know how to fix it." And so they recommended heart surgery for everybody. And, of course, they developed a huge volume of heart surgery. And they got very wonderful results because nobody comes through heart surgery better than the man who doesn't need it at all. (Laughter) And they made so much money that the hospital chain, which was Tenet, brought all its other hospitals — why can't you be more like Redding? And this is a true story. And it went on and on and on. And finally, there was some beloved Catholic priest. And they said, "You've got a widowmaker in your chest." And he didn't believe them. And he blew the whistle.

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:51）：He was a priest. You could see why he didn't believe them. (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（01:35:57）：At any rate — well, when you get a routine, you just keep using it, you know. A heart is a widowmaker. It's a widowmaker. Later, I met one of the doctors who threw these people out of the medical profession. And I said to him, "In the end, did they think they were doing anything wrong?" He said, "No, Charlie. They thought that what they were doing was good for people." That is why it's so hard to fix these things. The self — the delusion that comes into people as they make money and get more successful by doing God-awful things should never be underestimated. And it's — there's a lot — (Applause) A lot of that goes on. And you're (inaudible) such gross craziness. And you thought little Wells Fargo looks like innocence. He only has a little trouble with his incentive system. But the heart surgery rate was 20 times normal or something. You'd think you'd notice if you're running a hospital. And — but they did notice. They wanted the other hospitals to be more like it.

**Warren Buffett**（01:37:10）：They had a terrific success ratio.
