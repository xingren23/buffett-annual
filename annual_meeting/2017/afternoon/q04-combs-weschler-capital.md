## 问题

托德·康姆斯与泰德·韦施勒加入以来公司市值翻倍、现金接近 1000 亿美元，但他们似乎没有按同等比例获得新增资金。为什么？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特否认：托德加入时给了 20 亿美元，此后有相当规模的追加，且他们留存的收益本身也在增长
2. 按可交易证券衡量，两人管理的资本比例与各自加入时相当，甚至略高；泰德比托德晚一两年加入
3. 「管 100 亿比管 10 亿、20 亿要难」，预期回报随资金规模上升而下降
4. 两人用同样资金（现 200 亿，最初 20 亿）赚的钱比巴菲特自己多
5. 芒格：股东很幸运，因为两人「真正、深层地像股东那样思考」，这不是总部员工的常规思维
6. 巴菲特：他们的心态百分之百是「我能为伯克希尔做什么」；分给自己的钱已超过目前能处理的量

## 关键引述

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:12:10）：安德鲁？

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（00:12:15）：沃伦。托德·康姆斯和泰德·韦施勒加入以来，公司市值翻了一倍，手上的现金也接近 1000 亿美元。看起来他们两人并没有按同等比例获得新的资金配置。为什么？

**Warren Buffett**（00:12:31）：嗯，其实我认为是有的。我们最初给的是 20 亿，这数字可能记错，但我记得托德来的时候是 20 亿，所以后来有相当大的追加。当然，他们管的资本本身也在增长——某种意义上他们留存了自己的收益。所以他们管理的伯克希尔资本比例（按可交易证券算），我认为跟各自刚加入时差不多，甚至略高一点。泰德比托德晚一两年加入。我想他们会同意：管 100 亿比管 10 亿、20 亿要难。预期回报会随着资金规模变大而下降。但请他们来这个决定非常出色。他们把可交易证券管得很好，用同样的资金（现在是 200 亿，最初是 20 亿），赚的钱比我多。

而且在资金管理之外，他们在很多方面给了我们巨大帮助。所以这个决定非常非常好。他们很聪明，有钱商，具体到投资管理上很擅长，而且绝对是第一流的人，非常契合伯克希尔。托德是查理的功劳——他先认识的查理；泰德算我的。我想我们俩对这些决定都很满意。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:14:44）：嗯，我认为股东们非常幸运有他们，因为两人的思维方式都像股东。

**Warren Buffett**（00:14:50）：完全正确。

**Charlie Munger**（00:14:49）：毕竟他们就是这么来的。而这并不是总部员工的常规思维方式——大家都假装如此，但现实并非如此。这两个人是真正、深层地像股东那样思考。他们年轻、聪明、有建设性。所以我们都很幸运有他们在。

**Warren Buffett**（00:15:10）：对。他们的心态百分之百是「我能为伯克希尔做什么」，而不是「伯克希尔能为我做什么」。相信我，这种事相处久了是能看出来的。而且他们极有才华。但你要知道，要找到年轻、有野心、非常聪明、又不把自己放在第一位的人，很难。我们每一次的经历都是：他们没有把自己放第一位，他们把伯克希尔放第一位。有人说：「那你为什么不再给他们各加 300 亿？」我不认为那会改善他们的生活或业绩。他们管理的规模会随时间增加，但事实是，分给我的钱已经超过我目前能处理的量——证据就是我们手上有这 900 多亿美元。我认为把它投出去有合理的前景，但如果你让我今天就必须投出去，我不喜欢这个前景。查理，有补充吗？

**Charlie Munger**（00:16:36）：嗯，我当然同意。现在比过去某些时候难得多。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:12:10）：Andrew ?

**Andrew Ross Sorkin**（00:12:15）：Warren. Since Todd [Combs] and Ted [Weschler] joined Berkshire, the market cap of the company has doubled, and cash on hand is now nearly a hundred billion dollars. It doesn't look like Todd and Ted have been allocated new capital on the same relative basis. Why?

**Warren Buffett**（00:12:31）：Well, actually, I would say they have been. I think we started out with two billion. That could be wrong, but my memory was two billion with Todd when he came with us. And so, there have been substantial additions. And, of course, their own capital has grown just because — say, in a sense, they retain their own earnings. So yeah, they are managing a proportion of Berkshire's capital — also measured by marketable securities — I think they're managing a proportion that's pretty similar, maybe even a little higher than when each one of them entered. And Ted entered a year or two after Todd. You know, they — I think they would agree that it's tougher to run 10 billion than it is to run one or two billion. I mean, your expectable returns go down as you get into larger sums. But the decision to bring them on has been terrific. I mean, they have — they've done a good job of managing marketable securities. They made more money than I would've made with that same, what is now 20 billion, but originally was two billion. And they've been a terrific help in a variety of ways beyond just money management. So that decision, I'll — you know — that's been a very, very good decision. And they are — they're smart. They have money minds. They are good, specifically, at investment management. But they're absolutely first-class human beings. And they really fit at Berkshire. So, that was — Charlie gets credit for Todd. He met Charlie first. And I'll claim credit for Ted. And I think we both feel very good about the decisions. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:14:44）：Well, I think the shareholders are very lucky to have them because they both think like shareholders.

**Warren Buffett**（00:14:50）：Totally.

**Charlie Munger**（00:14:49）：After all, it came up that way. And that is not the normal way headquarters employees think. It's a pretense that everybody takes on, but the reality is different. And these people really, deeply think like shareholders. And they're young, and smart, and constructive. So we're all very lucky to have them around.

**Warren Buffett**（00:15:10）：Yeah. Their mindset is a hundred percent, "What can I do for Berkshire," not, "What can Berkshire do for me?" And, believe me, you can spot that over time with people. And on top of that, you know, they're very talented. But, you know, it's hard to find people young, ambitious, very smart, that don't put themselves first. And I would — every experience we've had, they did not put themselves first. They put Berkshire first. And believe me, I can spot it when people are extreme in one direction or another. Maybe I'm not so good around the middle, but you've got — You couldn't have two better people in those positions. But — and you say, "Well, why don't you give them another 30 billion each or something?" I don't think that would improve their lives or their performance. They may be handling more as they go along, but the truth is, I've got more assigned to me than I can handle at the present time, as proven by the fact that we've got this 90 billion-plus around. I think there are reasonable prospects for using it. But if you told me I had to put it to work today, I would not like the prospect. Charlie, anything?

**Charlie Munger**（00:16:36）：Well, I certainly agree with that. It's a lot harder now than it was at times in the past.
