## 问题

中美正在激烈谈判甚至被称作贸易战。往前看一步，两国能实现双赢吗？还是世界太小容不下两个赢家？

## 回答要点

1. 中美将在很长时间里是经济超级大国，共同利益远大于分歧
2. 1970 年美国进出口各占 GDP 约 5%；如今出口约 11% 出头、进口约 14.5%，逆差约 3 个点
3. 贸易逆差对应投资顺差——世界在积累对美债权，但差距不宜过宽
4. 两党总体上都支持自由贸易；两国不会因贸易分歧牺牲全球繁荣
5. 芒格：中国高储蓄率加技术吸收，发展快于成熟经济体是必然；两国都该明白最不该做的是心存恶意

## 关键引述

> **“it is a win-win situation when the world trades, basically.”**
>
> 「世界贸易总体上是双赢的。」

> **“the last thing they should do is have any ill will for the other”**
>
> 「最不该做的事就是对彼此心存恶意。」

**Warren Buffett**（00:29:34）：谢谢。我想提一件事。八月我就满 88 岁了，八月是一年的第八个月，而今年年份也以八结尾。你我都知道，八在中国是特别吉利的数字。所以如果你在那边给我找到什么好机会，现在就是出手的时候。全是八。

**Audience Member**（00:30:00）：包在您身上。（巴菲特笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:30:04）：美国和中国在未来很长很长的时间里都会是全世界在经济和其他方面的两个超级大国。我们有很多共同利益，跟任何两个大经济体一样，有时也会有摩擦。但世界贸易总体上是双赢的。中国和美国是其中两个大角色，但世界上还有很多人也牵涉其中。毫无疑问，这个国家有个好处是民主党和共和党总体上都相信自由贸易的好处。我们彼此之间会有分歧，跟其他国家在贸易上也会有分歧。但利益太大、太明显了，全世界的进步很大程度上依赖于此，两个聪明的国家不会做出极端愚蠢的事。我们双方都可能时不时做点小蠢事，显然也需要互相让步。

1970 年美国的出口和进口各占 GDP 约 5%。也就是说我们当时卖出去的东西占 GDP 的 5%，买进来的也差不多。现在很多人觉得我们出口不多。其实出口已占 GDP 的 11% 出头，在不断增长的 GDP 中份额翻了一倍多。但进口约占 14.5%，有 3 个点左右的逆差。我不希望这个差距拉得太大。

但仔细想想，有人送你大量你想要的商品、你给他一些纸片，这其实不算世界上最坏的事。因为平衡项是，贸易有顺差或逆差，投资上就会反过来。所以全世界对美国的债权凭证在增加，他们可以买政府债券，也可以买企业。时间长了，你不会希望差距太宽，因为你发给全世界的债权凭证在某些情况下可能不太妙。但我们的贸易做得非常好，中国做得非常好，世界各国都做得非常好。所以这是双赢。唯一的问题是一方或另一方想赢得太多时，就会出现紧张。但我们不会——我是说全世界——不会因为贸易上的分歧牺牲全球繁荣。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:34:10）：是的，我认为两国都在进步。当然中国经济进步更快，因为它起点更低，而且他们在储蓄率上的自律几乎超过世界上任何国家。一个长期深陷贫困的国家，吸收了世界的先进技术，又有很高的储蓄率，当然会比英国或美国这样的成熟国家发展得更快。事实就是如此。但我认为我们相处得不错，我非常乐观，两个国家都足够聪明，会认识到最不该做的事就是对彼此心存恶意。

**Warren Buffett**（00:30:04）：The United States and China are going to be the two superpowers of the world, economically and in other ways, for a long, long, long time. We have a lot of common interests, and like any two big economic entities, there are times when there'll be tensions. But it is a win-win situation when the world trades, basically. And China and the U.S. are the two big factors in that, but there's plenty of other citizens of the world that are involved in how this comes out. And there is no question — The nice thing about in this country I think is that both Democrats and Republicans basically, on balance, believe in the benefits of free trade. And we will have disagreements with each other. We'll have disagreements with other countries on trade. But it's just too big and too obvious for — that the benefits are huge, and the world's dependent on it in a major way for its progress, that two intelligent countries will do something extremely foolish. We both may do things that are mildly foolish from time to time, and there is some give and take, obviously, involved. But U.S. exports in 1970 and U.S. imports in 1970 were both about 5 percent of GDP. I mean, here we we were, selling 5 percent of our GDP and buying up 5 percent of our GDP, basically. Now people think we don't export a lot of things. Our exports are 11 and a fraction percent of GDP. They've more than doubled as a share of this rising GDP. But the imports are about 14 1/2 percent, so there's a gap of three percent or thereabouts. And I would not like that gap to get too wide. But when you think about it, it's really not the worst thing in the world to have somebody send you a lot of goods that you want and hand them little pieces of paper. I mean, because the balancing item is, if you have a surplus or deficit in your trade, you're going to have a surplus in investment. And so the world is getting more claim checks on the United States, and they — to some extent they buy our government securities, they can buy businesses. And over time, you don't want the gap to get to be too wide because the amount of claim checks you are giving out to the rest of the world could get a little unpleasant under some circumstances. But we've done remarkably well with trade. China's done remarkably well with trade. The countries of the world have done remarkably well with trade. So it is a win-win situation. And the only problem gets to be when one side or the other may want to win a little bit too much, and then you have a certain amount of tension. But we will not sacrifice — the world, I mean — will not sacrifice world prosperity based on differences that arise in trade. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:34:10）：Yeah, well I think that both countries have been advancing. And of course China is advancing faster economically, because it started from a lower base and they've had a little more virtue than practically anybody else in the world in having a high savings rate. And of course, a country that was mired in poverty for a long, long time, and that assimilates the advanced technology of the world, and has a big savings rate, is going to advance faster than some very mature company like Britain or the United States. And that's what's happened. But I think we're getting along fine, and I'm very optimistic that both nations will be smart enough to realize that the last thing they should do is have any ill will for the other.
