## 问题

巴菲特还在参与喜诗糖果和《布法罗新闻报》的定价决策吗？还深度介入哪些子公司？

## 回答要点

1. 很久不参与子公司定价了——喜诗糖果每磅多少钱都答不上来
2. 但与阿吉特·贾因经常就大型风险各自定价再比对，「做了三十年」
3. 芒格：「沃伦还在做、还在跟阿吉特聊——那是因为阿吉特喜欢这样」
4. 与最成功子公司的 CEO 十年只说过三次话；「也许不说那三次他会干得更好」
5. 伯克希尔的特色：管理者想不想交流取决于他们自己

## 关键引述

> **“part of the fun of my job”**
>
> 「这是我工作乐趣的一部分。」

> **“It's very unusual, and it's worked beautifully.”**
>
> 「这很不寻常，但效果非常好。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:38）：好，3 号站台。

**Audience Member**（01:04:43）：我是托德·利希特（音），来自科罗拉多州博尔德。巴菲特先生，您还在参与喜诗糖果（See's Candies）和《布法罗新闻报》的定价决策吗？还有哪些伯克希尔子公司您会深度介入？

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:08）：对，你说得对。曾经有一段时间——相当长一段时间——查理和我都参与喜诗糖果的定价。当然还有些年，尤其是《布法罗新闻报》能不能生存下去都成问题的时候，我确实参与了那些决策。两种情况下我们都有好的经理人，但我们觉得那些决策很重要。但我们参与这类事情已经是很久很久以前了。我说不出喜诗糖果一磅多少钱——因为时不时有人免费寄糖给我，欢迎加入这个行列。（笑）《布法罗新闻报》的价格我也说不上来。我只知道广告提价一般来说非常非常难。所以我们——唯一的是，阿吉特和我经常交流。如果有特别大的风险——比如有人要给某化工厂买 50 亿的超赔保障、起赔点 30 亿之类——我们会各自在脑子里定价，然后比较答案。这类风险你没法翻书查到精算参数。我喜欢思考这类定价，尤其喜欢跟阿吉特比一比。这些只是些特殊情况，但我们做这种事已经三十年了。这是我工作乐趣的一部分。糖果价格的事要抱怨，你们得找查理。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:07:47）：嗯，答案是沃伦还在做、还在跟阿吉特聊——不过那是因为阿吉特喜欢这样。

**Warren Buffett**（01:07:55）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（01:07:56）：我们这个地方很特别——沃伦跟组织里各色人等的接触，主要取决于对方想不想，而不是他想不想。

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:11）：我们最成功的子公司之一的 CEO——

**Charlie Munger**（01:08:13）：这很不寻常，但效果非常好。

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:16）：我们最成功的子公司之一的 CEO，我大概——除非在这里碰到打个招呼——过去十年里跟他说过三次话。他干得非常好。（笑）也许我不跟他说那三次话他会干得更好。（笑）另一方面，阿吉特和我交流非常频繁。一方面，保险业务我确实比很多其他业务更了解。而且很有意思。我们评估的是你在书上查不到的东西。精算才能在阿吉特跟我讨论的事情上不是最重要的——在整个保险业务中当然非常重要。但在这些具体案例里，我们做的是判断，而他的判断比我的好。但我就是想听听。都是些有趣的命题。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:38）：OK, Station 3.

**Audience Member**（01:04:43）：I'm Todd Lichter (PH) from Boulder, Colorado. Mr. Buffett, are you still involved in pricing decisions at See's Candies and The Buffalo News? And with what other Berkshire subsidiaries do you take more than a hands-off approach?

**Warren Buffett**（01:05:08）：Yeah, you're correct that at one time I, and for some — for quite a while — both Charlie and I took part in the pricing decisions at See's Candy. And certainly, for some years, particularly with the question of the survival of The Buffalo News was really in question, I definitely took part in those decisions. In both cases, we had good managers, but still we wanted to — we thought those decisions were important. But it's been a long, long time — very long time — since we've participated in anything like that. I can't tell you what the per pound price is for See's Candy, which is because people, and you're invited to join this group, send me free candy from time to time. (Laughter) And I can’t — I really, I can't tell you the prices at The Buffalo News. All I know is it's very, very, very hard to move up prices on advertising, generally. So no, we — The only thing is, Ajit [Jain] and I talk frequently. And if there's some very big risk, if somebody wants a $5 billion cover on a chemical plant some way excessive loss of over 3 billion or something — we have a certain amount of fun with him deciding on the price in his head. And I decide in my head, and then we compare notes. It's the kind of risk that you really can't look up in a book and see, actuarially, what it’s fairly — the parameters — are fairly likely to be. I enjoy thinking through the pricing of that, and I particularly enjoy comparing it with Ajit. So the — These are just oddball situations, but we do that sort of thing, and we've done it for three decades. And it's part of the fun of my job. The candy prices, if you got to complain about those, you have to go to Charlie. (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（01:07:47）：Well, the answer is, Warren is still doing it and talking to Ajit, and — but that's because Ajit likes it that way.

**Warren Buffett**（01:07:55）：Yep.

**Charlie Munger**（01:07:56）：We have a very peculiar place where the — where Warren's contact with the various people elsewhere in the organization largely depends on what they want, not what he wants.

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:11）：The CEO of one of our —

**Charlie Munger**（01:08:13）：It's very unusual, and it's worked beautifully.

**Warren Buffett**（01:08:16）：The CEO of one of our most successful subsidiaries, I may have talked to — unless I saw him here and just said hello — I probably talked to him three times in the last ten years. And he does remarkably well. (Laughs) He might have done even better if I hadn't talked to him those three times. (Laughter) And on the other hand, Ajit and I talk very, very frequently. And he's got the kind of business, A, I do know — I know more about the insurance business than I know about a good many of the other businesses. And it's interesting. And we are evaluating things that you don't look up in a book, you know. I mean, actuarial talent is not what's important in the things that Ajit talks to me about. It's plenty important throughout our insurance operation. But in these particular cases, you know, we're making judgments, and his judgment's better than mine. But I like to — I just like to hear about them. They're interesting propositions.
