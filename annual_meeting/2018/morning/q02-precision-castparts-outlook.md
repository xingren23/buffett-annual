## 问题

飞机产量在增长，精密铸件的营收和利润却未见起色，行业消息称其市场地位在竞争和技术变革中走弱。它需要做什么才能实现收购时预期的增长？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特称精密铸件是「非常好的生意」，航空客户极度依赖零件质量和交期可靠性
2. 合同前置时间很长，竞争对手交不了货时制造商反来找精密铸件救急，但要求签长约
3. 每年约 4 亿美元出头的商誉摊销不是经济成本，利润上可加回
4. 马克·多尼根是出色的经理人，「没有他掌舵我不会买」
5. 芒格：「有机会明天再买一个一模一样的我们也愿意」

## 关键引述

> **“it's a very good business.”**
>
> 「这是一门非常好的生意。」

> **“I think we'd buy another one just like it tomorrow if we had the chance.”**
>

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:00）：好，乔纳森·勃兰特。

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:24:03）：沃伦、查理，你们好。考虑到飞机产量在增长，精密铸件（Precision Castparts）的营收和利润却没有起色，这看起来有些出人意料。我理解新旧机型切换带来的颠簸，但我也从行业消息人士那里听到，精密铸件的市场地位在竞争加剧和技术变革中已经不如从前。它需要做什么来巩固和加强在航空客户中的领先地位，从而实现伯克希尔收购时预期的增长？

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:35）：嗯——

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:24:36）：更宽泛地说，收购两年后，你们对这项业务的前景怎么看？

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:40）：最后一部分再说一遍。前景。

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:24:43）：更宽泛地说，收购两年后，你们对这项业务的长期前景有何更新？

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:49）：哦，长期来看——包括不太长的短期——这是一门非常好的生意。你提到了飞机，但我们还进入其他行业。不过飞机当然是最重要的。飞机制造商对零件质量和交货及时性都非常依赖。你总不能让一架价值 7500 万、1 亿甚至 2 亿美元的飞机等着一个零件。所以可靠性——质量和交期——极其重要。我们的合同一签就是好多年，有时飞机还没开始生产我们就拿到订单了，前置时间很长。过去一年——其实更早就遇到过，但去年我知道几个具体案例——其他供应商交不了货，制造商就来找我们说「能不能帮个忙」。我们说「当然可以，但我们想签个五年合同，因为我们不想老是替别人擦屁股」。这类事情的前置时间很长。这门生意非常好。有一点你会看到，他们每年要摊销约 4 亿美元出头的无形资产——这种商誉摊销是不可抵扣的——在我看来这不算经济成本。伯克希尔有不少这样的摊销，但最大一块来自精密铸件的收购。所以你看到的利润数字上可以加回约 4 亿，那不是真正的经济开支，不过会计会不同意。但钱是我们的，所以按我的算。（笑）管理这门生意的马克·多尼根非常了不起，他不仅是个出色的经理——没有他掌舵我不会买这个公司——他还在其他领域帮了我们很多，而且乐在其中。作为管理者和为伯克希尔竭尽全力的人，没人比他更强。这是一笔非常好的收购，产品周期很长。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:28:09）：嗯，是的，如果有机会，明天再买一个一模一样的我们也愿意。

**Warren Buffett**（00:28:14）：对，这就是答案。（笑）话不多，但说到点子上了。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:00）：OK, Jonathan Brandt.

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:24:03）：Hi Warren. Hi Charlie. Given the growth in airplane build rates, it seems surprising that Precision Castparts isn't doing better on the top or bottom line. I understand the issue with a bumpy transition from old to new programs, but I've also heard from industry sources that Precision's market position is not as strong as it used to be amid intensifying competition and some technological disruption. What does Precision need to do to solidify and strengthen its preeminent position with its aerospace customers so that it can deliver the growth you expected when Berkshire acquired it?

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:35）：Yeah —

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:24:36）：More generally, two years after the acquisition, what is your outlook for that business?

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:40）：Give me the last part again. The outlook.

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:24:43）：More generally, two years after the acquisition, what is your updated outlook for that business longer term?

**Warren Buffett**（00:24:49）：Oh, longer term, I think — and in the reasonably shorter term — it's a very good business. I mean, you were — You mentioned aircraft, but we get into other industries. But certainly aircraft's the most important. You have manufacturers that are very dependent on both the quality of the parts and the promptness of delivery. You do not want to have an aircraft with 75- or 100- or maybe $200 million and be waiting for a part or something of the sort. So it's — Reliability is, both in terms of quality and delivery times and all of that sort of thing, is enormously important. And we get contracts that extend out many years. And sometimes we — I mean, we will get them well before the plane even starts in production. So there's very long lead times. And we have found in the last year — found it earlier, but I know of some specific cases in the last year — where other suppliers have failed in their deliveries and then the manufacturers come to us and say, "We would like you to help us out." And we say, "Well, we'd be glad to help you out, but we'd like about a five-year contract, if we're going to do it because we're just not going to make up for these other guys' shortfalls periodically." But that sort of thing has a very long lead time. The business is a very good business. One thing you will see their earnings charged with is about $400 million — little over $400 million a year — of intangible — nondeductible in that case — amortization of goodwill, which is really — is not an economic cost in my view. We have a significant amount of that through Berkshire, but by far, the largest amount is related to the Precision acquisition. So whatever you see, you can add about 400 million that in my view is not an economic expense, but the accountants would argue otherwise. But it's our money, so we'll take my view. The — (Laughter) Mark Donegan, who runs that operation, is incredible, and he has been not only — he's a fabulous manager. I wouldn't have bought it without him in charge. He also has been very helpful to us in other areas, and he loves to do it. So you can't beat him, both as a manager in his own operation, but with his devotion to really doing everything that will help Berkshire. It was — it's a very good acquisition with very long tails to the products that are being developed. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:28:09）：Well, yeah, I think we'd buy another one just like it tomorrow if we had the chance.

**Warren Buffett**（00:28:14）：Yeah, that's the answer. (Laughter) Man of few words, but he gets the point. (Laughter)
