## 问题

零基预算在卡夫亨氏和 3G 合作中非常有效。伯克希尔其他子公司的管理者会采用这些降本技术吗？

## 回答要点

1. 一般不需要零基预算——「你为什么不一直这样想？」
2. GEICO 从 8000 人增到 3.9 万人，「每个人都非常有生产力」
3. 3G 进入的是人员臃肿的状况；烟草公司因太赚钱养了不需要的人
4. 总部 30 人、一半是内部审计——低开销分散化模式「远远划算」
5. 芒格：「官僚主义有点像癌症。它的运作方式也像癌症。」

## 关键引述

> **“I think a bureaucracy is sort of like a cancer.”**
>
> 「我觉得官僚主义有点像癌症。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（02:07:31）：好，贝基？

**Becky Quick**（02:07:36）：这个问题来自伦敦的安格斯·汉顿（音），他说他们持股伯克希尔 30 多年了。他说：「我们都读到过零基预算在卡夫亨氏以及你们与 3G 合作的其他投资中非常有效。伯克希尔其他子公司的管理者会不会也采用这些降本技术？」

**Warren Buffett**（02:08:06）：嗯，总的来说，我们并不期望经理人们处于需要搞「零基预算」那种大改的状态。换句话说，你为什么不一直那样思考？3G 的人进入的是某些特定局面（大概主要是人力，但也有其他费用），那里有大量开支，每一美元花出去换不回一美元的价值。所以他们很快做了改变，去纠正一个本来就不该存在的局面。

而我们希望我们的经理人，比如 GEICO。GEICO 自我们控股以来，从大约 8,000 人增长到 39,000 人，但每个人都非常有生产力。你没法用 3G 的办法从这里拿走几千人。反过来说，我也能想到一些组织，可以拿走一大批人却没人动手，因为那门生意本来就很赚钱。烟草公司其实就是这样：利润太高，养了一堆其实并不需要的人，钱照样流进来。

所以我们的经理人各有各的办法，去跟踪、去在客户满意度最大化的同时不产生不必要的成本。我认为有些经理人大概确实用零基预算或类似的东西。他们不向我提交预算，从来不用，我们从没有要求过。伯克希尔从来没有预算，我们也不做月度合并报表。每家公司的报告我都会看，但没必要为了在 4 月底、5 月底出一份合并数字而多花时间。我们知道自己在什么位置。

我相信我们是全《财富》500 强里唯一不这么做的公司。但在伯克希尔，我们不做不必要的事。大公司做的很多事情都是不必要的，这正是 3G 时不时能找到机会的原因。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（02:10:52）：嗯，如果总部 30 人、一半是内部审计，这不是美国大公司正常的运作方式。有意思的是，我们显然损失了一些规模优势，但也避免了大官僚机构、总部无穷无尽会议的劣势。总的来说，我们低开销、分散化的模式远远划算。而且这让我们的公司对有能力、正直的企业主很有吸引力。总的来说，现有体系对我们非常好。我不认为我们有大量人员可以像其他地方那样有效裁减。我们的方法太成功了，不太可能改变。

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:59）：对。总部可以说在做「零下预算」。（笑）我们希望总部的榜样在很大程度上被——

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:12）：不仅是降本。消除官僚主义后决策质量也会提高。

**Warren Buffett**（02:12:20）：哦对。

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:21）：我觉得官僚主义有点像癌症。它的运作方式也像癌症。（掌声）所以我们非常反官僚。这给我们带来了很多好处。在这方面我们跟巅峰时期的百威英博很不一样。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:07:31）：OK, Becky?

**Becky Quick**（02:07:36）：This question comes from Angus Hanton (PH), who — he and his wife are based in London, and he says they've been shareholders in Berkshire Hathaway for over 30 years. He says, "We have all read about the zero-based budgeting that has been so effective with Kraft Heinz and other investments that you've done with 3G Partners. Can we expect these cost-reduction techniques to be used by your managers in other parts of the Berkshire Hathaway enterprise?"

**Warren Buffett**（02:08:06）：Well, in general, we do not expect the managers, generally, to get in the position where there would be a lot of change in terms of zero-based budgeting. In other words, why in the world aren't you thinking that way all of the time? The 3G people have gone into certain situations where there were — probably primarily in personnel, but in other expenses as well — a lot of expenses that were not delivering a dollar of value per dollar expended. And so, they made changes very fast that — to a situation that probably shouldn't have existed in the first place. Whereas, we hope that our managers — take a GEICO. GEICO's gone from, I think, 8,000 to 39,000 people since we bought control. But they're all very productive. I mean, you would not find a way for a 3G operation to take thousands of people out of there. On the other hand, I can think of some organizations where you could take a whole lot of people out, where it isn't being done because the businesses are very profitable to start with. That's what happened with the tobacco companies, actually. They were so profitable that they had all kinds of people around that didn't — weren't really needed. But they — the money just flowed in. So I — our managers have different techniques of keeping track of — or of — trying to maximize customer satisfaction at the same time that they don't incur other than necessary costs. And I think, probably, some of our managers may well use something that's either zero-based budgeting or something akin to it. They do not submit budgets — never have — to me. I mean, they've never been required to. We've never had a budget at Berkshire. We don't consolidate our figures monthly. I mean, I get individual reports on every company. But there's no reason to have some extra time spent, for example, by having consolidated figures at the end of April, or consolidated figures at the end of May. We know where we stand. And — you know, I'm sure we're the only company that — probably in the whole Fortune 500 — that doesn't do it. But we don't do unnecessary things around Berkshire. And a lot of stuff that's done at big companies is unnecessary. And that's why a 3G finds opportunities from time to time. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:10:52）：Well, if you've got 30 people at headquarters and half of those are internal auditors, that is not the normal way of running a big company in America. And what's interesting about it is, obviously, we lose some advantages from big size. But we also lose certain disadvantages from having a big bureaucracy with endless meeting after meeting after meeting around headquarters. And net, I think we've been way ahead with our low overhead, diversified method. And also, it makes our company attractive to very able, honorable people who have companies. So generally speaking, the existing system has worked wonderfully for us. I don't think we have the employment that could be cut effectively that a lot of other places have. And I think our methods have worked so well that we'd be very unlikely to change them.

**Warren Buffett**（02:11:59）：Yeah. I think if some — at headquarters, you could say we have kind of subzero-based budgeting. (Laughter) And we hope that the example of headquarters is, to a great extent, emulated by our —

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:12）：But it isn't just the cost reduction. I think the decisions get made better if you eliminate the bureaucracy.

**Warren Buffett**（02:12:20）：Oh yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（02:12:21）：I think a bureaucracy is sort of like a cancer. And it functions sort of like a cancer. (Applause) And so, we're very anti-bureaucracy. And I think it's done us a lot of good. In that case, we're quite different from, say, Anheuser-Busch at its peak.
