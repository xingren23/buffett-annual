## 问题

伯克希尔能源不必分红，积累近 120 亿资本。税收抵免退坡后资本开支会减少、闲置资本会增加吗？

## 回答要点

1. 太阳能和风电税收抵免退坡后资本投入会减少
2. 公用事业和发电的改进需要「几千亿甚至上万亿美元」
3. 「伯克希尔能源大额分红的概率约等于零」
4. 有资金也有人才——「除了伯克希尔你还能去哪找这笔钱？」
5. 三个股东：伯克希尔 90%、阿贝尔家族和斯科特家族持其余

## 关键引述

> **“the chances of a big dividend is approximately zero”**
>
> 「大额分红的概率约等于零」

**Warren Buffett**（02:22:28）：好，格雷格？

**Gregg Warren**（02:22:33）：沃伦，伯克希尔能源在伯克希尔旗下获益良多。因为不必每年把 60%—70% 的利润分红出去，过去五年积累了 90 亿资本、过去十年接近 120 亿，可用于收购和资本开支——尤其是可再生能源。虽然太阳能税收抵免明年才到期，但我们已经看到伯克希尔能源对太阳能项目的资本投入大幅减少。风电产能投资今明两年仍较高，但 2020 年风电生产税收抵免退坡后会回落。如果没有重大新增资本项目承诺，伯克希尔能源看起来会积累大量闲置资本……

**Warren Buffett**（02:23:36）：对。你说的税收抵免退坡是对的。不过过去也有延期立法。谁知道政府对各种替代能源的激励态度会怎样？但我猜——从公用事业和发电各方面可能需要的合理支出来看——会花很多钱。问题是我们能不能花这些钱并获得合理回报。我们会做合理的事。伯克希尔能源基本上有三个股东——伯克希尔持股 90%，格雷格·阿贝尔和家人、沃尔特·斯科特和家人持有其余……

**Charlie Munger**（02:25:18）：对。我认为伯克希尔能源在未来可见的时期内有巨大的机会去明智地配置资本。所以大额分红的概率约等于零。

**Warren Buffett**（02:25:48）：对。我们不仅有几乎任何公用事业公司都没有的资金——还有人才。我们有非常有才华的团队。这是个大领域，我们的股东是资本家，我们的经理人非常出色。你会觉得我们长期总能找到聪明的事做。到目前为止确实如此。我们持有它近 20 年了，配置了大量资本，到目前一切顺利。看看美国公用事业系统可以做的改进——你谈的是几千亿甚至上万亿美元。除了伯克希尔你还能去哪找这笔钱？（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（02:22:28）：OK, Gregg?

**Gregg Warren**（02:22:33）：Warren, Berkshire Energy has benefited greatly from operating under the Berkshire umbrella. By not having to pay out 60 to 70 percent of earnings annually as a dividend, the company was able to amass 9 billion in capital the past five years, and closer to 12 billion in the past ten, money that can be allocated to acquisitions and capital spending, especially on renewables. While tax credits for solar energy don't run out until next year, we've already seen a dramatic reduction in Berkshire Energy's capital commitment to solar projects. And even though spending on wind generation capacity is projected to be elevated this year and next, it does wind down in 2020 as the wind production tax credits are phased out. Absent a major commitment to additional capital projects, it looks like Berkshire Energy's expenditures in 2021 will be its lowest since 2012, leaving the firm with more cash on hand than it has had in some time. Do you think it is likely at that point that Berkshire Energy starts funneling some of that cash up to the parent company? Or will it be earmarked for debt reduction, or just be left on the balance sheet as dry powder for acquisitions?

**Warren Buffett**（02:23:36）：Yeah. The — you're right about when tax credits phase out and all of that. Although, as you know, they've extended that legislation in the past. Who knows exactly what the government's position will be on incentivizing various forms of alternative energy? But my guess is — I mean, if you take the logical expenditures that may be required in all aspects of the public — like regeneration and the utility business generally — I think there'll be a lot of money spent. And the question is whether we can spend it and get a reasonable return on it. There again, we'll do what's logical. There are three shareholders, basically, of Berkshire Hathaway Energy. Berkshire Hathaway itself owns 90 percent of it. And Greg Abel and his family, perhaps, and Walter Scott and, again, family members — own the other 10 percent. And we all have an interest in employing as much capital as we can at good rates. And we'll know when it can be done and when it can't be done. And we'll do — there's no tax consequences to Berkshire at all. So — but the three partners will figure out which makes the most sense. But when you think of what might be done to improve the grid in the U.S. and the fact that we do have the capital, I wouldn't be surprised if we find good uses for capital in Berkshire Hathaway Energy for a long time in the future. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（02:25:18）：Yeah. Well, I think there'll be huge opportunities in Berkshire Energy as far ahead as you can see to deploy capital very intelligently. So I think the chances of a big dividend is approximately zero.

**Warren Buffett**（02:25:48）：Yeah. And we've not only got the money to an extent that virtually no utility company does — we've also got the talent, too. I mean, we've got a very, very talented organization there. So it's a big field and we've got shareholders that are capitalists. And we've got managers that are terrific. And you would think we'd find something intelligent to do over time in the field. So far, we have. I mean, we've owned it now for close to 20 years. And we've deployed a lot of capital and so far, so good. I mean, it's — If you look at the improvements that can be made in our utility system in the United States, you're talking hundreds and hundreds and hundreds of billions of dollars, if not trillions. So — you know, where else but Berkshire would you look for that kind of money? (Laughs)
