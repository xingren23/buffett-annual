## 问题

巴菲特和芒格在位时能拿到高盛、美国银行等的高票息和优厚条件。不在位后，这些「租金」会低几个百分点吗？

## 回答要点

1. 2008 年高盛和 GE 的交易其实可以谈到更好条件，但没推到极限
2. 泰德和托德也在带来交易机会，交易对手不会因接电话的是他们就犹豫
3. 芒格：那些时期是 50 年来最糟的，未来做类似交易会更难
4. 巴菲特：「我想我会被人怀念那么一点点，但你不会察觉到的。」

## 关键引述

> **“I like to think I'll be missed a little bit”**
>
> 「我想我会被人怀念那么一点点」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:42）：好，格雷格，格雷格·沃伦。

**Gregg Warren**（00:59:45）：早上好，沃伦。我对贝基的问题做一点跟进。2014 年年会和今天早上您都提到，伯克希尔的品牌和声誉、加上强大的资产负债表，能让下一任管理者复制许多您作为公司门面所带来的优势——其中包括在财务困境时以资本注入和「巴菲特背书」换取高额回报的能力。资产负债表的说服力我认同，卖方仍会排队加入伯克希尔大家庭，特别是如果下一任管理者手里保留大量现金。但我并不完全相信他们能拿到像高盛和美国银行在困难时期给您的那种 8%、9%、10% 的票息和其他附加条件。您不在位之后，那些租金至少会低几个百分点——直到新管理者建立起配得上更高回报的声誉。我这样想对吗？

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:01）：我不确定。你提到了高盛，2008 年 9 月或 10 月初我们还做了通用电气。我们其实可以谈到更好的条件。不过我觉得那样可能适得其反——如果我们真等到恐慌进一步蔓延再出手——因为我不知道它会蔓延到什么程度——三四五个月后买到的会好得多。但我们也不想把条件谈到太高、让交易对高盛或 GE 不利。他们会接受我们开的条件，但我们没有推到极限，因为当时确实没有别人在做。我们现在也在看一笔交易，大概率不会成。规模不大。但这次托德和泰德都给我带来了机会。其中一个人带来的项目，他的思路跟我想的一样，是他回了对方的电话。我不认为交易对手会因为在电话那头是泰德而不是我就有所顾虑。历史上有些时候可能有一点差别。但我们会继续坚持自己对资金价值的判断标准。泰德和托德在这一点上跟我想得一样好。偶尔会有电话被打爆的时候。如果他们需要钱，不会因为接电话的不是我就挂断。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:03:36）：嗯。他提到的那些时期，很多都是 50 年来最糟糕的时候。那是非常罕见的情况。我们也没做那么多笔交易。所以我认为他说得对——未来要做类似的交易会更难。

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:06）：对，现在的问题更多是金额规模，而不是条款怎么定。有时候我们能谈到自认为合理的条件，有时候——今天大多数时候——不能。但你也许会看到一两笔交易——不是收购企业而是买证券——你会觉得是投资伯克希尔资金的相当不错的方式。而且它们可能是通过托德或泰德来的，而不是直接找到我。我想我会被人怀念那么一点点，但你不会察觉到的。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:59:42）：OK. Gregg, Gregg Warren.

**Gregg Warren**（00:59:45）：Good morning, Warren. I have a little bit of a follow-up on Becky's question. At the 2014 annual meeting, as well as this morning, you noted that the power of Berkshire brand and its reputation, as well as the strength of Berkshire's balance sheet, would allow the company's next managers to replicate many of the advantages that have come with your being the face of the organization, one of which has been an ability to extract high rents from firms in exchange for a capital infusion and the Buffett seal of approval during times of financial distress. I buy the argument about the strength of the balance sheet and believe that deals will continue to be done with sellers still lining up to become part of the Berkshire family, especially if the company's next managers are allowed to keep a ton of cash on hand. But I'm not entirely convinced that they'll be able to garner the same 8, 9, 10 percent coupons, as well as other add-ons, that you've been able to extract from firms like Goldman Sachs and Bank of America in times of distress. I'd expect those rents to be at least a few percentage points lower once you're no longer running the show. That is, until those managers build up a reputation to warrant higher returns. Am I right to think about it that way?

**Warren Buffett**（01:01:01）：I'm not sure. The — when we, in two — you mentioned Goldman Sachs, and we also did with General Electric, in September or early October of 2008. We probably could actually have extracted better terms. You know, I think it might have been counterproductive in the end, but I was — we would have done better, incidentally, financially, if we'd really waited until the panic developed further — because I didn't know how far it would develop — but we could have made a lot better purchases three or four or five months later than we did at that time. And we also did not want to do something that looked to be so high as to in — make the transaction disadvantageous to Goldman or to GE. They were going to take the terms we offered, but we actually didn't push it to the limit, because there really wasn't anybody else around. I think — and we're working on something right now that probably won't happen. It's not huge. But actually, in this case, both Todd [Combs] and Ted [Weschler] have brought deals to me. One of them brought something to me, and, you know, he was thinking in the same terms that I got — was thinking about — and he's the one that returned the call that he had received about a transaction. And I do not think the party on the other side is going to care about the fact that they had him on the phone rather than me on the phone. I — You know, there may — there could be just a little bit at certain times in history. But, you know, we will continue to have our standards of what we think money is worth at any given time. And Ted and Todd think just as well about that as I do. And there will be times, very occasionally, when our phone will ring a lot. And I don't think they'll hang up because I don't answer it, if they need the money. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:03:36）：Well. The times he's referring to, a lot of them, were like the worst in 50 years. So that's a really rare kind of an occurrence. And we didn't make all that many deals. So I think he's right that it'll be harder for us to make similar deals in the future.

**Warren Buffett**（01:04:06）：Yeah, the problem is the sums involved now, more than the problem of deciding what the proper terms should be. And sometimes we can get what we think is appropriate and sometimes we — most of the time, today, we can't. But you may see a transaction or two that — not in terms of buying business but in terms of securities — that strike you as perfectly decent ways to invest Berkshire's money. And they may well have come through Todd or Ted instead of directly to me. I like to think I'll be missed a little bit, but I — you won't notice it. (Laughter)
