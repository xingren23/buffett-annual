## 问题

金霸王 2017 年税前利润 8200 万仍远低于宝洁子公司时期。是过渡成本还是品类商品化？一季度回升是否意味着 2018 年贡献更大？

## 回答要点

1. 「金霸王现在赚的应该更多，将来也会」
2. 巴菲特曾在吉列董事会目睹金霸王充分管理时的表现
3. 换股规则限制了过渡期很多合理操作
4. 品牌和产品线很强；制造布局调整和费用消退后会回到应有盈利
5. 芒格：「我比你还看好它。」

## 关键引述

> **“Duracell should be earning more money than it is now, and will be.”**
>
> 「金霸王现在赚的应该更多，将来也会。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:56:57）：乔纳森。

**Jonathan Brandt**（01:56:58）：金霸王 2017 年税前利润 8200 万美元，仍远低于它作为宝洁子公司的水平。能否说明过渡成本或购买价格会计在分部层面的影响在去年是否仍是暂时性拖累？或者利润差距只是反映了品类商品化——毕竟亚马逊进入了电池市场？我看到金霸王一季度盈利在回升。这是 2018 年及以后贡献更大的信号吗——随着制造布局调整完成、收购相关费用消退？

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:42）：对。金霸王现在赚的应该更多，将来也会。如你所说，它正在朝那个方向走。但以公司历史来看，它现在的盈利水平不合适。吉列收购金霸王的时候我在场。我是吉列的董事。我见过它被充分管理时是什么样。我见过吉姆·基尔茨在吉列是怎么管它的。那次收购的过渡问题更多。比如我们用宝洁股票换金霸王有很多规则限制。从宝洁管理过渡到我们管理的那段时间，很多合理的事不能做。但金霸王——品牌很强。非常强。产品线很强。我们在赚更多的钱。我们应该、也相信我们会赚到这门生意真正能赚的钱。应该不久就能达到。但你说得对，从利润角度看，表现不佳。我们正在做很多调整。有些涉及一些国家：在那里，改变用工成本非常高，比如关厂或做类似调整时要付一大笔钱。但我对金霸王这笔交易的喜欢程度，跟当初做的时候完全一样。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:54）：我比你还看好它。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:00）：不。金霸王是非常、非常——就是我们喜欢的那类生意。

**Charlie Munger**（02:00:04）：是的。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:56:57）：Jonathan.

**Jonathan Brandt**（01:56:58）：Duracell's $82 million of pretax profits in 2017 were still well below what it earned as a subsidiary of P&G. Can you clarify or quantify to what extent transition costs or purchase price accounting impacts at the segment level were still temporarily burdened last year? Or is it possible that the gap in earnings contribution simply reflects a commoditization of the category given the entry of Amazon into the battery market? I did see that Duracell's earnings were up in the first quarter. Is that a sign of a more meaningful contribution in 2018 and beyond, as you finish right-sizing the manufacturing footprint and acquisition-related charges fall away?

**Warren Buffett**（01:57:42）：Yeah. Duracell should be earning more money than it is now, and will be. And as you mentioned, it's well on its way there. But it is not earning an appropriate amount now, based on the history of the company. I was around when — I was on the board of Gillette when Gillette bought Duracell. And I've seen what it does when it is managed to its full extent. And I saw what Jim Kilts did with it at Gillette when he ran it. And there were a lot more transition problems in the purchase. For one thing, there's a lot of rules connected with our swap of our stock in P&G for Duracell. There are a lot of things which you cannot do that made sense to do in that period of transition from P&G's management to ours. But Duracell — the brand is strong. Very strong. The product line is very strong. And we are making more money. And we should, and I believe we will earn, really, what the property is capable of earning. We should be earning that relatively soon. But you're absolutely right that it is — from a profit standpoint — is underperforming. We're making a lot of changes. And some of those are involved in jurisdictions — countries — where it is really expensive to change in terms of employment — payments that have to be made if a plant is changed or something of the sort. But I like the Duracell deal absolutely as well as when we made it. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:59:54）：I like it better than you do. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（02:00:00）：No. Duracell is a very, very — is our kind of business.

**Charlie Munger**（02:00:04）：It is.
