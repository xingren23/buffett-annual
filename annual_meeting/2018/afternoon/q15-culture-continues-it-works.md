## 问题

子公司的管理者往往是卖方家族成员。他们退休后如何确保伯克希尔文化延续？

## 回答要点

1. 文化非常强大，且被股东群体强化
2. 芒格每次参加管理者午餐会都更确信「文化和价值观会延续很久」
3. 「它没那么容易复制」
4. 巴菲特：「它管用。」芒格：「它会继续管用。」

## 关键引述

> **“It works.”**
>
> 「它管用。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:24）：好，格雷格。

**Gregg Warren**（01:09:28）：沃伦，你多次说过，伯克希尔各子公司共享一套很强的共同文化：以诚实正直为底线、着眼长期、重视客户服务。收购经营公司时，找到与伯克希尔契合的文化也至关重要。

多数情况下，现在经营这些子公司的经理人，就是当年把公司卖给伯克希尔的那个家族的成员。他们对自己经营的企业有切身利益，也与伯克希尔共有的文化有很强的联系。

在我看来，更大的挑战在于：那些被收购、且占伯克希尔整体价值相当份额并不断上升的大型上市公司，如何让它们坚持这条路。你能不能谈谈情况是否如此，以及对你和查理来说，最大的挑战是什么——不只是维持伯克希尔的文化，还有找到能与你们所建立的一切相契合的公司。

**Warren Buffett**（01:10:19）：是的，我认为这套文化非常、非常强。而且我认为它在被强化——坦白说，是被我们的股东强化。

我们的股东结构和别人不同，我们看待股东的方式，也和许多公司不太一样。有不少上市公司恨不得自己没有公众股东。我们乐于有公众股东，而且我们喜欢个人股东，不偏爱机构。我们不会给业绩指引，也不会在投资者电话会上专门对机构讲话。我们希望直接面对股东，希望股东本质上是合伙人。文化就从这里开始。

然后是董事。我在 19 个董事会待过，从没见过像我们这样的董事会。我们董事会的人，很多本身就代表大量股份，他们没有拿到特殊安排；这是一群以所有者为导向、有伯克希尔意识、懂商业的股东。我们不会因为有谁是知名教育家之类就请他进董事会。我们要的是那种会思考「如何为自己和合伙人把生意经营好」的人。

再往下是经理人——他们属于这种文化，是选择带着公司加入我们的。有时已经是第二代、第三代，比如内布拉斯加家具城，也共享这套文化。

它完美吗？不，远不完美。你不可能让所有人都用同一种方式思考。我们有很多极具独立思考的人经营着各项业务，有些人的政治信念不同，看事情的角度多少也和我们不同。

但说到共同、强大、正向的文化，我不认为有哪家大型上市公司比伯克希尔更好。我认为这还会延续，因为人们在很大程度上是主动选择加入它的。文化会传递：你做与文化一致的事，你说什么就做什么。你不会一边说「我们是了不起的合伙关系」，一边给自己投出巨额期权；然后一帮人又给自己发次级期权，免得看起来像把好处全占了——前几天我还读到一笔交易，兑现起来可能是几百亿、几千亿美元。名字我就不点了。

我们的文化已经是最好的那一档，而且我认为总体上还在变强。总有少数人并不完全认同，它不是 100%。但已经非常接近，而且随着时间越来越接近。我们会努力保持行为一致，强化它而不是稀释它。我认为这不仅对查理和我有效，对我们的继任者也会非常有效。它不会完美。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:14:05）：每次来开这种会、坐在管理者午餐会上，午餐结束时我都更强烈地感到伯克希尔的文化和价值观在现有管理层离开后还会延续很久。事实上，我认为在所有现有管理者都走了以后仍会延续。我们在这里开创的东西足够好、会持续。原因之一是它没那么容易复制。

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:57）：但它不会产生过去那样的回报。

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:01）：不，我认为它会延续很久，原因很简单——

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:08）：它管用。

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:09）：——它值得延续很久。

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:10）：它管用。

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:11）：而且它会继续管用。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:09:24）：OK, Gregg.

**Gregg Warren**（01:09:28）：Warren, you've noted time and again that there is a strong common culture shared across Berkshire subsidiaries built on a commitment to honesty and integrity, a focus on the long-term, and an emphasis on customer care. And it's also critical to find cultures that mesh well with Berkshire's when acquiring operating companies. In most cases, the managers that are currently running these subsidiaries are the same individuals who are members of the families that originally sold their firms to Berkshire, leaving them with a vested interest in the businesses they are running and a strong connection to the culture they tend to share in common with Berkshire. It seems to me that the greater challenge is in ensuring that the large publicly-traded firms that have been acquired and account for a meaningful and growing amount of Berkshire's overall value, stay the course. Could you comment on whether or not this is the case and what the greatest challenge is for you and Charlie when it comes to not only maintaining Berkshire's culture but in finding firms that would fit in well with what you've built?

**Warren Buffett**（01:10:19）：Yeah, I think the culture is very, very strong. And I think it gets reinforced — frankly, I think it gets reinforced by the shareholders we have. I mean, we have a different body of shareholders and we look at those shareholders, I think, in a somewhat different way than a good many other companies do. I mean, I think there are a fair number of public companies that wish they didn't have, you know, public shareholders. We're happy to have public shareholders. And we like having individual shareholders. And we don't favor institutions. And we're not going to, you know, give guidance and talk especially to them on investor calls and all that sort of thing. We want our — we want it to be directly with — we want shareholders who are partners, basically. And it begins with that. It goes to the directors. We have directors who are not — well, I've been on 19 boards, and I've never seen another board like ours. And I think it's terrific that we've got the people who represent, in many cases, lots of shares themselves. They didn't gets special deals. It's a group of owner-oriented, Berkshire-conscious, business-savvy owners. And we don't have anybody on the board because they're a leading, you know, educator or whatever it may be. We want people who, basically, think about how to run a business well for themselves and for their partners. And we've got managers who fit into that culture, who have chosen that culture in coming with us. And sometimes we have the second or the third or fourth generation, say at the Nebraska Furniture Mart, that share that. Is it perfect? No, it's far from perfect. I mean, you don't get everybody thinking the same way. We have people — we have people that are very independently minded running a lot of businesses. And some of them have different political beliefs, they have different — they see through different lenses than we do, to some degree. But in terms of having a common, strong, positive culture, I don't think there's any big public company that has it any better than Berkshire. And I think that will continue because people opt into it to a great deal. Cultures get passed along. You do things that are consistent with the culture, so you do — what you talk about is what you do. And you don't find people saying, you know, "We're a wonderful partnership," and then voting themselves, you know, huge options. And then a whole bunch of other people will say options beneath them because they can't look like they're taking it all for themselves, and arranging — I read about some deal where it could pay off with many, many, many billions of dollars, the other day. We won't name names. But we've got as good a culture as you can get. And I would say, net, it grows stronger. We have a few people all of the time that really don't buy into it entirely. I mean, it is not 100 percent. But it's as close to it. And I think it gets closer all the time as we go along. And we will keep — we will try to keep behaving in a way that reinforces it and doesn't dilute it. And I think that will not only work for Charlie and me but it will work for our successors very well. It won't be perfect. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:14:05）：Every time I come to one of these meetings and sit in the manager's luncheon, I feel more strongly at the end of the luncheon that the culture and values of Berkshire Hathaway will go on and on for a long time after the present management is gone. In fact, I think it'll go on after all of the present managers are gone. I think we've started something here that will work well enough that it will last. And one of the reasons it will last is it's not that damned easy to duplicate. So the one that is present is likely to just keep going and going. Think of how little direct copying of the Berkshire system there's been.

**Warren Buffett**（01:14:57）：But it won't produce the returns it's produced in the past even.

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:01）：No, I think it's going to last a long time for a very simple reason. It's going to —

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:08）：It works.

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:09）：— deserve to last a long time.

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:10）：It works.

**Charlie Munger**（01:15:11）：And it's going to work.
