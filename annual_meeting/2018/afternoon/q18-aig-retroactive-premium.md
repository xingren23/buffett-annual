## 问题

AIG 追溯保费 102 亿。若最终赔付 182 亿正确，浮存金成本会不会高于借款成本？

## 回答要点

1. 「以成本会更低的预期进入交易」
2. AIG 先付前 250 亿，之后伯克希尔承担
3. 全球最大的商业财产险公司之一的巨额追溯安排
4. 「我们仍然感觉还好」

## 关键引述

> **“we certainly go in with the idea that it will be — the cost will be lower”**
>
> 「我们当然是以成本会更低的预期进入的」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:32）：好了，乔纳森？（笑）

**Jonathan Brandt**（01:25:34）：伯克希尔去年初从 AIG 收到 102 亿美元的追溯保费。如果最终赔付从 182 亿上调的估计被证明正确，考虑有利税务属性后，浮存金成本会不会高于伯克希尔借 100 亿类似期限债务的成本？

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:55）：嗯，我们入场时当然是以「成本会更低」为前提的。这个情况很有意思。

本质上，AIG 是全球最大的财产意外险公司之一，在商业财产意外险领域尤其如此。他们说：「我们想把 2015 年 12 月 31 日之前国内业务很大比例上发生的损失全部给你，前 250 亿美元我们自己承担。我们付完 250 亿之后，你再承担接下来 250 亿中的 80%。」为此他们付给我们 100 亿美元。

如果我们对赔多少、什么时候赔的估算是对的，结果就应该好于我们借同样金额的钱。我们做过十来笔，也许 12 笔，这样的大交易，保持着纪录。十多年前我们为伦敦劳合社做过，现在又跟 AIG 做了一笔。

有时候我们的估算偏低，有时候到目前为止偏高。AIG 刚说，我想他们在 2015 年 12 月 31 日之前的这些损失上已付了 150 多亿美元。但随着时间离损失发生越来越远，赔付会慢慢渗出来。

所以我要说，我们对此感觉还不错，而我们总会在某个方向上出错。估算二三十年后才结清的损失，人人都会出错。但到目前为止，这一组交易整体上我们是妥当的。AIG 这笔我认为我们会妥当，我想 AIG 也认为我们会妥当：他们入场有自己充分的理由。所以看起来没问题。

抱歉讲了这些技术性的东西，但乔纳森总问我这类问题，所以我得准备好，把它们答清楚。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:32）：Anyway, Jonathan? (Laughs)

**Jonathan Brandt**（01:25:34）：Berkshire received a 10.2 billion retroactive premium from AIG early last year. If the upwardly revised estimate of 18.2 billion of ultimate claims proves to be correct, will the cost of float, adjusted for favorable tax attributes, likely be lower or higher than what Berkshire would have paid to borrow 10 billion for a similar duration?

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:55）：Well, we certainly go in with the idea that it will be — the cost will be lower. And it's an interesting situation. We — essentially, AIG, which is one of the largest property-casualty, particularly commercial property-casualty companies in the world, said, "We want to give you all of the losses that we incurred in a very big percentage of our domestic business before December 31st of 2015, and we will pay the first 25 billion. And then after we pay 25 billion and AIG pays $25 billion, then you pay 80 percent of the next 25 billion." And they gave us $10 billion for doing that. And that's — If we are correct about our estimates of how much money will be paid and when it will be paid, we should come out being better off than if we had borrowed a similar amount. We have a history of doing 10 or so — maybe 12 — big deals like that. We hold the record. We did it for Lloyd's of London 10 or more years ago, and we did it now with AIG. And sometimes we've been on the low side in our estimate, and sometimes we've been on the high side so far. AIG just said that they — I think they paid 15-and-a-fraction billion on these pre-12/31/2015 losses. They paid 15-and-a-fraction billion. But the payments tend to trickle down over years as you get further away from when the losses occurred. So I would say that we still feel OK about it, and we'll be wrong one way or the other. Everybody is when you estimate losses that may not get settled for 20 or 30 years. But so far, on the group as a whole of these deals we've done, we've been OK. And I think on the AIG thing, we think we'll be OK. And I think AIG thinks we'll be OK. I mean, they entered into it for good reasons of their own. So it looks OK. Sorry to get into this technical stuff, but Jonathan always asks me questions like that, so I have to be ready to — I want to answer them.
