## 问题

退出卡夫亨氏董事会正值公司要做大收购。你是否从根本上反对敌意收购和激进投资？

## 回答要点

1. 「不认为敌意收购有什么根本错误」——只是我们不做
2. 芒格：「已经有钱了还卷入纷争——太疯狂了。」
3. 3G 的人是极好的管理者和伙伴
4. 巴菲特做过 19 家上市公司董事，决定不再加入；芒格只有好市多和《每日期刊》
5. 「一般来说你影响力很小、花很多时间」

## 关键引述

> **“It's insane.”**
>
> 「太疯狂了。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:52）：好，贝基。

**Becky Quick**（01:35:56）：这个问题来自 Brightstar Capital Partners 的约翰·赫加蒂（音）。他说：「沃伦，你在卡夫亨氏正要做大收购（比如联合利华）时退出了董事会。你是否从根本上反对敌意收购、激进投资和竞争性委托书争夺？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:21）：嗯，我们自己不会发起敌意收购。我不认为这个做法本质上有什么错。拿《财富》500 强来说，我确信这 500 家并不都是由世界上最好的管理层经营，有些甚至算不上对投资者最友好的管理层。所以我不觉得对一家公司发起敌意收购是什么邪恶的事。

只是我们不会做，也不想卷进去。我们喜欢被我们加入的管理层喜欢，因为我们指望他们经营公司，我们并不带一批擅长改造企业的人进去。在涉及委托书争夺这类事情上，我们很少站在管理层的对立面——非常少。但我们也不排除。

我们不认为每个管理层都有权永远待在自己的位子上，他们对企业并没有终身所有权。但发起这种事完全不是我们的风格；我们会极其、极其不可能去支持一场争夺。不过 50 年里，我们确实对管理层提出的几项股票期权议案投过反对票；几年前在可口可乐，我们用弃权表达过看法。

我们并不认为，股东在「谁该经营公司、薪酬是否合适」这类问题上持不同意见就是邪恶的。公司终究还是股东的。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:38:32）：我没什么要补充的。我不羡慕那些总卷入不友好纷争的人。想想你已经有钱了还干这个。太疯狂了。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（01:38:51）：是的，是的，我们绝对不是主动找这种事。确实有些公司该被挑战，它们偶尔也会提出该被挑战的议案。但再说一次，这不是我们的主要活动。

顺便说，这个问题是就卡夫亨氏问的。3G 的人是极其出色的经理人，一直是极好的伙伴。在跟他们合作之前，我已经决定不再担任任何上市公司董事。我当过 19 家公司的董事，那很花时间。他们问我能不能去一段时间，我去了。但那其实是每年七天半左右——如果你在银行董事会，可能还要多不少。

在上市公司董事会，通常就是每季度一次会，也许再加一次。87 岁了，我想我已经明白那是怎么回事，也没什么问题，但我不想一年花七天半——我五分钟就能打电话给我信任和敬佩的董事，问清楚发生了什么，或者有什么问题。所以我们是他们的伙伴，也很高兴做他们的伙伴。现在卡夫亨氏董事会有我们两个人，他们可以跑路，我可以待在家里。

查理，你一生中当过多少家上市公司董事？当然你在 Costco……

**Charlie Munger**（01:40:52）：哦，好市多——除了——

**Warren Buffett**（01:40:55）：堪萨斯城电力。

**Charlie Munger**（01:40:56）：——除了像《每日期刊》我自己持有部分、好市多是唯一的上市公司董事会——

**Warren Buffett**（01:41:02）：你在堪萨斯城电力待过。

**Charlie Munger**（01:41:05）：——除了伯克希尔或我个人持有的。

**Warren Buffett**（01:41:08）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（01:40:59）：我在堪萨斯城电力和照明公司待过。天啊，那可真久远了。但基本上没再进过。我不羡慕到处赶董事会的人。

**Warren Buffett**（01:41:07）：对，一般来说你影响力很小、花很多时间。麻烦是你去开董事会——尤其是国际的——他们觉得必须占用你足够的时间才不枉你飞几千英里。所以你听到很多走过场的东西——我会走神。好。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:35:52）：OK, Becky.

**Becky Quick**（01:35:56）：This comes from John Hegarty (PH) at Brightstar Capital Partners, who writes, "Warren, you're stepping down from the Kraft Heinz board at a time when the company's looking to do a large acquisition: Unilever, for example. Do you fundamentally disagree with the combative nature of hostile bids, activist investing, and competitive proxy contests?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:36:21）：Well, we will not make hostile tenders ourselves. I do not believe that there's anything fundamentally wrong with the idea. I mean, if you take the Fortune 500 companies, I'm sure that all 500 are not managed by the best, or in some cases even the friendliest to investor managements in the world. So I don't think it's evil or anything to conduct a hostile offer for a company. It's just we won't do it, and we don't want to get into that. We like being liked by the managements that we join, because we're counting on them to run the company, and we're not bringing in a whole bunch of people that know how to change businesses. We seldom take a position opposite the management — very seldom — on anything involving a proxy, but — contest of sorts. But we don't rule it out. We don't think every management is entitled to be — you know, they don't have a lifetime hold on their business. But it's not our style at all to — well, we won't do it in terms of initiating it ourselves, and we'd be very, very, very unlikely to support a contest. But we have voted against a couple of propositions over 50 years that managements have had — made — in relation to stocks options. We withheld a vote at Coca-Cola a few years ago to express our opinion. But we don't think it's evil for the shareholders, in some cases, to have different opinions about who should run the company, or whether compensation's appropriate, or matters of that sort. The stockholders still own the company. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:38:32）：I've got nothing to add to that. I don't envy these people that are in these unfriendly uproars all the time. Imagine doing that after you're already rich. It's insane. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:38:51）：Yeah. Yeah, we are definitely not looking for it. We don't — There are certainly companies that deserve challenge. And they propose things that deserve challenge occasionally. But again, it's not our main activity. And incidentally, this has — the question was asked in reference to Kraft Heinz. The people at 3G are great, great managers. They've been wonderful partners. I had made a determination, before we got involved there, I was going to be on no more public boards. I'd been on 19 of them, and it takes a lot of time. And they asked me if I'd go on for a while, and I did. But it really is, like, seven-and-a-half days or something. And if you're on a bank board, it may be quite a bit more than that. I mean, there just — Being on a public board usually means quarterly meetings plus maybe an extra one. And, you know, at 87, I think I've now learned what happens, and it's fine, but I don't want to spend seven-and-a-half days a year when I — maybe I can call up people that I trust and admire who are on the board in five minutes, you know, and find out what's going on or whatever it may be, any questions that come up. And so we are their partners, and delighted to be their partners. And now we have two people on the board of Kraft Heinz, and they can do the traveling and I can stay home. Charlie, how many public — you're on Costco, of course, over the — your lifetime, how many public boards?

**Charlie Munger**（01:40:52）：Oh, I — Costco — except for something —

**Warren Buffett**（01:40:55）：Kansas City Power.

**Charlie Munger**（01:40:56）：— like The Daily Journal where I own part of it, Costco's the only public board —

**Warren Buffett**（01:41:02）：You were on Kansas City Power.

**Charlie Munger**（01:41:05）：— if it wasn't Berkshire or something I owned personally.

**Warren Buffett**（01:41:08）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（01:40:59）：I was on Kansas City Power and Light. Boy, that goes way back. But basically it hasn't happened. I don't envy people who float around to a lot of different board meetings.

**Warren Buffett**（01:41:07）：No, generally speaking you have very little influence and spend a lot of time. And the trouble is, if you're going to a board meeting, particularly if you get to the international, and sometimes they feel they have to have one that's international, you know, they feel they have to take up a fair amount of your time or it wouldn't have been worth coming, you know, thousands of miles for. So you get a lot of the show and tell stuff and — that — I find my mind drifting. OK. (Laughter)
