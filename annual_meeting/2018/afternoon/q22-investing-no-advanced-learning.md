## 问题

今天要成功是否仍回到《聪明的投资者》第 8 章？怎么看 CFA 等证书？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特上过三所商学院，每所都遇到一两个好老师——「我们不反商学院」
2. 格雷厄姆的方法「不可扩展」——600 万美元的基金
3. 芒格：格雷厄姆晚年承认其方法不一定在所有条件下管用
4. 「你以前知道的永远不够」——「独腿人踢屁股比赛」的比喻
5. 「听到理论被形容为『优雅』时要当心」
6. 芒格用过时白内障手术解释为什么金融老师教错东西

## 关键引述

> **“What you formerly knew is never enough.”**
>
> 「你以前知道的永远不够。」

> **“You're like a one-legged man in an ass-kicking contest.”**
>
> 「你就像踢屁股比赛里的独腿人。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:01）：6 号站台。

**Audience Member**（01:45:04）：你好，我是密苏里州圣路易斯的布雷迪·里奇（音）。1996 年起就是股东。

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:09）：太好了。

**Audience Member**（01:45:10）：沃伦，你和查理过去批评过商学院及其教学。关于价值投资，在《格雷厄姆-多德都市的超级投资者》中，你们展示了不同背景投资者的回报，核心教训是「遵循理念是关键」。今天要成功，是不是仍然回到《聪明的投资者》第 8 章？你们怎么看 CFA 等项目和证书？

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:39）：最后一部分是什么？

**Charlie Munger**（01:45:41）：嗯，他在说——我们怎么看——

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:43）：对，商学院等等。

**Charlie Munger**（01:45:45）：——商学院等等。

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:46）：我没听到最后一——

**Charlie Munger**（01:45:48）：他们更好了——

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:49）：哦，他刚才向我挑战打一局桥牌。好。（笑）我上过三所商学院，每一所都遇到一两位好老师。有一所我是专门为某位老师去的。但在每一所，我都找到了一两位真正让我受益很多的老师。

所以我们完全不反对商学院。我们确实认为，比如 30 年前、40 年前那个「教士团」——有效市场理论那一套——在我们看来偏离投资的现实太远了。

如果让我在前两三所商学院排名前五的毕业生里挑人，另一个选择是某个聪明、但把《聪明的投资者》第八章完全吃透的人（那对他来说是天然的，是长在骨头里的），我会选那个「第八章」的人。

我们做的事并不复杂。它必须是有纪律的，但不需要超高的智商。有几条基本原理极其重要，你确实得懂会计；出去跟消费者聊聊、在很多方面开始像消费者那样思考，也有帮助。但它不需要高深的学问。

我自己确实……你知道，我当年不想上大学，所以如果高中毕业就停学、只读我读过的那些书，我会做得更好还是更差，我也不知道。我认为，如果你碰上几位伟大的老师，他们在某种程度上真的改变了你看世界的方式，那你就很幸运。在学术界能找到，在日常生活里也能找到。

我极其幸运，遇到过伟大的老师，包括查理。查理就是一位了不起的老师。任何地方，只要能找到一个人给你关于你原本不懂之事的洞见、甚至让你成为比原来更好的人，那就是极大的幸运，你要把它用足。如果在学术界找到，就用足；如果在生活其他部分找到，也用足。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:08）：嗯，你发现格雷厄姆时他不按常规出牌，而且非常聪明。当然对你很有吸引力。然后你发现它管用、坐着就能赚大钱（笑）你立刻就皈依了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:47）：现在仍然吸引我。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:53）：但世界变了。本·格雷厄姆去世前就认识到，他寻找低估公司的那个具体办法，未必在任何时代、任何条件下都管用。对我们来说确实如此。

我们逐渐转变成：去买好公司，在它们被低估的时候；而不是去买烂公司，在它们被低估的时候。当然，这对我们很有效。

格雷厄姆本人，在很大程度上活得比他那一套玩法更久，他亲眼看着这套东西大部分消失。比如说，找到一家公司，市价只有营运资本的三分之一，你算出来它很容易清算、每一美元市价能分到三美元——祝你好运，在今天市场上找找看。就算找得到，也太小了，对伯克希尔毫无用处。所以我们得学另一套玩法。

这也是给在座所有年轻人的一课：如果你要活得久，就得一直学。

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:58）：对。

**Charlie Munger**（01:50:59）：你以前知道的永远不够。如果你不学会不断修正以前的结论、变得更好——我总用同一个比喻——你就像踢屁股比赛里的独腿人。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:22）：如果有人能想出另一个比喻，请寄给我。（笑）

顺便说，格雷厄姆有一点很重要：他的方法不可规模化。你没法用真正的大资金去做。我在格雷厄姆-纽曼公司工作时，他是所有分析师中的泰斗，那个时代他的智力无人能及。但我们那只投资基金是 600 万美元，与之并行运作的合伙基金也大约 600 万，所以我们能动用 1,200 万美元。

当然你可以按通胀做调整，但那仍然是很小的数目，本质上不可规模化。事实是格雷厄姆并不在意，因为他对自己赚很多钱本来就没兴趣，所以他没有理由去寻找那种能不断做大、越滚越大的方法。

所以，第八章把股票看作一门生意，这个视角价值巨大；第二十章讲安全边际，价值巨大。但那都不是复杂的东西。

**Charlie Munger**（01:52:44）：我终于想通了为什么公司金融老师总教一些错的东西。我年轻时眼睛有问题，去看了一个非常有名的眼科医生。我发现他在做一个完全过时的白内障手术——有了更好的方法他还在用刀割。我说「你为什么在顶尖医学院做完全过时的手术？」他说「查理，那手术教起来太美妙了。」（笑）公司金融就是这样。他们拿到这些公式，教学体验很好。（笑）你给一个公式、出一个题，学生套公式，你会有一种做了有价值的事的感觉。（笑）那里只有一个东西。其余全是胡扯。

**Warren Buffett**（01:53:32）：对，每当听到一个理论被形容为「优雅」时，要当心。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:53:41）：没错。

And I'd like to challenge you to a round of bridge tomorrow. (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（01:45:41）：Well, he was talk — what do we think about —

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:43）：Yeah, business schools and all that.

**Charlie Munger**（01:45:45）：— business schools and all that.

**Warren Buffett**（01:45:49）：Oh, he's challenged me to a round of bridge. OK. The — (Laughter) I went to three business schools, and at each I found a teacher or two. I went to one specifically to get a given teacher. But at each one of them I found a teacher or two that I really got a lot out of. The — so we're not anti-business school here at all. We do think that the priesthood, say, 30 years ago, for example, in terms of — or 40 years ago — in terms of efficient market theory and everything. They strayed pretty far, in our view, from the reality of investing. And I would rather have a person — if I could hire somebody among the top five graduates of number one, two or three of the business schools, and my choice was somebody that had — was bright — but had chapter eight of "The Intelligent Investor" absolutely — it just was natural to them — they had it in their bones, basically — I'd take the person from chapter eight. This is not — what we do is not a complicated business. It's got to be a disciplined business, but it is — it does not require a super IQ or anything of the sort. And there are a few fundamentals that are incredibly important, and you do have to understand accounting. And it helps to get out and talk to consumers and start thinking like a consumer in many ways in certain — and all of that. But it just doesn't require advanced learning. And — I — I certainly — you know, I didn't want to go to college, so I don't know whether I would have done better or worse if I'd just quit after high school, you know, and read the books I read and all of that. I think that if you run into a few great teachers, and they really change the way you see the world to some degree, you know, you're lucky. And you can find them in — you can find them in academia and you can find them in ordinary life. And I've been extraordinarily lucky in having great teachers, including Charlie. I mean, Charlie's been a wonderful teacher. And, you know — Anyplace you can find somebody that gives you insights into things you didn't understand before, maybe makes you a better person than you would have been before, you know, you get — that's very lucky, and you want to make the most of it. If you can find it in academia, make the most of it. And if you can find it in the rest of your life, make the most of it. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:08）：Well, when you found Ben Graham he was unconventional, and he was very smart. And of course, that was very attractive to you. And then when you found out it worked and you could make a lot of money while you're sitting on your ass — (laughter) — of course you were an instant convert. And so —

**Warren Buffett**（01:49:47）：It still appeals to me, actually. I mean — (Laughter)

**Charlie Munger**（01:49:53）：But the world changed. Before he died, Bill Graham — I mean, Ben Graham — recognized that the exact way he sought undervalued companies wouldn't necessarily work for all times under all conditions. And that's certainly the way it worked for us. We gradually morphed into trying to buy the better companies when they were underpriced, instead of the lousy companies when they were underpriced. And of course, that worked pretty well for us. And Ben Graham, he outlived the game that he played personally most of the time. He lived to see most of it fade away. I mean, just to find some company that's selling for one third of its working capital, and figure out it could easily be liquidated and distribute $3 for every dollar of market price. Lots of luck if you can find those in the present market. And if you can find them, they're so small that Berkshire wouldn't find them of any use anyway. So we've had to learn a different game. And that's a lesson for all the young people in the room. If you're going to live a long time, you have to keep learning.

**Warren Buffett**（01:50:58）：Yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（01:50:59）：What you formerly knew is never enough. So if you don't learn to constantly revise your earlier conclusions and get better (inaudible), you are — I always use the same metaphor. You're like a one-legged man in an ass-kicking contest. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（01:51:22）：If anybody has suggestions for another metaphor, send them to me. (Laughter) Graham, incidentally, one point, important point. Graham was not scalable. I mean, you could not do with really big money. And when I worked for Graham-Newman Corp, here he was, the dean of all analysts. And you know, he was an intellect above all others around that time. But our — the investment fund was $6 million, and the partnership that worked in tandem with the investment company also had about $6 million in it. So we had 12 million bucks we were working with. Now, you can make adjustments for inflation and everything. But it was just a tiny amount. It wasn't really scalable. And the truth is that Graham didn't care, because he really wasn't interested in making a lot of money for himself. So he had no reason to want to find something that could go on and on, become larger and larger. And so the utility of chapter eight, in terms of looking at stocks as a business, is of enormous value. The utility of chapter 20 about a margin of safety is of enormous value. But that's not complicated stuff.

**Charlie Munger**（01:52:44）：I finally figured out why the teachers of corporate finance often teach a lot of stuff that's wrong. They were still cutting with a knife after better procedures had been invented. I said, "Why are you in a great medical school performing absolute obsolete operations?" And he said, "Charlie, it's such a wonderful operation to teach." (Laughter) Well, that's what happens in corporate finance. They get these formulas, and it's a fine teaching experience. (Laughter) You give them a formula, you present the problem, they use the formula. You get a real feeling of worthwhile activity. (Laughter) There's only one there. It's all balderdash.

**Warren Buffett**（01:53:32）：Yeah, whenever you hear a theory described as elegant, watch out, you know. (Laughter)
