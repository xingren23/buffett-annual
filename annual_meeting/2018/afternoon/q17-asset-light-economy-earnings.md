## 问题

1999 年您说税后利润不会长期超 6%。2008 年以来利润一直很高。预测错了吗？

## 回答要点

1. 美国市值最大的四家公司「本质上不需要任何净有形资产」
2. 芒格：「预测有点偏差，但投资没做错。经济预测很难。」
3. 巴菲特：「之后股市的表现跟我们的预测挺吻合的。」
4. 「理由错了但结果对了。」

## 关键引述

> **“it's hard to make these economic predictions”**
>
> 「经济预测很难」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:19:59）：卡罗尔？

**Carol Loomis**（01:20:00）：你在 1999 年《财富》杂志那篇文章里说，你认为税后企业利润不可能长期维持在 6% 以上——不仅因为竞争，也因为公共政策。你在文中写道：「如果企业投资者作为一个整体，要吃掉越来越大的经济蛋糕份额，其他群体就只能接受更小的份额。这将理所当然地引发政治问题。」

2008 年以来，税后企业利润占 GDP 的 8% 到 10%。你认为这是美国经济的永久性转变吗？当然，我们还得考虑最新的税改法案。还是说企业利润会回到 20 世纪常态的 GDP 的 4% 到 6%？

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:01）：嗯，这段时间的发展很有意思，其实起点还要再往前一点。现在美国市值最大的四家公司——在一个 30 万亿美元的市场里——这四家本质上不需要任何净有形资产。

往回看很多年，如果看当年市值最大的公司（卡罗尔以前编《财富》500 强），那是 AT&T、通用汽车、埃克森美孚这一类，都是要靠大量资本才能产生盈利的公司。所以过去二三十年，从整体上看，美国产业的盈利能力提高了惊人的幅度。

你看标普 500 的回报、看盈利占有形净资产的比重。其余的部分是这样：如果你买一家净值 100 万美元的公司，付了 10 亿美元，它的净值仍然只有 100 万，你只是付得更贵。所以企业基本盈利能力是巨大的，哪怕你的投资价格高得多。

所以发生的事是：如果把标普 500 有形净资产的盈利能力和 20 年前比，那真是惊人。原因在于，这个经济体在某种程度上已经变成「轻资产经济」。那四家公司占整个美国上市公司市值的将近 10%，而它们基本上不占用资金。世界在变。税率下降后它们会赚得更多。我认为人们还没有完全消化这些信息对市场意味着什么。

卡内基建钢厂，还清贷款，再借钱建下一座，如此反复，那是资本极度密集的。一个行业接一个行业都是如此，AT&T 也是资本极度密集的。现在钱在轻资产里，不只是轻资产，还有负资产。比如 IBM，它没有有形净资产，是负的有形净值。这没什么不好，非常好。但这不是我们 30 年前生活的世界。

从这个意义上说，1999 年我写下那些话时，没预见到这一点。它并没有特别改变重资产公司的盈利能力，不像石油。如果你拿 90 年代资本最密集的五个行业来看，我不认为它们有形资产的盈利大幅上升了。但你会发现，另一群人搬到了一个根本不需要净有形资产、或者只需要极少净有形资产的地方。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:24:47）：还有很多金融工程推高了杠杆，即使在资本密集型行业。沃伦写那篇学术文章时预测可能有点偏差，但他投资没做错。这说明经济预测很难。

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:18）：好，乔——

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:19）：我们那次不太对，沃伦。

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:21）：对，实际上，之后股市的表现跟我们的预测挺吻合的。（笑）

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:27）：是的。确实如此。

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:28）：对。理由错了但结果对了。（笑）或者理由对了但结果错了。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:19:59）：Carol?

**Carol Loomis**（01:20:00）：In your 1999 article in Fortune magazine, you stated your belief that after-tax corporate profits were unlikely to hold much above 6 percent for any sustained period, due not only to competition but also to public policy. You stated in the article, "If corporate investors, in aggregate, are going to eat an ever-growing portion of the economic pie, some other group will have to settle for a smaller portion. That would justifiably raise political problems." Since 2008, after-tax corporate profits have been 8 to 10 percent of GDP. Do you believe that is a permanent shift in the U.S. economy? And of course, we have to think about the latest tax bill. Or will corporate profits revert back to the 4 percent to 6 percent of GDP range that was normal in the 20th century?

**Warren Buffett**（01:21:01）：Well, it's been an interesting development during that period. It goes back a little bit before that period. But you now have the four largest companies, by market value, in the United States — a $30 trillion market — you have four companies that essentially don't need any net tangible assets. And if you go back many years, I mean, if you looked to the largest companies — Carol used to put out the Fortune 500 list. And you know, it would be AT&T and General Motors, and it was companies that — Exxon Mobil — it was companies that just required lots of capital in order to produce earnings. So American industry has gotten incredibly more profitable, in aggregate, in the last 20 or 30 years. You look at the return on the S&P 500, the earnings as a percent of net tangible assets, and the rest is just, you know, if you buy a company that has a million dollars' worth of net worth and you pay a billion for it, it still only had the million dollars' of net worth. I mean you just paid more for it. So the basic profitability of the company is huge, even though your investment may be at a significantly higher price. So that what has happened is that, I think if you look at the earnings on tangible net worth of the S&P 500 and compare it to 20 years ago, it is amazing. And that is really due to the fact that this has become somewhat, you could call it an asset-light economy. And you know, those four companies that earn 10 percent of the — they comprise close to 10 percent of the market value of the entire publicly-traded corporate America, they don't — and they don't take any money, basically. And that is a changing world. And they will earn even more money with the tax rate going down. And I don't think people have quite processed all that information in terms of what has gone on in the market. You don't — you know, [Andrew] Carnegie built a steel mill, and then he paid it off. Or he borrowed a little money, and then he built another steel mill, and all of that sort of thing. But it was enormously capital-intensive. And one industry after another. AT&T was enormously capital-intensive. And now the money is in the asset-light — I mean, huge money is in the — not only asset-light business — but the negative asset. You know, IBM even, you know, had — it has no tangible — it has a net — minus tangible net worth. There's nothing wrong with that. It's terrific. But it is not the world we lived in 30 years ago. And in that sense, I didn't see that coming in 1999 when I wrote whatever I wrote there. It hasn't changed the profitability of the asset-heavy companies particularly. I mean, it isn't like oil. If you take the five most capital-intensive industries in the '90s, I don't think you'll find that their earnings on tangible asset have increased a lot. But you will find that this group has moved in that really doesn't — they don't need any net tangible assets at all, or they need very minor amounts. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:24:47）：There's also a lot of financial engineering that's raised leverage, even in the capital-intense businesses. And you know, while Warren may have predicted a little wrong when he wrote that very scholarly article, he didn't invest wrong. And so it just shows that it's hard to make these economic predictions.

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:18）：OK, Jon —

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:19）：We weren't very right on that one, Warren.

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:21）：Yeah, actually, the performance of the stock market since then has been pretty accurate. (Laughs)

**Charlie Munger**（01:25:27）：Yes. That's true.

**Warren Buffett**（01:25:28）：Yeah. Being right for the wrong reason or something. (Laughs) Or wrong for the right reasons.
