## 问题

TTI 自收购以来税前利润翻倍到 4 亿。行业竞争格局和 TTI 的优势是什么？

## 回答要点

1. 保罗·安德鲁斯经营出色，业务基本翻两番
2. 平均单价不到五美分——「像做糖豆生意」
3. 芒格：「太难做了、竞争对手不想碰」——用死马回收生意做比方
4. 管理近百万种零件、快速全球交付——「不容易管理」
5. 「马会消失，但这些零件不会。」

## 关键引述

> **“if it can't stand a little mismanagement, it's no business”**
>
> 「经不起一点管理不善，它就不是好生意」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:40）：好，乔纳森？

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:42:43）：TTI 自 11 年前被收购以来是个不错的增长故事，税前利润翻了一倍多到约 4 亿美元。一季度似乎加速了。能否谈谈电子元件分销行业的竞争格局和 TTI 的优势？是行业好还是 TTI 的模式和管理团队特别出色？

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:13）：嗯——

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:43:14）：你预计它会继续是伯克希尔增长最快的非保险子公司之一吗？

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:19）：TTI 由一位叫保罗·安德鲁斯的人经营，他为我们做得极其出色。他是很好的人，也是很好的经理人。这几年他把业务规模做到了原来的四倍，去年还在加速，一直加速到今天。

他们分销小的电子元件。他们是一家几十亿美元规模的企业，而平均单价不到五美分，所以有点像做软糖豆生意。只不过这些东西最后装进各种我不懂的精巧机器里。我们的业务总部在达拉斯—沃斯堡地区，全球经营，是一个人在 45 到 50 年前离开通用动力的某个部门后一手建起来的。

一步一步把生意做大——比如我们两个月前刚在韩国收购了一家企业，会是又一个可观的增量。我们在全球做生意，电子元件去年彻底起飞了。他们用一个指标，本质上衡量的是订单储备，他们叫「订单出货比」，只是个专门说法。这个指标去年大幅改善，而且一个月接一个月地改善。

所以外面确实有事在发生，因为没人买这些东西是为了堆在地下室。这些电子元件是要用掉的，有些还在配额供应。我们与供应商关系很好，与客户关系也很好，因为我们的库存比多数竞争对手多。所以尤其在供应紧张时，我们能交货，而且交得很漂亮。

这要归功于保罗。他几年前就开始扩建厂房，幸亏他做了，因为以现在的业务量，我们根本接不住。但这是竞争激烈的生意。你看纽约证券交易所上的艾睿电子，我们有竞争对手。我认为保罗比他们做得好，而且好不少。我很高兴他是伯克希尔大家庭的一员。

将来总有业务放缓的时候，因为他们的客户有自己的周期，业绩会下滑。但长期看，这门生意会增长。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:49）：是的，这是门好生意，因为它太难做，竞争对手不愿意碰。我住在奥马哈的时候，有个人过得非常富足，几乎不用干活。他的生意是收死马、送去炼油。他从来没有竞争对手。（笑）

他常去奥马哈俱乐部，上午 11 点就开始喝酒。那不是什么难做的生意，但从来没有人挤进来跟他竞争。如今很少有人愿意去分销数以亿计、每个值五美分的电子元件。那非常复杂。当然，这门生意在这方面极强，而且越来越强。

所以你说得对。这是一门巨大的成长型生意，某种程度上相当于「收死马炼油」的电子版。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:47:46）：想象一下管理近一百万种不同零件、每种价值很小、还要快速送到全世界客户手里。不容易管理。

**Charlie Munger**（00:48:01）：而且要保持这么多品种有库存。非常复杂。他们非常擅长。

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:11）：对，我们很幸运——

**Charlie Munger**（00:48:12）：当然会增长。马会消失，但这些零件不会。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:38）：查理专门研究那些业主可以整天坐着喝酒的生意（笑）他觉得那才是我们应该竞争或买入的地方。

**Charlie Munger**（00:48:30）：我的理论是，沃伦，如果一门生意经不起一点管理不善，它就不是好生意。（笑）

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:39）：对，我们有时在检验这一点。（笑）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:42:40）：OK, Jonathan?

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:42:43）：TTI has been a nice growth story since Berkshire acquired it 11 years ago, more than doubling its pre-tax earnings to about $400 million due to fine organic growth and at least two successful bolt-on acquisitions. Business momentum appeared to accelerate in the first quarter. Can you please talk about the competitive landscape in the electronic components distribution industry and what TTI's advantages are? Is it just a great industry to be in or is TTI's business model and/or management team special?

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:13）：Well —

**Jonathan Brandt**（00:43:14）：Do you expect it to continue to be one of Berkshire's faster growing non-insurance subsidiaries?

**Warren Buffett**（00:43:19）：TTI is run by a fellow named Paul Andrews who's done an absolutely sensational job with us. He's a wonderful man. He's a wonderful manager. And in the last — he's quadrupled the business, basically, but in the last year and accelerating right to this point. They distribute little electronic components. They actually — their average — they're a many-billion dollar business — and their average item is less than a nickel that they sell. So it's kind of like being in the jellybean business or something like that. Except these things go into all kinds of fancy machines that I don't understand. And we have a worldwide operation based in the Dallas, Fort Worth area. And built by one man who left a division of General Dynamics 45 or 50 years ago. And step-by-step built up this business — like we just bought within the last two months, we bought an operation in South Korea that will be another substantial addition. We do business worldwide. And electronic components that have absolutely taken off in the last year. And they use something called, you know, well, it's essentially a measure of backlog. And book-to-build is the ratio they call it. But it's just kind of a special term. The — but, it's grown — I mean, it's just improved dramatically in the last year. And it continues month after month. So something is going on out there because nobody buys these things to store them in their basement or anything of the sort. I mean, these get used, these electronic components. Some of them are on allocation. We have a great relationship with suppliers. We have a very good relationship with our customers because we carry more inventory than most of our competitors. So particularly when the business is tight we can deliver and do a very first-class job doing it. So I give credit to Paul. He increased his physical facility, started on that a few years ago. And it's a godsend that he did it because with the business going through there now we wouldn't have been able to handle it. But it's a competitive business. I mean, if you look at Arrow Electronics, you know, on the New York Stock Exchange — we've got competitors. I think Paul is doing a better job, by a considerable margin, than they are. And I'm delighted as a part of the Berkshire family. There will be times when that business slows down because their customers, you know, will have their own cycles. And what it does will go down. But over time that business is going to grow. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（00:46:49）：Yeah, it's a wonderful business because it's so difficult to do that competitors don't want try it. When I lived in Omaha there was a man who lived in great prosperity and almost no work. And his business was gathering up and rendering dead horses. And he never had any competitors. (Laughter) He used to come up to the Omaha Club and start drinking about 11 in the morning. It was not a difficult business. But nobody ever crowded him with new competition. And very few people want to distribute zillions of electronic parts that are worth a nickel each. It's very complicated. And of course that business is terribly good at it. And it keeps getting more and more of the same. So you're right. It's a huge growth business which is sort of the electronic equivalent of gathering up and rendering dead horses. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:47:46）：Imagine keeping track of close to a million different items, you know, with very small values attached to them and getting them out to your customer fast because they want them fast, all over the world. You know, and those things are not easy to manage. I mean, yeah.

**Charlie Munger**（00:48:01）：And staying in stock on so many items. It's very complicated. And that business is very good at it.

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:11）：Yeah, we're luck —

**Charlie Munger**（00:48:12）：And of course it'll grow. The horses went away but these parts aren't going to go away. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:38）：Charlie made a profession of studying businesses where the owners could sit around and drink all day and have — (laughter) — he thought that was where we ought to be competing but — or buying.

**Charlie Munger**（00:48:30）：My theory, Warren, is if it can't stand a little mismanagement, it's no business. (Laughter)

**Warren Buffett**（00:48:39）：Yeah and we're testing that sometimes. (Laughter)
