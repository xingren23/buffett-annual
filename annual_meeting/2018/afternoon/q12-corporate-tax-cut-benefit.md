## 问题

企业减税的好处最终落到消费者还是股东？

## 回答要点

1. 人们总是找对自己有利的经济学解释
2. 受监管公用事业：减税好处全部归客户——「不因税率变了就该拿超额回报」
3. 芒格：「我没什么要补充的。」

## 关键引述

> **“the benefits are all supposed to go, and will go, to the utility customer”**
>
> 「减税好处应该全部、也确实会归公用事业客户」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:59）：好，加里。

**Gary Ransom**（00:57:03）：是的，我有一个关于企业税率的问题。我们投资界在争论减税的好处落到哪里。共识是通过竞争最终归消费者。但也许有一部分归股东。长远来看，您认为有一部分好处会留在股东手里吗？也许不只是车险？也许还有您其他业务。

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:34）：嗯，人们通常的做法是：先认定自己想要的答案，然后去雇一位经济学家——或者干脆依附于某位经济学家——用更复杂的方式告诉你一切都会很好，因为它已经发生了。

但答案是：对我们受监管的公用事业来说，好处应该、而且确实会全部给到用电客户，因为我们业绩好才有权获得股本回报，而无权因为税率变了就取得超额回报。同样，如果税率回升，我们预计会得到补偿。所以在这个领域——对我们来说是 50 亿或 60 亿美元——毫无疑问，好处归用户、归消费者。本来也该如此。

剩下的问题是：剩余部分会不会被竞争掉？答案是：有时会，而且很快、幅度很大；有时会慢一些；有时大概不会。

有一点要清楚：企业所得税法的变化总体上有利于股东，也有利于伯克希尔股东。国会通过的就是这个，其意图必然是：既然减税，这一次股东要拿到特别大的一块。你们中有些人政治上认同这一点，有些人不认同。但你们都会同样受益。（笑）

而且我认为这是人性：自己得了好处，就说这对所有人都会好得不得了。究竟会不会，我们以后会知道。在经济学里，衡量单一变量的影响非常、非常、非常困难。你不可能在经济学里只做一件事。人们在物理课上大概学过这个道理，谈什么中国的蝴蝶之类。经济学里每一个问题、每一句论断，你都应该追问「然后呢？」一旦开始追问「然后呢」，你就会开始偏爱那些在政治生活中给出答案、而那答案通常多少对你有利（包括对你的钱包有利）的人。这次我们也看到了这一点。它帮了伯克希尔·哈撒韦的股东。

我要说：一部分会被竞争掉，一部分依法归公用事业，还有一部分惠及伯克希尔股东。查理？

**Charlie Munger**（01:00:36）：我没什么要补充的。

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（00:56:59）：OK, Gary.

**Gary Ransom**（00:57:03）：Yes, I had a question on the corporate tax rate. And we have a debate in my investment world about where the benefits of that cut fall. And I'd say the consensus is going to the consumer as it gets competed away over time. But perhaps some of it sticks to shareholders. And my question is, do you think, over the long run, some of the benefits sticks to shareholders? And maybe it's even beyond auto insurance? Maybe it's other businesses you have as well.

**Warren Buffett**（00:57:34）：Well, what people do generally with that is they take what they want to be the answer for them and then they hire — or they just attach themselves to some economist that gives them a more complicated way of saying it's all going to be wonderful because it's happened. But the answer is that in the case of our regulated public utilities, the benefits are all supposed to go, and will go, to the utility customer, because we're entitled to a return on equity if we perform well. And we're not entitled to get excess returns because our tax rates changed. And similarly, if tax rates would go back up, we would expect to get compensated for that. So in that area — and that was 5 or $6 billion for us. But in that area, absolutely, it goes to the user, the consumer. And it should. Then the question is, with the remainder, does it get competed away or not? And the answer is sometimes it does, sometimes it gets competed very quickly and substantially. Sometimes it may be slow. And other times it probably won't. The one thing to know is that the change in the corporate tax law was good for shareholders, generally, and Berkshire shareholders. I mean — and that's what Congress passed. And the intent had to be that if you were going to cut taxes that shareholders would get a particularly large portion this time. And some of you will agree with that politically and some of you won't agree with it politically. But you'll all benefit equally. (Laughs) And I think it's human nature if you're getting a break to say it's going to work wonderfully for everybody else. And we'll find out whether it will or not. It's very, very, very difficult in economics to measure the impact of single variables. You cannot just do one thing in economics. People kind of learn that in physics and talk about butterflies in China and all that sort of thing. But the — every question you get in economics the next, any statement, you should say "and then what?" And when you get into the "and then whats" you get start favoring people who give an answer to that in political life that happens to usually help you in some way or another, including your pocketbook. And we've see that with this. And it's helped the shareholders at Berkshire Hathaway. I would say that some will be competed away. Some, by law, will go to the utilities and some will benefit Berkshire shareholders. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger**（01:00:36）：I have nothing to add.
