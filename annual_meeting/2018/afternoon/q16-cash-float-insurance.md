## 问题

现金加固定收益总在浮存金 100% 左右。1280 亿中 1160 亿是支持保险运营所需的吗？

## 回答要点

1. 「答案是不」——「我们宁愿数字接近 200 亿而不是 1160 亿」
2. 并不把浮存金和现金挂钩；浮存金一直在增长
3. 部分现金已买了「远比现金更喜欢的证券」——「敬请期待」
4. 一季度营业利润 50 多亿 + 浮存金净流入 20 亿 = 70 亿，但现金反而下降了

## 关键引述

> **“The answer is no.”**
>
> 「答案是不。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

> 与下方「英文原文」逐段对应，按发言顺序排列；术语与专名首次出现时保留英文。

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:14）：好。（掌声）4 号站台。

**Audience Member**（01:15:23）：我叫克里斯蒂安·马克思，来自德国科隆的骄傲股东。很高兴来到这里。我的问题关于伯克希尔的保险业务。看过去二十年的季度资产负债表，现金加固定收益总是在保险浮存金的 100% 左右。因此，从 3 月 1280 亿现金加固定收益中，是否可以说 1160 亿实际是支持保险运营所需的？

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:18）：不，我很感谢——

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:19）：答案是不。对。

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:20）：答案是不担心。不过他值得听一个解释，说说这件事是怎么回事，也许我看这件事的角度和他不同。

我们宁愿手上是接近 200 亿美元现金，而不是 1,160 亿。而且我们并不会把浮存金和「该留多少现金、多少固定收益」挂钩去做测算。事实是：我们的浮存金一直在增长，这是有意设计的，对我们极好；同时现金及等价物也在增长，因为收购的竞争变得强多了，买家手上的钱堆起来了，债务又那么便宜，各种因素叠加。

但我不认为这些是永久性的。实际上，说它们不是永久性的，大致是对的。问题只是它们什么时候变。

正如查理所说，我们完全不把现金及等价物和浮存金绑在一起。浮存金一季度增加了 20 亿美元，而它在任何短期内都不可能大幅缩减。它的结构决定了它不会缩水，也不能缩水。实际上我认为它还会小幅增长一段时间。我一直对它增长之多感到惊讶：我们的浮存金比世界上任何一家财产意外险公司都多得多。而这一切都出自杰克·林沃特盖的那栋小楼。他选那个位置，是因为离网球场近。（笑）好，卡罗尔。哦，还有查理。

**Charlie Munger**（01:18:32）：最近有一些令人鼓舞的进展。部分现金已经出去买了我们远比现金更喜欢的证券。还有很多现金可以部署到比国债好得多的证券上。敬请期待。

**Warren Buffett**（01:18:55）：是的，说得简单点：一季度我们从经营中赚了 50 亿美元出头。我们只花了大致相当于折旧的钱，通常会花得比这更多一些。这是 50 多亿。另外净额上有 20 亿来自浮存金。所以一季度本可以有 70 亿美元加进现金，如果我们什么都没做的话。

但事实上我们的现金及等价物下降了，因为我们净买入股票的金额，比流入的这 70 亿还要多出不少。不过我们的处境是：即便浮存金不变，每周也有约 4 亿美元流进伯克希尔，这很舒服。（笑）我们要做到的是，让流出到生产性资产的钱超过这 4 亿。一季度我们做到了。净额算下来，一季度我们的位置改善了。

**Warren Buffett**（01:15:14）：OK. (Applause) Station 4.

**Audience Member**（01:15:23）：My name is Christian Marx. I'm a proud shareholder from Cologne, Germany. It is my pleasure to be here. My question relates to the Berkshire insurance operations. When I look at the quarterly balance sheets of the last two decades, I noticed a pattern that I kindly ask you to discuss. The sum of cash plus fixed income always hovers around 100 percent of the amount of insurance float. Therefore, my question is, is it fair to say that from the 128 billion of consolidated cash plus fixed income as of March, 116 billion are actually needed to support the insurance operations?

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:18）：No, I appreciate —

**Charlie Munger**（01:16:19）：The answer is no. Yes.

**Warren Buffett**（01:16:20）：The answer is no. But he deserves an explanation of how this — maybe I haven't looked at it the way he's looked at it. We would much rather have a number closer to 20 than to have 116. And we do not correlate or, in effect, measure the float and then decide how much to put or leave in cash, in fixed income. The fact —our float keeps growing. And lately our — which is by design and has been terrific for us — and our cash and cash equivalents have has grown because the competition for acquisitions has become much stronger as — both as money has piled up with the buyers of businesses and because debt has been so cheap and a variety of factors. But I don't think those are necessarily permanent. In fact, it would be reasonably true they aren't permanent. It's just a question of when they change. We are not tying, as Charlie said, we're not tying the cash and cash equivalents at all to float. The float is (inaudible). The float went up $2 billion in the first quarter. And there is no way that that that float can shrink a lot in any short period. It just structurally has been set up in such a way that it will not — it cannot shrink. And actually, I think it'll grow a little bit for a while. I mean, I've always been amazed by how much it has grown. We've got so much more float than any property-causality company in the world. And it's pretty amazing that it all came from that little building that Jack Ringwalt built and picked the location because it was near the tennis courts. (Laughs) OK, Carol. Oh, and Charlie.

**Charlie Munger**（01:18:32）：There are encouraging recent developments. Some of the cash has gone out recently for securities we vastly prefer over the cash. And we have a lot of cash that could be — remaining — that could be deployed in securities we might like a lot better than Treasury notes. So stay tuned.

**Warren Buffett**（01:18:55）：Yeah, to make it very simple, in the first quarter we earned five and — from operations we earned a little over $5 billion. Now, we only spent about our depreciation. Normally we would spend somewhat more than that. But that's 5-and-a-fraction billion. Two billion came, in net, from float. So that's $7 billion that — basically in the first quarter — that would have been added to our cash if we hadn't done something with it. And instead our cash and equivalents went down, because we, net, invested more in equities by some margin than the seven that came in. But we do have this position where, even absent a change in float, about 400 million comes into Berkshire every week, which is very comfortable. (Laughs) And we will — we want to get it so that more than 400 million is going out into productive assets. And we succeeded in doing that in the first quarter. And, net, net we improved our position in the first quarter.
