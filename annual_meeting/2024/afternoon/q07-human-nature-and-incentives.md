## 问题

芒格先生谈委托—代理问题时说，管理者同时拥有财产时，资本主义往往运转得最好。近年一些养老金和资产管理机构的代理人，在伯克希尔并无实质性个人持股，却提出不符合股东经济利益的提案。在你今后无法再投下关键反对票的几十年里，怎样才能限制这些代理人的负面影响？

## 回答要点

1. 巴菲特称这是「很有洞察力的问题」，但承认目前只是被「临时性地」解决了。
2. 他的第一条原则：伯克希尔必须被视为国家的资产而不是威胁，这样才找得到更多解决办法。
3. 他要求董事自己思考、不向常规智慧低头，因为带着资源的人几乎都想把你的资源变成他的好处。
4. 按资产收费的经理若从 1965 年起收 1%，今天每年能从拿不到股息的客户身上收 80 亿美元。
5. 芒格懂心理学与激励，却认为用这些手段操纵他人有失身份；他承认自己用过几次，并以此告诫巴菲特，同时推荐《穷查理宝典》读三四遍。

## 关键引述

> **“But you have to be alert to how - what human nature does to both other people and to you.”**
>
> 「但你必须警惕——人性会对别人、也会对你自己做什么。」

> **“And the best thing to do is just pay them a commission one time and own the stock.”**
>
> 「最好的办法就是一次性付他们一笔佣金，然后自己持有股票。」

> **“but you dont have to stoop to using them yourself.”**
>
> 「但你不必屈尊自己去用它们。」

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:04）：现在——（掌声）——回到贝基。

**Becky Quick**（01:03:12）：这个问题来自匹兹堡的股东 Denny Poland。「芒格先生在描述委托代理问题时说过：当管理财产的人同时拥有财产时，资本主义往往运转得最好。「近年来，一些养老基金和资产管理公司的代理人——他们自身并未持有可观的伯克希尔股份——提出了不符合股东经济利益的议案。「在你不再能用选票压制他们之后的几十年里，怎样才能限制这些代理人的负面影响？」

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:45）：这个问题很有洞察力。它已经被临时性地解决了，但谁知道将来会怎么发展。我只知道：伯克希尔·哈撒韦手里握着好牌。

但你得想得通政治现实：首先，你要被这个国家当作资产。因为被当作资产，你会找到更多的解决空间，这本来也是你欠这个国家的。而如果被当作恶人，更糟的是名副其实的恶人，你的空间就小得多。所以这件事我们时时放在心上，也应该放在董事们心里。而且董事们要自己想，不要向陈规俗见折腰。

从某种意义上说，你不想对人生玩世不恭。但只要你手握重金，几乎每个走近你的人，都在琢磨怎么把你的资源用成他的利益。（笑）政客如此，投行家如此，向你推销什么的人皆如此。我不想冒犯任何人。人寿保险推销员当然觉得买寿险有好处；按管理资产拿报酬的投资经理，自然热衷于向你推销服务。

想一想：假如在座每个人都听某位顾问的建议，每年付 1%，他告诉你钱怎么投。我们 1965 年起步时，他会说：买伯克希尔·哈撒韦。如果他活到今天、还收着 1%，他每年会从我们股东手里收走 80 亿美元，而这些人可从我们这儿一分钱股息都拿不到。（笑）

所以，他们跟你签合同时的利益，跟你自己的利益必然不同。最好的办法就是：付一次佣金，然后自己持有股票。

但你要对人性保持警觉，它对别人做什么，也对你做什么。如果你把这些想得透彻，真听进查理告诉你的话，你就领先绝大多数人了。

说到查理，有件事我得提，真是非常有意思：他很早就弄明白了心理学、人类行为、激励这些东西的分量。他做过演讲，讲 25 种左右，确切数字我记不清了，一个人可以借人性去占另一个人便宜的不同方式。他把这些讲得精彩绝伦，主张要理解别人会怎么做；但他认为亲自用这些手段去操纵人，有失身份。

这是一个多么有意思的人：想透了人类行为的心理学，弄得清怎么成为伟大的保险推销员、华尔街明星，或者靠理解别人的弱点发大财的管理资产赢家；然后决定，识别这些招数至关重要，你甚至要比使招的人更懂它们，但你自己不必俯身去用。

查理告诉我，他一生中弄明白这些之后，用过几次。他不以此为荣，但他也从不对我说谎。所以他对我说：有几次他用过这类技巧，但不再打算用；同时他也要我知道，如果我做了类似的事，也不算十恶不赦，他容得下人会犯浑。

我敢肯定，婚后我在跳舞之类事情上的热情，比婚前收敛了一些。他说：我们都这样，但别再犯。（笑声）

这就是获得人间智慧的一部分。说到人间智慧，查理就出了一本《穷查理宝典》，值得一读再读，三四遍不嫌多。本·格雷厄姆的书我读了五六遍，每读一遍都发现，有些东西我只是需要想得更深一层。它们并不复杂。但如果你手里有像查理给出的这种好教材，把它读几遍，胜过把图书馆里每本书都只翻一遍。

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:04）：And now we move - (applause) - now we move to Becky.

**Becky Quick**（01:03:12）：This question comes from Denny Poland, a shareholder from Pittsburgh. "When describing the principal-agent problem, Mr. Munger said that capitalism often works best when the people managing the property also own the property. "In recent years, agents of pension funds and asset management firms who do not have significant personal ownership stakes in Berkshire have forwarded proposals that were not in the economic interest of shareholders. "What can be done to limit the negative influence of these agents in the decades after youre no longer able to cast significant votes against them?"

**Warren Buffett**（01:03:45）：Well, thats a very perceptive question. And its been answered in a temporary manner. But who knows what these - how the situation will develop in the future. All I know is that you have a wonderful hand at Berkshire Hathaway. But you have to - you have to be able to think your way - I mean, obviously - you have to think your way through political realities. You have to think your way for what - who caused - A, you want to be on - you want to be regarded as an asset to the country, because youll find more solutions if you are an asset. You owe it to the country anyway. But beyond that, youll find more solutions than if youre regarded as evil or something, and worse yet if you deserve it. So its something thats constantly on our mind. And it needs to be in the mind of the directors. And they need to think for themselves on this rather than bow to conventional wisdom. Which - you know - in a sense, you dont want to become the cynic about life. But almost everybody that approaches you, if you have tons of resources, has got some interest in figuring out how to use your resources to their advantage. (Laughs) And thats true whether theyre in politics or whether theyre in investment banking or whether theyre selling you - well, whatever it may be that theyre selling. I dont want to do any injury to anybody. But, you know, life insurance agents see the advantage of buying life insurance. And investment managers, who get paid based on assets managed, get interested in selling you their services. Imagine if everybody in this room were following the investment advice of somebody that said, you know, for 1 percent a year, Ill tell you how to invest your money, and in 1950 - when we started in 1965, they wouldve - they would’ve said, well, buy Berkshire Hathaway. And if they were around now and they still had their 1 percent deal, theyd be collecting 8 billion dollars a year from people who arent getting any dividends from us. (Laughs) So yeah, they would have a different interest in the kind of contract they worked out with you than you would have. And the best thing to do is just pay them a commission one time and own the stock. But you have to be alert to how - what human nature does to both other people and to you. And then you, you know, if you think it - if you think it too well, and you actually listen to what Charlie has told you, youll have a big head start on most people. Charlie - theres one thing that I should mention that really is terribly interesting about Charlie. Charlie knew the importance of psychology and human behavior and incentives and all of that. He figured that out very early. And, of course, he gave some talks, even on 25 or so ways - whatever it happened to be, I dont remember the exact number - but the different ways that one person could take advantage of another by understanding how humans behave. And then, after doing a magnificent job of explaining it, he believed in understanding what others would do. But he thought it was beneath him to actually use those methods to manipulate people. Thats a really interesting human being that thinks through the psychology of human behavior and figures out, you know, how you become a great insurance salesman or manager on Wall Street or accumulative assets under management, or whatever it may be. And you get very rich by understanding the weaknesses of others, to some extent, and then decide that its very important for you to recognize these when they occur. Its very important for you to know them better than the person that actually is using them, but not - but you dont have to stoop to using them yourself. And Charlie told me that - that, you know, in his lifetime, after he figured this out, there were a couple of times when he used them. He wasnt proud of it, but he also never lied to me. So he explained to me that, you know, there were a couple times when he used some of these techniques. But he - it wasn't - he didnt plan on using them anymore. But that he also wanted me to know that if I ever did something like that, I wasnt really behaving terribly, that he allowed for the fact that humans may misbehave. So Im sure that I behaved somewhat better before my marriage than I did afterwards in my enthusiasm for different activities, like dancing or something. And he said, thats, you know, we all do it. But dont do it again. (Laughter) And so thats part of acquiring human wisdom. And speaking of human wisdom, weve just got that one book out there by Charlie. I mean Poor Charlies Almanack. And thats worth reading three or four times. I think I read Ben Grahams book about five or six times. And each time I read it, I realized that I just needed to think a little more deeply about certain things. They werent complicated or anything. But, you know, its better to - if youve got some great instruction, like you get with Charlie, its better to read it several times than to just figure youll just read every book once thats in the library.
