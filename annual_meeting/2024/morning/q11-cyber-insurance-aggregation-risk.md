## 问题

地缘政治动荡、武装冲突和贸易摩擦加剧，网络攻击风险随之上升。你如何看待面向零售、小企业、大公司以及电厂、港口、机场、核电站等关键基础设施的网络安全险？这个险种有盈利潜力吗，主要挑战是什么？

## 回答要点

1. 网络安全险已成全球至少 100 亿美元的市场，近年约 20% 的保费转化为利润。
2. 阿吉特最担心单次事件的损失规模和聚合风险，尤其某家云服务停摆可能引发巨额连带赔付。
3. 过去四五年赔付不超过每美元 40 美分，但数据不足，无法据此确定真实赔付成本。
4. 阿吉特已要求承保团队回避该险种；若客户确有需要，也要假设每张保单都在亏钱。
5. 巴菲特以 1968 年城市骚乱为例，说明「一次事件」的界定可能把上千张保单连成一体。
6. 热门险种容易让保险公司和代理人兴奋，但正如芒格所说，它可能是「老鼠药」。

## 中文全文（逐段翻译）

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:17）：好，贝基。

**Becky Quick**（01:30:20）：这个问题来自欧洲瑞士的 Karel De Gend，同时问巴菲特先生和贾因先生。「随着武装冲突数量上升、贸易关系紧张，世界政治不稳定在加剧，网络攻击的风险也在上升。你们怎么看网络安全保险？我问的是整体情况：零售、小企业、大公司，包括电厂、港口、机场、核电站这类关键基础设施。你们认为网络安全保险有盈利空间吗？主要难题是什么？」

**Ajit Jain**（01:31:06）：好，我先说。网络安全保险这几年成了很时髦的产品，目前全球至少是 100 亿美元的市场。盈利能力也相当高：总保费里至少 20% 变成了保险巨头口袋里的利润。

话虽如此，伯克希尔在承保网络风险时非常非常谨慎，原因有两个。第一，很难知道单次事故可能造成的损失量级有多大。网络损失的累积潜力——尤其如果某家云服务商停摆——可能极其巨大。没法给最坏情况设上限，这是我们害怕的地方。第二，也很难判断我们所说的「损失成本」、也就是销货成本会是多少，不只是单次损失，而是跨越时间的累计损失。迄今为止控制得还算不错：每一美元保费里，过去四五年赔款大概没超过 40 美分，留出了不错的利润空间。但没有足够的数据可以让你把帽子挂上去，说你的真实损失成本就是这个数。

所以在我们保险业务里，我告诉负责的人：我不鼓励他们承保网络安全保险。为了满足某些客户需要，确实得写一些，我就跟他们说，不管你收多少保费，你都要告诉自己，每写一张网络安全保单，你就在亏钱。亏多少可以争，但心态必须是——你不是在赚钱，是在亏钱。然后我们从那个起点往前走。

这业务预计会很大。我猜某个时点它会变得很大，但同时可能伴随着巨额损失。我们的做法是眼下先躲开，直到能拿到有意义的数据、能把帽子挂在数据上。

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:36）：你刚才这番话，正好说明了阿吉特为什么无可替代。因为承保一件事的时候，你真正要想的是：最坏能亏多少。

我记得第一次碰上这类问题，大概是 1968 年，好几个城市发生骚乱——我想是博比·肯尼迪遇刺引发的，或者马丁·路德·金遇刺，我记不清是哪一个。总之，你开的保单上有赔偿限额，可问题在于：什么算「一次事件」？如果某座城市有人被刺杀，导致全国成千上万家商户受损，而你承保了这成千上万张保单，那算一次事件，还是算一千次事件？

没有哪个领域比网络风险更容易掉进这个困境。你想想，假设每个风险你给 1000 万美元限额，你觉得没问题，真赔 1000 万你扛得住。可如果那一次事件波及一千张保单，而法院认定它们以某种方式互相关联，那你写下的东西，收的价钱远远不够，足以把公司打垮。

我跟你说，写保险的时候，大多数人都想挤进任何时髦的领域。网络保险好写，量能做得很大，中介喜欢——每写一张保单他们都有佣金。你必须有一个人管事，他明白你可能碰上做梦都想不到的风险累积，比某地一场地震还糟，只因为你手上有一大堆百万限额的保单。

而人性就是这样：多数保险公司会非常兴奋，他们的中介会非常兴奋，这东西很时髦，还挺有意思。可正如查理会说的：这可能是老鼠药。（笑声）

## 英文原文（CNBC 官方逐字稿）

**Warren Buffett**（01:30:17）：OK, Becky.

**Becky Quick**（01:30:20）：This question comes from Karel De Gend in Switzerland, in Europe. And this is for both Mr. Buffett and Mr. Jain. "As political instability in the world is growing with the rise in the number of armed conflicts and trade tension, there is also increasing risk of cyberattacks. What are your views on cybersecurity insurance? I'm asking this question, in general, for retail, small businesses, and large companies, including critical key infrastructure, such as power plants, harbors, airports, nuclear plants, et cetera. Do you see a potential for profit making in cybersecurity insurances? And what are the key challenges?"

**Ajit Jain**（01:31:06）：OK. Let me start. Cyber insurance has become a very fashionable product, these days. Over these last few years, it is, at least, a 10-billion-dollar market right now, globally. And profitability has also been fairly high. I think profitability of at least 20 percent of the total premium, has ended up as profit in the pockets of the insurance barons. Now, having said that, we at Berkshire tend to be very, very careful when it comes to taking on cyber insurance liabilities actually, for two reasons. One is it's very difficult to know what is the quantum of losses that can be subject to a single occurrence. And the aggregation potential of cyber losses, especially if some cloud operation comes to a standstill, you know, can aggregation potential can be huge. And not being able to have a worst-case cap on it is what scares us. Secondly, it's also very difficult to have some sense of what we call lost cost, or the cost of goods sold, could potentially be. It's not just for a single loss, but for losses across over time. They have been fairly well contained. Out of a hundred cents of the dollar, the premium losses over the last four of five years, I think have not been beyond forty cents on the dollar, leaving a decent profit margin. But having said that, there's not enough data to be able to hang your hat on and say what your true lost cost is. So, in our insurance operations, I have told the people running the operations is I have discouraged them from writing cyber insurance. To the extent that they need to write it so as to satisfy certain client needs, I have told them, no matter how much you charge, you should tell yourself that each time you write a cyber insurance policy, you're losing money. We can argue about how much money you're losing, but the mindset should be, you're not making money on it, you're losing money. And then, we should go from there. So it is projected to be a huge business. My guess is, at some point, it might become a huge business, but it might be associated with huge losses. And our approach is to sort of stay away from it right now, until we can have access to some meaningful data and hang our hat on data.

**Warren Buffett**（01:33:36）：Well, you've just made you’ve just heard heard why Ajit is invaluable, because When you insure something, you really want to think of what how much can you lose. And the question I remember the first time it was it happened, I think, in the 1968, when there were the riots in various cities because I think it was the Bobby Kennedy death that set it off or the Martin Luther King death. I'm not sure which one. But, in any event, when you had a policy, you have a limit in that policy. But the question is, what is one event? So, if somebody is assassinated in some town and that causes losses at thousands of businesses all over the country, if you've written all of those thousands of policies, do you have one event or do you have a thousand events? And there's no place where that kind of a dilemma enters into it more than cyber, because if you think about it, if you know let's say you're writing 10 million dollars of limit per risk. And you decide that's fine, and if you lose 10 million, you know, for some event, you can take it. But the problem is if that one event turns out to affect a thousand policies and somehow they're all linked together in some way, and the courts decide that way, you've written something that, in no way we're getting the proper price for and could break the company. And I will tell you that most people want to be in anything that's fashionable when they write insurance. And cyber's an easy issue. You can write a lot of it. The agents like it. You know, they're getting a commission on every policy they write. And you've got to have somebody in charge of things that understands that you may get an aggregation of risks that you never dreamt of and, maybe, worse than some earthquake happening some place just because you have a whole bunch of policies with a million-dollar limit. And I would say that human nature is such that most insurance companies will get very excited. And their agents will get very excited. And it's very fashionable. And it's kind of interesting. And, as Charlie would say, it may be rat poison. (Laughter)
